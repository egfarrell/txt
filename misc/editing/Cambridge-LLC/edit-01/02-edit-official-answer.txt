Barlow (2004) observes that psychological interventions are
efficacious and safe for the treatment of social anxiety
disorder. More recently, following a systematic review of the
literature and large scale meta-analysis, Mayo-Wilson et al.
(2014) concluded that cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) was
actually more effective than pharmacotherapy with selective
serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs), such as sertraline, for
the treatment of social anxiety. Additionally, while sertraline
has been regarded as the gold standard of pharmacological
interventions for social anxiety, Gordon and Melvin (2013) report
on a number of disconcerting side effects, especially in
adolescent patients, that would suggest that sertraline might not
be the ideal first line of treatment. Notwithstanding, while CBT
might be a safer and more effective treatment for social anxiety
than SSRIs, it can also be taxing in terms of resource usage.
Priyamvada et al. (2009) report a 17-session treatment regimen
for social anxiety,  with each session lasting approximately
1-hour. Morris, Mensink and Stewart (n.d.) describe a 12-session
regimen, at 2.5-hours per session. From this, we can conclude
that CBT demands a investment of time.

In the Australian context, this commitment might be highly
problematic given that clinical psychologists comprise the bulk
of cognitive behavioural therapists. According to the 2014
National Health Workforce Data Set, there were an estimated
23,878 registered (i.e. clinical) psychologists in Australia in
2014 (AIHW, 2016). This would suggest a ratio of 87 clinical
psychologists per 100,000 head of population, which is in no way
adequate in terms of the scope of the problem posed by anxiety
disorders. Moreover, not all these psychologists work in
healthcare or in clinical roles, so the true figure is no doubt
considerably lower. Training new clinical psychologists might
seem like the logical response to this shortfall; however, the
minimum training time to qualify as a clinical psychologist in
Australia is 6 years: 4 years honours and 2 years provisional
registration (AIHW, 2016). Either our approach to training
clinical psychologists must change, or we must look elsewhere to
meet the workforce demands for cognitive behavioural therapists
in order to respond to the high incidence of social anxiety.
