### Raising Queries
	- $MY_IMAGES/misc/editing/example-author-queries.jpg
    - Do NOT ask authors ...
        - about issues you can decide for yourself or can refer
          to your line manager or client for guidance, e.g.
          typographic style; basic spelling, punctuation and
          grammar; house style.
    - DO ask authors questions ...
        - you cannot answer yourself, e.g.
        - because the text is ambiguous or you are not
          a specialist in the subject.

    - If you think you know how to correct or make the text
      clearer, suggesting changes can save time.

    - CHECK STATEMENTS
        - if think the info isnt clear or may be inaccurate.
        - Use up-to-date and authoritative sources: Wikipedia and
          other online sources may be a starting point but they
          are not necessarily authoritative.
        - Be aware, that authors may have quoted from a different
          edition of the Bible or works of Shakespeare etc, than
          the source you have consulted. Include this possibility
          in your query.

    - PHRASE
        - suggested longer changes in the author’s voice, not
          your own. To do this, study the pattern of the author’s
          writing, eg the length, complexity and syntax of their
          sentences and their use of modifiers. Try to make your
          changes as economically as possible.

    - ASK AUTHORS TO CONFIRM
        - suggested changes that might alter the factual content
          of the text, or its meaning or nuance.
        - If you have altered, or suggested altering,
          information, it is usually helpful to tell the author
          the source you have used.
        - When you ask for confirmation of all such changes,
          authors have the opportunity to agree or to revise the
          text.

    - MAKE YOUR QUERIES ...
        - polite,
        - focused,
        - as concise as clarity allows,
        - and objective.

    - NEUTRAL
        - Your queries should be about the material, not the
          author.
        - Yes: 'The text says...'
        - No:  'You say...'
