Theoretical physics is in crisis. Following years of success
the field has stagnated and researchers are feeling
desperate. Experimental tests no longer have the power to
verify the latest theories and the delicate balance between
mathematical theory and physical experiment has been broken.
The result? Wildly speculative ideas such as multiple
universes and astrophysical worm-holes are now being
promoted as hard science, even in the absence of
experimental evidence. So how has this impasse been reached? 


In the golden age of the early 20th century, Physics was
a simpler affair. Theorists examined the results of the
latest experiments for clues while experimentalists eagerly
tested the offerings of the theorists. Each group needed the
other. Theories guided the experimentalists on what tools to
build while the vivid imaginations of theorists were held in
check by cold experimental fact. Working together each group
helped build the modern edifice of Physics, standing on the
pillars of General Relativity and Quantum Mechanics. 

But by the late 1960's and early 70's the situation started
to change. The standard model of physics was born 

Some may dispute this simplified view but there is no doubt
that a symbiotic relationship between both camps yielded
impressive results. Einstein may have conceived of
relativity while staring out his office window but it only
became accepted after experimental confirmation in the

Quantum Mechanics was born from the results of thousands of
