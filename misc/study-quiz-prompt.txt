### Extra hardness
Make some questions that combine ideas from different parts of the notes (not just isolated facts).

### Basic prompt

- Context:
- I am doing independent study in foundational physics (QM, GR,
  philosophy of physics, mathematical physics). I keep informal
  notes in a .txt file.

- Task:
- Using the uploaded notes, generate multiple-choice questions to
  test my understanding.

- Instructions:

1. Create 5–10 questions.
2. Each question should:
   - Be short, but include a bit of context (not just a one-liner).
   - Clearly relate to a specific idea or distinction in the notes.
   - Test conceptual understanding, not memorisation.

3. Each question must have 4 answer options (A–D).

4. IMPORTANT: Include “trap answers”.
   - At least 2–3 options per question should be plausible.
   - Trap answers should:
     • Slightly misstate a correct idea
     • Confuse two related concepts
     • Use correct terminology incorrectly
     • Reflect common misunderstandings
   - Avoid obviously wrong or trivial answers.

5. Ensure:
   - Only one clearly correct answer per question.
   - The correct answer is not always the longest or most detailed.
   - Avoid patterns (e.g. always B or C).

6. Style:
   - Match the tone of thoughtful revision questions.
   - Use terminology from the notes where possible.
   - Keep things concise but meaningful.

7. After the questions, include an answer key with brief explanations:
   - Explain why the correct answer is right.
   - Briefly explain why each trap answer is wrong.

- Goal:
- These questions should help me detect whether I truly
  understand the distinctions in my notes, not just recognise
  familiar phrases.
