### 01 General
    - It’s not just about teaching and training, it’s about the
      LEARNING that takes place as a result.
        - You can teach as much as you wish, but if learning
          didnt take place then your teaching wasn't successful.

    - Becoming a good teacher includes being enthusiastic and
      passionate about your subject, being approachable and
      taking pride in your work, which should all be conveyed to
      your learners when you are with them

    - The way you dress, act, respond to questions and offer
      support, will also influence your learners.

    - If asked personal questions, try not to give out any
      information: by remaining a professional, and not becoming
      too friendly, you will RETAIN RESPECT.

    - Personal qualities such as arriving early so that you are
      organised, and smiling when your learners enter will help.

    - At all times, you should act in a professional manner and
      give a good impression; for example, time keeping, dress
      and behaviour towards your learners and others.

    - BOUNDARIES:
        - When you are with learners, you need to remain in
          control, be fair and ethical and not demonstrate any
          favouritism towards particular learners; for example,
          by giving one more support than the others.

    - BOUNDARIES:
        - You might feel it sensible to make a telephone call to
          a learner who has been absent but making regular calls
          would be inappropriate. Giving your personal telephone
          number to learners could be seen as encouraging
          informal contact.

    - You should never feel you have to resolve a situa?on on
      your OWN, there should be others who can help if necessary.

    - PROFESSIONALISM:
        - what do you think it means in relation to your role?
        - How does your role involve working with other
          professionals?

    - To teach effectively involves more than approaches you use
      to teach your subject, i.e. discussions, group work and
      paired activities.
        - There are many other factors before/ after the session.
        - This includes planning logically what you will cover in
          your sessions, preparing materials and resources,
          assessing that learning has happened, giving feedback,
          keeping records and evaluating yourself and the
          experiences your learners have had.
        - Never underestimate the amount of TIME you will need to
          dedicate to the role.


### 02 TLA Cycle
    - $MY_IMAGES/teaching/AET/TLA-cycle.jpg
    - All stages should happen for learning to be effective.
    - QA should take place continuously to ensure all aspects are
      being delivered and assessed well.

    - IDENTIFYING NEEDS
        - Find your learners needs, your organisations needs and
          your own needs too.
        - Find out why learners are taking the programme and
          their expectations.
        - Carry out initial and diagnostic assessments, agree
          individual learning plans, ensuring learners are able
          to progress to their chosen destination

    - PLANNING LEARNING
        - prepare teaching and learning plans, materials to
          ensure you cover the requirements of the programme,
          liase with others

    - FACILITATING LEARNING
        - teaching, training and facilitating learning using
          various approaches / resources.
        - Obtaining feedback to make improvements

    - ASSESSING LEARNING
        - checking your learners have gained the necessary
          skills, knowledge and understanding
        - Use formal and informal types and methods of
          assessment, obtaining feedback to make improvements

    - EVALUATING LEARNING
        - Obtaining feedback from others, evaluating your role,
          and all aspects involved with the learning process in
          order to make improvements for the future.
        - Evaluation could also take place atier each stage of
          the cycle

    - QA runs through it all. Good practice:
        - IQA: College will manitor and sample aspects of
          everything you do
        - External QA: person visits from organisation that
          awards the qual

### 03 Boundaries

    - PROFESSIONAL BOUNDARIES: you need to work within these
      boundaries and it’s important not to overstep them. eg:
      becoming too personal or friendly with your learners.
        - Boundaries are about knowing where your role as
          a teacher or trainer STOPS. You should be able to work
          within the limits of that role, but know that it’s ok
          to ask for help. Don’t try to take on too much, or
          carry out something that is part of someone else’s
          role.

    - "BOUND BY": Other boundaries include the things you are
      bound by. Eg, policies + procedures, administrative work
      you are expected to complete, or a lack of funding or
      resources. These boundaries can be interpreted as the
      negative aspects of your roles and responsibilities.


### 04 Quality Assurance
    - IQA: Internal Quality Assurance
    - STANDARDISATION is a process to enable consistency and
      fairness of all learner activities. If more than one person
      is teaching/assessing the same qual, its important that all
      agree how to interpret the requirements, deliver the
      content and make valid and reliable assessment decisions.
    - Attending a standardisation meeting is also an chance to
      develop/share good practice and be part of a team.
        - Double-marking can take place too..

### 06 Legislation
    - Legislation depends on CONTEXT/ENVIRONMENT you teach.
        - Regulators (eg Ofsted) who inspect provision
        - Awarding Bodies: will QA their qualifications
        - Funding Agencies: will want data and stats

    - Children Act (2004) "Well-being" is used in the Act to
      define five 'Every Child Matters' outcomes:
        - be healthy
        - stay safe
        - enjoy and achieve
        - make a positive contribution
        - achieve economic well-being

    - Copyright Designs and Patents Act (1988) relates to the
      copying, adapting and distributing of materials. Licensees
      may be needed

    - Data Protection Act (1998) applies to the processing of
      information relating to individuals, including the
      obtaining, holding, use or disclosure of such information.
      It was amended in 2003 to include electronic data.

    - Equality Act (2010) It provides rights for people not to be
      directly discriminated against or harassed because they
      have an association with a disabled person or because they
      are wrongly perceived as disabled.
        - There are 9 protected characteristics: age, disability,
          gender, gender identity, race, religion and belief,
          sexual orientation, marriage and civil partnership,
          maternity and pregnancy.

    - Freedom of Information Act (2000) allows learners to
      request the info public bodies hold about them.

    - Health and Safety at Work etc Act (1974) imposes
      obligations on all staff within an organisation.
        - Risk assessments should be carried out where necessary.
        - In the event of an accident, an investigation by the
          Health and Safety Executive may result in the
          prosecution of individuals found to be negligent as
          well as the organisation.

    - Protection of Children Act (POCA) (1999) Local Authorities
      are responsible for making enquiries if contacted about
      child abuse.

    - Safeguarding Vulnerable Groups Act (2006) created a vetting
      and barring scheme to decide who should be barred from
      working with children and vulnerable adults. Teachers may
      need to have their criminal background checked via the
      Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS).

### 07 Regulation
    - Regulations are rules that state the mandatory requirements
      that must be met (if applicable to your job role)
    - Public bodies / agencies create these regulatory
      requirements. Eg Ofqual is not directly controled by the
      Government but report to Parliament.  They are responsible
      for maintaining standards about qualifications, exams and
      assessments in England.

    Other regulations include:
        - Control of Substances Hazardous to Health (COSHH)
          Regulations (2002) applies to working with hazardous
          materials.
        - Food Hygiene Regulations (2006) applies farming,
          manufacturing, distributing and retailing food.
        - Health and Safety (Display Screen Equipment) applies to
          using display screen equipment e.g. computers.
        - Manual Handling Operation Regulations (1992) relates to
          risks  of manual handling injury.
        - Privacy and Electronic Communications (EC Directive)
          Regulations 2003 applies to all electronic
          communications such as e-mail and mobile phone
          messages.
        - Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous
          Occurrences (RIDDOR) Regulations (1995) requires
          specified workplace incidents to be reported.
        - Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order (2005) places the
          responsibility on individuals within an organisation to
          carry out risk assessments to identify, manage and
          reduce the risk of fire

### 08 Codes of Practice
    - Usually produced by organisations and professional bodies
    - They may be mandatory or voluntary
    - Your organisation will have documented codes of practice
      such as:
        - acceptable use of IT
        - behaviour
        - code of conduct
        - conflict of interest
        - disciplinary
        - dress
        - environmental awareness
        - sustainability
        - timekeeping
    - Also "The Professional Standards" were developed in 2014 by
      the Education and Training Foundation (ETF). They are
      divided into 3 main areas:
        - Professional values & attitudes
        - Professional knowledge & understanding
        - Professional skills


### 09 Teacher Roles (Concise Guide)
    - As a teacher it is important you:
    - understand the scheme/schedule of work
    - understand the assessment criteria
    - design programmes of study
    - carry out initial assessments
    - communicate clearly to others
    - provide a safe learning environment
    - promote appropriate behaviour for learning; manage the groups
    - and their learning
    - promote inclusion
    - be knowledgeable in the subject
    - know how to gain additional support for you and your learners
    - be organised
    - carry out summative and formative assessments; provide
    - appropriate feedback
    - keep records of progress; monitor atiendance and punctuality;
    - enter learners for examinations.

### 10 Teacher Responsibilities 
    - more "personal"
    - adhering and applying policy and legislation;
    - complete your own CPD so you stay up to date; 
    - setting and maintaining high standards;
    - collaborating with others in the team;
    - being dependable, trustworthy and conscientious;
    - abiding by any codes of conduct for your sector; 
    - doing a good job such as differentiating and personalising
      learning to meet individual needs; 
    - keeping accurate records;
    - able to refer learners for additional support eg finance.
