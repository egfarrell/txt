### THIS IS YOUR BRAIN ON COMMUNICATION
    - PHYSICAL entrainment/alignment
        - metronomes balanced on wood all click in phase

    - NEURAL entrainment/alignment (during story-telling)
        - is it the _sounds_ of the speakers words?
            - or is it the _words_?
            - is it the _meaning_ of the words?
        - answer: only when we have deeper coherent story-telling
          does the entrainment spread deeper into the brains (eg
          frontal AND parietal cortex)
            - so we think its the _MEANING_ that induces this
              response
            - (they also played the same story to 2 different
              ethnic groups, English and Russian, and their brain
              scans responded similarly for higher order brain
              functions, although not at the auditory level - cos
              of languages differences)
    - hypothesis: this sort of entrainment/alignment is necessary
      for communication
    - but now another question: what happens in the speakers
      brain when they talk?
        - to their surprise, they saw that all the (complex)
          patterns in the listeners brains actually came from the
          speakers brain
        - so production and comprehension rely on very similar
          processes
        - and the stronger the similarity between the speakers
          brain and the listeners brain, the better the
          communication
    - so can we use this research outcome to transmit a memory
      from the speakers brain to the listeners brain?
        - well they found you need _COMMON GROUND_. (and
          understanding and shared belief systems)
        - If you tell a story to a USA audience and use the term
          "hackney cab" instead of "cab" you'll lose the
          connection.
        - sometimes people understand the exact same story in
          different ways if they have been "primed" differently:
        - eg before a story, they told one group that the wife
          was having an affair, and another group that the
          husband was very jealous. The groups responded to the
          story very differently.
    - so what are the implications if half the population watch
      FOX NEWS and the other half NYT?
    - Maybe we need to re-introduce DIALOGUE.
    - Two way communication to keep "coupled" to other people!

    - Engaging with students is about being able to conveying
      information. If you can tap into how students brains
      communicate, by telling stories, you can better engage all
      you students at once. This will help you to create a more
      effective learning environment.


### Teach girls bravery not perfection
    - Speaker ran for congress
    - actually failed and got 19% of the vote
    - but it was the first time she done something brave and
      didn't worry about being _perfect_
    - but as school children, boys are raised to be "brave" and
      take risks, and girls are raised to be perfect
    - The speaker started a company to teach "girls to code"

    - The role of FE teachers can be a varied one, with many
      elements making a great lecturer. But at this formative
      stage for students, this talk shows that women especially
      need to be encouraged to take risks in the classroom,
      striving for excellence instead of perfection, failures and
      all.

    - Your role as a new teacher is to provide equal opportunity
      representation and opportunities for the women in your
      classroom, encouraging bravery, not perfection.


### Creating a safe space in the classroom
    - The danger of hiding who you are
    - The speaker is gay and after 16 years came out
    - Even with diversity programs at work, most employees hid
      some aspect of themselves to fit in
    - people who hide their identity have a reduced life
      expectancy (up to 12 years?)
    - there are social consequnces of staying silent
    - Toni Morrison quote: "There are more scary things inside
      than outside"

    - Building a better classroom often means making sure that
      you are creating a learning environment where everyone can
      feel able to speak. Open communication is one of the
      foundations for growth and learning. For LGBT students,
      their sexuality can create a barrier to open dialogue, one
      that teachers can break down by ensuring their classroom is
      a safe space.


### Make stress your friend
    - New job means new stresses. Getting to grips with a new
      career in FE causes extra pressures, not to mention
      learning to speak in front of students on a daily basis.

    - Kelly McGonigal says: those who viewed stress as harmful
      were damaged by it, but those who viewed it as not harmful
      were less likely to be hurt by stress. Even if they
      experienced more stress than other people. She argues that
      not fearing stress is your key to a healthy life in and out
      of the FE classroom.

    - A study tracked 30,000 US adults for 8 years and asked them
      how much stress they had. They also asked them if they
      thought stress was bad for their health.

    - Result: those under stress had a 43% increased risk of
      dying - but only if they thought stress was bad for your
      health!

    - Actually those who thought stress wasn't bad for you had
      one of the lowest death rates!

    - so can changing how you think about stress make you
      healthier? yes!

    - imagine if you were told that sweating and increased heart
      rate was just making your body ready to meet the challenge?

    - and if you view stress differently, your blood vessels
      don't constrict when feeling anxiety!

    - one good side-effect of stress: it makes you social!
      (hormone: oxytocin) Oxytocin is pumped out when you're
      under stress, just as much as adrenaline. And oxytocin is
      a natural anti-inflammatory, is good for your blood
      vessels, and helps heart cells re-generate.

    - Oxytocin can make us reach out to others which is also good
      for our health

    - Harmful effects of stress aren't inevitable. How you think
      about it and how you act (helping others) creates a biology
      of courage, and when you connect with others under stress
      you can create resilience.

    - Stress gives us access to our (compassionate) heart.

    - Question: If someone is making a choice between a stressful
      and non-stressful job does it matter which they go for?
    - It's equally wise to go for the stressful job as long as
      you believe you can handle it?
        - Speaker: Well we know that chasing _MEANING_ in your
          life is better than avoiding discomfort. So I'd say
          thats the best way to make decisions.
        - Go after what creates meaning in your life and trust
          yourself to handle the stress that follows.
