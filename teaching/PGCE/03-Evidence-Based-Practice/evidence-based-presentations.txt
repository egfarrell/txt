### Peer tutoring

    working in pair and small grops
        1. older learner takes the lead (cross-age)
        2. Maths peer assisted learning
        2. reciprocal peer tutoring - swap roles

    High impact for low cost (£80 per pupil per year)
    teaching assistants: high cost, low effect
    feedback / peer tutoring: low cost high effect

    equivalent to 5 months additional learning
    works for all learners, but biggest impact SEN
    consolidate, not introduce material
    certain trial: new peer tutoring not effective (but maybe doing
    peer tutoring already)

    Conclusion: effective, promotes interaction, low cost

### Teaching assistants
    role: adults who support teacher
    duties can vary widely: 

most evidence shows TA have positive impact (depends on
personality of TA)

Usually most positive when TA and teacher work together

Cost: TA costs 18,000 p.a. Overall costs are high.

positive impact: increased learner retention, better behaviour,
allows all students to feel included

negative impact: too much support. unable to build positive
relationships, sometimes tension between TA and teacher

### digital technology
    - 2 main areas for teachers
        - learning platform
        - whiteboards

    advantages:
        = +4 months
        - can learn outside the class

    disadvantes:
        - high initial cost
        - continual support costs and training

### Streaming:
    setting: same year group for same subject
    streaming: blanket grouping regardless of subject


    advantages
        less distraction
        improve results
        more competitive environment

    Disadvantages
        put in the wrong group
        reduce motivation/competition
        labelling for life
        
    evidence
        - streaming: small negative impact on low performing
        students but small positive impact for high achievers
        - for maths/english: no relationshop for streaming

    other factors:
        - who is included in the analysis
        - where it was carried out

    implement:
        - ensure resources in place
        - ensure all pupil performance is accurate
        - stream by topic not by class


Mastery learning
    - remove idea that all students on same timeframe
    - aims sto differentiate more: master something before moving
      on
    - change mindset to fixed: I can't grow, to growth mindset.

    - started with Aristotle
    - USA 1920s research: not enough evidence to roll it out
    - Skinner: provides resources to move at their own pace
    - Salaman Khan: construction Alalogy

    - eventually everyone will catch up.

    advantages:
        higher achievement
        acquire skills before moving on
        breaks cycle of failure

    disadvantages
        - not all make the same progress
        - may have to hold back some students
        - more work for teachers (tracking)
        - need more resources
        - how do you measure? regularly test?
