### 2 Force Laws, Lewis Structures and Resonance
    - what force holds atoms together
    - because if we really understand the force that holds them
      together, we got chemsitry solved  
    - Newtonian force: no mechanism for it. No springs. Action at
      a distance.
    - Newton Optiks 1717: He posed extra "queries" about
      chemistry at the end. He speculated that electricy could
      attract at very small distances! 
    - Also his quote: "For Nature is very consonant and
      conformable to her self". Yes!
    - Binding energies (Potential) at 1.5Å:  
    - Coulombic  : Proton - Electron, 216 Kcal/mol 
    - Magnetic  : Electron Spin 0.00014 Kcal/mol
    - "Strong" binding : Proton-Neutron in nucleus 5 x 10^5
      kcal/mol)
    - Gravitational   : 2 Carbon atoms 3 ∙ 10^-32 kcal/mol)
    - Chemical bond  : Carbon - Carbon 90 Kcal/mol, not far from
      the Coulombic attraction
    - Force laws and molecular structure:  
        - you stretch a string of atoms, how far until the string
          snaps?  
        - for a "SPRING"  
            - `F = -k ∆x` (Force proportional to displacement)
            - `E = k/2 (∆x)^2`  
            - The potential energy is a parabola
            - the SLOPE of the parabola = the FORCE  
            - add another atom, there's a point where the two
              parabolas overlap: 
            - the sum gives a SINGLE "balanced minimum"  
        - for ELECTRICAL charges  
            - different force law  
            - `F = k / (∆x)^2`  
            - `E = -k/(2∆x)`  
            - no balanced minimum, not stable  
            - no singe minimum, double minimum  
            - because inverse law?  
    - So with springs (ie Hooks law) you might make a stable
      polyatomic molecule from point atoms (but not with ions or
      magnets) 
    - But Hookes Law bonds would never break  
    - Morse Potential: not a law of nature, proposed 1929,
      mathematically convenient  
    - the chain pops when the inflection points cross. Snaps at
      the inflection point where the force changes from direct to
      inverse.
    - force laws make a lot of difference how atoms behave  

    - What are bonds?  
    - 19th century experiments led to Valence numbers  
    - but they wondered: why do elements differ? Why don't they
      all have the same valence  
    - Gertrude and Rober Robinson (1917)  
        - latent valence, hidden valence loop  
    - Electron discovered 1897  
    - GN Lewis associated electrons with valence  
    - if electrons at the corners of a cube, then 8 a special
      number because all corners occupied  
    - octets predicted shared pair bonding: two "7"s join
      together and share an edge  
    - or two oxygens (6 electrons): push faces together  
    - or nitrogen (5 electrons): collapse one edge to
      a tetrahedron. then go from cubic octet to tetrahedral
      octet.
    - Bookkeeping of "formal" charges. each atom is assigned half
      interest in bonding pairs
    - Resonance is when the true structure is inbeween he
      structures you draw (Nirogen in the middle)  
    - Lewis: failure of notation, true structure is a single
      minimum  
    - Must choose between Resonance and Equilibrium
