### Geometrical Methods of Mathematical Physics
- Bernard Schutz
- $MY_BOOKS/Physics/Spacetime/Schutz-geometrical-methods.pdf

### Books I recommend :
    - Frankel      Geometry of Physics: Intro Tensors and Manifolds
    - Wasserman    Tensors and Manifolds: With Applications to Physics https://www.amazon.com/Tensors-Manifolds-Applications-Robert-Wasserman/dp/0199564825
    - Tu           Introduction to Smooth Manifolds
    - Lee          Introduction to Manifolds
    - Stillwell    Naive Lie Theory
    - Tapp         Matrix Groups for Undergraduates
    - Hall         Lie Groups, Lie Algebras Representations: Elementary Intro Lie groups
    - Weintraub    Differential Forms: Integration on Manifolds and Stokes's Theorem
    - Darling      Differential Forms and Connections
    - Morita       Lucid and concise "Geometry of Differential Forms"


- The exposition in this book is concise, intuitive, quite lucid.
  It's good for getting the larger view, relating key
  mathematical concepts to applications in physics.

- As an autodidact, I've found it productive to use this book
  along with a more detailed treatment of some topics, e.g.
  tensors, representation theory for groups and the relationship
  between that and Lie groups and algebras.

- I also found it enlightening to supplement this with the superb
  expositions in "Frankel's The Geometry of Physics" and in
  Wasserman's phenomenal "Tensors and Manifolds: With
  Applications to Physics"

- With some foundation/supplementation, the book can be used to
  solidify one's intuitive understanding and understand how
  they're used in physics.
    - One can also use the book to identify weakness in
      understanding and see what needs to be studied.

- In addition, Schutz provides solutions or hints to the
  exercises. It's a comparatively quick read and overall, quite
  enjoyable. Highly recommended for self-study but see the
  caveats below.

- Despite my high praise, I think this book is best used as
  a supplement to more thorough treatments of the math covered
  (mainly, differential manifolds, forms, Lie derivatives, Lie
  groups).

- Schutz states the aim of "this book is to teach mathematics,
  not physics".
    - In general, I do not think one's math should be learned
      solely from physics books (having experienced the
      inadequate job done on mathematics in typical GR and QM
      books).
    - This book is no exception despite its exceptional lucidity.
      The claim that one only needs reasonable familiarity with
      "vector calculus, calculus of many variables, matrix
      algebra ... and a little operator theory ..." is overly
      optimistic.
    - In some narrow sense, it might be true this is all that's
      needed to follow the basic logic of the mathematical
      development, but to really understand the text, I believe
      some background is required in differential geometry, forms
      and Lie groups -- preferably acquired from math books
      written by mathematicians.

- As I said, despite the caveats, I found the book illuminating
  and enjoyable to read. I often return to it quite to refresh my
  memory.
