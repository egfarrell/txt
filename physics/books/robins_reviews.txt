### Hidden in Plain Sight 3: The Secret Of Time (Paperback)
    - I found interesting things in this book - the discussion of
      Newton's bucket, Machs principle and some recent thoughts
      on this problem. Also some recent thinking on the big bang,
      and even the strange, though opaque, implication of time
      moving at light velocity.
    - It's well written and seems very logically developed. But
      this is illusory. The problem is that sitting in the midst
      of the book, never addressed, is such a massive gorilla,
      namely the ubiquitous, standard orthodoxy on `special
      relativity` with its massive contradictions, that undercuts
      the entire book.

    - Thomas is an adherent of what Bergson called
      "half-relativity" (Duration and Simultaneity, 1922).

    - Thomas highlights the Hafele-Keating experiment: placed
      clocks on a jet, the jet circles the globe, lands, the
      clocks are slower, compared to clocks at rest on the ground
      (via an amount specified by the Lorentz equation). End of
      story.

    - Time dilation, something Thomas premiers and uses
      throughout the book, is proved again. But this is half the
      story. There is the nagging "reciprocity of reference
      systems" feature of relativity - a word (reciprocity), that
      is "hidden," i.e., never seen in the Thomas book!

    - Time-distance intervals are invariant in relativity only
      via their embedding in the reciprocal transformations
      demanded by two observers, each of which can declare
      himself at rest, the other in motion.

    - Without this `reciprocal embedding` of the Lorentz
      transformations to achieve invariance, SR does not exist.

    - This reciprocal embedding of Lorentz's equations was the
      basis for physics' acceptance of Einstein as an explanation
      for Michelson-Morley.

    - In contrast, Lorentz had offered electro-dynamical
      arguments along with his equations - a physical model as an
      underpinning for the shortening of the apparatus arm in the
      direction of the ether motion. But Physics rejected this.
    - The shortening of distance was considered merely "a
      measurement effect." That is, the shortening was NOT
      considered an ontological effect, i.e., not physical,
      actual, or real.

    - Look up the "pole-barn" paradox, where a pole, normally too
      long to fit in a barn, now fits inside when moving at high
      velocity. But the barn can be considered in motion, the
      pole at rest; the pole now does not fit.
        - aside: GPT says the 'relativity of simultaneity'
          resolves this paradox.

    - Thus we are warned solemnly that we cannot take the
      distance changes (shortening) as ontological or real.
      Indeed, if we did, Einstein's explanation of
      Michelson-Morley is destroyed.
    - But distance and time changes in the equations are
      `compensatory`: time for distance, distance for time. That
      is, time units expand while distance units contract. So
      time changes cannot be deemed ontological or real, while
      distance changes are deemed non-ontological. Both must be
      of the same category.

    - Hence, given reciprocity, the observers at the airport
      should have stepped forward and said, "No, we were in
      motion, your jets were at rest, it is our clocks that are
      retarded."
        - But this didn't happen. Why? Because it is obviously
          absurd. Reciprocity just doesn't work here. It is the
          jet-carried clocks that really are slowed. Processes
          really do slow down with velocity; this is real.
        - SR just cannot be used to explain this.
        - Answer: Fix on observer `X` as stationary, observer `Y`
          in motion. Period. Do not consider the inverse. Do
          a one-way application of Lorentz. Use
          "half-relativity". Which is no relativity.

    - The problem, the unequal and illegitimate treatment of
      "time" changes as real (within SR), began in 1911 when
      Langevin described the twin paradox. The twin on the rocket
      comes back to the earth, having aged less.
    - But reciprocity destroys this story. The twin on the rocket
      could have declared himself stationary, the earth and
      earth-twin moving away and coming back. The earth-twin now
      ages less. Or, we should say, as is the case, these
      relativistic effects, both for time and distance, are only
      measurement effects, they have no ontological (real,
      physical status).

    - The flow of time, as Bergson argued, is invariant to both
      twins (one can see "The Mists of Special Relativity" for
      more on this).

    - But physics at the time had real, actual, physical effects
      to explain, namely the increasing lifespan of mesons with
      increasing velocity.
    - The one-way application of the Lorentz equations explained
      this nicely. Again, we must ignore that a tiny observer on
      the meson could have declared himself at rest, the
      observing physicist in motion. Now SR cannot explain it.

    - Physics needs a new, true explanation of the `retardation
      of processes` with velocity. You will not find one in
      Thomas. Hence, the only attempt by Thomas to address this
      reciprocity barrier is noted in this context of the twin
      paradox, and he takes the standard out:
        - there is an "asymmetry," the twin on the rocket
          experiences acceleration, acceleration causes the
          aging.

    - It is a standard part of the physics game re SR when
      reciprocity implications are pointed out: throw things into
      the GR.

    - There is absolutely no theory in physics that explains why
      an asymmetry can be imported into SR and the theory still
      be employed, but it is worse, for acceleration cannot
      possibly have the unique properties assigned to it by
      Einstein and current physics.

    - Velocity is the rate of change of position - the 1st
      derivative. Acceleration is the rate of change of the rate
      of change - the 2nd derivative.
    - If, as Bergson noted (and Wang indeed so derived, search:
      Ling Jun Wang Clock Paradox), we cannot derive the
      generalized Lorentz equation for `t'` in the context of
      acceleration, if we cannot integrate over infinitesimals,
      we have undercut all of physics and the calculus.

    - But with a generalized Lorentz equation for `t'` in the
      context of acceleration in hand, we are now thrown right
      back into the reciprocity problem - whether an accelerating
      rocket or not, the twins are equally subject to
      reciprocity.

    - Behind the invocation of acceleration as something absolute
      is the idea that we are now dealing with a "force."
    - But force, as `F=ma`, is a function of acceleration, and
      acceleration, like velocity, is a function of the rate of
      change of position. Force then is equally as relative with
      movements; it is as relative a quantity as mass or
      distance.

    - The supposed existence of the "block universe" - the
      structure Thomas discusses and assumes throughout the book,
      where all events (future, past, present) are laid out in
      a vast, frozen structure, rests totally on the supposed
      reality of the relativity of simultaneity, and this in turn
      trades on the complete misconstrual of relativity as being
      able to explain "time retardation" effects as real or
      ontological - as opposed to mere measurement effects.

    - Since these time changes are "real" and supposedly
      explained by SR, the relativity of simultaneity is
      considered "real."
    - The frozen block structure begs an explanation for our
      "illusion" of the passage of time. It has engendered
      theories of "consciousness fields" coursing along the world
      lines of this structure (e.g., Smythies, JJC Smart) - all
      with absurdities.

    - Thomas refers to his earlier theory of the "specialness" of
      each moment to explain the experience of time's motion - my
      day in 1952 is special, the day in 1963 is special, in
      1982... This, as I noted in the review of that book, is
      easily taken to absurdity. We must explain the experience
      of flows - a fly buzzing by, a leaf twisting and falling.

    - Since we are trying to explain the experience of flow by
      the "specialness" of each moment or instant along a static
      world-line in the block, then each "instant" in such a flow
      must be "special," and with not much effort, we would
      arrive at the logical consequence that the duration of each
      "special" instant could only be that of a mathematical
      point, i.e., no duration, therefore, no flow.

    - Thus we are back to a timeless, unchanging block where each
      mathematical point/instant along a world-line is "special",
      there is no explanation whatsoever of the experience of
      flow.

    - In this book, Thomas also introduces the "IGUS,"
      fundamentally a little computer/robot with a series of
      registers (based on Hartle's notion) where each register
      contains a successively more previous moment.

    - This now explains the experience of flow too, I guess.
      Ignoring the naïve theory of the perception of
      time-extended events, the obvious problem (except
      apparently to a relativist) is that the IGUS is just
      a bundle of static world lines in the static, frozen block.

    - Why should the IGUS experience anything, anything more than
      would another bundle of world lines, say, a rock? But the
      committed relativist can believe anything.

    - Consider the notion of Thomas that we have car, `X`, in
      motion, pulling a cable that runs a windmill. Car `Y` pulls
      alongside and equals `X`s speed. `X` is now at rest
      relative to `Y`. Per Thomas, the cable on `X` should now go
      slack. Really? Thomas should do the experiment.

    - The books employing this standard, but utterly wrong
      interpretation of relativity are ubiquitous and unending.

    - Even Smolin (Time Reborn), in attempting to introduce real
      motion back into physics, uses the same interpretation of
      SR. We will never reconcile this crippled version of SR to
      QM (as Thomas tried to do in his first book).

    - This review should have made it obvious that the problem is
      obvious. We need better. A theory with no ontological
      status is useless as a theory of time. There are real,
      physical explanations - actual physical models for the
      retardation of processes with velocity. We need real
      thought.
