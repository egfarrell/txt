- This is written for graduate students considering
  a dissertation in Philosophy of Physics.

- Here are pointers on getting up to speed on the relevant
  background material. The suggestions are drawn mainly from
  philosophy and physics; for a background in pure mathematics
  see:
    - ./philosopher_physics_maths_needed.txt


There are several ways of doing philosophy of physics.

1. Some philosophers such as Albert and Maudlin emphasize the
   `metaphysical` implications of physics, and forgo in-depth
   discussion of foundational (mathematical) issues.

2. Others like David Malament emphasize rigorous, mathematically
   valid proofs of philosophically significant claims.

3. And others such as John Norton tightly integrate philosophical
   and _historical_ issues.

- But these main divisions aren't sufficient to describe the
  richness of the field. Eg, Earman's work is highly technical,
  but also concerned with metaphysical and historical issues.

- Oliver Pooley maintains a page of philosophy of physics
  homepages. See also the list compiled by Christian Wüthrich.

- Handbook of Philosophy of Physics
    - A two-volume Handbook was published in 2007.
    - Tries to give a broad overview of the whole field. However,
      they are somewhat 'slanted'; they are less _dialectical_
      than many of the journal articles, and don't include
      articles by some more prominent metaphysically oriented
      philosophers (e.g. Albert and Maudlin).
    - GPT:
        - In this context, 'dialectical' refers to a method of
          `discussion` and `reasoning` that involves the exchange
          of arguments and counterarguments. 
            - It typically explores different perspectives and
              uncovers deeper truths through this kind of
              interactive, dialogic process.
        - Here 'less dialectical' implies they might lack
          a certain level of engagement with opposing viewpoints
          or a comprehensive debate-style exploration of ideas.
          Instead, the volumes might present a more one-sided or
          straightforward narrative, missing the dynamic
          interplay of different philosophical perspectives that
          is often found in journal articles. This could be why
          some metaphysically oriented philosophers of physics
          are not included, as their work might involve more of
          this dialectical approach.

# Algebraic Quantum Field Theory

    - AQFT is not a topic, but a collection of tools to reason
      about the structure of Quantum Field theories.
      Consequently, much of the background necessary for AQFT is
      mathematical.

    - One needs a strong grounding in functional analysis,
      specifically the theory of operator algebras. AQFT is also
      increasingly using category theory, and would be helpful to
      know that as well.

    - Update 2010: Laura Ruetsche's book on the philosophy of QFT
      will soon be published by Oxford. This highly recommended
      book sets its discussion in the context of AQFT.
        - $MY_BOOKS/Physics/QM/QFT/Ruetsche-Philosophical-Aspects-QFT-01.pdf
