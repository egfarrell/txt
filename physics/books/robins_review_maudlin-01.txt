"Physics as Geometry...of a Pretty Swampy Space"
Stephen E Robins

- This book is interesting. Maudlin starts with the base problem
    - the problem of absolute space and absolute motion. 
    - First, Newton's position on this absoluteness: 
        - his spinning bucket proof, his two globes linked by
          a cable, his argument with Leibniz, Leibniz versus
          Clarke, Galilean relativity. 

- Soon we are introduced to the general schema which informs
  Maudlin's view: a stack of time slices of space-time (modified
  later in its SR version). 

- A point in the same position (same space coordinates), slice
  after slice, is a motionless point over time - it traces
  a straight line through the stack of slices. 

- A point in linear motion (one space coordinate changing) traces
  a straight line at an angle relative to the former. 

- A point accelerating (a force must be applied for this) traces
  a curve through the stack. 

- 'Transforms' take us from one time-slice to another:
    - a _topological_ transform preserves the continuity of the
      lines, 
    - an _affine_ transform preserves the straight lines, though
      the line of a static point might now be angled (moving) and
      vice versa. 
    - There are no absolute motions, no absolute points in space. 

- Thus we come to SR, where the ride becomes rather strange.
    - Firstly, Maudlin announces that all standard explanations
      are wrong, and even Einstein misstated things, having
      presented the theory in terms of the equivalence of
      inertial frames and the constancy of the speed of light,
      leading to the Lorentz transformations which relate one set
      of coordinates to another. 

- Maudlin argues this approach "has already run off the rails."
  We must ignore any talk of "time slowing down" or "travelling
  near the speed of light," for (given the affine transform
  effect noted above): 
    > "..we must expunge all ideas of things having speeds,
    > including light." 

- We are going to be studying  "Maudlin Relativity", where we
  will find: 
    1. things SR explains 
    2. things Maudlin's view of SPACETIME GEOMETRY explains.

- The traditional approaches have largely been stuck on the twin
  paradox and the associated real physical "time-effects" that SR
  epparently xplains:
    - the longer living mesons (or muons) when travelling at high
      velocity, 
    - the Hafele-Keating experiment where the clock carried on
      the jet is retarded when arriving back at the airport when
      compared to another left sitting at the airport. 

- These effects are taken to be ontological, physically real. 
    - No one denies the retardation of the jet-carried clock. 
    - The twin effect (Langevin 1911) would indeed be _physically
      real_, should one twin have a long beard and grey hair, and
      the other looking like Brad Pitt. 


- The difficulty is that SR embeds the Lorentz transformations
  within a system of _reciprocal motions of two observers_,
  whereby one observer can claim to be stationary, the other in
  motion, or vice versa. 
    - In 1905, after Einstein notes the foreshortened ellipse in
      the moving system, he states "It is clear that the same
      results hold good of bodies at rest in the 'stationary'
      system, viewed from a system in uniform motion."

- In these equations, 'space changes' compensate for 'time
  changes' and vice versa. And since they are _COMPENSATORY_,
  both changes (space, time) must be of the same order:
    - either both non-ontological (ie measurement effects only)
    - or both ontological (real, physical)

- As the "stationary" observer claims these effects for the
  moving system (and vice versa), SR won acceptance within the
  physics community precisely because its effects are deemed
  measurement effects, i.e., non-ontological. 

- Thus the Michelson-Morley result was not considered the effect
  of an actual, physical contraction of the apparatus that lay
  parallel to its motion in the ether. 

- Lorentz, trying to save the Maxwell-Lorentz equations and their
  requirement for an absolute motion in the ether, had earlier
  offered a physical model of the apparatus contraction (or of
  any body in motion, to include a `reduction of frequency`)
  based on electro-dynamic forces. 
    - It was a rejection of the relativity inherent in Newton's
      first law. 

- Physics rejected Lorentz's offer; Einstein's new version of
  relativity, with its "measurement-only effects" solution, was
  accepted. 

- But this correct, consistent interpretation destroys SR's
  ability to explain any of the real, ontological time-related
  effects it is currently given credit for:
    - including the jet-carried clock, 
    - the long living meson, or 
    - the twin-effect (should it actually happen). 

- To be CONSISTENT with its structure, ALL EFFECTS, whether of
  time or space, must indeed be taken as measurement effects,
  that is, non-ontological. (really?)

- What happened is that since Langevin, two incompatible
  solutions have been fused into an unholy mess:
    - that of Lorentz (physical?)
    - and that of Einstein (measurement?)

- Maudlin's tries to steer us through this thicket and what he
  presents is truly new (at least I haven't seen these
  arguments).

- What one finds is that he posits: 
    - TWO LEVELS of time-related change, and 
    - TWO LEVELS of space-related change. 

1. Thus, for time, he insists that 
    - the slower aging twin-effect is very real, physical
      (ontological), 
    - but the "slowing clocks" is simply a "coordinate effect"
      (measurement effect). 
    - He never explicitly discusses, or locates within his
      scheme, Hafele-Keating or the muons.

2. Equally, there is 
    - a real, physical Lorentz contraction, 
    - but also another Lorentz contraction that is only
      a measurement effect. 

- Given this symmetry, he has saved the standard SR explanation
  of Michelson-Morley - the contraction of the apparatus arm is
  indeed only a measurement effect (ie NOT the actual, physical
  contraction that Maudlin holds can ALSO exist).  

- And he believes he can explain the twin effect as real,
  physical, and ontological. But, unfortunately, he leaves so
  many questions abegging.

- The twin effect is explained by Maudlin simply by means of
  geometry; by a Minkowski diagram (of sorts). 
    - The stationary twin is simply one of those straight lines
      through the time slices (such as time slices that are (or
      are not) in the Minkowski version of space-time). 

- The rocket-twin moves away at an angle, and then moves back
  again at an angle, meeting back up with the stationary
  earth-twin. 

- By Minkowski's math, the stationary line of the earth-bound
  twin is `100 years` long while the total of the two angled lines
  is `60 years`. 

- The rocket-twin has aged less (only `60 years`). 
    - The geometry (the shortened space-time path) explains it
      all; it is a "real, physical, ontological effect"
    - and "explained" by the _GEOMETRY_ alone! 
    - End of story.

- But this cannot be the end of the story. 
    - There is that `reciprocity` of systems that cannot be
      erased from Einstein's approach, despite Maudlin's disdain. 
    - Minkowski's geometric generalization simply extends the
      space-time interval invariance, already intrinsic in the
      Lorentz transformations, to all possible coordinate axes. 

- It does not remove Einstein's embedding of Lorentz's equations
  within his (Einstein's) reciprocity. 

- The rocket-twin has equal right to claim HE is stationary. We
  now need a new Minkowski diagram with a new origin for the
  coordinates. When we create a Minkowski diagram, we necessarily
  fix on one observer - all the rest are set (depicted) in motion
  with respect to him. There is but one real, actual observer in
  Minkowski space-time - the rest, moving at various speeds, are
  virtual - imaginary - with their proportions of space and
  "time" now adjusting ("time" being now a purely mathematical
  treatment of the 4th coordinate) relative to the one real,
  stationary observer. 

- With the new diagram, with the rocket-twin stationary, the
  situation is reversed. Now the rocket-twin ages more. This was
  a point of Bergson (Duration and Simultaneity, 1922), and so
  too by MIT physicist AP French (Special Relativity, 1968), who
  stated SR cannot be used to explain the longer living meson (or
  muon) speeding towards earth. 
    - In fact, we would need two Minkowski space-time diagrams,
      one for each case - the observer on earth, and
      a hypothetical observer on the meson. 

- Each can claim he is stationary, the other in motion. In other
  words, French argued, in SR the meson's "extended life" can
  only be treated as a measurement effect (just like the "length
  change" of M-M's apparatus arm), and SR is used illegitimately.
  i.e., a different theory is needed, like Lorentz, in explaining
  the phenomenon as a real, ontological effect, though
  ontological the effect most certainly is.

- The reciprocity problem, Maudlin dismisses with both a strawman
  and questionable reasoning, totally ignoring even Einstein,
  even his own affine transformations (which take a stationary
  object to a moving object - and vice versa) as "Confusion 1" re
  SR. 

- Yet "confusion 1" is precisely why there exists his "Confusion
  2," where folks are desperately trying to escape the
  reciprocity implications by appealing to accelerations (as did
  French) to explain the twins, the clocks, the mesons. 

- Hence Maudlin becomes very murky. Is not the longer living
  meson a real effect? Would Maudlin explain this too like the
  rocket-twin - by ignoring reciprocity? And the Hafele-Keating
  clock? Obviously a real effect. Explained similarly? 

- The explanation of these effects is obscure in Maudlin. 

- Note, we could we give the earth-twin a clock with markers for
  100 years, and put a similar 100-year clock on the rocket. The
  rocket-clock, on return, would necessarily point to 60, while
  the earth-twin's clock points to 100. Is this not one of those
  "clock slowings?" 

- Yet clock slowings are only a "coordinate effect" (not real)
  per Maudlin. Slower aging of the rocket-twin real, his
  clock-slowing not real? Hafele-Keating - not real? Murky. 

- In truth, there is even a _physical_ contradiction lurking.

- Maudlin's treatment is abstract, with no real velocity numbers
  given. In his purely geometric analysis of the rocket-twin's
  path, 40 years were knocked off. 

- Let us suppose the rocket is moving at `99.8%` the speed of
  light. At least 40 years will again be knocked off. The
  earth-twin, sitting in his kitchen, can send out a light ray at
  the same time the rocket leaves, bouncing it off a mirror
  placed where the rocket turns back and it returns. The light
  velocity is invariant, it is unaffected by the Minkowski
  (Lorentz) math applied to the rocket twin, it has nothing
  lopped off its path. Travelling always just barely faster than
  the rocket, it returns (much, 40+ years) later?

- In general, Maudlin is well aware that geometry alone is
  insufficient. For real, `physical effects` we must have
  explanations involving real `forces`. 

- His twin-effect model however rests solely on geometry. This
  reliance on geometry alone is echoed elsewhere - the line
  between geometrical explanation and actual physics is very
  vague throughout. The speed of light is explained simply by the
  "light-cone" in Minkowski space-time. 

Yet, for Newton, the maximum velocity in a medium is given as `v
= sqrt(L/D)`, where `L` is the elasticity, `D` the density of the
medium.

Flick a chunk of jello - the wave velocity passing through
the jello is determined by this ratio. Were we to assume
that Lorentz's "ether" is still lurking around, perhaps now
disguised as the "quantum vacuum," this medium should have
an `L` and a `D`, therefore determining a maximum velocity
within it. This would be a physical explanation. 

- Just saying... as an example. 

- Or the flow of time itself? In his Galilean exposition, time
  is simply the topological/affine transforms taking us from one
  time-slice of space to the next. What does this abstraction
  have to do with the dynamic evolution of the physical,
  universal field over time, i.e., the actual, physical
  transition from one time-slice of space to the next? 

- Would there not have to be some real, phsyical,continuous
  process “generating” each time-slice of (all of) space? 

- The "forces" creating curved lines - what are they other than
  ghostly creatures inhabiting this geometric space? And of
  course, in the Minkowski version, there is that famous
  space-time block where supposedly there is no flow of time...
  with all the attendant problems this generates 
    - (e.g., how do we account for the "illusion" of the flow of
      events)

- All this unexplored by Maudlin.

- The book is indeed a good mind trip, if only as a meditation on
  the struggles our current, very mathematical physics faces re
  what is actual "explanation." 

- It is also an interesting demonstration of how far SR (or
  better, the Lorentz-SR fusion) actually is from being "settled"
  (many seem to think the shooting is all over).

- There are many interesting things however in the book. I have
  picked on a few salient things here - out of some possible
  areas of critique. 

- How can Maudlin say that "we cannot compare speeds"? He has
  just done so in the twin paradox, while the light cone itself
  inherently compares speeds relative to everything moving slower
  than light. 

- Yes, I know he is thinking of that affine transformation that
  takes straight-through lines (stationary object) to angled
  (i.e., moving object) lines. But he has just fixed the
  earth-twin as stock still in space, and set the rocket twin
  moving (prohibiting the latter from any say as to whether he
  happens to consider himself stationary), and then, yes,
  compared. 
    - (ef-note: but Maudlin compares the `length` of the space-time
      interval, rather than any `speeds`?) 

- Yet we cannot compare. A little too Zen koan-like for me. 

- Just things that make the book less then satisfying as
  a treatment of the issues. 

- For escape from this swamp, for a deeper, more coherent view,
  imo, we have to return to Bergson (1922) and his "Duration and
  Simultaneity," though for the equation-hating reviewers here,
  I must warn that Bergson breaks Einstein and the Lorentz
  equations down in detail to view the concrete meaning of
  Einstein's system.
