### what is the Coloumb guage?
    - aka the 'Transverse guage'
    - problem: instantaneous travel? (NO)
    - key: in this gauge, the scalar potential `Φ` satisfies the
      Poisson equation. This scalar potential can have seemingly
      'instantaneous' effects.

- The Coulomb gauge is a specific gauge condition in EM, used
  particularly in QED.
- In this context, a gauge condition is a way to fix certain
  degrees of freedom in the EM potentials to simplify equations
  and calculations.
    - Aside: 
    - `Gauge fixing` ("choosing a gauge") denotes a mathematical
      procedure for coping with redundant degrees of freedom in
      field variables.

- The Coulomb gauge imposes the condition that the divergence of
  the vector potential `A` is zero: `∇⋅A=0`

- This condition is also known as the `transverse gauge` because
  it ensures that the vector potential `A` has no longitudinal
  component, meaning that it is perpendicular (transverse) to the
  direction of wave propagation in the case of EM waves.

- The main advantages of the Coulomb gauge include simplifying
  the equations describing EM fields and making certain physical
  interpretations more straightforward.
    - e.g, in this gauge, the scalar potential `Φ` satisfies the
      `Poisson equation`, which resembles the classical equation
      for electrostatics, allowing an easier separation of the
      effects of instantaneous electrostatic interactions from
      the propagation of EM waves.

- This gauge is particularly useful in QED and other areas of
  physics where the separation of longitudinal and transverse
  components of fields can significantly simplify the
  mathematical treatment of electromagnetic interactions...
    - This line is very interesting:
        - ...allowing for an easier separation of the effects of
          instantaneous electrostatic interactions from the
          propagation of electromagnetic waves.

- In the context of the Coulomb gauge and classical EM, when we

  violation of relativity.

- In classical EM, particularly within the framework of the
  Coulomb gauge, the scalar potential `Φ` appears to mediate
  electrostatic interactions instantaneously.

- This treatment stems from the Poisson equation for `Φ`, which
  is derived under the assumption of the Coulomb gauge (`∇⋅A=0`).

- The solution to the Poisson equation gives the electrostatic
  potential due to a distribution of charges WITHOUT EXPLICIT
  REFERENCE TO THE TIME it takes for changes in the charge
  distribution to influence the potential at a distance.

- This mathematical formulation simplifies calculations and is
  consistent with the static or quasi-static limits of EM, where
  changes in the EM field are slow compared to the time it takes
  for light to travel across the system under consideration.

- However, this doesn't mean that EM interactions truly happen
  _instantaneously_.

- According to SR, no information or physical effect can
  propagate faster than the speed of light in vacuum, `c`.

- This principle is fully respected in the full EM theory,
  including in the dynamic case where Maxwell's equations
  describe the finite propagation speed of EM waves and their
  interaction with matter.

- The seeming CONTRADICTION is resolved when we consider that the
  full description of EM phenomena, especially in dynamic
  situations, involves both
    - the scalar potential `Φ` and
    - the vector potential `A`,
    - and their interplay fully respects causality and the finite
      speed of light.

- In more advanced treatments of EM, such as QED, the propagation
  of interactions at the speed of light is explicitly accounted
  for, ensuring consistency with relativity.

- Thus, the "instantaneous" aspect in the Coulomb gauge is
  a mathematical convenience for certain static or quasi-static
  problems, rather than a statement about physical reality
  violating the speed of light limitation.
