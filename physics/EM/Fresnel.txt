## Fresnel anticipated frame dragging?!

- Yes — Augustin-Jean Fresnel (1788–1827) anticipated
  a superficially similar idea, but not `gravitomagnetism`
  itself. 
- His 1818 theory of partial 'ether' dragging provides a striking
  analogy to the later concept of frame dragging in GR.

### 1. Fresnel's idea (1818)

- Fresnel proposed that a transparent body moving through the
  luminiferous ether would partially drag the ether along with
  it.

- His theory predicted the Fresnel drag coefficient:
    - `f = 1 − 1/n²`
    - where `n` is the refractive index of the medium.

- For light travelling through a medium moving at speed v in the
  same direction, the first-order prediction was: `u ≈ c/n + v(1
  − 1/n²)`
    - where `c` is the speed of light in vacuum.

- This was not full ether ENTRAINMENT: the ether was supposed to
  be dragged only partially. The prediction received experimental
  support from Fizeau's 1851 experiment.

### 2. The connection to gravitomagnetism

- The analogy is suggestive, but the underlying physics differs.

 - Fresnel (1818)                              | Lense–Thirring (1918)
 - ---                                         | ---
 - Moving transparent matter                   | Rotating mass
 - Hypothetical ether is partially dragged     | Spacetime geometry is altered
 - Changes light propagation thru moving media | Changes inertial motion and gyroscope precession
 - Classical wave theory                       | General relativity

- The Lense–Thirring effect is a prediction of GR: 
    - a rotating mass influences nearby inertial frames through
      its effect on spacetime geometry.
    - This is described as `frame dragging`.

### 3. What is the conceptual connection?

- Matter affects its surroundings. Fresnel proposed partial
  entrainment of the ether; general relativity predicts
  rotational effects on local inertial frames.
- Motion matters. Fresnel's correction depends on the medium's
  velocity; gravitomagnetism arises from mass currents and
  angular momentum.
- The analogy is not a derivation. Fresnel neither predicted
  relativistic spacetime nor derived the Lense–Thirring effect.

- The crucial distinction is that Fresnel proposed that matter
  drags a hypothetical medium through which light propagates. In
  GR, rotating matter affects spacetime geometry itself. No
  physical ether is required.

### 4. Historical verdict

- Fresnel can reasonably be described as a conceptual precursor
  by analogy, but not as a discoverer of frame dragging or
  gravitomagnetism.

- The connection is historically intriguing because both ideas
  concern how the motion of matter influences the propagation of
  light or the behaviour of nearby physical systems. However,
  their mathematical foundations and physical interpretations are
  fundamentally different.
