- In chapter 5, we explored the possibility of interpreting
  ENERGY as:
    1.  real stuff `stored` by a body or a field or 
    2.  a real `property` possessed by a body or a field

- ...thereby securing the reality of fields and making EM
  influences obey spatiotemporal locality.

- With the aid of the Poynting vector, we can depict energy as
  flowing through the field, and although we have seen no
  conclusive argument for this interpretation, it seems
  attractive.

- In the words of Poynting and JJ Thompson:
    > There is something we call "energy", which may be
    > recognised in various forms. When it disappears in one
    > form, it re-appears in another... The belief in the
    > identity of energy is probably as metaphysical as our
    > belief in the continued existence of any bit of matter, and
    > its identity under various modifications.

    - However because the metaphysical ADDITION simplifies the
      form of the statement, and is never likely to lead us wrong
      in experimental interpretation, we see no reason to exclude
      it.

    - If we did exclude it, we'd have to speak instead of:
          * the CORRELATION of the energies
          * instead of the CONSTANCY of energy

     - E.g. Instead of describing the conversion of kinetic
       energy into heat, we'd say that kinetic energy disappeared
       and at the same time heat energy appeared.

    - Poynting & Thompson, A Text-book of Physics
    - Vol. 3 (9 Ed. 1928) (Page 100)


- However, according to Galilean space-time, energy cannot be
  real stuff or even an objective property of a system. 

- For if there's no fact regarding a body's velocity `ṽ`, then
  there is no fact reagarding its kinetic energy `1/2 mv^2`.

- Energy is NOT an invariant. This seems to have been one of
  Heaviside's principal reasons for disagreeing with Lodge's
  conception of energy as real, with each parcel of energy
  possessing their own identity like bits of matter.

In Galilean space-time, some bit of energy could only exist in
a frame-dependent way, not objectively. Heaviside wrote:

    > We need not assume the objectivity of energy. This is an
    > difficult notion, and seems to be rendered invalid by the
    > relativity of motion, on which kinetic energy depends.
    > Therefore we cannot individualise energy the same way as
    > matter. 
    > - (1925 II, 525-6, also 521: cf 1922: I, 75) 1925
    >   Electrical Papers 1922 - Electromagnetic Theory

- Strangely, Heaviside didn't seem to recognise that the same
  argument impugns not only the "thingness" of energy but also
  energy's status as a real property of bodies and fields. [^1]

- That a body only has a velocity relative to some inertial frame
  (as Galilean space-time says) is what Heaviside calls "the
  relativity of motion".

- Lets see how this doctrine undermines the reality of energy
    * as stuff that flows or
    * a property that can be treated as "flowing".

- Imagine two icy comets, each traveling inertially, which
  collide, stick together and then melt.

- In the reference frame where one comet is at rest (and kinetic
  energy = 0), the moving comet's kinetic energy is converted
  into heat energy by the collision, which then flows into the
  other comet, melting it. 
    - The direction of energy flow is thus OPPOSITE from each
      comet's viewpoint: each sees the flow as into itself from
      the other comet.

- So the path of energy flow is _not invariant_, and hence not
  real, _if all inertial frames are accurate_ in describing the
  universe. (are all inertial frames accurate?)

- Of course, Galilean space-time _denies_ the reality of momentum
  just as it denies objective energy: 
    - both ENERGY and MOMENTUM depend on `velocity` and according
      to Galilean space-time, a body's velocity has no objective
      reality.

- But if neither energy nor momentum is real, then we cannot
  argue for the EM field's reality from the fact that it posses
  energy and momentum (as explained in chapter 5). And if the
  field is not "real", we cannot portray electromagnetic
  phenomena as having local causes. (great stuff!)

    - In contrast to Heaviside's view of energy, Maxwell
      recognized that only energy _differences_ have observable
      consequences and yet apparently did not hesitate to believe
      that there was a hidden fact regarding the _absolute_
      quantity of energy possessed by a system:
         - "We cannot reduce a system to a state of no energy and
           energy which is never removed from the system must
           remain unperceived by us. Because it's only as energy
           ENTERS or LEAVES a system that we can take any account
           of it."
         - "Therefore we must regard the energy of a system as
           a quantity that we can ascertain the increase or
           diminutation of, as the system passes from one
           definite condition to another."
         - "The abolute value of the energy in the standard
           condition is unknown to us, and it'd be of no use to
           us if we did know it, because all phenomena depend on
           the _variations_ of energy, and not on its absolute
           value.
        - (Matter and Motion (1892): 167-8, also 134, 166)"
