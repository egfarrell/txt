Maxwell’s equations look deceptively simple, but they hide layers
of subtle structure—symmetry, constraint, and philosophy all
rolled together. Let’s write them in the usual differential form
(in vacuum, for simplicity):

### Gauss’s law for electricity.
    - `∇·E = ρ/ε₀`
    - This tells us that charge is the source of the electric
      field. But note the subtlety: it’s a constraint, not an
      evolution equation. It doesn’t tell you how E changes with
      time—only how it must look given `ρ` at each instant. 
    - In fact, it acts as a consistency condition that must hold
      at all times, and it will stay true automatically if you
      start with initial data satisfying it and then use the
      other equations to evolve forward. 
    - This is one reason Maxwell’s equations aren’t just “four
      equations for four fields”—two of them are dynamical, two
      are constraints.

### Gauss’s law for magnetism.
    - `∇·B = 0`
    - No magnetic monopoles. But this also encodes a deep
      topological statement: B field lines are closed loops or
      extend to infinity. It’s a geometrical conservation law—no
      beginning or end of a magnetic field line. You can view it
      as saying the magnetic field is a “pure curl”: B = ∇×A,
      which introduces the vector potential A. The existence of
      A (and its gauge freedom) flows from this one equation.

### Faraday’s law of induction.
    - `∇×E = −∂B/∂t`
    - Here comes the first real time evolution. Changing magnetic
      flux induces a curling electric field. This is what makes
      electric generators, but it’s also what tells you that
      E isn’t purely sourced by ρ—it can be generated dynamically
      by a time-varying B. Together with the next equation, this
      mixes E and B under time evolution and hints at their unity
      as components of one tensor in relativity.

### Ampère–Maxwell law.
    - `∇×B = μ₀J + μ₀ε₀∂E/∂t`
    - The first term, `μ₀J`, is ordinary current producing
      magnetic fields. Maxwell’s stroke of genius was adding the
      displacement current term, μ₀ε₀∂E/∂t, which restores charge
      conservation and symmetry between E and B. Without it,
      electromagnetic waves wouldn’t exist. This was the
      historical key that forced the recognition that light is an
      electromagnetic wave.

### Deeper, Balakrishnan-style “nuggets”:

- Counting degrees of freedom:
    - The electromagnetic field has six components (three each
      for E and B), but the two Gauss laws are constraints,
      leaving only four independent components. Then gauge
      freedom in the potentials removes two more, leaving just
      two physical degrees of freedom—the two polarizations of
      light. That’s a compact little miracle of field theory.

- Eight equations idea:
    - Each of the two curl equations has three components → 6,
      and each divergence equation adds 1 → 8 total component
      equations for the six field components. But they’re not all
      independent—there are consistency relations (like
      ∇·(∇×A)=0) that tie them together. So it’s an
      overdetermined system in form, but self-consistent.

- Continuity equation emergence:
    - Taking `∇·` of the Ampère–Maxwell law and using `∇·(∇×B)=0`
      automatically gives:
        - `∂ρ/∂t + ∇·J = 0` (charge conservation).
    - Maxwell’s equations require it; it’s not an extra
      assumption. This is the hallmark of a gauge theory: the
      symmetry (here U(1)) guarantees local conservation laws.

- Symmetry and relativity:
    - Before Einstein, these were already hinting at spacetime
      unity. Once you write them in covariant form, they condense
      into one tensor equation:
        - `∂μF^{μν} = μ₀J^ν`
        - `∂[μF_{νλ]} = 0`
    - What used to be four equations become two—relativity
      reveals the redundancy.

- Hidden assumptions:
    - The vacuum equations assume linear, isotropic, homogeneous
      space—ε₀ and μ₀ are constants. That’s not true in matter or
      curved spacetime. In those settings, the “medium” can mix
      `E` and `B`, or even break the simple wave structure.

- In summary, Maxwell’s equations are more like a self-consistent
  dance between constraints, evolution, and symmetry than a plain
  list of laws. They tell us what can exist and how it must move.
  And all of electrodynamics—from radio to quantum field
  theory—is just elaborating their choreography.
