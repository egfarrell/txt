- A classic example of a force that depends only on position but
  is NOT conservative is the magnetic force experienced by
  a moving charge in a magnetic field. Although it is a function
  of position, it fails the conservative test for the following
  reason:

- The magnetic force on a charged particle moving with velocity
  `v` in a magnetic field `B(r)` is given by:

```
F = qv ∙ B(r)
```

It’s not conservative because:

1. Dependence on Velocity:
    - The force depends on both the position (through `B(r)` and
      the particle’s velocity `v`. Even though `B(r) `could be
      a static field that varies only with position, the force
      itself is velocity-dependent.

2. Non-zero Curl:
    - In most cases, the curl of `F `in this type of magnetic
      force isn’t zero, which is a key criterion for
      a conservative field in 3D. Specifically, if you calculate
      the curl of `F = qv  ∙  B(r)`, you’ll find that it
      does not vanish in general, meaning there’s no scalar
      potential `U `such that `F = -∇U`.

3. Path-dependence:
    - Work done by the magnetic force along a closed path is not
      necessarily zero; it depends on the path taken by the
      particle due to the force's dependency on DIRECTION of
      motion.

- So, despite `B(r) `potentially depending only on position, the
  force itself fails to be conservative because of the non-zero
  curl and the dependency on velocity.
