### Leggett's inequality
    - tests the idea of "non-local realism" in QM.

1. `Realism`:
    - Particles have definite properties (e.g., position, spin)
      even when not measured.

2. `Non-locality`:
    - Measurements on one particle can instantaneously affect
      another, even at a distance.

- Leggett's inequality is derived under assumptions combining:
    - non-locality and realism.

- QM violates this inequality, meaning the theory does not
  support BOTH realism and non-locality simultaneously.

- Experiments confirm QM, rejecting such "non-local hidden
  variable" models.


### Leggett v Bohm?
    - Leggett's inequality does not rule out Bohmian Mechanics.
      Here's why:

    1. Leggett's Assumptions:
        - Leggett's inequality tests models that are *both*
          realistic (particles have definite properties) and
          non-local, but it assumes a specific type of
          correlation structure between the hidden variables and
          measurement outcomes. These assumptions do not align
          with how Bohmian Mechanics operates.

    2. Bohmian Mechanics:
        - Is indeed a non-local hidden variable theory because:
        - Non-locality:
            - The "pilot wave" influences particle motion
              instantaneously across distances.
        - Realism:
            - Particles have definite positions at all times.

        - However, the mathematical framework of Bohmian
          Mechanics does not satisfy the specific assumptions
          underlying Leggett's inequality.

    3. Experimental Violations:
        - Violations of Leggett's inequality demonstrate that
          nature doesn't fit a particular class of non-local
          realistic theories.
        - Bohmian Mechanics falls outside this class and remains
          consistent with QM, including experimental results
          violating Leggett's inequality.

    - In summary, Leggett's inequality doesn't challenge Bohmian
      Mechanics because its assumptions are too restrictive to
      encompass Bohmian dynamics.

### ANOTHER EXPLANATION

### 1) Experimental verification
- Leggett-type inequalities have been experimentally tested and
  violated, in agreement with quantum mechanics.

- Key papers:
    - An experimental test of non-local realism
    - Gröblacher et al. (2007), 
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/0704.2529

    - Testing quantum correlations versus Leggett’s model
    - Branciard et al. (2008), 
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.2413

    - Experimental test of Leggett-type inequalities using spin systems
    - Huang et al. (2019), 
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08214

- Conclusion:
    - Experiments rule out a class of non-local realistic
      hidden-variable theories (Leggett-type models).


### 2) Does this disprove Bohmian mechanics?

- No. Reason:
    - Leggett assumes a restricted form of realism 
        - (e.g. fixed local properties like polarisation with
          specific statistical constraints).
    - Bohmian mechanics:
      - is realist (definite particle positions)
      - is non-local
      - does NOT satisfy Leggett’s extra assumptions

Therefore:
    - Bohmian mechanics reproduces QM predictions
    - ⇒ it violates Leggett inequalities
    - ⇒ it is not ruled out by these experiments


### Conceptual hierarchy

- Bell inequalities → rule out: local realism
- Leggett inequalities → rule out: some non-local realistic models
- Bohmian mechanics → survives (outside Leggett’s class)


### Key insight

- Violating Leggett’s inequality shows:
    > Even SOME non-local realistic theories are incompatible
    > with experiment.

- But it does not eliminate ALL realistic interpretations — only
  those with additional classical-style constraints.
