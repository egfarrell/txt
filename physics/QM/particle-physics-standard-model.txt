
### Preptorial: Geometry of the Fundamental Interactions: (Maia)
    - "On Riemann's Legacy to High Energy Physics and Cosmology"
    - Riemann as the Great Field Theory Unifier
    - Maia strongly asserts Riemann's foundational role in
      unifying the math of three of the four forces (strong,
      weak, electromagnetic, excluding gravity). The book
      rigorously explores this math, focusing on Riemann geometry
      and the Riemann tensor, making a convincing case without
      straining credibility.
    - In about 150 pages, it covers Riemann curvature and tensor
      applications to manifolds, Lie groups and algebras
      (preparing for gauge theory), general relativity
      space-time, scalars, Nielsen-Olesen models, spinors,
      Noether's theorem, connections, gauge fields, and
      gravitation.
    - The promised "Cosmology" discussion is not explicitly
      covered unless it includes the "Riemann gravity" section
      and new approaches to "gauge gravitation," highlighting
      that Einstein gravity isn't gauge because it lacks
      Yang-Mills type Lagrangian rigor, curvature operators,
      defined local gauge symmetry, corresponding Lie algebras,
      and solvable Yang-Mills equations.
    - An interesting point is made about the potential need for
      a _new gauge model for gravity_, possibly involving
      Leibniz's "smoothest geometry" conjecture, corresponding to
      minimal Riemann curvature variations.
    - The book emphasizes the recurring importance of geometry in
      theoretical physics, providing an exciting and
      thought-provoking look at Riemann's legacy.
    - It hints at the deep question of whether math is a tool for
      investigating the universe or a tool used by the universe,
      noting that in quantum chemistry, math sometimes becomes
      part of the "reality."
    - There are only 21 illustrations, but they are exceptional,
      providing "aha" moments that formulas alone can't. More
      illustrations would have been better.
    - Highly recommended for those seeking a fresh perspective on
      Riemann's contributions, extending from quaternions to
      octavians.
    - The book convinced me that Riemann's contributions, like
      those of Gauss and Dirac, are far from fully exploited. The
      math requires postgraduate-level effort but is worthwhile.
    - Note: Amazon has little information on M.D. Maia. Searching
      for Marcio R. de Garcia Maia (Márcio R. de Garcia Maia)
      reveals over 100 books and citations (University of
      Brasil), highlighting his significant contributions to
      nuclear physics.
