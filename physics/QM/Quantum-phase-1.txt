# ChatGPT:
- Yes, gravitational potentials can change quantum phases. The
  idea is closely analogous to the electromagnetic Aharonov–Bohm
  effect, but goes by different names in gravity research. 

- The most direct example is the Colella-Overhauser-Werner (COW)
  neutron interferometry experiment (1975), where neutrons sent
  through two different paths at different gravitational heights
  acquired a measurable relative phase shift.

- Basic point: in QM, the phase of a wavefunction evolves
  according to the action `S`. If the gravitational potential `Φ`
  changes the energy, then it changes `S`, and therefore the
  phase.

- The phase shift for a particle in a potential is 
    - `Δφ = (1/ħ) ∫ (ΔE) dt`

- In a gravitational field, the potential energy difference is 
    - `ΔE = m g h` (near Earth's surface).

- So the phase difference becomes 
    - `Δφ = (m g h T) / ħ` 
    - where h is height difference and 
    - T is time spent at that height.

- This isn't hypothetical. It's measured experimentally.

### More Context: EM Aharonov–Bohm effect:

- The electromagnetic scalar or vector potential can change the
  PHASE even when no FORCE acts (no electric or magnetic field
  along the path). The phase shift is directly tied to the EM
  potential `A` and `φ`.


### Gravitational analogue:

- Gravity changes how clocks tick (gravitational redshift) and
  how proper time accumulates. Because quantum phases track
  proper time via `φ ∝ (mc² τ)/ħ`, a difference in gravitational
  potential leads to different amounts of proper time along two
  paths. So the phase shift is really quantum-mechanical time
  dilation.

- Another way to say it:
    - Quantum phase = clock ticking of the matter wave
    - Gravity = changes the rate of the matter-wave clock
    - Therefore gravity causes phase shifts.

- This has been confirmed not only with neutron interferometers,
  but also with atom interferometers (e.g., Kasevich–Chu
  experiments). These are used today as precision gravimeters.

- So yes: a gravitational potential shifts the phase of a quantum
  state.

### The interesting twist:

- In EM Aharonov–Bohm, the effect depends on potentials in
  a region with _zero force_.

- In gravity, we don’t yet have a universally accepted
  “gravitational potential” in the same gauge sense because full
  gravity is geometric. 

- But the phenomenon is real: 
    - a difference in gravitational potential changes the
      accumulated proper time, which changes the quantum phase.

- This is why QG research pays close attention to matter-wave
  interferometry. The gravitational-field-induced phase shift is
  one of the few experimental handles where both QM and GR make
  direct predictions that meet in the lab.
