### Computing since Democritus
    - $MYBOOKS/Physics/QM/Quantum-computing-democritus.pdf
    - QM is a generalization of the laws of probability:
        - a generalization based on the 2-norm rather than the
          1-norm, and on complex numbers rather than nonnegative
          real numbers.
    - It can be studied completely separately from its
      applications to physics (indeed it provides a good starting
      point for learning the physical applications later).
    - This probability theory naturally gives rise to a novel way
      of computing called the quantum computing model.
    - It challenges conventional notions of computation that were
      once deemed unquestionable. Theoretical computer scientists
      might have created this model for their own needs, even if
      it had no connection to physics.
    - In short, while QM was invented to solve technical problems
      in physics, today it can be fruitfully explained from an
      totally different perspective:
        - as part of the history of ideas, in math, logic,
          computation, and philosophy, about the limits of the
          knowable.

    - The rules all concern how to construct sentences that are
      valid – which, informally, means “tautologically true”
      (true for all possible settings of the variables)

    - The Axiom of Choice turns out to be equivalent to the
      statement that every set can be well ordered.
