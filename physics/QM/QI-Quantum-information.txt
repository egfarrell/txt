### Computing since Democritus
    - $MY_BOOKS/Physics/QM/Quantum-computing-democritus.pdf
    - QM is a beautiful generalization of the laws of
      _probability_: 
        - a generalization based on the 2-norm rather than the
          1-norm, and on complex numbers rather than nonnegative
          real numbers.
    - It can be studied completely separately from its
      applications to physics (and indeed, provides a good
      starting point for learning the physical applications
      later).
    - This probability theory naturally gives rise to a novel way
      of computing called the quantum computing model. It
      challenges conventional notions of computation that were
      once deemed unquestionable. 
        - Theoretical computer scientists might have created this
          model for their own needs, even if it had no connection
          to physics.
    - In short, while QM was invented a century ago to solve
      technical problems in physics, today it can be fruitfully
      explained from an totally different perspective: 
        - as part of the history of ideas, in math, logic,
          computation, and philosophy, about the limits of the
          knowable.

    - The rules all concern how to construct sentences that are
      valid – which, informally, means “tautologically true”
      (true for all possible settings of the variables)

    - The _Axiom of Choice_ turns out to be equivalent to the
      statement that every set can be well ordered.
