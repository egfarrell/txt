### 1. The Notation: "Everything depends on everything"

- First, look at the shift in notation Peres proposes:
    - _Old way:_ `r_jα`
        - This implies the result (`r`) for particle `j` depends
          only on the local setting (`α`).

    - _New way:_ `r_jα(βγδ...)`
        - This implies the result for particle `j` depends on `α`
          (local setting), but *also* on `β` (what the other guy
          is doing far away), and potentially `γ,δ` (everything
          else in the universe).

- This is the _holistic_ nature of QM. You cannot mathematically
  isolate the particle from the context of its partner, even if
  they are galaxies apart.

### 2. The First Formula: The Mask of Randomness

- `(1/N) Σ_j r_jα(βγδ…) = 0`

- This formula represents the _local view_.
    - Imagine Alice is operating detector A. She measures
      thousands of particles (`N`).
    - The results are either +1 or -1.
    - The sum is 0. This means the average is 0.
    - _Translation:_ Alice sees pure, white noise. 50% up, 50%
      down.

- CRUCIAL POINT: Notice that `β` (Bob's setting) is inside the
  function `r(...)`, but it _does not change the sum_.

- No matter what Bob does with his detector (`β`)—or if he
  destroys it, or takes it to Mars—Alice’s average remains 0.
  Because Bob’s choice doesn't change Alice's local statistics,
  Alice cannot tell what Bob is doing. _This prevents
  faster-than-light communication._

### 3. The Second Formula: The "Spooky" Connection

- `(1/N) Σ_j r_jα(βγδ…) r_jβ(αγδ…) = − α · β`

- This formula represents the _global view_ (correlations).
    - `− α · β` is the cosine of the angle between the detectors.
      This is the specific prediction of QM.
    - As Peres proved earlier in the paper, this specific value
      violates Bell's Inequality.
    - A "classical" world (where objects have pre-existing
      properties) cannot produce a correlation this strong.

- Crucial Point: 
    - The only way to get a correlation this strong is if Alice's
      individual result `r_jα` WAS actually influenced by Bob's
      choice `β`.
- The universe had to "know" both angles (`α` and `β`) to
  generate the correct pair of outcomes (+/-) that would satisfy
  this formula.

### 4. Why this "Beggars Belief"

The "subtlety of nature" Peres refers to is how the universe
manages to balance these two equations.

- To satisfy Equation 1:
    - Nature must completely _hide_ this dependence from the
      observer. The influence of `β` on Alice's result must be
      hidden perfectly within the randomness of quantum
      probability.

- To satisfy Equation 2:
    - Nature must be _non-local_. What happens at `A` must depend
      on what is measured at `B`. The systems are not isolated;
      they are one single entity.

- Yet, they whisper so quietly that if you look at just one of

### 1. The Limit: Tsirelson's Bound

- Peres notes that classical correlations satisfy Bell's
  inequality (specifically the CHSH inequality, `S ≤ 2`), and QM
  violates it (`S = 2√2`).

- The Question: Why does QM stop at `2√2`? Why not 4 (the
  algebraic maximum)?

- The Insight:
    - Boris Tsirelson proved that within the framework of
      a Hilbert space, even with infinite entanglement, you
      cannot exceed `2√2`. This suggests that the geometry
      of Hilbert space itself imposes a limit on how "spooky"
      nature can get.

- Reading:
    - Look up _Tsirelson's Bound_. It defines the exact perimeter
      of the "quantum set" of correlations.

### 2. The "What If": Popescu-Rohrlich (PR) Boxes

- This is the most fruitful next step. In 1994, Sandu Popescu and
  Daniel Rohrlich asked:
    - "Is it possible to have a theory that is more non-local
      than QM but still prohibits faster-than-light
      communication?"

-   The Result:
        - Yes. They constructed a theoretical device (a PR Box)
          that reaches the algebraic maximum (`S=4`)—meaning
          perfect correlation—while maintaining perfectly random
          local marginals (Equation 1 holds, Equation 2 is
          maximized).

-   The Implication:
        - This proves that the "No-Signaling" principle alone is
          not sufficient to derive QM. Nature is non-local, but
          not *maximally* non-local. Why? That is one of the
          biggest open questions in foundations today.

-   Paper:
        - Popescu, S., & Rohrlich, D. (1994). "Quantum nonlocality as an axiom".

### 3. The Context: The Kochen-Specker Theorem & Peres-Mermin Square

- Peres mentions "unperformed experiments have no results." This

- The Insight:

### 1. The "Information is Physical" View (Zeilinger/Brukner)

- Anton Zeilinger (Nobel 2022) suggests that the distinction
  between reality and information is artificial.

- The Mechanism: Treat a quantum system not as an object, but as
  a carrier of a finite amount of information (1 bit).
- The Explanation: If an entangled pair carries 2 bits of
  information, and the "relationship" (the correlation) consumes
  those bits, there is simply no information left to define the
  individual properties.
- The individual results *must* be random because the system has
  "spent" its information budget on defining the correlation
  `-\alpha \cdot \beta`. The randomness of Equation 1 is not
  a mask; it is a consequence of the fact that the correlation is
  the fundamental reality, and the individual particles are just
  derivative shadows.

### 2. Retrocausality (The "Zig-Zag" Path)

- This is a minority view, but it is gaining traction (e.g., Huw
  Price, Ken Wharton) because it solves the tension neatly.

- The Mechanism:
    - Standard physics assumes the past influences the future
      (time-symmetric equations, but boundary conditions usually
      set in the past). If we allow boundary conditions in the
      future to constrain the past, the paradox vanishes.
- The Explanation:
    - When Alice chooses setting `\alpha`, this information
      propagates backwards in time to the source, affecting the
      emission of the particles. The particles "know" `\alpha`
      and `\beta` at the moment of creation because the future
      measurement *caused* the past state.

- This restores Locality (the influence travels along the light
  cones, just zig-zagging back and forth in time) and explains
  the correlations without needing "spooky action at a distance."

### 3. Entanglement Monogamy

- This is less of an "interpretation" and more of a structural
  feature of QM that ensures the balance.

- The Mechanism:
    - There is a strict trade-off called "Monogamy of
      Entanglement." If Particle A is maximally entangled with
      Particle B, it cannot be correlated at all with Particle C.

- The Explanation:
    - If Alice could control her local statistics (Equation 1) to
      signal Bob, she would need to correlate her particle with
      something external (her will/data). But her particle is
      already busy being entangled with Bob's. To signal, she
      would have to break the entanglement.

- Nature pulls off the balancing act by making Entanglement and
  Signaling mutually exclusive resources. You can have strong
  correlations (shared history), or you can have signaling (new
  information), but the mathematical structure of the density
  matrix prevents you from having both simultaneously.

# Answer

- This is a fantastic question because it touches on the
  historical origin of Bell’s Theorem.

- The short answer is: YES.
    - In fact, John Bell was explicitly inspired by De
      Broglie-Bohm (dBB) mechanics. Bell did not write his
      theorem to disprove Bohm; he wrote it to see if the
      _non-locality_ inherent in Bohm’s theory was a necessary
      feature of *any* hidden variable theory.

Here is how dBB "fits into" the theorem, how it handles the "delicate tension," and how it extends your double-slit analogy.

### 1. The dBB "Take" on Bell: "Explicit Non-Locality"

- Standard QM (Copenhagen) abandons _Realism_ (particles don't
  have values until measured) to save _Locality_ (or at least, to
  avoid talking about non-locality).

- De Broglie-Bohm mechanics does the opposite. It doubles down on
  _Realism_ (particles exist and have positions at all times) and
  accepts that the cost is _Locality_.

In dBB, the "state" of a two-particle system is defined by:
1.  The Wavefunction: `Ψ(x_1, x_2, t)`
2.  The Actual Positions: `Q_1(t)` and `Q_2(t)`

- The Bell Mechanism in dBB:
    - In dBB, the wavefunction lives in _Configuration Space_ (a
      6-dimensional space for 2 particles), not in 3D physical
      space. Because the particles are entangled, the
      wavefunction `Ψ` cannot be factored into
      `\psi_A(x_1)\psi_B(x_2)`.

- The equation of motion for Particle 1 (Alice's side) is roughly:

`v₁ = (ℏ/m) Im( (∇₁Ψ) / Ψ )`

- Because `Ψ` is entangled, the value of this velocity field
  `v_1` depends instantly on the coordinates of _both_ particles
  (`x_1` and `x_2`) and the boundary conditions (magnets) at
  _both_ ends.

- If Bob (at `x_2`) rotates his Stern-Gerlach magnet, he changes
  the Hamiltonian boundary conditions for `Ψ`. This change
  propagates instantly through the 6D configuration space,
  altering the shape of the wavefunction everywhere.
  Consequently, the "velocity vector field" guiding Alice’s
  particle (`v_1`) changes _instantaneously_.


### 2. Fitting into the Double-Slit Analogy

You mentioned the dBB explanation of the double-slit:

- Standard QM: The particle goes through "both" slits.

- dBB: The particle goes through _one_ slit, but the pilot wave
  goes through _both_ and interferes, creating a "channel"
  (Quantum Potential) that guides the particle to the bright
  fringe.

- Now, apply this to Bell (The "Two-Slit" becomes "Two-Magnets"):
    - Imagine the Bell experiment as a sort of
      "hyper-double-slit."

        1. In the double-slit, the particle "knows" if the other
           slit is open or closed because the _Wave_ feels it.

        2. In the Bell experiment, Alice's particle "knows" how
           Bob set his magnet because the _Wave_ feels it.

- The "Quantum Potential" (`Q`) in dBB is the mathematical object
  that encodes the shape of the wave. In the Bell scenario, the
  Quantum Potential is non-local:
    - ` Q(x_1, x_2) `

- The force acting on Alice's particle (`F = −∇Q`) depends
  explicitly on the position of Bob's particle. The trajectory of
  Alice's particle is physically pushed or pulled by a force that
  is generated by the configuration of Bob's apparatus and Bob's
  particle.

### 3. Resolving the "Delicate Tension"

- You asked earlier about the tension between Eq 1 (No Signaling)
  and Eq 2 (Strong Correlations). Here is how Bohm deals with it:

- Equation 2 (`− α · β`):
    - This is easy for Bohm. The particles are riding a non-local
      "fluid" (the wavefunction). The fluid dynamics in 6D space
      are naturally coordinated.
    - The trajectories are steered explicitly to ensure that if
      Particle A goes "UP", Particle B is steered "DOWN"
      (relative to the angles). The non-local force enforces the
      correlation.

- Equation 1 (No Signaling - Average is 0):
    - This is the brilliant part of dBB. If the trajectories are
      being nudged instantly by Bob, why can't Alice see it?
    - The Answer: "Quantum Equilibrium."
        - In dBB, we assume the initial distribution of particle
          positions is `ρ = |Ψ|^2`.
        - If we knew the exact initial position of the particle,
          we _could_ predict the trajectory perfectly and seeing
          a deviation would allow superluminal signaling.
        - However, we cannot know the position better than
          `|Ψ|^2`.

- The "nudges" from Bob's non-local influence are hidden exactly
  inside the statistical uncertainty of Alice's initial
  conditions. The distribution of trajectories shifts
  individually, but the ensemble average remains invariant.

### Summary

- De Broglie-Bohm mechanics is the "existence proof" that Bell's
  Theorem does not rule out hidden variables; it only rules out
  _local_ ones.

   - Standard View:
        - "Spooky action is impossible, so particles must not
          have properties before measurement."

   - Bohmian View:
        - "Particles definitely have properties, so spooky action
          must be real."

- Bell himself put it best in his 1982 paper on the subject:

> "The de Broglie-Bohm pilot wave theory... shows explicitly how
> properties can be attributed to the system... while the
> 'measurement' is a particular interaction... _But this is at
> the cost of the locality of the theory._ The pilot wave of one
> system depends instantaneously on the position of the other...
> the 'ghost' fields... exert a force."
