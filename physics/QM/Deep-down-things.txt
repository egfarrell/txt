### Residual Force
    - Yet, it is this relatively weak _residual force_ between
      the strong-force-neutral nucleons that provides the
      tremendous power released in nuclear explosions and power
      generation.

    - However much respect we hold for this conventional
      (residual) nuclear force between the nucleons in the
      nucleus, it is only the tip of the iceberg with respect to
      the strength of the true, underlying _strong force_.

    - Were we able to harness this force, modern nuclear power
      would seem feeble in comparison.

### Lie Groups
    - Most lie groups are non-commutative aka non-Abelian.
    - The set of real numbers form a continuum of numbers. There
      are no gaps between successive real numbers, no holes where
      you could squeeze in another number that might be the
      result of a physical measurement. The real numbers under
      addition form a continuous group.
    - Similarly, all Lie groups are continuous. In fact, the set
      of real numbers under addition a straightforward example of
      a Lie group, though not a very useful one to show the
      special properties of Lie groups.
    - Eg: most Lie groups are non-Abelian, but the group of real
      numbers under addition is Abelian because for any real
      numbers `x_1•x_2 = x_2•x_1`
    - Rotation group: the ELEMENTS of the set are the various
      rotation angles. Rotation by an angle is NOT the OPERATION
      of the group! Instead the operation is the successive
      application of 2 rotations. Counter-intuitive!
    - EXPLANATION
        - Deep down things
        - `Lie Group` (e.g. SO(2)):
        - Elements: rotations R(θ) (θ is just a label)
        - Operation: composition → R(θ₁)R(θ₂) = R(θ₁ + θ₂)
            - ie successive application of 2 rotations
        - Describes finite / `GLOBAL transformations`
        - Key link:
            - `R(θ) = exp(θJ)`
            - The generator `J` (infinitesimal rotation) builds all finite rotations.
