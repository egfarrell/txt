- Tim Maudlin’s interpretation of Bell’s theorem is a compelling
  and rigorous one.
- He argues forcefully that nonlocality is the core lesson of
  Bell’s result, regardless of hidden variables.

- Here's a breakdown of that view:

### Tim Maudlin’s View (In Favour)

1. Bell’s original motivation:

    - Bell explicitly set out to test locality, not just hidden
      variables.
    - The EPR paper (1935) posed the question:
        - Can a theory be both local and complete?
        - Bell framed his theorem to target `locality`, assuming
          EPR’s reasoning was correct about completeness.

2. The theorem’s assumptions:
    - Bell assumes:
        - Locality (no superluminal influences).
        - Statistical independence (no hidden conspiracies).
        - Outcome determinacy (more generally a realistic model)
    - Violation of Bell inequalities in experiments (e.g. Aspect
      et al.) shows that at least one of these assumptions must
      be wrong.
    - Maudlin’s claim: It’s locality that fails, not realism or
      hidden variables.

3. Hidden variables are not the target:
    - Quantum mechanics already violates Bell inequalities
      without hidden variables. So, it’s not hidden variables per
      se that are the problem.
    - The nonlocality is in quantum predictions themselves, not
      only in HV theories (e.g., Bohmian mechanics).

4. Bell’s own statements:
    - Bell himself later said the key assumption was `local
      causality`, not the existence of hidden variables.
    - He became increasingly explicit that nonlocality was the
      unavoidable conclusion.

### Against Maudlin’s View (Counterpoints)

1. Multiple assumptions involved:

- Bell’s theorem depends on more than just locality:
   - Realism/determinism
        - (a well-defined outcome exists before measurement).
   - Statistical independence
        - (settings are not influenced by hidden variables).
- Some interpretations reject realism or determinism, not
  locality (e.g. Copenhagen, QBism).

2. Operationalists’ view:

    - Many physicists argue: QM is not about an underlying
      reality but about predicting measurement outcomes.
    - Bell’s theorem, to them, just tells us we can’t have local
      hidden variables—not that nature itself is nonlocal.
    - So the issue is with realistic completions, not locality.

3. Many Worlds (Everett):

    - Everettian interpretations retain locality in the unitary
      evolution (no collapse, no superluminal influence).
    - Maudlin argues MWI has nonlocal correlations “in the
      structure,” but its advocates say it doesn’t require
      dynamical nonlocality.

4. Epistemic views of `ψ`:

 If the wavefunction is about knowledge, not reality
  (ψ-epistemic), then Bell’s violation may be due to our
  conceptual framing—not physical nonlocality.
 Maudlin would dismiss this, but it shows that Bell's
  implications depend on one's metaphysical commitments.


### Summary

- Maudlin’s position:
    - Bell proved locality must be false.
    - Hidden variables are secondary.
    - In favour: Clear in Bell’s writing; supported by
      experimental results; fits deterministic interpretations
      (e.g., Bohmian). Against: Depends on assuming
      realism/determinism; can be dodged by operationalism,
      epistemic interpretations, or Many Worlds.
