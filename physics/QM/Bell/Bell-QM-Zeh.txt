  latter (wavefunction) describes reality.

- In Varenna, Bell began by arguing that all physical systems are
  described by means of two different concepts:
    - classical properties `Λ` 
    - and a wave function `ψ`

- 'Subjective' state of incomplete info
    - He suspected `ψ` to be merely "subjective". Today we'd call
      it an epistemic concept, representing incomplete
      information, but "information" would only make sense to him
      with respect to possible questions "about what?" and "by
      whom?". 

- 'Objective' state of incomplete info
    - An objective "state" of incomplete information (an
      unspecified ensemble of potential elementary states) may
      also be operationally defined by a certain (incomplete)
      preparation procedure. 

- He insisted that not being able to distinguish in practice
  between a _superposition_ and an _ensemble_ consisting of its
  components does not prove them to be the same.

- This is in clear contrast to his earlier interpretation of `ψ`
  as a "subjective" concept. As only one set of `λ`’s is assumed
  to be real (located somewhere in the myriads of branches of the
  universal wave function), he compares `ψ` with _Maxwellian
  fields_, which are similarly _assumed_ to exist even where no
  charged "test particles" are present, but adds that...

- Bell initially reports being (not?) quite sure if he
  understands Everett correctly, but then claims a previously
  unknown "close relationship between Everett and Bohm". 

- He says that "all instantaneous classical configurations `λ`
  are supposed to exist" in Everett’s theory (a conclusion he
  regarded as extravagent). 

- This would come close to Deutsch’s identification of (many)
  "worlds" with a continuum of trajectories in configuration
  space. 
    - Deutsch has indeed repeatedly called Bohm’s theory
      a "many‐worlds theory under permanent denial". 

- Bell’s remark indicates that he too prefers understanding
  physical reality in traditional classical terms, probably
  a major motivation for his preference for Bohm’s theory. 

- So Bohm and Deutsch presume classical concepts; this explains
  why neither need decoherence to justify them.
