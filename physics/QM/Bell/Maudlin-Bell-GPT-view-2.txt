### 1. Did Bell assume both locality and reality?

- The consensus among careful historians (Tumulka 2015, Bricmont,
  Norsen, and indeed Bell’s own later remarks) is that Bell’s
  theorem assumes locality only. 
    - The “hidden variables” or “realism” component is DERIVED,
      not presupposed.

- Here’s the logic chain made explicit:

    - EPR (1935) argue:
        - if the results of spacelike-separated measurements are
          perfectly correlated, and if influences cannot travel
          faster than light (locality), then the results must
          have been fixed in advance — i.e. predetermined by some
          variables (call them λ). That’s the famous inference
          from locality to determinism.

    - Bell (1964) starts his paper by summarizing that EPR
      reasoning: “if” we assume locality, we are led to hidden
      variables.
        - Then, assuming locality again, he shows that any such
          local hidden-variable theory implies an inequality (the
          Bell inequality) that quantum mechanics violates.

    - Thus, locality → hidden variables → contradiction with experiment.
    - So the assumption that breaks is locality, not “realism”.

- Bell later complained that almost everyone “missed part one of
  the proof” and thought he had assumed hidden variables from the
  start. Tim Maudlin’s argument simply restores Bell’s own
  intent: the theorem is a no-locality theorem, not a no-realism
  theorem.

- So, yes — I side with Maudlin and Tumulka. Bell assumes
  locality; realism (determinism, hidden variables) appears as
  a CONSEQUENCE of locality plus the EPR correlations, not as an
  independent assumption.

### 2. Does anything in your notes suggest a “solution” to the nonlocality problem?

- Several lines of escape show up, but none are wholly convincing yet. The landscape looks like this:

- Retrocausality (effects before causes).
    - Lewis notes that if measurement choices can influence the past state of the system, then all influences remain within each particle’s light cone — locality saved, at the cost of temporal order. Elegant in principle, but no full theory yet.

- Superdeterminism or measurement-independence violation (Sabine Hossenfelder, ’t Hooft).
    - Deny that detector settings are statistically independent of hidden variables. That loophole restores locality, but at a heavy conceptual cost — it requires deep correlations between cosmic initial conditions and experimental choices.

- Nonseparability / holism.
    - Instead of “spooky” FTL signals, say the entangled pair is one extended system across spacetime, not two independent parts. Then the correlations are internal features of a single object, not inter-object influences. This shifts from “nonlocality” to “non-separability,” though it abandons the classical picture of distinct localized systems.

- Preferred frame (Bell, Bohm, Lorentzian ether).
    - Keep realism and determinism, accept an empirically hidden frame in which influences propagate instantaneously. This resolves the math but sacrifices the spirit of relativity.

- Emergent-space ideas (Smolin, holography).
    - If spacetime itself is emergent from entanglement, “locality” may only be approximate. Then Bell nonlocality is a symptom of spacetime’s underlying quantum connectivity rather than true FTL causation.
