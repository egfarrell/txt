- This contribution in honour of JS Bell will probably differ
  from others, in particular since only part of it will cover
  technical arguments.

- In fact I consider it appropriate to share with the physicists
  interested in foundational problems the repeated interactions
  I had with him in the last four years of his life, the deep
  discussions regarding the elaboration of collapse theories and
  their interpretation, the contributions he gave to the
  development of this approach, both at a formal level, as well
  as championing it on repeated occasions.

- The 2nd problem arose from the fact that Bohmian mechanics
  exhibited a very peculiar feature:
    - it was basically nonlocal.

- John tried hard to work out a similar theory which was free of
  nonlocality, but did not succeed. So he entertained the idea
  that one might prove that nonlocal features would characterize
  ANY THEORY whatsoever which reproduces the quantum predictions.

- Secondly, it's important to remember that while relating his
  derivation to Gleason's theorem, Bell stresses that in the case
  of _dispersion-free states_, to avoid a contradiction one must
  abandon the ASSUMPTION that measurement of an observable must
  yield the same value independently of what other measurements
  may be made simultaneously.
    - In brief he also clarified the unavoidably _contextual
      nature_ of any deterministic completion of quantum
      mechanics, a point of remarkable relevance ...

- I consider this a result which makes of John one of the
  greatest physicists of the past century, since he has made
  crystal clear something that nobody had ever conceived and
  which implies a radical change in our views about the world
  around us:
    - Nature is nonlocally causal!


- The other problem of QM which had worried John since
  university, is the one of resorting to two separate dynamical
  evolution principles: 
    - the 1st described by the linear and deterministic
      Schrodinger equation and 
    - the 2nd when measurements are performed and described by
      the projection postulate (von Neumann), which is
      fundamentally nonlinear and stochastic.

- And this is not the only point.

- The crucial fact is there is nothing in the theory which marks
  the borderline between the range of applicability of these two
  dynamical principles.
    - This fact worried John and he made systematic reference to
      the "shifty split". His position is wonderfully summarized
      by his remark:
        - There is a fundamental ambiguity in QM, in that nobody
          knows exactly what it says about any particular
          situation, for nobody knows exactly where the boundary
          between the wavy quantum world and the world of
          particular events is located....
        - every time we place that boundary, we must put it
          somewhere, we are arbitrarily dividing the world into
          two pieces, using two quite different descriptions ...

- But this is not the whole story:
    - ... he also analyzed many of the proposed solutions to this
      problem and proved that almost all of them are
      characterized by imprecise, vague, _verbal_ assumptions
      aimed to avoid the real contradiction which, in a quantum
      view, occurs between the waviness of quanta and our
      definite perceptions.


- Our approach is based on the idea that[13]:
    - Schroedinger's equation is not always true...

- Because this was one of his most stimulating talks, when my
  friends organized a meeting for my 70th birthday (2005),
  I worked to get access to the video of his lecture and produced
  a DVD version of it.

- However, due to the recording not being ideal and our desire to
  make the talk accessible to all, A. Bassi and myself decided
  to “decode” it and to publish it in the special issue of
  Journal of Physics A: The Quantum Universe.

- I highlight the fact that although various collections of his
  writings have been published, all omit the above mentioned
  contributions of his final years as well as his talk "Towards
  an Exact Quantum Mechanics" delivered on the 70th birthday of
  Julian Schwinger
    - (It's published by World Scientific in 1989 in: Themes in
      Contemporary Physics II, S. Deser and R.J. Finkelstein
      eds.)

- We must point out that, while all deterministic hidden variable
  models, and thus Bohmian mechanics, admit relativistic
  generalizations which require the consideration of a (hidden)
  _PREFERRED REFERENCE FRAME_, collapse models admit genuinely
  Lorentz invariant generalizations.

- One year after John's death, Mary Bell, John Ellis and D. Amati
  devoted a meeting at CERN to honor this great scientist. The
  proceedings have been published under the title "Quantum
  Reflections". The invited speakers were:
        1. R. Penrose,
        2. H. Rauch,
        3. A. Aspect,
        4. G.C. Ghirardi,
        5. J.M. Leinaas,
        6. A. Shimony,
        7. K. Gottfried,
        8. N.D. Mermin
        9. R. Jakiv.


- Here I intend to briefly reconsider Bell's position concerning
  __nonlocality__.
    - The main motivation is that Bell himself has always been
      aware of basic misunderstandings of the deep conceptual
      implications of his work, even by great physicists.
    - Moreover, this fact upset John.


- I will first mention an explicit statement, from "Bertlmann's
  socks & the nature of reality", where he gives clear voice to
  his disappointment about the interpretation of his work:
    > It is remarkably difficult to get this point across, that
    > determinism is not a presupposition of the analysis ... My
    > first paper starts with a summary of the EPR argument FROM
    > locality TO deterministic hidden variables. But the
    > commentators have almost universally reported that it
    > begins with deterministic hidden variables.

- The term _determinism_ (or equivalently _realism_) is used to
  assert that the observables of a physical system have definite
  values (coinciding with one of the eigenvalues of the
  associated self-adjoint operator) EVEN BEFORE the measurement
  process is performed.

Nature is nonlocally causal.
