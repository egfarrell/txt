### Real QM

- “Real quantum theory” means the usual postulates, but with
  real-valued states and operators, and composite systems built
  by the usual tensor product.

- For a single isolated system this is indistinguishable from
  complex QM (Stueckelberg showed in 1960 that you can mimic
  complex numbers with an extra operator `J` satisfying `J²=−1`).

- The trouble appears for composite systems.

### Experiment

- Renou, Trillo, Weilenmann, Le, Tavakoli, Gisin, Acín and
  Navascués designed a network scenario:
    - two independent sources, each sharing entangled states with
      neighboring parties (Alice-Bob, Bob-Charlie).
    - They derived a Bell-type inequality that real-valued
      quantum theory must satisfy, but that complex quantum
      theory can violate, though only by a small margin.

- Experiments soon followed in 2022 (photonic and
  superconducting-qubit setups, published in Physical Review
  Letters), and they found violations well beyond the error bars,
  favoring complex quantum theory.

### Caveats

- The result assumes the sources are independent and that
  composite systems combine by the tensor product. Also: it rules
  out a particular version of real QM, but not every conceivable
  real-number reformulation.

- The gap between the real bound and the complex quantum value is
  small, so the experiments are demanding.

- It’s a statement about the structure of the theory, not a claim
  that complex numbers are “physically present” in nature. But it
  does move them from “convenient” to “operationally needed” for
  describing multi-party correlations.
