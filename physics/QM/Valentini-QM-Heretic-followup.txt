## What happened after the article?

- The article dates from around 2010-2013, when the Planck
  satellite's data had not yet been fully released. Valentini's
  research continued, but did it produce evidence for quantum
  non-equilibrium?

### 1. Valentini developed a concrete theoretical model

- In 2015, Antony Valentini and Samuel Colin published
  "Primordial quantum nonequilibrium and large-scale cosmic
  anomalies".

- They used numerical simulations to investigate whether quantum
  non-equilibrium in the early universe could explain the
  suppression of power observed at very large scales in the CMB.

- Their model could reproduce approximately the observed deficit
  for suitable cosmological parameters. However, showing that
  a model can explain an anomaly is not the same as proving that
  it is correct.

- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.8262


### 2. Valentini's collaborators went on to analyse Planck data

- In 2019, Sandro Vitenti, Patrick Peter and Valentini published
  "Modeling the large-scale power deficit with smooth and
  discontinuous primordial spectra".

- They fitted modified primordial power spectra to Planck
  temperature data, temperature and polarisation data, and other
  cosmological observations.

- Their analysis reported a characteristic length scale of around
  2,200 megaparsecs, above which primordial scalar power would be
  reduced by roughly 20%.

- This was a genuine attempt to connect theoretical predictions
  with observations. However, a modified power spectrum fitting
  the data does not uniquely support de Broglie-Bohm (dBB)
  theory. Other mechanisms could potentially produce similar
  effects.

- Paper: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.100.043506


### 3. Did Planck confirm the hypothesis?

- No definitive confirmation emerged.

- Planck's final results continued to show unusual large-scale
  temperature patterns. However, polarisation data did not provide
  convincing independent confirmation of corresponding anomalies.

- The observed large-scale power deficit had limited statistical
  significance. Depending on the analysis, its significance was
  around 1.1-1.6 sigma, making an ordinary statistical fluctuation
  a plausible explanation.

- Sources: 
    - [Planck Collaboration (2018), "Isotropy and Statistics of the CMB"]
    - https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201935201

- The important point is that an anomaly is not automatically
  evidence for new physics. It must be statistically robust, and
  the proposed theory must explain it better than competing
  explanations.


### 4. Valentini continued developing the theory

- In subsequent work, Valentini investigated whether
  quantum-gravitational effects could generate quantum
  non-equilibrium in the early universe.

- However, in a 2021 paper, he concluded that the resulting CMB
  effect in his particular model would be too small to observe.

- This illustrates that developing a theoretical mechanism does
  not necessarily produce a measurable experimental prediction.

- Related research:
  https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Antony+Valentini&searchtype=author


## Verdict

- Valentini made theoretical progress and published research
  connecting pilot-wave theory with possible CMB anomalies. But
  the hoped-for experimental breakthrough has not occurred.

- The distinction is crucial:

- The CMB exhibits unusual large-scale features.
- Some modified primordial power-spectrum models can fit those
  features.
- It has not been established that quantum non-equilibrium caused
  them.
- No compelling observational evidence has established violations
  of the Born rule or confirmed dBB theory's distinctive predictions
  over standard quantum mechanics.

- Verdict: Valentini developed a potentially testable route to
  distinguishing dBB theory from standard QM, but the Planck data
  have not delivered a decisive result.
