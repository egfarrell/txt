
- Question: "Could quantum theory work with REAL Hilbert spaces
  from the start?"

### Madelung (1927)

- Write the wavefunction in polar form,
    - `ψ = √ρ · e^{iS/ħ}`,
    - with `ρ` the probability density and `S` a real phase.

- Plug it into the Schrödinger equation and separate real and
  imaginary parts, and you get two real equations:

    - A continuity equation,
        - `∂ρ/∂t + ∇·(ρ∇S/m) = 0`,
        - which says probability is conserved and flows like
          a fluid with velocity `v = ∇S/m`.

    - A Hamilton-Jacobi-like equation for `S`,
        - which is the classical one plus an extra term, the
          “quantum potential”
        - `Q = −(ħ²/2m)(∇²√ρ)/√ρ`.

- So QM looks like a FLUID obeying classical mechanics plus
  a strange extra force. This is the seed of Bohm’s pilot-wave
  theory (1952), where `Q` is taken seriously as a physical
  influence on particles.

- Two caveats:

    - First, the phase `S` is only defined `mod 2πħ`, so you have
      to impose a quantization condition on its loop integrals
      `(∮∇S·dl = nh)` by hand, and the equations break down at
      nodes where `ρ=0`.

    - Second, this doesn’t really eliminate the complex
      structure. It just encodes it in two real fields, so `ρ`
      and `S` together are a complex `ψ` in disguise. 

    - But that’s different from asking whether QM could work with
      real Hilbert spaces from the start.
