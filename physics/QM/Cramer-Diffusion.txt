
### Question
    - WHY does a diffusion process have to be described by
      a combination of a backwards AND forward time process...
    - Why can't an intermediate time be described simply by
      rolling FORWARD in time from an initial point... or
      reversing BACKWARDS in time from an end point?


Extra:
    - The Schrödinger Bridge Problem, 1931


### Summary

- Can diffusion be described forwards alone? 
    - Yes.
- Can it be described backwards alone? 
    - Mathematically, with qualifications; physically, reverse
      diffusion needs care.
- Why combine forward and backward solutions?
    - To condition an intermediate state on both endpoints.
- Does QM require two physical time directions?
    - No.
- Why multiply `ψ` by `ψ*`?
    - To obtain the probability density, |ψ|².


# Why Diffusion Between Two Fixed States Requires Forward AND Backward Processes

- The reason relates to the difference between 
    - an INITIAL VALUE PROBLEM 
        - predicting the future from the past
    - and
    - a BOUNDARY VALUE PROBLEM 
        - deducing what happened inbetween two fixed points in
          time.


### 1. SETUP: TWO FIXED ENDPOINTS
- In normal physics, if you open a perfume bottle at time `t₀`
  with distribution `ρ₀`, you only need to run the diffusion
  equation forward to find the distribution at an intermediate
  time t.

- However, Schrödinger posed a different problem:
  - "Suppose you observe the particles at `t₀` with distribution
    ρ₀, and you also observe them at a later time `t₁` with an
    improbable distribution `ρ₁`. Given that BOTH observations
    are true, what was the most probable distribution at an
    intermediate time `t`?"

- Crucially, `ρ₁` is NOT simply whatever natural forward
  diffusion produces. It is a constrained, observed target (e.g.,
  thermal fluctuations caused particles to congregate in an
  unlikely spot).

### 2. WHY ONE-WAY PROPAGATION FAILS

- IF YOU ONLY ROLL FORWARD FROM THE START (t₀):
    - Standard diffusion disperses particles toward maximum
      entropy. If you run forward from ρ₀, the system follows its
      unconstrained, default path. When it reaches t₁, it spreads
      into the generic "average" state and COMPLETELY MISSES the
      target distribution ρ₁. It has no knowledge of the future
      boundary condition.

- IF YOU ONLY ROLL BACKWARD FROM THE END (t₁):
    - Mathematically, running the heat/diffusion equation
      backward in time is ill-posed (fluctuations blow up
      exponentially). But even conceptually, rolling backward
      from ρ₁ carries no memory of where the particles started.
      It would arrive at t₀ in an arbitrary state that DOES NOT
      MATCH the known starting distribution ρ₀.


### 3. THE PROBABILITY LOGIC (BAYES' RULE)

- To determine the probability that a particle was at position
  `x` at intermediate time `t`, you must ask two questions
  simultaneously:

  1. How likely was it to arrive at `x` starting from `t₀`?
     --> Requires propagating FORWARD from `t₀` to `t`.

  2. How likely was it to reach the target at `t₁` starting from `x`?
     --> Requires propagating BACKWARD from `t₁` to `t`.

By Bayes' Theorem and the Markov property:

  `P(at x at time t | start, end)` ∝ `P(start → x) × P(x → end)`

- Because both conditions must be satisfied at once, the
  intermediate density splits into the PRODUCT of two functions:

  `ρ(x, t) = φ(x, t) · φ̂(x, t)`

Where:
  - `φ̂(x, t)` 
        - obeys the FORWARD heat equation (carrying memory from
          the past).

  - `φ(x, t)` 
        - obeys the BACKWARD heat equation (carrying constraints
          from the future).

Neither function alone represents physical reality; only their product gives 
the true probability distribution at time t.


### 4. LINK TO QM
- Schrödinger was struck by how closely this mirrors quantum theory:

- In QM, the probability density of finding a particle is given by the Born rule:
    `P(x, t) = ψ(x, t) · ψ*(x, t)`

- Because Schrödinger's equation contains the imaginary unit 'i'
  (`iħ ∂ψ/∂t`), taking the complex conjugate `ψ*` is
  mathematically equivalent to time-reversal `(t → -t)`.

- Just like the diffusion bridge, quantum probability at any
  moment is formed by the overlap/product of a forward-evolving
  wave (`ψ`) and a backward-evolving wave (`ψ*`).

- Decades later, John Cramer used this exact concept to develop
  the Transactional Interpretation of Quantum Mechanics (TIQM),
  treating quantum events as a time-symmetric "handshake" between
  an offer wave (`ψ`) moving forward in time and a confirmation
  wave (`ψ*`) moving backward in time.
