## Starting point (Hossenfelder’s claim)

- " `Flip`  the spin of one particle in an entangled pair...
  NOTHING happens to the other."

- This seems to contradict the usual intuition about entanglement.

## Key clarification:
    - Sabine's talking about a _local OPERATION (unitary)_, NOT a `measurement`.
    - Entanglement concerns _correlations_, not signals or influences.


## 1. Example: entangled spin state

Singlet state:

`ψ = (1/√2)( |+>_L |->_R − |->_L |+>_R )`

Properties:
- Each particle individually: random (50/50)
- Joint outcomes: perfectly anti-correlated

## 2. Apply a local operation (spin flip on LEFT)

Flip operator on left swaps:
`|+> ↔ |->`

New state:

`ψ' = (1/√2)( |->_L |->_R − |+>_L |+>_R )`

Observations:
- Still entangled
- Correlations changed (now aligned instead of anti-aligned)

BUT:

Reduced state on right unchanged:
`ρ_R = Tr_L(ρ_LR)`

→ still 50/50 outcomes

Conclusion:
- No observable change on the right particle alone
- No signal transmitted

## 3. What actually changed?

NOT:
- The physical state of the right particle (locally)

BUT:
- The _joint correlation structure_

Key idea:
> Local operations change global entanglement structure, not distant local statistics.

## 4. Measurement vs operation (critical distinction)

- ### (A) Local operation
    - Deterministic, unitary
    - Changes correlations
    - No signalling

- ### (B) Measurement
    - Outcome random
    - After measuring left, you can predict right

BUT:
- Right particle’s local statistics still unchanged
- No controllable influence

## 5. Why no faster-than-light signalling?

Because:

`ρ_R = Tr_L(ρ_LR)`

Local actions on `L` do not change `ρ_R`

Therefore:
- No experiment on `R` alone can detect what was done on `L`
- No superluminal communication possible


## 6. Bell’s theorem (precise statement)

Bell assumes:

1. Locality  
   No superluminal causal influence

2. Realism  
   Outcomes determined by pre-existing properties (λ)

3. Statistical independence  
   Measurement settings independent of λ

Then derives inequalities.

Experiment:
- Inequalities violated

Conclusion:
→ At least one assumption is false

## 7. The “fork” in interpretation

- ### Option A: `Drop realism`
    - No definite values before measurement
    - Outcomes created upon measurement

    - Keeps:
    - No signalling
    - Operational locality

- ### Option B: `Drop locality`
    - Keep hidden variables
    - Accept superluminal dependence
    - Example: Bohmian mechanics
    - Feature: Particle dynamics explicitly nonlocal


## 8. Two meanings of “locality” (source of confusion)

- ### (1) Operational locality
    - No faster-than-light signalling
    - QM satisfies this

- ### (2) Bell locality (local causality)
    - No superluminal causal dependence
    - Requires factorisation:

- `P(a,b | A,B,λ) = P(a|A,λ) P(b|B,λ)`
- This factorisation expresses Bell locality (local causality):
- Once `λ` is specified, Alice’s outcome depends only on her
  setting `A`, and Bob’s outcome depends only on his setting `B`.
- QM violates this.
- Note:
    - `A`, `B` 
        - measurement settings
        - (e.g. which spin direction Alice and Bob choose)
    - `a`, `b` 
        - measurement outcomes
        - (e.g. spin up/down → `±1`)
    - `λ` 
        - “hidden variables”
        - (a complete specification of the system’s physical
          state, beyond QM if needed)
    - `P(a, b | A, B, λ)` 
        - joint probability of getting outcomes a and b given:
        - both settings `(A, B)` and the underlying state `λ`
     


## 9. Reconciling the views

QM is:
    - Local (no signalling)
    - Nonlocal (violates Bell locality)
- So:
    - Hossenfelder: emphasising no signalling
    - Maudlin: emphasising failure of local causality
- Both correct, but different notions of “locality”


## 10. Final takeaway

- Common misconception:
    > Entanglement involves signals or influences travelling
    > between particles

- Correct view:
    - No signals
    - No local change at a distance
    - But correlations cannot be explained by local hidden variables

- Core statement:
    > Entanglement = non-classical correlations without
    > signalling

- Or:
    > QM violates local realism, not relativistic signal locality
