### Measurement "Extras"
    - The _reduction postulate_ and _Born rule_ are add-ons to
      the Schroodinger equation. 
    - According to the Copenhagen interpretation, the
      Schroodinger equation applies when a microscopic system,
      e.g. silver atom, is time-evolving in ISOLATION. 
    - But when the atom interacts with a macroscopic measuring
      apparatus (e.g. Stern–Gerlach setup), you have to use the
      reduction postulate and Born rule.

    - A related question is where the probabilities come from? 
    - QM has probabilities without a sample space! 
        - An example of a sample space is a population of people,
          40% blonde and 60% brunette. 
        - If you pick a person at random from the population,
          there is 
            - `Prob_blond    = 0.4` of a blonde, and 
            - `prob_brunette = 0.6` for brunette.
        - But the population (the sample space) is composed of
          individuals, with definite hair coloring – the
          probabilities only reflect _our ignorance_ of the
          details if we make a random pick without looking.

    - Again, the probabilities are just reflections of our
      ignorance of the details, but the details are there.
    - So we have the question: 
        - Are there hidden details underlying the probabilities
          in QM? Is there a _hidden sample space_?



- Now we retur to the original question – where is future work to
  deal with these problems headed? Two routes proceed within
  quantum theory: 
    - (i) 
        - We try to change the interpretational rules. 
        - Examples are the so-called “many worlds” interpretation
          (all possibilities are there, we only see one), and the
          “histories” program, which sets up an observer-free
          generalization of the Copenhagen rules. 
    - (ii) 
        - We say there is a _sample space_, but we don’t see it.
        - One example is Bohmian trajectories as the sample space
          underlying the Born rule. 
        - Another example comprises various statistical
          interpretations, which attribute the probabilistic
          outcomes to different internal states of the apparatus
          and or its environment, with the aim of deriving the
          Born rule.

- The other possibility is to MODIFY THE FOUNDATIONS of QM:
    - Specifically, one gets a sample space by postulating
      additional _degrees of freedom_, so called “hidden
      variables”. 
    - There are two possibilities for hidden variables: 
        - they can be LOCAL
        - or NONLOCAL. 

### Local Hidden Variables
    - Bell’s theorem asserts that _local hidden variables_ plus
      the usual rules for probabilities imply certain
      inequalities that are NOT satisfied by QM systems.
      Experiments confirm these inequalities are in fact
      violated. There is much discussion of possible loopholes,
      but I believe the result is robust, and local hidden
      variables are excluded

### Non-local Hidden Variables
    - The hidden variables could instead be non-local:
        - the hidden variables can act faster than the speed of
          light to establish correlations (as long as no faster
          than light signaling is possible).
        - The hidden variables could also obey a non-commutative
          multiplication law.
    - For the remainder I'll focus on the possibility of
      _non-local hidden variables_: this is my research interest.

- I've worked on phenomenological reduction models, but my main
  interest is at the _foundational level_.
    - I'm trying to make an analogy between QM emerging from
      a possible pre-quantum theory, and the fact that
      thermodynamics emerges from the laws of statistical
      mechanics.
    - Thermodynamics, the science of heat and work, reflects
      averaged properties of huge numbers of atoms. 
    - It is a complete, consistent system by itself, and
      remarkably was discovered in the 19th century before the
      existence of atoms was established.
    - But from statistical mechanics (the laws of motion of large
      systems of atoms), one can deduce the laws of
      thermodynamics, together with details of _fluctuation_
      corrections to thermodynamics, so called Brownian motion
      corrections.
