        - Note: Two points about Bohm in the Appendix:

        - (3) Locality is violated. The communication between the
          quantum potential and the particle occurs faster than
          light. The violation of locality is of course an
          important aspect of Bells correlations. However in the
          context of the mainstream interpretation of QM this
          violation has to do with the transition from the
          potential to the actual (see Chapter 16, Section 9).
          This communication cannot be used to transmit signals
          (Chapter 7, Section 7). But in Bohms interpretation,
          locality is violated in the transmission of information
          between two actual entities - a clear violation of
          Einsteins SR. (Hmmm... splitting hairs a little??)

        - (4) Bohm's interpretation cannot be reconciled with the
          principle of relativity: In the relativistic context it
          cannot be valid in all inertial frames of reference. It
          can be true only in one "preferred" frame. This has to
          do with the relativity of simultaneity (Chapter 2,
          Section 6). The communication between the particle and
          the pilot wave is supposed to be instantaneous. But
          that which is instantaneous in one inertial frame is
          not instantaneous in another.

        - I found the essays of the founders of QM engaging but

        - Heisenberg was the first to draw attention to the fact
          that the concept of matter in QM is very different from
          the one normally associate with the word "matter" and
          is largely a _Platonic_ one. We will supplement our
          study of Whiteheads philosophy by delving into its
          Platonic origins (Plotinus). Essential questions: are
          there different levels of being. Are some aspects of
          reality "higher" or "deeper" than others?

        - Schrodingers discovery of "wave mechanics" led to
          insights concerning the nature of electrons etc. When
          left alone, electrons aren't "things". The do not
          actually exist in space and time; their existence is
          merely potential. They emerge into momentary actualy
          existence by acts of measurement. Hence, unlike
          classical measurements, QM measurements are creative.
          They literally create the entities that are measured.

    - What is an electron? When facing this question we are
      essentially asking "what is matter"? After all, an electron
      is just a small bit of matter, and all the strange aspects
      of it's behaviour is shared by protons and neutrons. We
      keep speaking of electrons just to keep the presentation
      clear.
        - Anyway, an electron is an item that in the double-slit
          experiment gives rise to a dot on the screen, the
          location of which is subject to a wave-like probability
          distribution.

        - The probability is "wave-like" because its obtained
          through the principle of superposition.

        - Can we come up with a concept of an electron that
          encompasses both the particle-like dot on the screen
          and the wave-like probability distribution?

        - Maybe we should have a _concept_ of an electron rather
          than an image of it. Well Aristotle speaks of matter in
          terms of a capacity or a _potentiality_. This concept
          of matter is very different from Newtons "massy, hard,
          impenetrable, moving particles"

        - Page 190
            - The assumption of locality says: "Nothing can
              propagate faster than light." But in the case of
              a propagation of an influence from point A to point
              B, the influence is not transmitted by a physical
              entity that departs from A and arrives at B.
              Rather, a measurement carried out at A changes the
              _potentialities_ that affect the creation of
              a single actualy entity that is localised at both
              points A and B, and this change is not subject to
              the speed-of-light barrier.

            - This state of affairs leads to the conclusion that
              the statement "Nothing can propagate faster than
              light" can be given two different interpretations:

              1. No physical entity propagates faster than light.
              - [NOT VIOLATED]

              2. nothing that happens at point A can _affect_
                 what takes place at point B faster than the time
                 it takes light to travel from A to B.
              - [VIOLATED]
