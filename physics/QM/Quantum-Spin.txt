- An electron's spin can be measured along different directions (e.g. north/z or
  east/x), with either spin-up (+ℏ/2) or spin-down (−ℏ/2) as the outcome.

- Spin-up along north and spin-up along east are different quantum states.

- The corresponding spin operators do not commute: [Sₓ, S_z] ≠ 0. Therefore, no
  quantum state has simultaneously definite spin along both axes.

- We can measure both sequentially, but the first measurement affects the state:
  - Measure north → spin-up.
  - Measure east → 50% up, 50% down.
  - Measure north again → 50% up, 50% down.

- Why? A definite spin state along north is an equal superposition of spin-up
  and spin-down along east. Measuring east destroys the previous definite
  north-spin state.

- **Weinberg's philosophical point:** We can calculate P(spin-up along north)
  and P(spin-up along east), but asking for the probability of BOTH
  simultaneously is problematic: QM does not assign simultaneous definite values
  to incompatible observables.

- **Key distinction:**
  - Classical ignorance: properties have definite values, but we don't know
    them.
  - Quantum uncertainty: incompatible observables cannot simultaneously have
    definite values in a quantum state.
