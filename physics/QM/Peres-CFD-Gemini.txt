### Gemini

- The short answer is: mathematically, Counterfactual
  Definiteness (CFD) enters right at the beginning of Bell's 1964
  paper in his definition of the measurement functions
    - `A(a⃗, λ) = ±1` and
    - `B(b⃗, λ) = ±1`

- However, your intuition is completely justified. Bell himself
  famously insisted that he did *not* assume CFD (or determinism)
  as an independent postulate. To Bell, causal locality was
  indeed the only fundamental philosophical premise.

- The appearance of CFD in Bell's theorem, and why Asher Peres
  wrote a paper specifically targeting it, comes down to a subtle
  interplay between the maths and Bell's reasoning.


### 1. Where CFD appears mathematically in Bell (1964)

- In the second section of his 1964 paper (*"Formulation"*), Bell
  writes the outcome of Alice's measurement along axis `a⃗` as:
    - `A(a⃗, λ) = ±1`
- and Bob's along axis `b⃗` as:
    - `B(b⃗, λ) = ±1`
- where `λ` represents the complete state (hidden or otherwise)
  of the particle pair.

- Notice what this notation implies:
    - For a given single pair with state `λ`, the function `A(a⃗,
      λ)` assigns a definite number (+1 or −1) to every possible
      choice of `a⃗`.
    - When Bell later computes the difference between two
      correlation experiments:

    - `P(a⃗, b⃗) − P(a⃗, c⃗) = ∫ ρ(λ) [A(a⃗, λ) B(b⃗, λ) − A(a⃗, λ) B(c⃗, λ)] dλ`

    - he writes `B(b⃗, λ)` AND `B(c⃗, λ)` inside the _same
      integral_, evaluated on the _same state λ_.

- In the real world, you can measure `B` along `b⃗` OR along `c⃗`,
  but never both on the SAME PARTICLE.

- Writing both values as simultaneously existing for a single
  system `λ` is the exact definition of _Counterfactual
  Definiteness_.

- It assigns a definite outcome to the unperformed experiment
  ("what Bob *would* have gotten if he had chosen axis `c⃗`
  instead of `b⃗`").


### 2. If it's in the maths, why did Bell say he only assumed Locality?

- Bell did not view CFD / determinism as an arbitrary postulate;
  he believed he had DERIVED IT using EPR's argument.

- In the intro of the 1964 paper, Bell references the EPR paper:
    1. QM predicts that if Alice and Bob both choose the _same_
    axis (a⃗ = b⃗), their outcomes are 100% anti-correlated 
        - `(A = −B)`.
    2. Alice and Bob make their measurements at spacelike
    separation. Under _causal locality_, Alice's choice of
    setting cannot instantaneously affect Bob's particle.
    3. Therefore, Bob's result must have already been
    predetermined before Alice made her choice.

- As Bell later put it in his 1981 paper `Bertlmann's
  Socks...etc` 
    > "It's important to realize that in these experiments, we
    > are not just assuming determinism. EPR showed that locality
    > plus the quantum predictions for parallel analyzers ENTAILS
    > determinism."

- Because Bell considered determinism/CFD a *logical consequence*
  of locality applied to the singlet state, he viewed the
  subsequent inequality as testing locality itself.


### 3. Why the summary (and Peres' 1978 paper) lists CFD separately

Physicists who followed the Copenhagen tradition (such as Asher Peres) rejected the EPR deduction.

Their counterargument was:
> *"EPR's argument only forces determinism on you if you accept that unperformed experiments have definite answers. But in quantum mechanics, _unperformed experiments have no results_."*

If you refuse to grant EPR's step, insisting that Bob's particle simply has no outcome along axis c⃗ until a measurement along c⃗ physically occurs, then locality does not force determinism on you.

When you separate the logic into its purest mathematical components:
1. _Locality:_ Alice's choice doesn't influence Bob's outcome, and vice versa.
2. _Counterfactual Definiteness (Realism):_ It is meaningful to assign truth values to the outcomes of measurements that were not actually carried out.
3. _Statistical Independence:_ Detector settings are uncorrelated with λ.

Together, (1) + (2) + (3) mathematically yield Bell's inequalities. Since quantum mechanics violates these inequalities:
* _Bell's camp concluded:_ Locality is dead (nature is non-local).
* _Peres' camp concluded:_ Counterfactual Definiteness is dead. QM is locally causal (no faster-than-light signals can be sent), but standard "realism" fails because *unperformed experiments have no results*.

This is why Peres structured his 1978 paper as he did: he aimed to derive the contradiction with quantum mechanics purely by showing that assuming definite values for hypothetical, unperformed setups leads to algebraic nonsense, without having to abandon local interactions.
