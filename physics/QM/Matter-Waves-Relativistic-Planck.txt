      making Planck’s relation `relativistically invariant`.
    - Einstein could have seen this years earlier, but didn’t.

### Planck's Relation

- First, Planck originally gave us:
- `E = h f` or 
- `E = ℏ ω` (`ω = 2πf`)

- This was introduced for light, and later Einstein used it for
  photons. At this stage, `f` is just “the frequency of something
  oscillating”, and there's no SPATIAL WAVE built in yet.

### Now add SR
- In SR, energy and momentum are not independent.
- They form a 4-vector: `(E/c, p)`

- Under Lorentz transformations, energy and momentum mix with
  each other.
- So if `E` is proportional to `ω` (Planck's relation), 
    - then `p` had better be proportional to something that mixes
      with `ω` in the same way.

- This is the key inference.

### What mixes with frequency?
- Note: In relativity, time and space mix.

- The time component of a WAVE `4-vector` is:
    - the frequency `ω` 
- The spatial components of that wave `4-vector` is:
    - the wavevector `k`.
- The WAVE 4-vector is: 
    - `(ω/c, k)`


- This transforms under Lorentz transformations in exactly the
  same way as `(E/c, p)`

Aside: My note:
    - Now we've two vectors:
        - Energy momentum 4-vector: `(E/c, p)`
        - Wave 4-vector:            `(ω/c, k)`


- If you want `E = ℏω` to be true in all inertial frames, not
  just one, consistency almost forces `p = ℏk`

- That’s the whole punchline. You don’t assume matter waves — you are led to them.

### de Broglie wavelength

- Physically this means once you accept `p = ℏ k` you immediately
- get a wavelength:
    - `λ = 2π/|k| = h/p`
    - (note: wave vector `k = 2π/λ`)

- That is exactly the de Broglie wavelength. 
- A free particle with definite momentum must be associated with
  a spatial oscillation.

- At this point, “wave nature of matter” is no longer an extra
  hypothesis — it is the relativistically consistent extension of
  Planck’s relation.

### Why Einstein didn’t do it
- (and why deBroglie did)

- Einstein already had:
    - `E = ℏω`
    - + relativistic energy–momentum relations

- But he mainly thought of `E = ℏω` as a property of LIGHT
  quanta, not a universal rule for all particles.

- deBroglie made the conceptual leap:
    - if photons obey this correspondence between particle
      quantities and wave quantities, why not electrons, atoms,
      anything with energy and momentum?

- Once you impose Lorentz covariance, the leap becomes almost unavoidable.

### Summary

- At undergrad level, the logic can be summarized as:
    - Planck: `E = ℏ ω`
    - Relativity: `(E, p)` must transform together
    - Waves: `(ω, k)` transform together
    - Consistency → `p = ℏ k`
    - Therefore matter with momentum `p` has wavelength `λ = h/p`

- So the wave nature of matter is not guessed — it drops out of
  demanding that Planck’s relation make sense in relativistic
  spacetime.
