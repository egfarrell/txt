- A quirky bit of quantum lore is hiding in that note. 
- And yes: the surprising claim is true in Bohr’s model, and your
  intuition that “the emitted light frequency should match the
  electron’s orbital frequency” is exactly what CLASSICAL PHYSICS
  would predict — but QM broke that expectation in a spectacular
  way.

### 1. Classical expectation (the intuition you learned)

- A charge in circular motion radiates electromagnetic waves at
  its orbital frequency and its harmonics.
- So if an electron orbited a nucleus like a tiny PLANET, you’d
  expect:
    - emitted light freq `ν_em ≈ ν_orbit (orbital frequency)`

- This is what Lorentz and classical EM worldview predicted.

### 2. Bohr destroys that expectation (1913)

- Bohr asserted three audacious rules:

    - Electrons occupy discrete stationary orbits with energies
      `Eₙ ∝ −1/n²`.
    - While in a stationary orbit, an electron does not radiate
      despite being accelerated.
    - Light is emitted only when the electron jumps between
      orbits, with frequency
        - `ν = (Eₙ − Eₘ) / h`

- This frequency is not the orbital frequency. 
    - It’s set by the DIFFERENCE of the two allowed energies.

- That is the “theory of Bohr must then be right” line in your
  note: spectroscopy confirmed that the transition frequencies
  matched `ΔE/h` precisely, even though they did not match `ν_orbit`.

### 3. What does “halfway between the orbits” refer to?

- Gilder is alluding to an old observation of Bohr’s:
    - The classical orbital frequency for a Bohr orbit `n` is
    - `ν_orbit(n) = (2 R c) / n³`

- And Bohr noticed that for large n, the transition frequency
    - `ν ≈ (Eₙ − Eₘ)/h`
    - is approximately the average of the two orbital frequencies
      when `n` and `m` are close.

- In symbols (`n−m = 1` for adjacent levels):
    - `ν ≈ 1/2 [ν_orbit(n) + ν_orbit(m)]`

- This is the “halfway” remark: in the semiclassical limit, the
  quantum-jump frequency tends toward the midpoint between the
  two classical frequencies — but never equals either one. 
    - It’s a faint bridge between quantum and classical worlds.

- Bohr used this as evidence that his quantized atom still
  whispered classical hints in the limit of large quantum
  numbers.

### 4. The actual correct modern statement

In full QM:
    - The electron in a stationary state has no classical orbital
      frequency at all — it is a standing wave, not a circling
      bead.
    - Light is emitted at frequencies determined solely by `ΔE
      = Eₙ − Eₘ.`
    - There is no meaningful “electron frequency” that the photon
      tracks.

- The Bohr model’s orbital frequencies are vestiges of
  semiclassical intuition, not physical oscillations of the
  electron’s position.

### 5. So is the note “true”?

Yes, historically and conceptually:

- In Bohr’s model, the emitted photon's frequency is not the
  orbital frequency.
- It is determined solely by the energy difference between
  levels.
- In the semiclassical (large-n) limit, `ν_em` lands roughly
  halfway between the two classical orbital frequencies — but
  this is a curiosity, not a fundamental rule.
- Modern QM supports Bohr’s `ΔE/h` rule, but rejects the idea of
  a literal orbital frequency altogether.
