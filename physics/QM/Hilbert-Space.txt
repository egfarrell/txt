- You’re mixing two levels of “geometry” that sound similar.
  First, separate three ideas often blurred together:

1. vector space itself
2. a basis (a set of coordinate axes)
3. a single axis (one basis vector)

- Now apply that to Hilbert space and to measurement.
- Start with an ordinary analogy, because the logic is identical.

In ordinary 3D space:
- The space is `ℝ³` itself.
- A coordinate FRAME is a choice of basis, e.g. `{x̂, ŷ, ẑ}`.
    - ie a bunch of mutually perpendicular axes
- A coordinate AXIS is one element of that basis, e.g. `x̂` alone.

- The frame is the whole mutually perpendicular set.
- An axis is just one direction inside that frame.

- You were right to feel something was off: an axis is not the
  frame; it is part of the frame. Now translate this to QM.

### Hilbert space:
    - The vector space
        - is the Hilbert space of states `|ψ⟩`.
    - A coordinate frame
        - corresponds to choosing an OBSERVABLE to measure.
    - A coordinate axis
        - corresponds to choosing one EIGENSTATE of that
          observable.

- More explicitly, choosing a physical quantity (observable) `A`:
    - Position x, momentum p, energy H, spin Sz, etc.
    - This fixes a particular orthonormal basis `{|aₙ⟩}` of
      Hilbert space.
    - This whole set `{|aₙ⟩}` is the coordinate FRAME.

- Choosing a value of that quantity:
    - A particular eigenvalue `aₙ`.
    - This singles out one basis vector `|aₙ⟩`.
    - That is a single coordinate axis.

- So the correct hierarchy is:
    - Hilbert space
    - → observable (operator)
    - → eigenbasis (coordinate frame)
    - → eigenvector (coordinate axis)

- Now let’s rewrite your last section with corrected language:
    - “The choice of which physical quantity to measure dictates
      the choice of coordinate frame (a complete orthonormal
      basis) in Hilbert space.
        - The choice of which value of the quantity to detect
          fixes a particular coordinate axis (one basis vector)
          within that frame.”

### Why the confusion feels natural:

- In CM, we often say “the x-axis” and mean “the x-coordinate
  system,” because we’re implicitly holding `y` and `z` fixed in
  the background.
- In QM, each observable brings along its entire basis, so the
  distinction becomes unavoidable.

- One more key conceptual payoff:
    - The wave function `ψ(x)` is not “the state” (note the 'x') 
        - It is the components of the abstract vector `|ψ⟩` in
          the position basis.

- Switch observable → switch basis → same vector, different components.

- That’s exactly why your “vector metaphor runs deep”: it’s not
  a metaphor at all; it’s literally linear algebra in an
  infinite-dimensional space.
