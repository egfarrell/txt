
Perimeter Quantum Foundations (2019)

### 2nd quantum revolution
    - Heisenberg's matrix mechanics originally based on the idea
      that systems were always in stationary states and would from
      time to time jump between them indeterministically
      (inspired by the Bohr atom)
    - Heisenberg found he needed the physical quantities
      (observables) to be matrices to get this to work.
    - Non-stationary states were a latter addition (with Born and
      Jordan) and quite alien to Heisenberg's initial thinking.
      e.g. superposition of energy states

    - Schrodinger intitially thought of his wavefunction as
      a physical field (like the EM field). Particles were
      supposed to emerge somehow from the dynamics. ie some kind
      of singular solutions that looked like particles.

    - But these particle-like solutions generally don't exist for
      realistic Hamiltonians. Wavefunctions spread in time.

    - Enganglement also makes the physical field interpretation
      difficult. (Its a wave in configuration space)

    - The final theory was a mish-mash of these two approaches.
      Heisenberg had to borrow Non-stationary states from
      Schrodinger. And Schrodinger had to borrow the idea of
      probabilities which were very natural in Heisenbergs
      picture.

    - Two heuristic points of view in Quantum Information theory:

    - "Church of the Larger Hilbert Space" 
        - QM is a dynamical theory, much like classical field
          theory, but with a weirder objected called
          a wavefunction replacing the classical field. All is to
          be derived from a _wavefunction_ evolving unitarily in
          time.
        - Why "Larger": Theres some pure state of a larger system;
          this is what is like the classical field and obeys the
          Schrodinger eqn. That's what's always fundamental. So
          when you see a density operator, your first thought is
          theres a larger HIlbert space and this is the partial
          trace over that.

    - "Church of the Smaller Hilbert Space"
        - Something weird happened to the algebra of observables:
          they became non-commutative. QM is the only consistent
          _probability_ theory for such observables.

    - There's something special about QM that you can derive the
      whole theory starting from two totally different points of
      view.

    - In the 1950s some people became dissatisfied with the
      "_split_" between the classical and quantum worlds. In
      principle, you should be able to describe the whole
      universe as a quantum system, there is no "classical"
      world.

      extra functional analysis properties to make it behave
      nicely in the continuum case.
