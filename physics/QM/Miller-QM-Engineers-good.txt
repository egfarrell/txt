### Photons have momentum
    - In QM, momentum is a more useful quantity than velocity`*`
      and we prefer to think of classical kinetic energy as:
        - `KE = p^2/2m`.
        - `*` There are many reasons to prefer momentum, but
          a basic one is _photons_ have momentum even though they
          have no mass and travel at the velocity of light.

### Potentials more useful than Force
    - In QM, we do have particle motion corresponding to Newton’s
      2nd law, but we cannot use that particular expression as
      a way to start out in QM.
    - Indeed, in QM we seldom if ever directly use the idea of
      force, or Newton’s second law.
    - This is partly because we can also express force as the
      _gradient of a potential energy_, and hence knowledge of
      the potential energy everywhere contains all we need to
      know about forces.

### Diffraction: Huygens' Principle
    - In general, diffraction effects are difficult to calculate
      exactly because wave equations are difficult to solve
      exactly when one includes boundary conditions for objects.
    - A simple principle that often gives a useful approximation
      to diffractive effects is `Huygens' Principle`.
    - It states that one can calculate the next wave-front by
      presuming that each point on the previous wave-front is
      a source of spherically expanding waves. 
        - Though not absolutely exact, it's quite accurate
          (especially with minor adjustments), and we use it in
          the main text to discuss elementary diffractive
          effects.
    - Huygens’ principle was published in 1690 (delayed due to
      translation from French to Latin)
        - The principle is not quite correct, because it predicts
          _backwards waves_ that don't exist.
            - This problem was fixed in an ad-hoc way by Fresnel,
              and the resulting `Huygens-Fresnel theory` of
              diffraction works well for simple wave problems.
        - A rigorous solution, involving two different kinds of
          sources on the wavefront, is given by Helmholtz’ (and
          later Kirchhoff’s) integration of the wave equation.
        - More recently it's been shown that a different kind of
          single source works very well, restoring Huygens’
          original concept. 
            - (DAB Miller, Optics Lett. 16, 1370-1372, (1991))
    - The simple spherical wave is, though, a good approximation
      for small angles if we are only interested in the wave
      propagation in the forward direction.
