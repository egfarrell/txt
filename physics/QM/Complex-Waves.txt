### Claude
- At least for the classical waves, “Cannot be ignored” is too strong.

- Classical waves: 
    - Everything can be done with real functions like `cos(kx
      − ωt)`. Complex exponentials are a convenience. 
    - They turn trig identities into simple algebra, make phase
      shifts a multiplication, and make linear equations easy to
      solve. 
    - But the physical quantity (a string’s displacement, an
      electric field) is real, and you take the real part at the
      end. 
    - Physicists did all of this with sines and cosines long
      before complex notation caught on. In 1922 nothing about
      wave physics forced complex numbers on anyone.

- What Gribbin is highlighting, with hindsight: 
    - Schrödinger's equation, `iħ ∂ψ/∂t=Ĥψ`, has an explicit `i`

- This is different from the classical wave equation, which only
  has second derivatives in time and so has real solutions. 

- The first-order-in-time structure with an `i` is what makes `ψ`
  complex in an essential way, and it’s the setup for what
  Schrödinger did in 1926. 

- So Gribben's sentence is really a forward reference to QM, but
  makes it a bit misleading about waves in general.

### But... Madelung...
    - $MY_NOTES/physics/QM/Real-QM-Madelung.txt

    - Even there, “necessary” is debatable. You can split `ψ`
      into real and imaginary parts and write two coupled real
      equations (this is essentially Madelung’s hydrodynamic
      form). 

    - So complex numbers are arguably a compact bookkeeping
      device even in QM. But the story has an interesting twist. 


### Real QM, Renou
    - A 2021 result (Renou et al., Nature) showed that a QM
      restricted to real numbers makes different predictions from
      standard complex QM in certain multi-party network
      experiments, and experiments have since favored the complex
      version. 

    - So for QM there’s a sense in which the complex structure
      isn’t just convenience; it goes beyond what the classical
      wave case supports.

- So: for the 1922 waves, 'cannot be ignored' is too strong. For
  QM, Gribbin’s hindsight is much better justified, though he
  overstates it by attaching it to waves generally.
