- There is evidence that Einstein’s deepest objection was
  locality, and he regarded quantum indeterminism as the route by
  which non-locality entered, not as the ultimate sin by itself.
  Your notes (and Maudlin’s phrasing) are in line with Einstein’s

### 1. Einstein’s priority: separability and locality, not determinism per se

- Einstein repeatedly said what concerned him most was that
  spatially separated systems have their own real physical states
  (what he called separability or locality). Determinism was
  secondary.
- A clear statement appears in a 1948 letter to Born:
    - Albert Einstein:
    > “I'd like to state a theorem which at present cannot be
    > based upon anything more than a faith in the simplicity of
    > nature: the real factual situation of the system `S2` is
    > independent of what is done with the system `S1`, which is
    > spatially separated from the former.”

- This is not a determinism claim. It's a locality claim.
- Einstein was willing to tolerate probabilistic laws in
  principle — for example in statistical mechanics — provided
  probabilities reflected ignorance about underlying local facts.

### 2. The EPR argument: 
    - indeterminism + distant measurements = incompleteness 
- In the 1935 EPR paper, Einstein’s chain of reasoning is
  essentially:
    - QM is indeterministic.
    - Therefore it does not assign definite values to CERTAIN
      OBSERVABLES.
    - But locality implies that distant measurements cannot
      change what is real here.
    - Therefore those values must already exist.
    - Hence QM is incomplete.
- Crucially:
    - Einstein does not conclude “QM is wrong because it is
      indeterministic.”
    - He concludes: either QM is incomplete or locality is false
      (unstated, because Einstein assumed locality was true?).
    - Einstein consistently chose incompleteness over abandoning
      locality.

### 3. Einstein explicitly links indeterminism to non-locality
- This is the key point. In later writings, Einstein becomes
  increasingly explicit that if one insists QM is complete, then
  indeterminism forces non-locality.
- From Einstein’s 1949 “Autobiographical Notes” 
    - (source Maudlin often cites):
    - Paraphrased faithfully:
        - If the `ψ-function` is taken as a COMPLETE description
          of reality, then the measurement on one system brings
          about a real change in the physical state of another
          distant system. This contradicts locality.
- This is almost exactly the claim in your notes:
    - indeterminism → collapse → non-locality.
- Einstein saw collapse not as an `epistemic update` but as
  a `physical change` if QM is complete. That physical change
  would have to propagate instantaneously.

### 4. Why indeterminism mattered to Einstein at all
- So why did Einstein harp on about indeterminism?
- Because determinism allows locality to be preserved:
    - If outcomes are predetermined by local variables, then no
      distant measurement needs to do anything.
    - If outcomes are genuinely created at measurement, then
      distant measurements must coordinate outcomes non-locally.
- In other words, for Einstein:
    - determinism → locality possible
    - indeterminism + completeness → non-locality unavoidable
- This is exactly the structure Maudlin emphasizes.
- Tim Maudlin argues that Einstein’s real target was
  non-locality, and that indeterminism was objectionable only
  because of its implications for locality.

### 5. Bell vindicates Einstein’s diagnosis (but not conclusion)
- John Bell later showed that:
    - No `LOCAL` theory — deterministic or indeterministic — can
      reproduce QM.
    - Einstein was right that QM implies non-locality.
    - Einstein was wrong that incompleteness could save locality.
- Bell himself explicitly said Einstein saw the problem earlier
  and more clearly than Bohr.

### 6. Answering your parenthetical question directly
- “So in Einstein's mind the source of the non-locality was the
  indeterminism?”
- Yes — given the assumption of completeness.
- More precisely, Einstein believed:
    - Indeterminism alone is not fatal.
    - Indeterminism plus completeness forces non-local state change.
    - Therefore either QM is incomplete or nature is non-local.
- He refused to accept non-locality, so he rejected completeness.
- Maudlin’s phrasing “indeterminism and thus non-locality” is an
  accurate reconstruction of Einstein’s reasoning, not
  a distortion.

  focused on the "Principle of Local Action."

- He argued that for physics to be possible, objects in different
  parts of space must have a "being" independent of one another.

    > "If one renounces the assumption that what exists in
    > different parts of space has an individual, independent

- The Logic:
    1.  If the wave function provides a *complete* description of
    reality (as Bohr claimed), then measuring Particle

    2.  This "instant change" is non-local (spooky action at
    a distance).
    3.  Since Einstein believed non-locality was physically
    impossible, he concluded the wave function *cannot* be
    a complete description.
    4.  Therefore: The "indeterminism" (the fact that we can't
    predict the outcome) isn't because nature is random; 
        - it's because the theory is missing the "hidden
          variables" that would restore both determinism and
          locality.

### 2. The EPR Paper (1935)
- The famous Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen (EPR) paper is often taught
  as an argument for determinism, but its foundation is Locality.

- The paper states: *"If, without in any way disturbing a system,
  we can predict with certainty the value of a physical quantity,
  then there exists an element of reality corresponding to that
  quantity."*

- Einstein uses locality to force a choice:
    *  Either QM is incomplete (there are hidden variables we
       don't see).
    *  Or QM is non-local (measuring A affects B).

- Einstein chose "incomplete" because he believed locality was
  a non-negotiable requirement for any field theory (like GR). To
  Einstein, indeterminism was just the "price" Bohr paid for
  keeping a non-local theory.

### 3. The Correspondence with Max Born
- In a 1947 letter to Max Born, Einstein clarifies that his
  problem isn't the "dice," but the "spooky action":
    > "I cannot seriously believe in [QM] because the theory
    > cannot be reconciled with the idea that physics should
    > represent a reality in time and space, free from spooky
    > actions at a distance."

- Later, in 1948, he wrote to Born again, explaining that his
  rejection of QM was based on the "principle of contiguity" (the
  idea that things only affect their immediate neighbors). He
  noted that if you abandon this principle, you lose the ability
  to define closed systems and do science at all.

### 5. Summary of Einstein’s Chain of Logic
- To Einstein, the chain worked like this:
    1. Observation: QM is indeterministic

    3. Deduction:

    4. Conclusion:
