### Claude
- Your confusion is totally understandable — the note is using
  "frame" and "axis" in a way that can easily blur together.
  Here's the distinction:

- A coordinate frame = a whole bunch of axes together
    - Think of ordinary 3D space: a coordinate frame is the full
      set {x, y, z} — three perpendicular axes considered as
      a system. You choose a frame when you decide *how* you want
      to describe the space (e.g., Cartesian vs. spherical
      coordinates).

- A coordinate axis = one single direction within that frame
    - Within your {x, y, z} frame, the x-axis is just one of the
      three. Picking a specific axis means picking one particular
      direction.


- Now translating to Hilbert space:
    - Choosing which `physical quantity` to measure (e.g., spin vs.
      position vs. momentum) selects the coordinate frame;
      a whole organized set of perpendicular axes, one for each
      possible outcome of that measurement.

    - Choosing which `specific value` of that quantity you're
      asking about (e.g., spin-up *specifically*, rather than
      spin in general) selects one particular axis within that
      frame.


- A concrete example:

- Say you decide to measure spin. That decision picks the "spin
  frame" — which has two axes: one for spin-up, one for
  spin-down. But "spin-up" and "spin-down" are the individual
  *axes* within that frame. You can't have a frame with just one
  axis — the frame is the whole coordinated set.

- So the hierarchy is: quantity → frame → value → axis. Your note
  is saying these are two separate choices, made at two separate
  levels.
