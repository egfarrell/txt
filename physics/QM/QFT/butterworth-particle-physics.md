why large ring?
    - a big ring has gentler curves than a small one so the
      bending force generated by the magnets dont have to be as
      strong and the protons can get to higher momentum without
      skidding

    - there is a trade off between how high the proton momentum
      can be and how sharp the bend is. i.e. for a van going
      round a bend, theres a max speed it can go without
      skidding.

    - later: the limiting factor on the maximum energy protons
      can reach at the lhc is the centripetal force - the force
      needed to bend them round the corners so they stay in the
      ring.

problems with electrons?
    - They give off _synchrotron radiation_ like a speedboat
      making a wave when it turns in the water.

    - As a charged particle _accelerates_ around a corner photons
      fly off and carry away __energy__.

    - the synchrotron radiates depends strongly on the mass. it
      decreases like the 4th power of the mass and is actually
      much worse for low mass particles.

    - an electron is 1800 times lighter than a proton so the
      energy lost for electrons is 1800^4 more than a proton
    - i.e. 11 trillion times!

    - related to the 4th power of the mass gives off 11 trillion
      more synchnotron radiation than protons?

    - sychronised EM fields boost electrons around the circuit
      but when you loss as much energy to synchrotron radiation
      as you gain with the oscillating EM boost, then youve hit
      the max energy.

    - Again curves are an issue with electron colliders too
    - sharp turns would lose more energy to synchrotron

- force carriers
    - are called vector bosons
    - light - photons
    - strong force - gluons (only felt by quarks)
    - weak force - w and z bosons

- vector boson scattering
    - w and z bosons actually have mass!
    - as protons collide theres a small chance each they will
      radiate a w boson - and smaller chance the w bosons will
      hit each other.
    - i.e. WW scattering, or happen with Z's too

    but if you dont include the higgs, as you

### resistance
    - 12000 amp currents will really heat up the conducting
      material!
    - so super-conducting materials come to the rescue

    - at low temp the electrons form pairs and start to behave
      like bosons. they sink into a quantum condensate, and it
      would take a lot of energy to affect any pair, cos that'd
      mean changing the whole condensate.

    - so the colliskions in the material dont have enough energy
      to affect the electrons (esp if the material cold) so the
      pairs of electrons flow unimpeded and experience no
      resistance.
