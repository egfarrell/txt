-            Dear Eliáš, now you talk about the ambiguity of the vacuum state,
             not the whole reps.
-
-            The vacuum state MEANS the ground state of the energy, the
             eigenstate with the lowest eigenvalue. If there is no unambiguous
             Hamiltonian, there can be no unambiguous vacuum. Common sense.
-
-            There is no privileged definition of a Hamiltonian in a generic
             curved spacetime. So there is clearly no unique vacuum state there.
-
-            These statements are facts deduced from the long distance
             approximation within its range of validity (they really follow from
             the general principles of relativity) and they cannot be changed by
             a more precise theory. In a fancier theory, there can be a detailed
             discussion of vacuum like states but not a unique one.
-
-            In special relativity, energy is P0, a component of a 4-vector, so
             there is a dependence on the inertial frame, mixing with the
             momentum. General relativity allows "different speeds at different
             points of space" and mixing the Hamiltonians for different inertial
             observers at various points. That's why in a generic background
             without special-relativity-like asymptotia, there is a natural
             non-uniqueness of the Hamiltonian. These claims don't really depend
             on the precise dynamical theory or UV completions. They are
             statements that follow from the general principles of relativity
             that we want any good theory to reproduce in one way or another.
-
-            De Sitter space has a special symmetry and that is why one can talk
             about various vacuum-like states there but not a truly "unique
             vacuum".

-                For Wightman axioms to hold literally, the theory should be
                 healthy up to arbitrarily high energies. This is because
                 according to one of the axioms, fields are operator-valued
                 tempered distributions, and the latter are functionals on the
                 Schwartz space, which includes test functions of arbitrarily
                 small support in spacetime. So Standard model on its own
                 probably doesn't satisfy the axioms, due to the Landau pole.
                 There may exist some generalization of the Wightman axioms to
                 EFTs, where there's an explicit UV cut-off on the size of the
                 test-functions, but I haven't looked into it.
