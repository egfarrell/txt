### What are 'Loops'
    - This “loops” language is the visual shorthand of quantum
      field theory (QFT), and it’s tied to how we actually
      calculate quantum corrections.


- Now, a “loop” appears in a diagram when a particle is emitted
  and then reabsorbed in a virtual process.

- A “virtual” particle isn’t one you can detect directly — it
  exists only as a fluctuation that lives for a very short time,
  borrowing energy from the vacuum according to the uncertainty
  principle ΔE·Δt ≈ ħ.

- Inside the math of QFT, that process corresponds to an integral
  over all possible momenta that this internal (virtual) particle
  could have.

- Graphically, that integral shows up as a loop in the diagram:
  one line goes out, curls back, and rejoins.

- So for example, imagine a Higgs particle sitting in empty
  space. Even if nothing seems to be happening, quantum mechanics
  allows a Higgs field quantum to emit a virtual top quark and
  reabsorb it a moment later. That’s drawn as a loop of top quark
  lines attached to the Higgs line.

- Each type of particle the Higgs interacts with — quarks, W and
  Z bosons, itself — contributes such a loop.

- Each loop adds a correction to the Higgs’s mass, energy, and
  interactions.

- Here’s the intuitive picture:

- “Virtual particles popping in and out” = temporary excitations
  of fields that mediate interactions.

- “Loops” = diagrams where those virtual particles form closed
  paths, representing internal quantum fluctuations.

- Those loops modify measurable quantities like mass and charge
  — that’s what “radiative corrections” really are.

- In the case of the Higgs, those loop diagrams don’t just tweak
  the mass a little — they blow it up quadratically with the
  cutoff energy Λ. That’s why the hierarchy problem shows up so
  dramatically: the mathematics of these loops refuses to leave
  the Higgs mass small unless something cancels the huge
  contributions.

- The poetic version: even in a vacuum, the universe is buzzing
  with ghostly particles constantly creating and annihilating
  themselves — and the Higgs field, being sensitive to
  everything, feels every one of those ghosts.
