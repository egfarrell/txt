### 5 Problems 
    - Gauge Problem
        - The gauge group structure of the standard model
          (SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1)) is somewhat ad hoc. Why exactly this
          group and this charge structure?

    - Charge quantization
        - (why is the magnitude of the electron charge equal to
          that of the proton) remains unexplained.
        - There are three independent gauge couplings – one
          wonders about unification or deeper structure.
        - So the issue is: we have a mathematically consistent
          gauge theory, but its gauge structure seems unmotivated
          within the SM itself.

    - Fermion Problem
        - The masses of the fermions (quarks + leptons), their
          mixings (CKM matrix, neutrino mixing) and the
          replication of families (generations) are unexplained.

    - Neutrino masses (and whether they are Dirac or Majorana)
      require physics beyond the SM.
        - CP violation in the SM is insufficient to explain the
          baryon asymmetry of the Universe.
        - So: large parts of flavour physics and the fermion
          mass/mixing hierarchy aren’t addressed.

    - Higgs / Hierarchy Problem
        - In the SM the Higgs mass squared receives large
          radiative corrections (e.g., δM² ∼ (Λ²) when Λ is the
          cutoff) so to keep the Higgs at the weak scale requires
          fine-tuning if new physics sits at a high scale.
        - The question: why is the weak scale (∼10² GeV) so much
          smaller than, say, the GUT or Planck scale?
        - So the SM is technically unstable under radiative
          corrections unless there is some mechanism (SUSY,
          compositeness, extra dimensions, etc.) controlling it.

    - Strong CP Problem
        - Within QCD one could write a CP-violating θ-term, yet
          experimentally θ must be extremely tiny (θ < 10⁻⁹ from
          neutron EDM bounds) whereas the weak interactions would
          naturally generate θ­effects of order ~10⁻³.
        - So: why is QCD so CP‐conserving (in this particular
          way)? Mechanisms exist (Peccei-Quinn symmetry, axion)
          but SM alone doesn’t explain it.
        - Note: sometimes this is considered a subset of fermion
          flavour/CP issues, but Langacker lists it separately.

    - Graviton (Gravity) Problem
        - The SM does not incorporate gravity (quantum gravity is
          not renormalizable in the usual sense).
        - Also the cosmological constant (vacuum energy) issue:
          spontaneous symmetry breaking (e.g., Higgs) gives
          a vacuum energy many orders of magnitude bigger than
          observed.

    - So the SM is incomplete: it doesn’t unify with gravity, and
      the cosmological constant problem points to deep issues.
