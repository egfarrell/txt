# Ramsey Interferometry: Quantum Phase and Physical Oscillations


## 1. What is actually oscillating?

Imagine an atom with two energy eigenstates:

- |1⟩, with energy E₁.
- |2⟩, with energy E₂.

A microwave pulse prepares the atom in a superposition:

`|ψ(0)⟩ = (|1⟩ + |2⟩)/√2`

Now comes the extraordinary bit.

QM tells us that each energy eigenstate evolves in time according to:

- |1⟩ → e^(−iE₁t/ℏ)|1⟩
- |2⟩ → e^(−iE₂t/ℏ)|2⟩

Consequently:
- |ψ(t)⟩ = (e^(−iE₁t/ℏ)|1⟩ + e^(−iE₂t/ℏ)|2⟩)/√2

Notice something.

The two components accumulate different phases because they have different energies.

The physically relevant quantity is their relative phase:

`Δφ(t) = (E₂ − E₁)t/ℏ`

And therefore:

`Δφ(t) = ΔE·t/ℏ`

The relative phase rotates continuously, at an angular frequency:

ω = ΔE/ℏ

Or, in ordinary frequency:

f = ΔE/h

That's the atomic transition frequency.


## 2. Abstract phase -> physically measurable

Here's the crucial step.

Suppose we measure an observable that can connect the two energy states.

Its expectation value can contain terms proportional to:

⟨ψ(t)|A|ψ(t)⟩ ∝ cos(ΔE·t/ℏ)

In other words, the relative phase appears in the predictions for an actual physical measurement.

The oscillation isn't necessarily the atom physically moving backwards and forwards.

Rather, some measurable property of the atom oscillates because of the evolving quantum interference between its two states.

This is not just mathematical decoration.

The interference is experimentally observable.

## 3. Ramsey's experiment makes it tangible

The experiment exploits precisely this phenomenon.

1. First microwave pulse: 
        - prepares a superposition of two atomic energy states.

2. Free evolution: 
        - the relative phase accumulates at a rate determined by the energy difference.

3. Second microwave pulse: 
        - makes the two quantum amplitudes interfere.

4. Measurement: 
        - detects which energy state the atom occupies.

The probability of finding the atom in either state depends on the accumulated phase.

For an idealised Ramsey experiment, one obtains an oscillatory probability of the form:

`P₂(t) = [1 + cos(Δω·t + φ₀)]/2`

where `Δω` is the difference between the atomic transition frequency and the microwave frequency.

By varying the microwave frequency, one finds the condition for maximum resonance.

This allows extraordinarily precise measurements of atomic transition frequencies.


## 4. The really profound point

There is a subtle distinction worth appreciating.

A `global phase` is physically unobservable:

|ψ⟩ → e^(iφ)|ψ⟩

It changes nothing measurable.

But a `relative phase` between components of a superposition is physically significant.

For example:

(|1⟩ + |2⟩)/√2

and

(|1⟩ + e^(iφ)|2⟩)/√2

can produce different measurement probabilities.

So we have something rather remarkable:

- Hilbert space contains vectors.
- Superpositions contain relative phases.
- Time evolution changes those relative phases.
- Relative phases affect interference.
- Interference changes measurable experimental outcomes.

- The mathematical phase becomes experimentally consequential.

- One final thought: 
    - The atom doesn't need to be spatially oscillating for there
      to be a genuine physical oscillation. QM allows an internal
      state to evolve in a way that produces periodic changes in
      observable quantities.

That's what makes atomic clocks so beautiful: they turn the time evolution of a quantum state into an extraordinarily stable physical reference for time itself.
