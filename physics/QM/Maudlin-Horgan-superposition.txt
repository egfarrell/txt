### Entanglement
    - Subtle: not every engangled state would lead to violations
      of locality. But violations of locality always stem from
      entangled states.
    - Horgan:
        - So you can have local forms of entanglement?
    - Maudlin:
        - Yes. You can have a single particle with a DOF for
          position, and a DOF for spin. And these two can be
          entangled, just for a SINGLE PARTICLE.
        - But there's no non-locality because only one particle
          is involved. To get non-locality we must do experiments
          on 2 objects that are separated.

### Superposition
    - Horgan: I've realised entanglement is a special form of
      "`superposition`" (?)
    - Maudlin: No! That's often said, but not correct!
        - `Superposition` just means we have a `vector space`,
          and in a 2D case, when you add one vector to another
          you get a "superposition" of 2 vectors.
        - And every vector is a superposition of other vectors in
          infinitely many ways.
        - So you can't say this state (vector) is a superposition
          and that one isn't. They are ALL superpositions.
        - But they're NOT all ENTANGLED though!
        - A state is either entangled or it isn't.

### Wave function
    - Don't call the `wave function` a probability WAVE.
    - Call it a probability AMPLITUDE because you have to square
      it to get a probability.
    - You never get destructive interference between
      probabilities, because probabilities are always positive.
    - However at the level of AMPLITUDES (before you do the
      squaring) you can get `destructive interference` there.
        - Because the amplitudes are just like vectors.

### Product states
    - Prepare `state A` in room 1.
    - Prepare `state B` in room 2.
    - So you have "product" states: `AA`, `AB`, `BA` etc.
    - You might think that's all the states you can have.
        - But that's not true.
    - You can have ENTANGLED states which are a superposition of
      PRODUCT states. But "they" (the engangled states??)
      themselves are not product states.
        - ie, it's not possible to understand the entangled state
          as just `(a state here) + (another state there)`
        - ie it's NOT this part on it's own, plus this part on
          it's own and the whole state is just the logical sum of
          those two...

### - Entangled states:
    - they are: NON-PRODUCT states of separated parts of a system
    - (You don't get this in classical physics)

    - Einsteins problem: Einstein letter to Bohr:
        - If I take any little piece of space, I should be able
          to say what's happening there, completely independently
          of what's happening anywhere else.
        - (But that's what entanglement denies)
