### GPT analysis:
    - Bohmian mechanics (deBB pilot-wave theory) sits in
      a fascinating middle ground:
        - it’s ontic, but not in the same way as standard QM

    - How it fits into that earlier picture:

    - Ontology:
        - In Bohmian mechanics, particles always have definite
          positions and trajectories. There are no literal
          “superpositions of matter” in the ontological sense.
          Each particle is somewhere specific in 3-space at all
          times.
        - The wavefunction, however, is also real: it’s a guiding
          field defined on configuration space that determines
          the particles’ motion.

    - Coupling to gravity:
        - Since the actual mass distribution is always definite
          (the particles’ positions), a classical gravitational
          field could, in principle, couple directly to those
          positions. There's no need for spacetime to respond to
          a probabilistic or superposed source. That’s a clear
          advantage if you want to keep gravity classical.

    - The catch:
        - The guiding wave lives in an extremely high-dimensional
          `configuration space`, not in ordinary spacetime.
        - If gravity is a field on spacetime, then how this
          nonlocal wave interacts with spacetime curvature is
          conceptually murky.
        - You could let gravity couple only to the particles’
          positions, but then the wavefunction—despite being
          physically real—would influence matter without being
          influenced by curvature, breaking the symmetry that
          usually makes physics elegant.

- Outlook:
    - Some researchers (notably Ward Struyve, Detlef Dürr, and
      others) have explored “Bohmian semiclassical gravity,”
      where the metric remains classical and evolves under the
      actual Bohmian energy–momentum of particles.
    - It’s internally consistent at a non-relativistic level, but
      it hasn’t yet been unified with GR in a satisfactory,
      covariant way.

- So: in Bohm’s view, gravity wouldn’t need to couple to
  superpositions because there aren’t any in physical space.

- But the cost is a kind of ontological duality:
    - particles live in spacetime, the wavefunction lives in
      configuration space, and how spacetime geometry talks to
      that wave remains an open riddle.

- It’s a lovely irony:
    - Bohmian mechanics removes the “superposition problem,” but
      then hands us a new one:
        - how to make spacetime geometry respond to a hidden,
          nonlocal pilot field that’s not really part of
          spacetime at all.
