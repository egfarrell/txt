## The “menu” he’s referencing

- The comment is summarizing constraints coming from things like
  Bell's theorem and experiments testing Bell inequalities:

You can’t have all of:
* Locality (no faster-than-light influence)
* Realism (properties exist independent of observation)
* Determinism
* Definiteness (single outcomes)
So interpretations “drop” one or more.

That part is broadly accurate.

## “Zero Worlds” likely means

“Zero worlds” ≈ no underlying objective reality of quantum states

This lines up closely with:

### 1. QBism (Quantum Bayesianism)

Associated with people like Christopher Fuchs.

- Core idea:
    - The wavefunction isn't real
    - It represents an agent’s personal beliefs about outcomes
    - Measurement outcomes are experiences, not revelations of pre-existing facts

- In that sense:
    - Many-worlds: “too many worlds”
    - Copenhagen: “one classical world but fuzzy rules”
    - QBism: “no objective quantum world at all” → jokingly “zero worlds”

### 2. Instrumentalism / anti-realism

More broadly:

* QM is just a tool for predicting observations
* Asking “what’s really happening underneath?” is considered meaningless or misguided

This attitude goes back (in spirit) to parts of the Copenhagen camp, especially associated with Niels Bohr, though QBism is a more modern, explicit version.

## Why call it “giving up realism”?

Because in these views:

* There is no observer-independent quantum state
* Properties don’t exist until measured (or at least aren’t meaningful to talk about)
* Reality isn't something fully specified “out there”

So compared to the list:
- Pilot wave → gives up locality
- Many-worlds → gives up single definite outcomes
- Copenhagen → gives up determinism
- “Zero worlds” → gives up realism entirely

## Important nuance

The “pick one to give up” framing is a bit oversimplified:

- Bell's theorem actually rules out local hidden-variable
  theories, not “determinism” or “realism” in all forms
- Different interpretations slice these assumptions differently
- “Definiteness” vs “realism” vs “counterfactual definiteness”
  are often conflated in casual discussions

So the comment is a useful intuition pump—but not technically precise.

## Bottom line

* “Zero worlds” isn't an official interpretation
* It’s informal shorthand for anti-realist / QBist-style views
* These interpretations:

  * Treat the wavefunction as information, not reality
  * Avoid Bell-type constraints by denying observer-independent properties
