### Bohms important contribution
    - De Broglie’s theory was revived by Bohm 25 years later
      (Bohm 1952a,b), although with the dynamics written in terms
      of a law for acceleration instead of a law for velocity.
    - Bohm’s new and important contribution was a pilot-wave
      account of the general quantum theory of `MEASUREMENT`,
      with macroscopic equipment (pointers, etc.) treated as part
      of the quantum system.
    - In effect, Bohm provided a detailed derivation of quantum
      phenomenology from de Broglie’s dynamics of 1927 (albeit
      with the dynamical equations written differently).

### Influence of deBroglie's 'phase waves'
    - ... another historical misconception: Nowadays, dBB theory
      is often presented as a ‘completion’ of quantum theory.
    - Critics sometimes view this as an arbitrary addition to or
      amendment of the quantum formalism, the trajectories being
      viewed as additional ‘baggage’ appended to an already given
      formalism.
    - Regardless of the truth of pilot-wave theory as a physical
      theory, such a view doesn't do justice to the historical
      facts.
    - Because the elements of pilot-wave theory – waves guiding
      particles via de Broglie’s velocity formula – were already
      in place in de Broglie’s thesis of `1924`, before either
      matrix or wave mechanics existed.
        - $MY_NOTES/physics/QM/Bohm/DeBroglie-1924-Tentative-Quanta.txt
    - And following the lead of de Broglie’s thesis, Schrödinger
      developed the wave equation for de Broglie matter waves.
    - While Schrödinger dropped the trajectories and considered
      only the waves, nevertheless, historically speaking the
      wave function `ψ` and the Schrödinger equation both grew
      out of de Broglie’s `phase waves`.
