### 1. What the similarity really is (and what it isn’t)

- The unifying language is:
    - base space (spacetime) `+` 
    - fiber (internal data at each point) `+`

### 2. Why this doesn’t trivially produce a single unified classical field

- There are (at least) three big extra ingredients needed beyond “both look like curvature”:

### A) Different symmetry types
    - EM (and the Standard Model) are “internal” gauge symmetries (U(1), SU(2), SU(3)) acting on internal fibers.
    - GR’s symmetry is spacetime diffeomorphism invariance (plus local Lorentz symmetry in tetrad language). That’s not just another internal group stapled on; it’s the symmetry of the base manifold itself.

You can make GR look gauge-like (spin connection, tetrads, etc.), but diffeomorphisms change what you even mean by “point,” which makes quantization conceptually different from Yang–Mills.

B) Dynamical geometry
You already locked onto the key sentence: in GR the “arena” is dynamical; in usual QFT the arena is fixed. That feedback loop is not just “complicated,” it changes what counts as an observable and what “local” even means.

C) Locality vs background independence
Standard QFT’s notion of locality is defined relative to a fixed causal structure (a metric). If the metric is fluctuating quantum-mechanically, “local operator at x” becomes tricky because x is gauge (diffeomorphism) dependent. A lot of quantum gravity is really the struggle to recover approximate local QFT on emergent classical geometry.

3. Bell nonlocality vs GR: is it the core contradiction?
   It’s a tension, but it’s easy to overstate what Bell forces you to give up.

Bell says: no theory that is simultaneously (i) reproducing QM predictions, (ii) “local” in the Bell sense, and (iii) “realist” in the relevant sense can work. But:

* Standard relativistic QFT is (micro)causal: field operators commute at spacelike separation, so no signalling faster than light.
* Yet entanglement violates Bell inequalities.

So in practice, the “nonlocality” is about correlations / the structure of quantum states, not controllable influences. That’s why QFT lives happily with SR/GR causality constraints at the operational level.

Where gravity makes it sharper is: if spacetime geometry is dynamical, and entanglement is a property of quantum states defined on (or relative to) spacelike slices, then “what is the slice?” and “what is the subsystem?” become subtle and can be observer/region dependent. So Bell isn’t usually the technical roadblock; the bigger roadblocks are: defining observables, handling diffeo invariance, and taming UV behavior.

4. What “an overarching field theory” could realistically mean
   There are a few plausible senses of “subsumes QM and GR,” and they’re worth separating:

A) A single framework, not necessarily a single field
This is already real: “gauge theory + connections + fiber bundles + EFT” is a unified framework that expresses EM/Yang–Mills and GR in closely related geometric terms. It’s not a single field, but it is a single language.

B) A UV-complete quantum theory whose low-energy limits include:

* ordinary QFT on curved spacetime (for matter)
* classical GR (for geometry)
* plus the Standard Model gauge forces

This is the usual “quantum gravity” target.

C) A genuine unification where gravity and gauge forces arise from one underlying structure
Examples of this vibe (without claiming any is “the answer”):

* Kaluza–Klein-style ideas: gauge fields as geometry of extra dimensions.
* Various “emergent gravity” ideas: gravity arises from entanglement/thermodynamics/information-theoretic structure.
* String theory’s headline claim is in this category (gravity and gauge interactions from one underlying set of degrees of freedom), though the details depend on the model.
