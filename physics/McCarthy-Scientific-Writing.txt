### Cormac McCarthy's Tips on Writing a Great Science Paper

key: clearness, simplicity

- Cormac McCarthy has edited many papers for Santa Fe Institute
  (SFI) scientists over the past 20 years. He advised theoretical
  physicist Lisa Randall and physicist Geoffrey West, author of
  *Scale*.

- Van Savage, a theoretical biologist, met McCarthy in 2000 at
  SFI. McCarthy provided Savage with valuable editing advice for
  his papers. During a 2018 sabbatical, Savage and McCarthy
  summarized McCarthy's key writing tips, later combining these
  with insights from evolutionary biologist Pamela Yeh.

- McCarthy’s main advice is to keep writing _simple_ and tell a clear, engaging _story_.

### Key Tips:

- _Minimalism_: Use minimal punctuation and words. Remove
  unnecessary elements while keeping the message intact.

- _Theme and Key Points_: Define your paper’s main theme and key
  points. Ensure all content supports these.

- _Paragraphs_: Each paragraph should convey one message,
  potentially starting with a question and exploring an idea.

- _Sentences_: Keep sentences short, clear, and direct. Minimize
  clauses and transition words.

- _Flow_: Avoid footnotes and jargon. Do not repeat words
  excessively.

- _Simplicity_: Use adjectives sparingly and avoid redundant
  phrases. Focus on clarity over anticipating readers' questions.

- _Grammar and Style_: Prioritize being understood over strict
  grammar. Use commas to indicate pauses and dashes for emphasis.
  Avoid excessive semicolons and formality.

- _Engagement_: Use questions, personal tone, and concrete
  examples to maintain interest. Avoid impersonal text.

- _Equations_: Separate equations from text and explain them
  clearly.

- _Editing_: Read your work aloud, get feedback from a trusted
  editor, and make your paper easy to review (e.g., number pages
  and double-space).

- _Submission and Revision_: Follow journal guidelines and be
  open to feedback without compromising your core message.

- _Personal Satisfaction_: Write a paper that pleases you. Aim
  for clarity and engagement to inspire readers.

- Good writing ensures people want to read your work, benefiting
  both junior scientists and famous novelists alike.
