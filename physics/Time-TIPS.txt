### emergent time and block universe
    - $MY_NOTES/physics/People/John-Wheeler.txt
    - setup: time is emergent from micro-probabilities ala
      Boltzmann and Gibbs etc.
    - questino: so if time is 'emergent', then in a sense, time
      as we experience it is not 'real'... or rather there isn't
      a 'now' or 'becoming' as such... (ie we should veer more
      towards a 'block universe' approach)
    - Gpt: You’ve landed right in the swamp where physics and
      philosophy wrestle. 
        - The point about probabilities and entropy explains
          *why* we experience an arrow of time, but it doesn’t
          immediately answer the metaphysical question: "What is
          time really?" Still, the reasoning nudges toward
          something like the **block universe** (often called the
          "eternalism" picture), where all of time exists at
          once—past, present, and future as equally real. In that
          view, the "now" and the sense of becoming are features
          of our experience, not baked into physics.
        - Here’s how the pieces line up:
            * Equations of motion (Newtonian or even quantum,
              ignoring measurement for the moment) don’t include
              a preferred "now." They just describe how states
              relate across different values of a time parameter.
              That’s blocky.
            * Statistical mechanics shows that the *direction* we
              call "forward" emerges from initial conditions (a
              low-entropy past) plus overwhelming probabilities.
              Nothing in the equations says "things *must* run
              this way." That’s emergent.
            * Our psychology experiences a "moving now," but
              physics doesn’t supply such a moving spotlight.
              That sense of flow may be more about how brains
              encode memory and anticipation than about what
              spacetime fundamentally is.
        - But there’s a catch: calling time "not real" can be
          misleading. The block universe picture doesn’t deny
          time—it denies _becoming_. 
        - Time is still a dimension in the structure of the
          universe, but the idea of "the PRESENT ADVANCING" is
          not in the mathematics.
        - The rub is not all physicists agree the block universe
          is the whole story. E.g., approaches like Wheeler’s own
          "quantum foam" ideas, or causal set theory, or some
          interpretations of quantum mechanics, suggest that
          maybe "becoming" is fundamental and the block is too
          rigid a picture. These are still open questions.
        - So the takeaway is: the arrow of time being
          probabilistic doesn’t prove the block universe, but it
          strongly suggests that the flow we feel is emergent,
          not fundamental. Physics gives us spacetime; experience
          gives us "now."

### Is past, present and future compatible with GR?
    - Daniel Sega Neuman, Daniel Galviz
        - https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.10065
    - The notions of time and causality are revisited, as well as
      the `A` and `B` theories of time, in order to determine
      which theory is most compatible with relativistic
      spacetimes.
    - Using time-orientation as one of the fundamental parameters
      in our manifold, we describe the concept of time and
      time-series (the ordering of events in time) in SR and GR
      and their intrinsic differences.
    - The notions of A-theory and B-theory will be given
      mathematical interpretations within the scheme of GR.
    - As result, in time-orientable spacetimes, the notions of
      events being in the future and past, which are notions of
      A-theory, are more fundamental than the notions of events
      being earlier than or later than than other events, which
      are notions of B-theory.
    - This supports the A-theory of time versus the B-theory.
      Furthermore, we find that B-theory notions are are
      incompatible with some structures found in globally
      hyperbolic spacetimes, namely past and future inextendible
      curves.

### 3 definitions of time: (Sean Carroll course)
    1. LABELS:
        - Time labels moments in the universe.
        - It is a _COORDINATE_, helps locate things.

    2. DURATION:
        - Time measures the duration elapsed between events.
        - Time is what clocks measure.

    3. MEDIUM/FLOW:
        - Time is a medium THROUGH WHICH WE MOVE
        - Time is the agent of change. (Becoming?)
        - We move through it or equivalently time flows past us,
          from the past, through the present, towards the future
        - (bit like time is a PROCESS of things unfolding?)
