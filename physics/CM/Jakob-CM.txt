### Measuring Potential Energy
    - NOTE: we can’t measure the 'potential' or 'potential
      energy' of an object directly. We can only measure the
      resulting FORCE 
        - `F = − ∂V(x) ∂x`. 
    - This follows because we could add an arbitrary constant
      `V(x) → ˜V(x) = V(x) + c` without changing the resulting
      force.
    - This means we can shift the potential however we want. In
      geometrical terms, we can say that the force remains
      unchanged because the slope remains unchanged by constant
      shifts of the potential.
        - $MY_IMAGES/physics/potential-not-directly-measurable.jpg
    - Only the DIFFERENCE between the `PE` of an object at one
      position and its `PE` at some other _reference position_
      are physically important. 
        - We usually shift the potential so any object at the
          equilibrium position has zero `PE` and then specify the
          `PE` at other positions relative to this position.
    - Aside: 
        - Historically, physicists used this observation to
          conclude potentials are only convenient calculation
          devices that AREN’T REAL because we can’t measure them
          directly. 
        - Working with a potential is much simpler than with
          forces because a force is described by a vector
          function `F(x)` while a potential is an ordinary
          function (scalar?) `φ(x)`. 
            - Think my OU tutor said this in 2008?
        - Moreover, the freedom to shift the potential often
          allows calculations to be simplified. 
        - BUT ...
            - from a modern perspective, potentials ARE more than
              just mathematical tools. 
            - E.g., the Aharonov-Bohm experiment showed that
              PARTICLES CAN BE AFFECTED in regions where ONLY the
              potential is non-zero and everthing else is zero. 
                - alternative wording: PARTICLES CAN BE
                  INFLUENCED in areas where a potential is
                  present, even if NO FORCE is acting on them.
            - In other words, it has been experimentally verified
              that potentials are REAL. 
            - In some sense, potentials are at the heart of
              modern physics and if you want to learn more about
              them, see: Jakob Physics from Finance
                - $MY_BOOKS/Physics/Undergrad/Jakob-Schwichtenberg-Finance-Guage-Theory.pdf

### Two types of mass (Mach)
    - You may wonder how the TWO descriptions of mass actually
      fit together: 
        - as "gravitational charge" 
        - as "resistance to being pushed around" 
    - Historically physicists used two terms 
        - "gravitational mass" and
        - "intertial mass" 
        - because they thought the different roles of `mass`
          meant there was actually two types.
    - But the idea that gravitational mass and interial mass are
      the same thing (now simply called 'mass') was a key insight
      leading Einstein to GR.
    - From this perspective, the resistance of an object to being
      moved around (inertial mass) is a result of the
      GRAVITATIONAL PULL of all the objects in the universe.
        - This is 'Machs Principle'

### Notation:
    - We often use a SINGLE `q` as a shorthand for all
      coordinates.
    - e.g. `q ≡ (q1, ..., q2) ≡ (x, y, z)`
