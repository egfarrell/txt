### The Universe is not a computer
    - Paper: $MY_BOOKS/Physics/QM/Universe-not-computer-1211.7081.pdf
    - Arxiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.7081

### Abstract
    - To predict the future, we compute it from what we know
      about the present.
        - Specifically, we take a mathematical representation of
          observed reality, plug it into dynamical equations, and
          map the time-evolved result back to real-world
          predictions.
    - But while this computational process can tell us what we
      want to know, we have taken this procedure too literally,
      implicitly assuming the universe must itself compute in the
      same manner.
    - Physical theories that do not follow this computational
      framework are deemed illogical, right from the start.
    - But this anthropocentric assumption has steered our
      physical models into an impossible corner, primarily
      because of quantum phenomena.
    - Meanwhile, we have not explored other models where the
      universe is not so limited.
    - In fact, some of these alternate models have
      a well-established importance, but are thought to be
      mathematical tricks without physical significance.
    - This essay argues that only by dropping our assumption of
      the universe as a computer can we fully develop such
      models, explain quantum phenomena, and understand the
      workings of our universe.
    - (This essay was awarded third prize in the 2012 FQXi essay
      contest; a new afterword compares and contrasts this essay
      with Robert Spekkens’ first prize entry.)

- `NSU`  = Newtonian Schema Universe
- `LSU`  = Lagrangian(?) Schema Universe
- `HUP`  = Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle
- `FPI`  = Feynmann Path Integral
- `LQFT` = Lagrangian QFT

- Curiously, this “perfect match” is only one-way.
- The Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle (`HUP`) allows more ways to
  specify the initial data than is seemingly permitted by the
  Lagrangian Schema.
- For example, the `HUP` says one can know the initial position
  or the angle of the light ray, but Fermat’s principle only
  works with constrained initial positions.
- But this is not so much a problem as a suggested research
  direction, evident only to a Lagrangian mindset.
- Perhaps the `HUP` is telling us that we’ve been too restricted
  in the way we’ve fixed the initial and final boundaries on
  classical Lagrangians.
- The natural question becomes:
    - What would happen if we required action-minimization for
      any `HUP`-compatible set of initial and final constraints?
    - For classical fields, the answer is that such constraints
      must be roughly quantized, matching equations that look
      like quantum theory.

- Furthermore, there’s no longer a compelling reason to drop to
  the FIRST-ORDER Hamiltonian equations, the standard Newtonian
  Schema version of quantum theory.

- And since it's this leap from Lagrangian to Hamiltonian that
  introduces many of the deepest problems for QG (the “problem of
  time”, etc.), there are good reasons to avoid it, if possible.
    - Flounoy says Hamiltonian not great either
        - $MY_NOTES/physics/courses/flournoy-GR-2021/01-Intro.txt

- The Standard Model is not a set of dynamic equations, but
  instead a Lagrangian density, with deep and important
  symmetries only evident in such a framework.
    - Even Newtonian Schema Universe (`NSU`) based cosmological
      mysteries, such as why causally-disconnected regions of the
      universe are so similar, no longer seem as problematic when
      viewed in an `LSU` light.

- Constructing a complete theory built on the Lagrangian Schema
  is a vast project, one that has barely begun. The necessary
  first step, though, is to recognize that the `NSU` is an
  assumption, not a statement of fact.
