### Relationist Approaches
    - Approaches to dynamics that follow Mach have as their
      motivation the general theme of `relationism`.
    - Following Descartes, Leibniz and Huyghens, and in contrast
      to the `substantivalism` of Newton, they argue we should
      formulate theories so that only relationally acceptable
      spatial and temporal concepts are invoked.
    - No toleration of a substantival space, or even weaker
      substantival concepts like Galilean spacetime, should be
      allowed.
    - Nor should we take as given, any "absolute" measure of time
      taken from outside of dynamics itself.

### Chapter 20: Relationist dynamics
    - But Leibniz is stymied by Newton’s argument to the effect
      that some kinds of motion relative to space itself, e.g.
      accelerations including rotations, DO SHOW discernable
      effects.
    - Also: only one measure of a lapse of time will correctly
      SCALE to give the SIMPLE laws of dynamics:
    - DYNAMICALLY SCALED OR EPHEMERIS TIME
        - In CM, the condition that the laws retain their simple
          Newtonian form fixes the time parameter uniquely up to
          an overall constant shift and scaling. That measure of
          time lapse is determined by the kinetic energy T and
          potential energy V via:
        - `dt ∝ √(T / (E − V)) dλ`
    - This is the `Jacobi–Barbour–Bertotti` notion of emergent
      time.
    - It says:
        - Time is not fundamental; it is constructed from change.
        - Yet only one such construction makes the equations
          reduce to their simple Newtonian form (second-order,
          force = mass × acceleration).
        - This “correct” time corresponds to what astronomers
          historically called ephemeris time: the time variable
          inferred from planetary motions so that Newton’s laws
          hold accurately.

In relational dynamics, this unique time is the one whose “scaling behavior” preserves the simple form of the laws.


        - What is this one measure? 
        - Newton's absolute time?

    - But we can satisfy some of Newton’s needs and also
      neutralise some of Leibniz’s arguments without the need for
      a sophisticated Machian approach.
        - Allow the usual relationist spatial and temporal
          relations to be supplemented with additional _monadic
          predicates_ (?) applicable to dynamical systems.
        - These can include "magnitude of absolute acceleration"
          and "magnitude of absolute angular momentum."
        - An object accelerated with respect to space itself, for
          Newton, and wrt inertial frames, for a neo-Newtonian,
          is now thought of as having just a _monadic quantity_
          of absolute acceleration rather than the magnitude of
          some relational feature wrt space or the inertial
          frames of spacetime.
        - Other than this change of thinking about absolute
          acceleration and rotation, Newton’s theory, or its
          filled-out version by Euler, is left alone.

### Time
    - From an orthodox Newtonian standpoint, one could think of
      "absolute time" differently from the way Newton did.
        - Instead of talking about "time itself" "flowing
          equably" etc, just take Newton as having shown there is
          a profoundly _preferred way_ of scaling time intervals.
        - Take any isolated system. Refer its motion to an
          inertial frame. Then there'll be a way of scaling time
          that radically simplifies its dynamical equations: 
            - the scaling that is one of the preferred Newtonian
              measures of lapse of time.
        - It is a VERY DEEP FACT ABOUT THE WORLD, then, that the
          preferred time scalings for each of the isolated
          systems are all linearly related to one another.
          Absolute time for one system is absolute time for any
          other.

### Julian
    - The Barbour–Bertotti approach is subtly different from this
      way of reading the notion of absolute time.
    - One begins by finding the preferred _time scaling_ for the
      entire cosmos, the "ephemeris time" of the Universe. Only
      then, is it shown that this time scale will provide the
      usual Newtonian preferred time scaling for describing the
      motions of isolated fragments of the cosmic system.
    - But, just as in the previous interpretation, the
      "absoluteness" of absolute time is revealed as the
      simplifying time parameter for characterizing the motions
      under the Newtonian dynamics.

    - What about absolute space, or, rather, the absolute
      inertial frames?
    - If we stayed with the version of the Barbour–Bertotti
      approach that simply notes that a Jacobi-like variational
      principle in full `configuration space` can be replaced by
      such a principle in `relative configuration space` when
      a system has no spin, we wouldn't appear to have made much
      progress toward a relationist theory.
    - Introducing the cosmological elements takes us a little
      further. Here the fact that the cosmos has no rotation (if
      that is a fact) plays a role quite similar to that played
      in Mach’s original work by the important empirical
      observation that the rotation rate of the Earth as
      dynamically measured, was the same as that kinematically
      measured relative to the “fixed stars”.
    - According to Mach, this fact suggested that the
      irrotational frame was determined by the fixed-star frame.
    - In the new version the Machian once again asks us to
      consider the non-rotation of the cosmos not as a contingent
      fact but as defining what absolute non-rotation really is.
    - The claim of Barbour and Bertotti to explain the existence
      of inertial frames _in a deeper sense_ becomes much more
      plausible when their new variational method is introduced.
        - Applying this variational principle to the cosmic
          system demands that this system have no rotation as
          a consequence of the basic dynamical law.
        - From that point on, the existence of the usual inertial
          frames relative to which the motions of isolated
          systems are described by the usual Newtonian dynamics
          is assured from the cosmic `lawlike non-rotating
          frame`, just as the Newtonian absolute time for these
          individual systems follows from the `cosmic ephemeris
          time`.
        - So here is, surely, a deeper account of absolute motion
          than the mere positing of inertial frames in the usual
          Newtonian interpretations.


    - Conclusion
    - Of course the deep epistemological problem in both stories
      is that the Universe is given to us only once (per Mach).
    - If the cosmic rotation really is NULL, then, by design, the
      traditional Newtonian account or its neo-Newtonian
      revisions will have all the same empirical consequences as
      the Barbour–Bertotti neo-Machian account.
    - This leaves us with the issue of how we could possibly make
      a rational decision to accept one account or the other.
    - Some versions of positivism might argue that both accounts
      aren't actually two DISTINCT theories at all.
        - Mach sometimes talked this way about his “fixed-stars”
          version of absolute space versus that of Newton.
        - Some possible observation could distinguish the
          theories: finding the cosmos in absolute rotation for
          example.
        - But if the cosmic matter isn’t rotating, such
          a “possible” observation remains just that.
        - And for a radical positivist like Mach, equivalent
          empirical prediction in this world as it is, is enough
          for full theoretical equivalence.

    - Otherwise we may hope that some future development of
      foundational dynamics will give a deeper insight into which
      account is correct.
        - Invoking GR won’t help, since there are standard
          versions of that theory that can be shown to have
          distinctly NON-MACHIAN aspects, but Barbour–Bertotti
          have constructed _neo-Machian versions of GR_ as well.

### Chapter 21: Modes of Explanation
    - Two types of explanation:
    - One explanation pattern in dynamics might be called
      “Newtonian.”
        - Here one must posit an appropriate structure that
          admits a preferred metric of time and the existence of
          preferred inertial reference frames to which all motion
          is to be referred. 
        - Inertial motion with constant speed and direction is
          taken as the “natural,” “unforced,” state of a body.
    - The second pattern originated with James Bernoulli’s work
      on the compound pendulum.
        - Here the resources developed in _statics_ from ancient
          Greece onward are deployed for dynamics.
        - The principle of balancing, not just forces at a point,
          but moments of forces as well is crucial in statics.
        - Also crucial is the observation that:
            - the ratios of the lever-arm of the moments
            - ARE PROPORTIONAL TO:
            - the ratios of the “virtual motions” of points when
              the system is slightly displaced from static
              equilibrium.
        - Bernoulli’s great contribution is to generalize this to
          _dynamics_ by adding the negative force of `mass
          x acceleration` to the static forces for systems in
          motion.

        - This work was generalized by d’Alembert in his
          presentation that tries to ELIMINATE THE NOTION OF
          FORCE altogether in favor of "motions" and brought to
          full generality by Lagrange.
        - It is also Lagrange who realized the full power of the
          method of "virtual work" or of "d’Alembert’s
          principle."
        - Move to a description of the system in terms of
          generalized coordinates and velocities.
        - When the constraints on a system are _holonomic_, when,
          that is, they are presented as functionals of the
          original Cartesian coordinates, a great simplification
          of the description of the system is possible.
        - Even more simplification follows when all the forces
          are CONSERVATIVE.
            - One can drop all reference to COORDINATES where no
              virtual work is done in the motion, and one can
              drop all explicit reference to FORCES altogether.
            - The result is the famous Lagrange equation framed
              in terms of the kinetic and potential energies
              alone.

- Finally there is the explanatory approach that, starting from
  the work of Hero and Fermat on geometrical optics as derivable
  from a `least-time principle`, in the hands of Maupertuis
  became an extremal, `least-action principle`, fundamental for
  dynamics.
    - Maupertuis’ work was made mathematically respectable by
      Euler and later Lagrange. Later still, one gets revised and
      independently interesting new versions at the hands of
      Hamilton and Jacobi.

- And, in all the standard textbook accounts, the forces are,
  once again, taken to be CONSERVATIVE, that is to say derivable
  as derivatives of a potential that is at most a function of the
  generalized coordinates and velocities.
