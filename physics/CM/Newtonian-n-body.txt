    - First: what is the Newtonian n-body problem?
        - It’s about predicting the motion of n particles that
          interact with one another by Newton’s laws of motion
          and gravity. 
        - The "2-body problem" (like Earth orbiting the Sun) has
          an exact solution: ellipses. Once you add a third body,
          the math explodes in complexity. For n ≥ 3, we
          generally don’t have exact solutions.

    - Now: ergodicity and recurrence.
        - Ergodic 
        - ... means that, given enough time, the system explores
          all possible states consistent with its energy. Think
          of a gas in a box: the molecules bounce around so
          chaotically that every arrangement (microstate) gets
          visited eventually. Ergodicity makes statistical
          mechanics work: time averages equal ensemble averages.
        - Poincare recurrence 
        - ... is a theorem that says: in a finite, isolated,
          energy-conserving system, the system will eventually
          return arbitrarily close to its starting state, if you
          wait long enough. Like rewinding almost exactly to the
          beginning after wandering all over.

    - So what does Barbour mean?
        - If someone wrote "the Newtonian n-body problem cannot
          be ergodic, cannot have Poincare recurrences," they
          were pointing out something subtle about gravity:
        - Gravity has long-range, unshielded forces. 
        - Unlike molecules in a box, gravitational particles can
          _escape to infinity_. 
        - There is no finite "box" of states. That breaks
          ergodicity: the system never explores all possible
          states because some particles can leave forever.
    - No recurrence. 
        - Because the phase space (the space of possible
          configurations) is infinite, the Poincare recurrence
          theorem doesn’t apply. 
        - The system doesn’t have to come back near its original
          state. A star flung out of a galaxy will never return,
          and the galaxy will never reset.
    - In simpler words:
        - The note is a reminder that a system of gravitating
          bodies is not like a gas in a box. Instead of bouncing
          forever in a confined space, gravity can fling things
          out to infinity.
        - That means you can’t rely on the usual "statistical
          averaging" tricks from thermodynamics, and you can’t
          expect the system to ever rewind close to where it
          started. 
        - It’s open-ended chaos, not cyclical chaos.
    - This fragment sits right at the crossroads of physics and
      philosophy: 
        - the universe itself is an n-body problem.
        - Unlike gases in a jar, it doesn’t have to repeat or
          explore "all possibilities." 
        - That’s why cosmology can’t just be treated as
          statistical mechanics in the usual sense.
