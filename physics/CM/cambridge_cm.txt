### Foundations of Classical Dynamics (M8)

Non-Examinable (Part III Level)

Nicholas J. Teh and J. Brian Pitts

- Why are there _TWO_ different descriptions of classical
  mechanics, each with its own state space geometry? (ie
  Hamiltonian and Lagrangian)

- How should we think about the relationship between these two
  descriptions?

- Part 1 of the course will survey the history and philosophy of
  classical mechanics. We introduce the Lagrangian & Hamiltonian
  descriptions before describing the sense in which they describe
  the _same_ theory. (under appropriate conditions)

- We will also consider recent philosophical work that examines
  the idea that Lagrangian and Hamiltonian descriptions are
  _non-isomorphic_.

In Part 2 we'll investigate striking claims such as:

1. Hamiltonian General Relativity lacks real change,

2. observables in Hamiltonian GR must be integrated over the
   universe. But how does one get from a Lagrangian to
   a Hamiltonian if the Lagrangian doesn't permit a Legendre
   transformation, as electromagnetism, Yang-Mills (weak and
   strong nuclear forces), and GR do not?

- The standard answer, Dirac-Bergmann constrained Hamiltonian
  dynamics, will be discussed with attention to Maxwell,
  a simplified form of GR discarding spatial dependence (roughly
  Bianchi I cosmologies), and at times Proca's massive
  electromagnetism (to illustrate second-class vs. first-class
  constraints).

- Emphasis will be placed on the equivalence of the Hamiltonian
  and Lagrangian formalisms, especially in relation to gauge
  transformations.

### Pre-requisites
    - Familiarity with mechanics, electromagnetism, and perhaps
      even a bit of GR might be helpful
