      electricity, is the Shaggy Steed of Physics, guiding the
      reader to an understanding of forces and mathematical
      beauty hidden in the physical world.

### Link to Hyperbolic Functions
    - The symmetries of two-body motion with inverse-square
      forces are remarkable. They are both real and imaginary `4D
      rotations`.
    - The Rotational symmetry of a sphere embedded in 3D space is
      a generalization of the rotational symmetry of the sphere
      in 2D (the circle)
    - The Rotational symmetry of a sphere in 4D space is
      a generalization of the sphere embedded in 3D space.
    - All are the same object with an invariant radius, but in
      different dimensions.
    - A sphere with a _real_ radius results in ordinary
      trigonometric functions.
    - A sphere with  _imaginary_ radius has imaginary rotation
      angles, resulting in hyperbolic functions, rather than
      trigonometric (!!)

### two bodies
    - One of Newton’s laws of motion proposed in the Principia of
      1666 was that the force experienced by one body is equal
      and opposite to that experienced by the other.
    - This is true for gravitational forces in the solar system
      and electrical forces in the atom for which motion takes
      place at a small fraction of the velocity of light.
    - But in the realm of velocities near the speed of light
      electrical and gravitational forces are no longer
      instantaneously equal and opposite.
        - Instead, they propagate from body to body at a finite
          speed, the speed of light.
        - In that revolutionary discovery, space and time as
          established by Galileo and Newton is revealed to be
          only a limiting form of Einstein’s more graceful vision
          of relativistic space and time.
    - In the regime of motion in which forces propagate from body
      to body at a finite speed, two-body interactions _CANNOT_
      exist.
    - New particles enter the picture which carry the propagating
      force. Motion near the speed of light inherently couples
      infinitely many bodies, a situation radically different
      from the motion of TWO bodies.
    - In the ordinary world of electricity and gravity, with
      velocities small compared to the velocity of light, the
      two-body problem reaches another limit at precisely two
      bodies.
        - Ordinary motion beyond two bodies — even that with just
          three bodies (e.g. two planets and the sun) is not just
          quantitatively different from two bodies; it's also
          qualitatively different.
