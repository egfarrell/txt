- The concave shape of water in a spinning bucket arises due to
  the combined effects of `gravity` and the `centrifugal force`
  acting on the water in a rotating frame of reference.

- Here's a breakdown:

    - Forces in the Rotating Frame:
        - In the bucket's rotating frame, the water experiences
          two key forces:
            - `Gravity`:
                - Acts vertically downward, pulling the water
                  towards the bottom of the bucket.
            - `Centrifugal Force`:
                - Acts radially outward from the axis of
                  rotation. This is NOT A REAL FORCE but
                  a pseudo-force experienced in the rotating
                  frame due to the water's inertia.

        - The `resultant` of these two forces determines the
          effective force field experienced by the water.

    - Equilibrium of the Water Surface:
        - The water's surface adjusts itself to be perpendicular
          to the effective force at every point. This is because
          in a fluid at equilibrium, there can be no tangential
          components of force acting on the surface.

        - The effective force is the vector sum of gravity and
          the centrifugal force. As you move outward from the
          center of rotation, the centrifugal force increases
          (proportional to r, the radial distance), while gravity
          remains constant.

    - Mathematical Formulation:
        - The total effective force has components:
            - Vertical (gravity): `F_g = mg`
            - Radial (centrifugal force): `F_c = mω^2r`,
                - where `ω` is the angular velocity
                - and   `r` is the distance from the axis.

    - Parabolic Surface:
            - The resulting height function, `h(r)`, is quadratic
              in `r`, describing a parabola.
            - This shape is concave, with the water higher at the
              edges (larger `r`) and lower at the center (`r
              = 0`).

    - Physical Interpretation:
        - The centrifugal force pushes water outward, piling it
          up at the edges. The gravitational pull keeps the
          surface curved and prevents the water from completely
          flowing out.
        - The surface curvature is the equilibrium response of
          the water to these _competing forces_, ensuring that
          the net force is perpendicular to the surface
          everywhere.

- This principle has broader applications, such as in the design
  of parabolic mirrors using rotating liquids or understanding
  the behavior of fluids in rotating systems.
