### Chapter 12: Carnot
    - Two kinds of Perpetual motion p. 207
    - What is heat?
        - Sadi Carnot asked: What is the deeper understanding
          behind the motive power of heat? i.e., what is the
          idealization in the case of heat-engines? 
        - It is here that Sadi's genius came to the fore. 
        - He understood, for the first time, the quintessential
          nature of a heat-engine: 
            - it is a machine that does work as a result of heat
              falling between _two temperatures_.

    - Direction
        - Another crucial and fundamental understanding came in
          with Carnot: 
            - heat always flows from a high temperature to
              a lower temperature. 
        - This knowledge was as old as the hills, but who had
          thought to raise awareness of it and bring it into
          physics? 
        - Even Black (Chapter 6), who understood that separate
          bodies will lose or gain heat until they achieve
          a temperature equilibrium — even he did not bring out
          the fact that heat always flows from hot to cold. 
        - With Carnot, the direction (of a process) came into
          physics for the very first time.

    - Volume
        - Heat flow can generate work by causing a change in
          _VOLUME_
        - Any heat flow that doesn't result in a volume change is
          wasted. The potential for doing work was there, but not
          exploited. 
        - Therefore (?) an ideal heat engine should have no
          direct (thermal) _contact_ between bodies at different
          temperatures.

    - Requirements
        - The final feature of Carnot’s ideal heat-engine was
          that it should be `reversible`. His syllogistic
          argument required this feature. 
            - (Find syllogistic argument)
        - Altogether, the requirements for his ideal heat-engine
          were as follows: 
            - (1) 
                - A heat-flow from a high temperature to a lower
                  temperature. 
            - (2) 
                - No _direct_ heat-flow between these
                  temperatures (i.e. no thermal contact between
                  bodies at different temperatures). 
            - (3) 
                - Only a _reversible_ heat-flow between these
                  temperatures.

    - Infinitesimal <----
        - These multiple requirements appear incompatible, as we
          know that heat only flows where there is a temperature
          gradient, and then only in one direction, from hot to
          cold. 
        - Carnot’s remedy is novel, simple, and ingenious.
          Furthermore, it will become the basis of the whole
          approach of modern thermodynamics. 
        - In a nutshell: in an ideal heat-engine, every
          temperature gradient is _infinitesimal_, and every
          heat-flow shall occur only by infinitesimally small
          steps. 
            - This guarantees that each step will be individually
              reversible. 
        - Direct contact between bodies at different temperatures
          is allowed, but only where the temperature difference
          is infinitesimal. 
            - (In fact, such temperature differences must be
              allowed; otherwise no heat will flow.) 
        - Heat will still only flow from hot to cold, but the
          temperature gradient, being infinitesimal, can always
          be reversed at will.

### Energy not invariant, but 'Action' is
    - ... the complete answer is that energy is NOT AN INVARIANT
      quantity.
    - Yes, it is conserved in an isolated system and yes, as
      potential energy, it usually does remain unchanged between
      reference frames (because potential energy most commonly
      refers to quantities defined within a system, howsoever
      that system is viewed).
    - But, most generally, energy DOES VARY between frames of
      reference, and it is the total _ACTION_ between definite
      end-states that is the true invariant quantity.

### What is heat?
    - Heat is a new ‘block’ of energy and brings a completely new
      approach into physics: 
        - we can’t answer the question of what heat is just by
          reference to the macroscopic relations of classical
          thermodynamics, even with comprehensive statements such
          as the First and Second Laws. 
        - This is because heat is _statistical energy_.

    - For a complete answer, we must delve into the (once
      speculative) microscopic arena. Then we find, as suspected
      all along by Bacon, Boyle, Hooke, Daniel Bernoulli etc.,
      that heat is the motion of the ‘small parts’; 
        - the net effect of the random individual motions of an
          extremely large number of very small particles.
    - There are a number of qualifications to make. 
        - ‘Random’ doesn’t mean lawless (the particles still obey
          Newtonian or quantum laws, as appropriate) but means
          that the motions are not coordinated in a macroscopic
          sense.
        - (So the bulk motion in wind, or in a sound wave, for
          example, doesn’t count as heat.)
    - ‘Particles’ are almost always microscopic (for example,
      atoms, molecules, ions, electrons, ‘Brownian particles’)
      but could exceptionally be as large as stars.
    - The important thing is that the ‘particles’ _interact_,
      somehow (else equilibrium could never be achieved).
    - Finally, an ‘extremely large number’ means that there are
      so many that a statistical description is absolutely
      essential.
    - A collection of, say, five molecules cannot manifest heat.
      The _statistical_ outlook was a radically novel departure
      from what had gone before.


### One Universal Temperature
    - There are many different definitions of termperature `T`
      but they are all refer to the the same parameter `T` 
        - (See Chapter 18, p. 347) 
    - Reasons why there is just one universal temperature are: 
        - `T`, by its definition, only makes sense at
          equilibrium, and presupposes the very experimental
          conditions it requires.
    - Equilibrium, or ‘thermalization’, is achieved in one
      fundamental way: 
        - by random, microscopic, energy exchanges of size `½kT`. 
    - The molecules, or other thermalized entities, go around
      _singly_ and not in gangs of say, millions or billions.
    - There is only one equilibrium parameter, `T`, we are not
      trying to equilibrate some other attribute, such as
      ‘fabulousness’, at the same time.
