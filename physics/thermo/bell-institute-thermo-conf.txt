
### D. Albert: Reduce Thermodynamics to statistical dynamics
    - the "imperialist" reading of statistical mechaincs:
        - statistical mechanics provides the broad architecture
          of the scientific picture of the world(!)
    - Albert preamble: The way I understand statistical mechanics is the
      indivdiualist Boltzmann approach... (See Shelly below)
    - Note: the Newtonian equations of motion by themselves tell
      us nothing about how the world behaves: the equations allow
      a tiny particle with sufficient momentum to jolt the Earth
      off its orbit and directly into the sun. And because of
      time reversability, the Earth could eject a particle with
      high momentum and then recoil into the sun.
        - so the equations of motion by themselves are going to
          tell you almost nothing about how the world actually
          looks and what kind of things actually happen
    - so in addition to the equations of motion, you need some
      reason why certain solutions can be ignored (because they
      are fantastically unlikely)
    - so this introduces some notion of proability, chance...

### Shelly talk: Gibbs v Boltzmann entropy
    - https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jU838SXBrv4&list=PLioi_G6H3qYJOsNHb2KCPi7-j8HYEUVQN&index=6
    - entropy: lots of different notions of the word. 
    - What is the right notion? Depends on the context...
    - Entropy has to do with counting
    - theres equilibrium entropy and non-equilibrium entropy
      (controvesial but Boltzmann thought there was)
    - gibbs: ensemble approach
    - boltzmann: individual approach (Shelley thinks is correct)
    - note: theres a microscopic (MITE) and macroscope (MATE)
      definition of thermal equilbrium (one comes from Quantum
      entanglement)
    - remember: maybe entropy isnt defined in certain situations
      (e.g. for small systems, you could say entropy is an
      _emergent_ quantity)
    - note: energy may not always be defined either (e.g. GR).
      AND... energy in CM is a different notion to energy in QM
    - thermodynamic limit: number of particles goes to infinity
    - 2 approaches to Thermal Equilibrium in statistical
      mechanics:
        - _Individualist_: a system is in thermal Equilibrium if
          it is in an appropriate [PURE STATE], given by a point
          in phase space (Shelley likes this)
        - _Ensemblest_: a system is in thermal Equilibrium if it
          is in an appropriate [STATISTICAL STATE], given by
          a probability measure in phase space (Shelley doesnt
          like)
