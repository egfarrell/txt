### Etymology of "temperature"
    - "Temperature" measures "hot and cold" and comes from the
      Latin "temperare" - to mix. 

    - It was mostly used when liquids are mixed which cannot be
      separated afterwards. The passive voice "-tur" (present
      tense, third person) indicates that some liquid is being
      mixed with another one.

### Galen:
    - Assumed climate influenced the _mix_ of bodily fluids which
      determined the character (or temperment) of a person. 
    - In the cold north, people were savage. 
    - In the warm south they were meek and flaccid. 
    - Only in the well-mixed, _temperate_ zone did people with
      superior ability live (naturally the Greeks and Romans!)

### Scales
    - Around 1700 AD, thermometer scales were just as likely to
      run upwards as downwards(!) Apparently John Patrick had
      a scale starting at 90 degrees for extreme cold and
      0 degress for extreme hot! 

    - Even Celsius originally set his scale at `0` deg for
      boiling water and `100` deg for melting ice because he
      wanted to avoid negative numbers!

### minimal temperature
    - An _objective_ aspect of temperature measurement appeared
      with the idea that there was a lowest temperature, an
      absolute minimum. 

    - At first people were unimpressed, but in the 19th century
      it was realised temperature was a measure of the _mean
      kinetic energy_ of the particles (atoms) that made up
      matter. 
    - As the kinetic energy of the particles dropped, so did the
      temperature, so when the particles were at rest then there
      was no way to have a lower temperature.

    - In 1954 Kelvins scale was subtly changed and its two fixed
      points were were replaced with a single fixed point:
      `273.16` the tipple point of water. Its pressure is 6.1
      mbar and ice, liquid water and water vapour can all
      co-exist at this point.

### Definition of temperature
    - Early researchers thought there was no need to define
      temperature. They thought it was whatever the thermometer
      said when the stuck it into well water or the armpit of
      a man etc.

    - They were unaware of the _implicit assumption_ that the
      temperature of the thermometric substance e.g. mercury or
      gas or alcohol was equal to the temperature of the measured
      object.

    - But this is in fact the defining property of temperature:
      That the temperature field is continuous at the surface of
      the thermometer. Hence temperature is measurable!

    - Axiomatists call this the zeroth-law of thermodynamics
      because by the time they recognised the need for
      a definition of temperature, the 1st and 2nd laws were
      already established.

### Energy
    - The word energy is a technical term invented by Young in
      1807 . It comes from the Greek _evepyeia_ meaning
      "efficacy" or "effective force". 
    - He used it as a convenient abbreviation for the _sum_ of
      kinetic and gravitational potential energy of a mass and
      for the elastic energy of the spring to which the mass was
      attached. 
    - That sum was conserved although the individual
      contributions might change.

### Rumford
    - While drilling cannon barrels he concluded the caloric
      theory was bunk. He thought:
        > heat is just the same as what was supplied to the metal
        > continualy as heat was appearing in it namely: motion

    - Even 50 years later Mayer expressed the 1st law as: motion
      is converted into heat. But Mayer still shied away from
      saying:
        > heat is motion
