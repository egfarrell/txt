### - Explanation 1 (Scott)
    - https://www.scottaaronson.com/democritus/lec20.html
    - Bousso formalizes this notion with a "causal diamond," but
      I'd just call it a computation with an INPUT and OUTPUT.
    - The idea is you have some starting point `P` and some
      endpoint `Q`, and you look at the intersection of the
      forward light-cone of `P` and the past light-cone of `Q`.
      That's a causal diamond.
        - $MY_IMAGES/physics/Spacetime/causal-diamond.jpg
    - The idea is that for any experiment we could actually
      perform—any computation we could do—we're going to have to
      have some starting point of the experiment, and some end
      point where we collect the data (read the output).
    - What's relevant isn't the total amount of entropy in the
      universe, but just the total amount of entropy that can be
      contained in one of these causal diamonds.

### - Explanation 2
    - $MY_IMAGES/physics/Spacetime/causal-diamond.jpg
    - A causal diamond appears when you take two events:
        - initial event `P` where the observer begins
        - a later event `Q` along that same worldline
    - Then:
        - Causal Diamond =
        - `(Q's past lightcone)` ∩ `(P's future lightcone)`
    - So the diamond is not defined at one instant.
    - It's defined over an interval of proper time along the
      observer's worldline.



### Why it matters:
    - It defines what is physically knowable to the observer.
    - No matter how enormous the universe is, the observer can
      only ever interact with the degrees of freedom inside the
      diamond. Everything outside it is causally irrelevant to
      them.

    - This makes the causal diamond the natural “arena” of
      physics for that observer.

    - In QFT on curved spacetime & in gravitational settings, one
      often asks:
        - what's the `entropy` or `# of states` accessible to
          a given observer? The causal diamond defines that
          domain.


### Bousso bound / holography
- It links causality to information and entropy.
- There is a famous result (Bousso bound, and related holographic
  entropy bounds) that suggests the maximum entropy in a causal
  diamond scales with the `area` of the diamond’s boundary, not
  its volume.
- This is part of the deep link between gravity and information
  known from black hole thermodynamics.

- So the causal diamond is a natural setting for holography:
    - the idea that physics inside the region is encoded on its
      boundary.

### In QG: fundamental observable region.
- In ordinary physics we talk about “the state of a system at an
  instant of time.”
- But in GR, simultaneity is not absolute and time slices are
  arbitrary. So instead of a “state at `t = constant`,” one uses
  causal diamonds to define a region of spacetime that doesn’t
  depend on coordinate choices.

- Many modern QG formulations (causal sets, holography, even some
  interpretations of AdS/CFT) treat the causal diamond as the
  true basic unit of meaning.


### Cosmology: Observable Universe
    - In cosmology, each observer has their own finite diamond.
    - In an accelerating universe (like ours), every observer is
      surrounded by a cosmic horizon. Past some distance, light
      will never reach us. So each observer has a finite causal
      diamond. This is why the observable universe is not the
      same as “the universe.” The diamond gives a precise
      definition of “observable.”

### Scattery theory: cause-effect experiments
    - In scattering theory or experiments, it is the region where
      cause-and-effect is meaningful.
    - When we talk about running an experiment, sending a signal,
      getting data back, etc., everything that plays a role is
      inside the causal diamond between preparation and
      detection.
    - So causal diamonds formalize the idea of a causal
      experiment.
