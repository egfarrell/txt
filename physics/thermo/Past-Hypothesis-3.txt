### Question:

- Why does relativity plus gravity mean "smooth = low entropy,"??


### Answer
    - Gravity makes _clumping_ natural, whereas ordinary matter
      tends to spread out. A smooth universe leaves enormous
      scope for gravity to create higher-entropy structures.

    - Without gravity: 
        - spreading matter out is generally entropically favoured.
    - With gravity: 
        - clumping can unlock enormous amounts of entropy, especially through black-hole formation.

- The early universe was smooth, so it had not yet exploited this
  enormous reservoir of possible gravitational entropy.

- That is why a universe that was thermally equilibrated could
  nevertheless begin in a state of extraordinarily low total
  entropy.

    - 1. Without gravity: smooth = high entropy
        - Imagine a gas in a box. At equilibrium, its particles
          spread roughly uniformly throughout the box.
        - There are vastly more microscopic arrangements
          corresponding to a uniform gas than to one in which all
          the particles happen to occupy a tiny corner.
        - So uniformity corresponds to high entropy.

    - 2. With gravity: the picture changes
        - Gravity is attractive. Small density fluctuations grow
          because slightly denser regions pull in more matter.
        - Matter can progressively form:
            - Gas clouds and stars.
            - Galaxies and galaxy clusters.
            - Black holes.
        - These structures can have enormously greater
          gravitational entropy than a smooth distribution of
          matter.
