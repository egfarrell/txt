# The Past Hypothesis: A Contradiction?

# Question

- Entropy tends to increase over time. So, if we go backwards in
  time, entropy should decrease. Extrapolating back to the Big
  Bang, the universe must have started in a low-entropy state.

- But the early universe was extremely hot and close to thermal
  equilibrium. Since thermal equilibrium normally corresponds to
  maximum entropy, doesn't this contradict the Past Hypothesis?

# Answer

No — because `thermal equilibrium` does not necessarily mean
maximum total entropy when GRAVITY matters.

### 1. The Past Hypothesis

- The Past Hypothesis states that the universe began in an
  extraordinarily _low-entropy state_. This helps explain the
  arrow of time: why entropy increases towards the future.

### 2. The apparent contradiction

- The early universe was hot, dense and nearly uniform. Its
  matter and radiation were close to thermal equilibrium, which
  normally maximises entropy.

- So how could its entropy have been low?

### 3. The role of gravity

- Without significant gravity, a uniform distribution of gas is
  indeed typically a high-entropy state.

- But gravity changes everything. Matter can clump together,
  forming stars, galaxies and eventually black holes. Black holes
  possess enormous entropy.

- Therefore, the early universe was close to thermal equilibrium
  in its matter and radiation, but had extraordinarily low
  gravitational entropy.

- Its smoothness was the key: gravitationally, it was in a highly
  special state.

### 4. The deeper mystery

This resolves the apparent contradiction, but leaves a more
fundamental question:

**Why did the universe begin in such a low-entropy state?**

Our current physical theories do not fully explain this. The Past
Hypothesis effectively assumes this special initial condition.

Whether it can be derived from a deeper theory remains an open
question.

## Conclusion

The Big Bang was hot and close to thermal equilibrium, but that
does not mean the universe had maximum total entropy.

Gravity allows much higher-entropy configurations, particularly
those involving black holes.

The real puzzle is not how entropy could increase from the early
universe, but **why the universe began in such an extraordinarily
low-entropy gravitational state.**
