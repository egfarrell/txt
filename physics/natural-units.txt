- Now we’re hitting the philosophical and practical heart of natural units. Let’s go carefully.

### 1. The “unit” in natural units is still a unit

- Even in natural units, there is a real, physical quantity being
  measured. It just happens that we’ve chosen a convention that
  makes some fundamental constants equal 1, so the same unit can
  describe what used to be different dimensions.

- In SI, length and time are different dimensions with different
  units: meters vs seconds.

- In natural units (c = 1), we measure length in the same units
  as time, because the speed of light is exactly 1 unit of length
  per unit of time.

- So the “unit” still corresponds to something physical: for
  example, the time it takes light to travel one such unit. If we
  choose seconds AS THE BASE:

- `1 natural unit of length = 1 natural unit of time = 1 second of light travel`

- 1 natural unit = `c * 1 s` = 3 x 10^8 meters

- It’s still a unit, it has physical meaning, it’s just rescaled
  and shared across dimensions.


### 2. Are natural units “dimensionless”?
    - Not really — they’re still units of measurement, but:
    - Some derived quantities become dimensionless. For example:
    - Velocity: v = dx/dt = 1 (if light)
    - Often mass and energy are measured in the same units (if
      you also set ℏ = 1, we can measure mass, energy, momentum,
      and length all in the same “unit”).

    - So you don’t lose the idea of a “unit of something” — it’s
      just that the unit can now measure multiple things at once.
      That’s why particle physicists love it: the equations get
      simpler, fewer conversion factors clutter up the formulas.

### 3. How to think about the new unit

    - Pick a base unit: e.g., 1 second of light travel.
    - Measure length, time, velocity all in terms of it.
    - If you later want SI units, you just multiply/divide by c,
      ℏ, etc.

    - In short:
        - Natural units are not dimensionless — they are
          rescaled, physically meaningful units.
        - Some ratios (like `v/c`) become dimensionless
          automatically because the constants have been absorbed
          into the units.

        - Conceptually, the “new unit” is whatever you choose as
          your base length/time, often tied to fundamental
          constants like `c`.
