### Stemplots
    ```
    s |
    t | leaves...
    e | leaves...
    m |
    ```
    - stem plots of more than 50 values are unusual
    - "leaves": numbers after the decimal point
    - "un-ordered" stemplot - if the leaves after the decimal
      point aren't in size order... eg if its: `35 | 9 8 1`
    - add a KEY: `n=sample size`, `26 | 1 represents 26.1 mpg`
    - "stretch" a stemplot: if the stem is too short and too many
      leaves. Split each stem level into 2...

### Quartiles:
    - Lower Quartile (Q1): separates off the bottom 25%
    - Upper Quartile (Q3): separates the top 25%
    - Q2 is the median!
    - Alternative measure of spread:
        - Inter-quartile _Range_ IQR: Q3 - Q1
    - the IQR is independent of E_l and E_u, extreme upper and
      lower values
    - The normal range is not resistant to the effects of
      unrepresentative extreme values
    - The IQR is highly resistant measure of spread (not
      sensitive to effects of values lying outside middle 50% of
      the batch) and is generally the preferred choice.

### Unit 04 Sampling (GATHERING DATA!)
    - Representative Sample
        - The same proportions exist in our sample as exist in
          the population

    - Simple Random sampling
        - every possible sample is equally likely to be selected
        - One method: choose sample members one at a time such
          that
            - at each selection, every member of the target
              population is equally likely to be selected
            - Selecting one member of the target population has
              no effect on any other selection, apart from not
              selecting the same individual more than once.
        - So _LABEL_ each person in the population, then use
          random numbers to select from the labels (e.g. random
          number tables)
        - if a random number comes up that isn't one of the
          labels, ignore it and chose another random number


    - Systematic Sampling:
        - Have a random START: eg row 4
        - Then have a random INTERVAL: eg every 8th row
            - (this is the sampling interval)

### Scatter plots
    - Scatter plots: looking for 4 things:
        1.  positive or negative:
            - if you have a non-linear, or u-shaped curve, then
              "positive" or "negative" not really appropriate
              then
        2.  linear or non-linear
        3.  strong or weak
        4.  any unusual points
      - scatterplot very useful as an initial exploratino to see
        if it's worth doing more processing on the data
      - are there any outliers? that can be a little subjective.
      - in statistics we're quite optimistic:
            - we tend to call a relationship "strong" even when
              theres a fair bit of scatter
      - outliers: if you've 2 "outliers" you have to be cautious.
            - ie if you've only 12 points, you'd never discard
              over 10% of the data


    - We want to find the line that best represents the
      relationahip, regression
    - when summarising scatterplots, we want the simplest
      adequate curve.
    - This often amounts to choosing a straight line fit. This is
      called the 'fitted line' or 'fit line'
    - even if you think the relationship is non-linear `y = ax^2`
      you can transform the data and proceed with a straight line
      fit anyway (do a log transform?)

    - DFR equation:
        - Check if something is a reasonable line of best fit.
        - `Data     = Fit  + Residual`
        - `Residual = Data - Fit`

        - positive residual: above the line of best fit
        - negative residual: below the line of best fit

        - if the fit is poor, there may be a PATTERN in the
          residuals. Maybe the fit could be improved by using
          a higher order curve.

        - residual plot: check no OUTLIERS on it as well!

        - residual plot scale: easier to see how much the data is above
          or below the fitted line (shows you the range/spread of
          the residuals easier)

        - residual plot: if fit line is too high or too low, this
          will be obvious. Doesn't usually happen if computer
          calculates fit line, only if you do it by hand.
            - It means the y-intercept may be set too high.

    - Least squares regression line
        - line for which the sum of the squares of the residuals
          is minimised
        - the least squares line always gives the sum of the
          residuals = 0

    - influential points: could be an outlier that affects the
      line of best fit more than other points...
        - So anything towards the extremes can have a great
          effect on the regression line...

    - Reasonableness of predctions:
        - a prediction is only valid for the range of values
          `x_i` in the original sample

    - Terminology:
        - interpolating:  predicting within the range of data
        - extrapoloating: predicting outside the range of data
