# Identification of a gravitational arrow of time
    - Many phenomena in the Universe are time asymmetric and
      define an arrow of time that points in the same direction
      everywhere at all times.

    - Attempts to explain HOW this arrow could arise from
      TIME-SYMMETRIC laws often invoke a _past hypothesis_: 
        - the initial condition of the Universe must have been
          very special.

    - This is based on thermodynamic reasoning, which seems to
      make a SPONTANEOUS emergence of an arrow of time very
      unlikely.
      
    - Although thermodynamics works very well for subsystems,
      provided gravity is not a dominant force, SELF-GRAVITATING
      systems exhibit _anti-thermodynamic_ behavior that is not
      fully understood. 
        - See also:
        - $MY_NOTES/physics/thermo/past-hypothesis.txt
        - $MY_NOTES/physics/thermo/Past-Hypothesis-2.txt

    - Since the Universe is the ultimate self-gravitating system 
      and since it cannot be treated as a subsystem, 
      its behavior may well confound thermodynamic expectations

# Barbour, End of Time
    - Barbour talk:
        - Why does Newton want absolute space and time?
        - Because he needs to formulate his 1st law, which says
          that bodies undisturbed by any force travel at:
            - a constant uniform speed
            - in a straight line.
        - You can't have either of those two things without using
          ABSOLUTE SPACE AND TIME to verify them!

    - In Newtonian theory, there's always been a problem with
      absolute space and Newton wasn't entirely frank about it. 
        - In his guts, he believed in a state of absolute rest
          and when he introduced absolute space, his words
          suggested the existence of one unique framework of
          motion. Either you moved with respect to it or you
          didn't.
        - However in the Principia, he also stated and correctly
          used the RELATIVITY PRINCIPLE, according to which the
          motions within a system are completely unaffected by
          any overall _uniform motion_ the system has.
    - This seriously undermined the idea of a UNIQUE STATE OF
      REST. No criterion could establish if one was in this
      unique state, or moving uniformly.

    - It was expected that the relativity principle would only
      hold only for the mechanical processes described by Newtons
      laws, but not for optical and EM phenomena.

    - The aether proposed by Lorentz was devoid of all physical
      properties except rigidity. 
        - It was simply there to carry the excitations of
          Maxwell's EM field and to be the framework 'relative to
          which all observable motions of the celestial bodies
          take place'.
        - Therefore it supplied a standard of rest like pond
          water does for water waves.

    - Lorentz's response to the null Michelson-Morley result was
      piecemeal.
        - He suggested that for some physical reason the length
          of a body moving could be reduced by the amount needed
          to explain the null result.
    - Poincare responded that some `general principle` should
      rule out the possibility of detecting motion relative to
      the aether. It shouldn't be neccessary to invoke ad-hoc
      hypotheses. 
    - He began to think the relativity principle might hold
      universally and not just for mechanical phenomena.
