### What's wrong with SR

        - While many readers are well-versed in Einstein’s

          significance.

    - I'll walk through the proof, explains why scientists

    - Why Is The Proof Important?

        - Einstein’s 1905 SR paper consists of three parts: 
            - pre-proof, 
            - proof, and
            - post-proof. 
        - The pre-proof contains the assumptions and derivation,
          and the post-proof further develops the work in the
          context of relativity. 
        - Many people believe that relativity exists once the
          equations are derived. This is incorrect. 
        - Einstein realizes that assumptions and equations alone
          do not make the theory. He develops a proof that begins
          by saying he must demonstrate that his assumptions and
          equations are compatible. 
        - He uses the `Spherical Wave Proof` to show this
          compatibility. Relativity exists once the proof is
          successfully completed.

        - Any challenge to the assumptions or derivation alone
          (pre-proof) can be defended by asking: 
            - If Einstein’s work contains a significant mistake,
              why does the proof establishing relativity work? 
        - Not only does a failure to answer this question serve
          as prima facie acceptance of the theory’s validity, it
          also allows for a relativistic context to be used in
          the defense. 
        - Thus, a challenger is dismissed by saying they doesn’t
          understand the theory.  Similarly, any challenge to the
          paradoxes (post-proof), or to relativity in a general
          sense (post-proof), is defended in the same way.

        - A challenge against the proof, however, cannot be
          defended in the same way. This is an interesting area
          for a challenge because the rules of inference
          eliminates and prevents the use of the thing being
          proved – relativity (and a relativistic context) – as
          a defense. 
        - Not only would such use violate the rules of inference,
          its use in defending a challenge made against the proof
          is equivalent to saying: 
            - Relativity is right because it’s relativity.

        9.  Within the proof, Einstein’s second equation

        10. Einstein uses the second equation to evaluate the

        11. When evaluated, the equality of the second equation

        12. When the transformed shape is evaluated with the

### GPT analysis
    - Yes — I can read the post, I understand the author’s move,
      and I can explain both the mistake they make and the
      historical context (who else has raised similar
      complaints). Short version up front:
    - The blog’s author correctly quotes Einstein’s Section 3 and
      the algebra he uses, but then misinterprets what it is that
      Einstein actually proved.
    - The mistake is a confusion about simultaneity: they
      transform a set of events that are simultaneous in frame
      K and then expect those transformed events to be
      simultaneous in K′. That expectation is false in special
      relativity, and it is why the naive “draw the transformed
      sphere at the same single t′” experiment gives an ellipsoid
      rather than a sphere.
    - Many fringe authors and a few historical critics (e.g.,
      Herbert Dingle and more recent online authors like Steven
      Bryant) have raised similar objections; mainstream physics
      explains the issue as the simultaneity/misinterpretation
      error and accepts Einstein’s reasoning as correct.
      stevenbbryant.com  relativitychallenge.com 
    - Below I unpack that carefully and show a numeric example so
      you can see exactly what’s going on.
    - What Einstein actually did (in plain language)
    - Einstein considers a light pulse emitted at the common
      origin at t=0 t=0. The set of events reached by that pulse
      satisfy, in frame K,
        - x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = c^2 t^2      (1)
    - (that is the equation of a spherical light front: radius
      = ct). He then applies the Lorentz transformation (his
      coordinate transformation) to those event coordinates and
      shows that the transformed coordinates satisfy
        - x'^2 + y'^2 + z'^2 = c^2 t'^2   (2)
    - which means the transformed events also lie on a spherical
      light front in `K′` (with radius `ct′` in that frame). That
      is exactly what is required to show the two postulates are
      compatible: the same physical light front is spherical in
      both frames when each frame uses its own time coordinate.
      This is the standard, correct invariance-of-the-light-cone
      result. 

    - Where the Blog Author Goes Wrong (the Core Confusion)
    - The blog suggests: take a sphere at a fixed time t in frame K (for example t = 1/c with radius 1), transform those spatial points with the Lorentz formulas, plot the transformed spatial coordinates, and you will not see a sphere.
    - That is true — but it’s not a contradiction of Einstein’s proof. Why?
    - Because the set of events "the wave front at t = constant in K" maps to events in K' that generally occur at different times t'.
    - In other words:
    - Einstein proves: the same physical events satisfy r = c*t in K and r' = c*t' in K'.
    - The blog mistakenly insists that the transformed spatial coordinates of events that were simultaneous in K must all be simultaneous in K' (that is, must share a single t' value).
    - They do not. Simultaneity is frame-dependent in relativity.
    - That is the precise reason why the naive "draw the transformed shape at a single t'" experiment produces a distorted (non-spherical) spatial locus.
    - That distortion is expected and does not invalidate Einstein’s algebra.
    - A Tiny Numeric Demonstration (c = 1 units; arithmetic shown)
    - Choose units where c = 1.
    - Let the sphere be the light front at t = 1 in frame K (so its radius = 1).
    - Take velocity v = 0.6, so the Lorentz factor is:
    - gamma = 1 / sqrt(1 - v^2)
    -        = 1 / sqrt(1 - 0.36)
    -        = 1 / 0.8
    -        = 1.25
    - Now take two points on the original sphere:

    - Point A:
    - x = 1, y = 0, t = 1
    - Lorentz transform:
        - `x' = gamma * (x - v*t) = 1.25 * (1 - 0.6) = 0.5`
        - `t' = gamma * (t - v*x) = 1.25 * (1 - 0.6) = 0.5`
    - Check:
        - `r'^2 = x'^2 + y'^2 = 0.25`
        - `c^2 * t'^2         = 0.25`
    - => Satisfies `r' = t'`.

    - Point B:
    - x = 0, y = 1, t = 1
    - `x' = gamma * (x - v*t) = 1.25 * (0 - 0.6) = -0.75`
    - `y' = 1`
    - `t' = gamma * (t - v*x) = 1.25 * (1 - 0) = 1.25`
    - Check: 
    - `r'^2 = x'^2 + y'^2 = 0.75^2 + 1^2 = 1.5625`
    - `c^2 * t'^2 = 1.25^2 = 1.5625`
    - => Also satisfies `r' = t'`.

    - The key observation:
        - Each transformed event still satisfies the light-front equation
        - `r' = c*t'`.
        - However, the two events occur at different `t'` values (0.5 vs 1.25).

    - If you plot all the transformed spatial points that were
      simultaneous in K at t = 1, they will not all share the
      same t'. That’s why their spatial distribution appears
      distorted. But if you take all the events with the same t'
      in K', those will form a perfect sphere — exactly as
      Einstein’s proof requires.
