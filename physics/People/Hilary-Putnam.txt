### Hilary Putnam (1926–2016)
    - Originally proposed that QM required a revolutionary shift
      to "quantum logic". Later, rejected this as unworkable,
      instead advocating for a scientific realism that grapples
      with the ontological, or "what there is," implications of
      QM, particularly the interpretation of the wavefunction and
      the measurement problem.

### Key Aspects of Putnam, QM, and Ontology:

    - Rejection of Quantum Logic:
        - In the 1960s-70s, Putnam and David Finkelstein argued
          that QM required a revolution in logic itself, treating
          logic as empirical (similar to geometry).
        - By the 1990s, Putnam considered this "quantum logic"
          approach unworkable and returned to classical logic.

    - Scientific Realism:
        - Putnam's central goal was a "scientific realism"
          - understanding QM as a description of reality, rather
            than just a predictive tool.

    - Ontology of the Wavefunction:
        - He questioned what the mathematical formalism of QM
          tells us about the world.
        - A major ontological puzzle is that the wave function
          doesn't "live" in 3+1 dimensional spacetime, but rather
          in a higher-dimensional `configuration space`.

    - Rejection of Certain Interpretations:
        - Putnam criticized interpretations where "collapse" is
          brought about simply by measurement, as well as the
          Many-Worlds interpretation.

    - Evolution of Thought:
        - Putnam frequently revisited and altered his views,
          moving from a radical revision of logic to a more
          nuanced, yet still realist, approach to the ontology of
          QM.

- Putnam argued that while QM is highly successful, it poses
  a profound threat to our traditional, intuitive ontology; our
  deeply held, classical view of the material world.


### Putnam: Carnap Anecdote
    - https://putnamphil.blogspot.com/2014/09/an-anecdote-of-carnaps-in-1953-54-my.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com&m=1
    - In 1953/54, my first year of teaching at Princeton, Carnap
      was on leave at the IAS, and I often went to talk to him,
      and found him not only impressive intellectually, but also
      friendly and free of any “Herr Doktor Professor-ism”. 
    - One day he told me the following. I realized later that
      Carnap may never have written it down, and by publishing it
      here I could make sure it's on the record:

        - In 1918, after a few years as a student at the
          University of Berlin, Carnap enrolled as a graduate at
          the University of Jena to obtain a PhD. in Physics. 
        - As Carnap explained it to me, his plan was to formalize
          Einstein’s theory of relativity using notations from
          _Principia Mathematica_ (although the notation that
          figures in this anecdote is older, from the “algebra of
          relations” of Peirce and Schröder). 
        - Using the notion of the square of a relation (the
          product of a relation with itself), Carnap found that
          he could write the statement that the relation `T`,
          “earlier in time than”, is transitive and dense, simply
          as `T=T2`.  
        - When Carnap went to his professor’s office to discuss
          his plan (here Carnap imitated the professor’s pompous
          voice) the professor said;
            - “It seems to me that if `T=T2`, then either `T=1`
              or `T=0`”. Carnap explained that `T` wasn't
              a symbol for a number but rather for the temporal
              precedence relation, etc., and at the end the
              professor said, 
            - “It still seems to me that if `T=T2`, then either
              `T=1` or `T=0`. Young man, you had better try the
              philosophy department.”
