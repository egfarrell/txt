- The work habits of famous theoretical physicists of the 20th
  century varied significantly based on their personalities, work
  styles, and periods in their careers. Although exact figures
  are often hard to pinpoint, there are many anecdotes, letters,
  and biographical sources that give us a rough idea of how much
  time these great minds dedicated to their work. Here's an
  overview of the estimated daily working hours for some of the
  most renowned theoretical physicists of the 20th century:

### 1. Albert Einstein
   - Estimated hours per day: 10-12 hours
   - Details: Einstein was known for his deep thinking and long hours spent in thought experiments, problem-solving, and writing. He worked intensely when he was engaged in a problem, often forgetting meals or other activities.

### 2. Richard Feynman
   - Estimated hours per day: 8-10 hours
   - Details: Feynman had a playful approach to physics but worked intensely when inspired. He often balanced his time between teaching, research, and extracurricular hobbies like playing the bongos.

### 3. Niels Bohr
   - Estimated hours per day: 10-12 hours
   - Details: Bohr was known for his intense discussions with colleagues and long hours in the laboratory and study. His dedication to physics often kept him working late into the night.

### 4. Werner Heisenberg
   - Estimated hours per day: 8-10 hours
   - Details: Heisenberg, known for his uncertainty principle, maintained a rigorous schedule but also valued outdoor activities and social interactions, which provided him a balanced routine.

### 5. Paul Dirac
   - Estimated hours per day: 10-12 hours
   - Details: Dirac, famous for his work on quantum mechanics and quantum field theory, was extremely disciplined and dedicated much of his day to research and problem-solving.

### 6. Enrico Fermi
   - Estimated hours per day: 10-12 hours
   - Details: Fermi had a hands-on approach to physics, splitting his time between theoretical work and experiments. His rigorous schedule was balanced with time mentoring students.

### 7. Erwin Schrödinger
   - Estimated hours per day: 8-10 hours
   - Details: Schrödinger often worked in spurts of intense focus, interspersed with periods of relaxation and walks, which he believed helped his thinking.

### 8. Wolfgang Pauli
   - Estimated hours per day: 8-9 hours
   - Details: Known for the "Pauli effect" in labs, Pauli was a deep thinker who engaged in long hours of work but also valued discussions with colleagues to refine his ideas.

### 9. Max Planck
   - Estimated hours per day: 8-10 hours
   - Details: Planck’s structured approach involved regular hours spent at his desk. Even though he had other commitments, his dedication to physics was unwavering.

### 10. J. Robert Oppenheimer
   - Estimated hours per day: 10-12 hours
   - Details: Oppenheimer’s intense work ethic was evident both during his academic career and later at Los Alamos during the Manhattan Project, where he worked nearly around the clock.

### 11. John von Neumann
   - Estimated hours per day: 10-14 hours
   - Details: Von Neumann’s brilliant mind and multitasking ability allowed him to work long hours, often balancing multiple projects simultaneously.

### 12. Lev Landau
   - Estimated hours per day: 8-10 hours
   - Details: Landau, known for his theoretical insights, was rigorous in his approach but also known to maintain a balance between work and leisure.

### Summary
These physicists often worked between 8 and 14 hours a day, with a general tendency toward longer hours during periods of intense discovery or problem-solving. The common thread among them was not just the quantity but the quality and intensity of their work. They often immersed themselves deeply in their research, sometimes working late into the night when inspiration struck.
