### Linear forces
    - Pöschl used Tesla’s interruption to make a more general
      point. 19th century scientists were aware that the rotary
      motion needed to turn Industrial  machines wasn't easily
      available in Nature.
        - Many forces, such as gravity, magnetism, or electric
          currents, commonly manifest themselves as _linear
          forces_, in the sense they provide pushes or pulls in
          a single direction.
        - To secure the desired rotary motion from these linear
          forces, one needed some sort of conversion device.
        - For examples of conversion devices, notice how
          a waterwheel transforms the linear flow of a river or
          how the crank and flywheel on a steam engine converts
          the back-and-forth motion of the piston into rotary
          motion.
            - $MY_IMAGES/physics/misc/steam-engine-crank.jpg
        - For Pöschl, the commutator served as a conversion
          device, converting the linear electric current into
          a series of alternating pulses that caused the rotor to
          rotate.
        - Because these conversion devices always absorbed some
          of the energy being converted from linear into rotary
          motion, Tesla’s idea of a _commutator-less motor_ must
          have seemed to Pöschl like cheating Nature, and hence
          he derisively dimissed Tesla's "perpetual motion
          scheme".
            - Aside: Tesla invented an `induction motor` (didn't
              need a commutator).
            - The `stator` produces a rotating magnetic field
              when supplied with alternating current.
                - (apparently: 3 phase motor, energised by
                  three-phase AC supply)

### Instinct
    - "Instinct," Tesla later observed, "is something which
      transcends knowledge.
    - We have, undoubtedly, certain finer fibers that enable us
      to perceive truths when logical deduction, or any other
      willful effort of the brain is futile.
