### The shared geometric skeleton

    - spacetime  : a base space

    - curvature  : measure of failure of global consistency

### EM (quantum phase)

- Fiber      : At each spacetime point you attach a complex phase `e^iθ` — a U(1) circle.
- Connection : The vector potential `Aμ`
- Curvature  : `Fμν = ∂μAν − ∂νAμ` (this encodes E and B)

- Parallel transport:
    - Moving a charged wavefunction along a path rotates its

- Holonomy:

- If F = 0,
    - holonomy vanishes → phases globally alignable.
- If F ≠ 0,
    - holonomy is nonzero → global alignment impossible.

- That is exactly the structure of parallel transport.

### GR (spacetime directions)

- Fiber: At each spacetime point you attach a tangent space (local inertial frames).
- Connection: The Levi-Civita connection Γμνρ
- Curvature: The Riemann tensor Rμνρσ

- Parallel transport:
    - Moving a vector along a path rotates its direction.

- Holonomy:
    - Go around a loop → vector fails to return to its original
      orientation.

- Flat spacetime:
    - `R = 0` → global inertial frames exist.
- Curved spacetime:
    - `R ≠ 0` → no global inertial frame.

- Same logic, different object being transported.

- What differs is what is being transported:
    - EM transports quantum phase

    - geometry becomes dynamical

So EM is like the “training wheels” version of gauge geometry.
