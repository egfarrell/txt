# key point - Eddingtons Challenge!
    - In our experience, we are aware of a fundamental feature of
      REALITY that is _missing_ From physics: "_becoming_".

    - Eddingtons challenge: our private door of consciousness
      tells us something about the universe: do we ignore it?

    - Physicists are content to infer time from empirical
      impressions which have _no_ dynamic quality.

    - But then show us your good faith by reversing the dynamic
      quality of time (which you are free to do if it has no
      actual importance in Nature) and give us a picture of the
      universe passing from a _more random_ to a _less random_
      state.

    - Tell us about waves of light hurrying from the depths of
      space to condense onto stars, how a complex solar system
      unwinds itself into the evenness of a nebula...

    - A protest should be made against the entirely _one-sided_
      version that it currently thought! 
        - (this is like Lee Smolin's argument: Time is real)

# 'Dynamical' or 'Passage' view
    - Conventional view.
    - one or more of the following is assumed objectively true
      (even if missing from current physics):
        1. a distinguished _present_ moment
        2. an objective flow of time (_becoming_)
        3. an objective _direction_ of time
    - This is a minority view of physicists.

# 'Block' view
    - Time is one dimension in a 4D block
    - rejects the 3 elements above as the 'subjective' view of
      time

# Eddington
    - (Nature of the Physical World)
    - In a 4D world, the past and future events are spread out
      before us like a _map_.
    - The events are laid out in their proper spatial and
      temporal locations but nothing indicates they undergo the
      "_formality of taking place_".
    - Something must be _added_ to the geometrical conception of
      Minkowski's world
    - For Eddington, the _2nd law_ of thermodynamics is relevant
      (related to organisation).
    - It is in connection with _organisation_ that time-flow and
      the distinction between doing and undoing appear for the
      first time.
        > Unless we have been _misreading_ the world around us,
        > by interpreting it in terms of evolution and progress,
        > instead of static extension, then we _must_ regard
        > 'becoming' as a true mental insight into the physical
        > condition which determines it.
