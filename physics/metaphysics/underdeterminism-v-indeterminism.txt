### Symmetries and underdetermination / indeterminism
    - The problem is this: certain symmetries imply redundancies,
      and this leads to a theory's inability to ‘choose’ between
      states or configurations related by the symmetry.
    - In certain cases this leads to `indeterminism`, but in
      general we have an `underdetermination` of the state by the
      theory.

### Newton v Leibniz
    - The connection of this to ontological issues are old news:
      the debate between Newton and Leibniz over the nature of
      space and time was one such underdetermination problem (the
      ‘shift argument’).
    - Two sides were carved out of this debate:
        - absolutism (or ‘`absolutist substantivalism`’), which
          accepted the existence of multiple physical states that
          the theory could not distinguish between (these states
          differing solely haecceitistically), and
        - `relationism`, which denied such a profusion.

### The Hole Argument
    - As is well known, mutatis mutandis, the hole argument, as
      revived by `Σi John i` (`i` = Stachel, Earman, and Norton)
      hangs on the same choice, though here the
      underdetermination of states by theory is tantamount to
      indeterminism; the latter being understood as an
      unpalatable consequence of substantivalism, a mere
      metaphysical position.

### First, the setup:

- A symmetry often means: many mathematically distinct
  descriptions represent the same physical situation.
- If you insist they represent different physical situations,
  then the theory can’t tell which is the real one
  → underdetermination.
- In some cases (Newton’s shift argument), this is just “too many
  possible worlds” with no empirical difference. It’s
  metaphysically messy but doesn’t break the dynamics.

Now the last line is about why, in GR, that same move becomes
worse: it becomes indeterminism.


### 1. Newton–Leibniz shift argument 

- ... gives underdetermination, not dynamical indeterminism.

- Newton’s absolutist can say:
- A universe whose entire matter distribution is shifted 5 m to the east is a different physical state, even though all observable relations are the same.

- The theory itself still gives a unique future evolution from any initial state. You just have extra haecceitistic duplicates. The redundancy is metaphysical, not dynamical. Relationists say: the duplicates are not physically distinct.

- So the underdetermination is “metaphysical clutter,” but it isn’t dynamical indeterminism: the theory doesn’t fail to predict.


### 2. In the hole argument, 
    - ... taking the manifold points as “substances” creates not
      just clutter but actual dynamical indeterminism.

    - In GR:
        - A diffeomorphism that is the identity outside some
          region (the “hole”) but non-trivial inside produces
          a new metric field `g′` that differs from `g` only
          inside the hole.
        - `g` and `g′` both satisfy Einstein’s equations and
          match on the entire past region outside the hole.

- If you treat `g` and `g′` as different physical possibilities
  (the substantivalist move), then:
    - Two physically distinct futures correspond to the same
      initial data outside the hole.
    - Einstein’s equations no longer determine a unique future.
    - That is literal indeterminism: the laws fail to fix what
      happens next.

- So here the underdetermination among diffeomorphically related
  fields is not merely “two equally good but metaphysically
  distinct worlds.” It infects the dynamics: same initial data,
  multiple possible futures.


### 3. Why this is “an unpalatable consequence of substantivalism, a mere metaphysical position.”

- The idea is:

- Substantivalism about spacetime points (the analogue of Newtonian absolutism) forces you to regard diffeomorphically related metrics as representing distinct physical states.
- Doing so turns a harmless representational redundancy into real indeterminism.
- But nothing in physics demands this metaphysical commitment. It is an extra, optional stance.
- Therefore, the “indeterminism” is not a physical discovery; it is a self-inflicted wound caused by an unnecessary metaphysical picture.

- Hence the author’s phrase: “here the underdetermination is
  tantamount to indeterminism; the latter being an unpalatable
  consequence of substantivalism.”

### - In short:

    - In the Newton/Leibniz case, substantivalism gives extra
      states but does not break determinism.
    - In the GR hole-argument case, substantivalism turns the
      gauge redundancy into actual indeterminism.
    - Therefore, the argument pressures you to adopt relationist
      or anti-haecceitist views about spacetime structure to keep
      determinism intact.
