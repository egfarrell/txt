- Undoubtedly, Einstein's reading (or misreading) of Mach played
  a central role in the development of GR.

- The status of Mach's principle within Einsteins theory is the
  subject of _much dispute_, as the straw poll results show.

- The experimenters in particular are sharply divided whether GR
  contains any Machian effects at all.

- Ciufolini argues the 'dragging of inertial frames' effect of
  a rotating body, to be searched for by the GP-B and LAGEOS III
  experiments, is a physical manifestation of the general
  relativistic formulation of Mach's principle.

- However Will attributes dragging to a _gravitomagnetism_
  completely analogous to the electrodynamic case; 
    - (See also: $MY_NOTES/physics/Gravitomagnetism.txt)
    - Other effects that Will would class as 'Machian' are
      equivalent to violations of principles that most physicists
      hold dear:
        - the equivalence principle
        - local Lorentz invariance
        - local position invariance

- Since all tests for a violation of these principles have only
  returned NULL results, Will is moved to wonder,
    > 'whether Mach's principle has any meaningful content at
    > all'.

- Nortvedt, comments that _the near perfection of local
  laboratory physical law in the presence of the perceived
  imperfections in cosmological structure_ is a key mystery or
  clue.

- It either casts serious doubt on the Machian program or, more
  likely, confirms the modern 'field paradigm' of physics:
    - matter acts to influence other matter at a distance ONLY
      through the intermediation of fields

- and the cosmological field(s) must be dominated by the
  `gravitational metric` field, with participation by any other
  _cosmological fields_ being strongly suppressed, if not totally
  absent.
