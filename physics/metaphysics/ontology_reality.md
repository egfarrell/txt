- Should we agree with James Ladyman? 
    - Just think of reality in terms of different LEVELS of
      ontological objects without saying that any level is more
      fundamental than others, or is there a fundamental level?

- E.g., isn’t there a problem around _causality_, which seems to
  suppose a level of reality that presupposes a `SEPARATION`
  between space and time, and a fundamental physics which has
  space/time together

- I don’t think there’s a “problem” around causality, though
  there are many interesting “issues” that are worth figuring
  out.
    - My own ontological views are even more _eliminatavist_ than
      Alex Rosenberg’s – I don’t even believe in spacetime!
        - (Here and elsewhere “believe in” should be interpreted
          as “currently think this will be the best available
          explanation, subject to revision as more data and
          better ideas arrive.”)
    - Our best theories of reality are based on QM, not
      relativity, and I think we'll eventually understand
      spacetime as an emergent semi-classical property, not at
      all fundamental.
    - Time is crucial in QM, but space is only an approximate
      notion.

- I’m also an Everettian when it comes to QM, which means I think
  the underlying reality is described by perfectly reversible and
  deterministic differential equations.
    - So questions about “causality” in metaphysics should be
      related to fundamental physics by exploring how intuitive
      notions of cause-and-effect can arise in a deterministic,
      reversible theory (subject to a `past hypothesis`
      responsible for the arrow of time).
