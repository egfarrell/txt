### Difference between GR 'geometry' & metric
    - Monster was fooled into thinking he was doing some
      NON-TRIVIAL (significant) operation by the mathematical
      machinery we use in GR!
    - But according to the `Leibniz equivalence` option, the
      mathematics of such transformations are only
      REPRESENTATIONS that do not map onto the world: 
        - the world is best represented by the intrinsic
          structure that is invariant under such transformations:
            - this _equivalence class_ of diffeomorphic metrics
              is called the GEOMETRY by physicists, by contrast
              with the METRIC.

### 'Quotienting out' or 'modding' symmetry
    - There's a formal method to remove unobservable structure if
      we decide it is not to our taste.
    - We 'quotenient out' or 'mod out' the symmetry.
    - We form an _equivalence class_ of all the symmetric
      possibilities. 
        - (All possibilities directly related by some symmetry
          operation)
    - The `equivalence class` of possibilities has many elements,
      and each element would be 'up to the task' of representing
      the physical situation. 
        - Therefore there are many mathematical descriptions of
          the same situation.
    - The symmetry transformations (shifts, boosts etc) would
      take us from one possible description to another possible
      description. 
        - That is, from one element of the equivalence class to
          another element.
    - Quotienting: We then 'mod' or 'quotient' the equivalence
      class, reducing the multiplicity of indistinguishable
      possibilities to a _single possibility_. 
        - We have reduced the size of physically possible worlds.
    - Newton v Leibnitz centred on just this: 
        - how big is the space of possibilities?
    - Key:
        - Should we include INDISTINGUISHABLE possibilities or
          eliminate them?

### Unobservable structure
    - The debate is that for each set of parameters there's some
      piece of physical structure rendered `unobservable`.
        - In the case of the translation element of the Galilean
          group, (the bit that shifts objects in space), one
          cannot observe absolute locations (ie positions
          relative to space itself).
    - Invariance under 'time shifts' renders absolute time
      location also unobservable 
        - (you can't tell _when_ you are relative to time
          itself).
    - Invariance under rotations renders absolute orientation
      unobservable (you can't tell which way you are pointing
      relative to space itself).
    - And, finally, invariance under velocity changes renders
      absolute velocity (or absolute rest) unobservable (you
      can't tell how fast you are moving relative to space
      itself).
    - That's a lot of unobservable structure.
    - In general the more symmetries one has, the more
      unobservable structure one introduces, since such
      symmetries destroy our ability to discern differences
      between certain physical situations, so we can't tell which
      of some set of possibilities we are in fact observing
      (assuming there is indeed a fact of the matter).

### What is physically distinct?
    - Newton's laws of motion are 
        - independent of location
        - independent of time
        - independent of orientation
        - independent of state of uniform motion 
                        - (constant velocity)
    - Hence, they are Galilean invariant: 
        - they take the same form in all reference frames related
          by Galilean transformations.
    - This implies we can't detect `position` and `velocity` by
      any process governed by Newton's laws:
        - a kind of 'conspiracy of nature' hides any such
          transformations from our gaze.
    - This 'conspiracy' lies at the heart of a debate between
      Newton and Leibnitz. 
        - What happens when you do a Galilean shift on the entire
          contents of the universe?
        - A Galilean shift seems to result in something that
          DOESN'T DIFFER physically.
        - But if we think of space and time as containers, then
          they must be _physically distinct_ none-the-less.


### Possible worlds
    - if `x(t)` is a solution to some theory equations 
        - (ie, some trajectory of a system such as a particle) 
        - then so is the alteration `ψ(x(t))`, 
            - where `ψ` ∈ Galilean Group ("Gal").
            - Note: `ψ(x(t))` and `x(t)` represent PHYSICALLY
              DISTINCT SOLUTIONS.
            - They are different as physical states: 
                - different positions, velocities, etc. and yet
                  they are also the same in terms of their
                  intrinsic properties.
    - In terms of the "possible world" talk from earlier, we can
      say Galilean transformations leave a theory's space of
      _physically possible worlds_ the same, so that applying one
      of these transformations to a state (i.e. of a physically
      possible world) leads to another physically possible world
        - (see 3.2 for more on the connection between laws and
          possible worlds).


### Galilean Transformations (a group)
    - This family of transformation-types gives us _10
      parameters_ describing ways a system can be altered while
      leaving the LAWS undisturbed.
        - (it's Nature's symmetry group in a CM world)
    - 10 params:
        - `3` parameters to describe spatial _translations_
        - `1` parameter for _temporal_ translation
        - `3` axes of space: 
                - _reorientations_ about each axis
        - `3` velocity directions: 
                - _velocity_ 'boosts' in each direction
    - These are the 'Galilean transformations' and they satisfy
      the mathematical properties of being a _group_; 
        - basically a set of elements that must satisfy certain
          conditions about how the elements combine. (in this
          case: a set of transformations)
    - for an initial state `(x, t)` we consider transformations
      of coordinates of the form:
        - (x, t) -> (`A`x+`vt`+`b`,  t+`s`)
            - `A`:
                - rotate system by rotation matrix `A`
                - (an element of the rotation group SO(3))

            - `s`:
                - shift forward in time

            - `b`:
                - translate the point `x` by the point `b`
                - (where `b` is a vector in Space ℝ^3)

            - `vt`:
                - change the velocity by a velocity "boost"
                - `v` is a velocity vector in space

### Galileo: Ship
    - This passage destroys the intuitive reasoning for believing
      we live on a stationary Earth: 
        - why don't we feel the wind on our faces and why aren't
          we thrown off?
    - By linking the Earth whizzing through space with a ship
      cutting through the water, Galileo shows we wouldn't notice
      any of the effects we might naively expect ...
        - (Never knew Galileo's ship was really promoting the
          Copernican view about the Earth etc)
    - Also this passage contains a _symmetry principle_:
        - that unifrom motion is undetectable...
        - the LAWS of motion are insensitive to transformations
          that involve switching a pair of uniformly moving
          reference frames.
    - Hence, we have a _change without change_: 
        - an equivalence of motions of objects below the deck, 
        - coupled with a known PHYSICAL DIFFERENCE: 
            - the different states of motion of the ship.

### Symmetries
    - Key modern meaning: SYMMETRY = TRANSFORMATION that leaves
      some property invariant.
    - Originally 'symmetry' meant harmonious, balanced or
      well-proportioned. (Greek word meant: 'same measure')
        - But this definition is in a static sense: a figure or
          building can be symmetric without us doing anything.
    - But in modern terms, a 'symmetry' means observing something
      `before` and `after` some action has been performed on it.
    - It's kind of a 'change without a change':
        - operations or _transformations_ that leave something
          (or everything) about an object the same as before.
        - It indicates a kind of 'insensitivity' of some
          properties or relations to certain transformations.
            - e.g. transformations that rotate, shuffle, move,
              twist, push or modify a system in some way.
        - In such cases, the TRANSFORMATIONS for which this holds
          true are called 'symmetries' and the object is said to
          be invariant (or symmetric) under the transformation.

### Plenum
    - Aristotle’s account is more detailed. He has a ‘plenistic’
      view: space (the universe) is viewed as always full (no
      genuinely empty spaces):
        - if some portion of space is not occupied by water or
          matter it is occupied by air, each displacing one or
          the other as they are moved.
    - In 'Timaeus', Plato also defends a view the world is
      a plenum: matter and space are, in fact, identified.
    - Aristotle, however, introduces 'place' as an entity
      independent from matter, because although matter is
      essential to a body, place is not:
        - different objects can occupy the same space (Marc Lange
          says no), and the same object can occupy different
          places.
