
haecceity
“heck-SEE-uh-tee”

### haecceitist
    - Comes from medieval Scholastic Latin.
    - Root is `haecceitas`, coined by philosopher Duns Scotus
      (late 13th–early 14th century).
    - It comes from the Latin demonstrative `haec`, meaning
      “this”.
    - So haecceitas literally means “`this-ness`” — the property
      of being this very individual, beyond any qualitative
      features.

### Basics
    - A haecceitist difference is a difference in IDENTITY, not in
      PROPERTIES.
    - Two situations differ haecceitistically when all qualitative
      facts are the same, but a different individual occupies the
      role.
    - Basically Leibnitz's 'Identity of indiscernibles' is
      anti-haecceitist.

- More fancy:
    - A haecceitist difference is a difference of "`thisness`"
      rather than of any qualitative features.
    - Two situations differ haecceitistically when the only
      change is which individual is “where” or “which,” with all
      relational and qualitative facts held fixed.

- Examples
    - A haecceitist treats the differences below as genuine
      differences in reality.
    - An anti-haecceitist says that if all qualitative and
      relational facts are identical, the situations are the
      same; “thisness without qualitative difference” isn’t real.

    - Example 1
        - Two universes are perfect mirror images except that
          an electron in one is swapped with an
          indistinguishable electron in the other.
          Everything qualitative and relational is unchanged;
          only “which electron is which” differs.
            - This is a haecceitist difference.

    - Example 2
        - A Newtonian universe is shifted 5 m to the east.
          All relative distances and motions stay the same,
          but matter occupies different absolute positions.
          - If you treat absolute positions as physically
            real, the two universes differ only
            non-qualitatively — a haecceitist difference.

- In the context of symmetry and gauge:
    - Treating symmetry-related configurations as physically
      distinct generally commits you to haecceitistic
      differences.
    - Treating symmetry-related configurations as the same
      avoids haecceitistic surplus structure and prevents
      unnecessary underdetermination.

### Link to Leibnitz

- The "identity of indiscernibles" says:
    - If two things share all the same qualitative properties and
      relations, then they are not two things but one and the
      same.

- That is exactly the anti-haecceitist position: no differences
  without qualitative differences.

- So:
    - Leibniz’s identity of indiscernibles = a rejection of
      haecceitistic differences.
    - Leibniz is the classic anti-haecceitist: no “thisness”
      beyond qualitative structure.

- Haecceitists deny the identity of indiscernibles. They think
  two qualitatively identical items (or universes) can still
  differ by pure identity — “this one vs that one” — even with no
  qualitative distinction.

- So the whole haecceity vs anti-haecceity debate is basically
  the modern, sharpened metaphysics around exactly that old
  Leibnizian principle.
