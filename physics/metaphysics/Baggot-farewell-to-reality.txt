### Plato's cave
    - Plato’s allegory says that while our reality is derived
      from `things-in-themselves` (i.e. the real people that walk
      in front of the fire), we can only perceive
      `things-as-they-appear`: the 'shadows' they cast on the
      cave wall.
    - We never perceive reality for what it is; we can only ever
      perceive the shadows.
    - 'Esse est percipi', declared 18th century philosopher
      Berkeley: essence is perception, or to 'be' is to be
      'perceived'.
    - These arguments appear to link our ability to discover our
      external reality with the workings of the human mind.
    - A _disconnect_ arises because of the apparent unbridgeable
      distance between the physical world of things and the ways
      our perception of it shapes our mental world of thoughts,
      images and ideas.
    - This disconnect may arise because we lack a rigorous
      understanding of how the mind works. But knowing how the
      mind works wouldn't change the simple fact that _thoughts
      are different from things_.

### Veiled reality

### Position / Momentum problem
    - Quantum particles are also waves. Suppose we could localize
      a quantum wave particle in a specific region of space so
      that we could measure its position with arbitrary
      precision.
        - In the wave description, this is possible (in
          principle) by combining a large number of wave forms of
          different frequencies into a superposition called
          a 'wavepacket'. They all add up to produce a resultant
          wave which is large in one location in space and small
          everywhere else. Great. This gives us the position.
    - But what about the momentum? That's a bit of a problem. We
      localized the wave by combining lots of waves with
      different frequencies.
    - This means that we have a spread of `frequencies` in the
      superposition and hence a spread of `wavelengths`.
        - According to French physicist Louis de Broglie, the
          `wavelength` of a quantum wave is inversely
          proportional to the quantum particle's `momentum`.
        - Therefore, the spread of wavelengths means there's
          a spread of momenta.
    - We can measure the position of a quantum wave particle with
      arbitrary precision, but only at the cost of uncertainty in
      the particle's momentum. The converse is also true.

- The 'clumsiness argument' implied that quantum wave particles
  actually possess precise properties of position and momentum,
  and we could in principle measure these if we could just devise
  experiments of greater subtlety.
    - Bohr was adamant that these properties simply do not exist
      in our empirical reality. This is a reality that consists
      of `things-as-they-are-measured`; the wave 'shadows' or the
      particle 'shadows', as appropriate.
    - Bohr insisted on this fundamental _duality_, this
      complementarity of wave and particle behaviour that
      underlies the uncertainty principle, much as the
      explanation above suggests.
    - It's not possible for us to conceive experiments of greater
      subtlety, because such experiments are inconceivable.


### Perfect Vacuum / Fluctuations
    - Suppose we create a perfect vacuum, completely insulated
      from the external world. We could argue there is 'nothing'
      at all in this vacuum.
    - But what does this imply?
    - It implies that the energy of an EM field in the vacuum is
      zero. It also implies the rate of change of the amplitude
      of this field is zero too.
    - But the uncertainty principle denies that we can precisely
      know the energy of an EM field and its rate of change. They
      can't both be _exactly zero_.
    - What happens is the vacuum suffers a bad case of the
      jitters. It experiences fluctuations of the EM field which
      average to zero in terms of both energy and rate of change,
      but which are nevertheless non-zero at individual points in
      space and time.

### Casimir Force
    - Fluctuations in a quantum field are equivalent to quantum
      particles. The vacuum fluctuations of the EM field can be
      thought of as _virtual photons_;
        - 'virtual' because they are not directly perceived, NOT
          because they are 'unreal'. 
    - In an experiment devised by Casimir, the space between the
      plates constrains the types of vacuum fluctuations that can
      persist. Only fluctuations that 'fit' this space can
      contribute to the vacuum energy.
    - Alternatively, we can imagine that the narrow space between
      the plates reduces the number of virtual photons that can
      persist there. The density of virtual photons between the
      plates is then lower than the density of virtual photons
      elsewhere.
    - The net result is the plates experience a kind of virtual
      'radiation pressure'; the higher density of virtual photons
      on the OUTSIDE of the plates pushes them closer together.
    - The Italian physicists' painstaking measurements showed the
      magnitude of the Casimir force between the plates was
      precisely what Casimir himself had predicted.

### Emmy Noether
    - ... those are examples of `discrete` symmetry
      transformations. They involve an instantaneous 'flipping'
      from one perspective to another, such as left-to-right.
    - But the kinds of symmetry transformations identified with
      conservation laws in Noether's theorem are different.
    - They involve _gradual changes_, such as continuous rotation
      in a circle.
        - Rotate a perfect circle through a small angle measured
          from its centre and the circle obviously appears
          unchanged.
        - We conclude that the circle is symmetric to continuous
          rotational transformations.
        - We find we can't do the same with a square. A square is
          not symmetric in this same sense. It is, instead,
          symmetric to discrete rotations through right angles.
    - Noether discovered that the dynamics of physical systems
      (the disposition of energy and momentum during a physical
      change) are governed by certain continuous symmetries.
      These symmetries are reflected in the equations — and hence
      the laws — describing the dynamics.
        - The operation of these various symmetries means that
          the dynamical quantities they govern are also conserved
          quantities.
        - Thus, each conservation law (of energy and momentum) is
          connected with a continuous symmetry transformation.
    - Once the connection had been established, the logic of
      Noether's theorem could be _turned on its head_.
        - Suppose there is a physical quantity which appears to
          be conserved but for which the laws governing its
          behaviour have yet to be worked out.
        - If the physical quantity is indeed conserved, then the
          laws (whatever they are) must be invariant to
          a specific continuous symmetry transformation. If we
          can discover what this symmetry is, then we are well on
          the way to figuring out the mathematical form of the
          laws themselves.
        - Physicists found that they could use Noether's theorem
          to help find a short cut to a theory, thus avoiding
          a lot of hit-and-miss speculation

### Wavefunction Collapse
    - The only connection between the initial wavefunction and
      the measurement outcomes is that the modulus squares of the
      amplitudes of the various components of the wavefunction
      can be used to determine the probabilities for measurement
      outcomes.
    - The measurement itself is completely indeterministic. This
      is the 'collapse of the wavefunction'.

    - Von Neumann was also clear that this collapse or process of
      'projecting' the wavefunction into its final measurement
      state is not inherent in the equations of QM. It has to be
      postulated (ie has to be assumed).
    - By the way, there is no experimental or observational
      evidence for this collapse per se. We only know that we
      start with a wavefunction which can be expressed as
      a superposition of different possible outcomes and we end
      up with only _one outcome_.

    - In general, there are 3 ways to get around this assumption.
        - We can try to eliminate it altogether by supplementing
          QM with an elaborate scheme based on hidden variables.
            - As the experiments in Chapter 2 amply demonstrate,
              this scheme has to be very elaborate indeed.
            - We know that hidden variables which reintroduce
              _local reality_ (e.g. variables which establish
              localized properties and behaviours in an entangled
              quantum system) are pretty convincingly ruled out
              by experiments that test Bell's inequality.
        - We also know that experiments designed to test
          Leggett's inequality tell us that 'crypto' non-local
          hidden variable theories in which we abandon the set-up
          assumption won't work either.
        - This leaves no choice but to embrace a full-blown
          non-local hidden variables theory. Pilot Waves etc...!


### Decoherence
    - Dieter Zeh noted that the interaction of a quantum
      wavefunction with a classical measuring apparatus and its
      environment will lead to rapid, irreversible decoupling or
      'dephasing' of the components in a _superposition_, such
      that any interference terms are destroyed.
    - Each state will now produce macroscopically correlated
      states: different images on the retina, different events in
      the brain, and different reactions of the observer. The
      different components represent two completely decoupled
      worlds.
        - This decoupling exactly describes the ['collapse of the
          wavefunction'].
        - As the 'other' component cannot be observed any more,
          it serves only to save the consistency of quantum
          theory.
    - But why would this happen?
    - In the process of amplification, the various components of
      the wavefunction become strongly coupled to the innumerable
      quantum states of the measuring apparatus and its
      environment.
    - This coupling selects components that we eventually
      recognize as measurement outcomes, and suppresses the
      interference. 
    - The process is referred to as `decoherence`.
    - We can think of decoherence as acting like a `quantum
      friction`, but on a much faster timescale than classical
      friction.
    - It recognizes that a wavefunction consisting of
      a superposition of different components is EXTREMELY
      FRAGILE.
        - Interactions with just a few photons or atoms can
          quickly result in a loss of phase coherence that we
          identify as a 'collapse'.
        - This is fast but not instantaneous.

### Stripping away Reality

      not-so-gradual undermining of our naïve ideas about
      reality.
    - It details the erosion of common-sense, taken-for-granted
      notions and their replacement by uncertainty and doubt.
        - Can we expect this erosion of confidence to continue
          indefinitely? What if we stripped away everything we
          call 'properties', the things that define what it means
          for a quantum particle to be in a specific quantum
          state, the things that determine how the particle is
          going to behave... what would be left?
    - There are at least two possible answers:
        1. By stripping away all the empirical dressing, energy,
        mass, spin, space, time, etc, we are left with _abstract
        mathematics_.
        2. Or we could consider the irreducible 'stuff' of the
        universe to be _information_. (bits)

### Option 1: Tegmark: ERH / MUH
    - First, some groundwork. The hypothesis is premised on what
      Tegmark calls the External Reality Hypothesis (ERH).
        - There exists an external physical reality that is
          completely independent of human beings. This is hardly
          radical.
    - The ERH defines what it means to be a realist, though as
      I have explained, there can be no observational or
      experimental verification for such a hypothesis.
        - The assumption of an independent reality is really an
          act of faith, or if that's too theological, an act of
          metaphysics.
    - What is radical is what Tegmark concludes from the ERH:
        - 'The Mathematical Universe Hypothesis (MUH):
        - Our external physical reality is a mathematical
          structure.'

    - Tegmark 'Radical Platonism'
        - This is not an entirely new vision. Plato argued that
          the independent reality of `things-in-themselves`
          consists of perfect, unchanging 'forms'.
        - The 'forms' are abstract concepts or ideas such as
          'goodness' and 'beauty', accessible only through our
          powers of reason.
        - Our empirical reality is then an imperfect or corrupt
          projection of these forms into our world of perception
          and experience.
        - If we think of the 'forms' as abstract mathematical
          structures, then Tegmark's MUH is a kind of radical
          Platonism.
            - As Tegmark explained:
            - Galileo, Wigner and other scientists would argue
              that abstract mathematics 'describes' reality.
            - Plato would say that mathematics exists somewhere
              out there AS an ideal reality.
            - I'm working in between. I have a crazy idea that
              mathematics is so effective at describing reality
              because it is reality.
            - That is the mathematical universe hypothesis:
              Mathematical things actually exist, and they are
              actually physical reality


### Option 2: Universe is 'information'
    - The second possibility is the basic stuff of the universe
      is information. How does this work?
        - This logic stems from observing that the physical world
          appears to _consist of opposites_.
        - We have positive and negative, spin-up and spin-down,
          vertical and horizontal, left and right, particle and
          anti-particle, etc.
        - If we strip away the empirical dressing, such as
          charge, spin, etc., we are left with 'oppositeness'.
        - An elementary `on` and `off`.
        - Or, alternatively, an elementary `0` and `1`.
        - In one of the simplest mathematical STRUCTURES we can
          devise (or discover), the abstract entities are 'bits'
          which have only two possible values, `0` or `1`

### Information and Entropy
    - Although the universe could be considered one vast quantum
      computer, we don't get any closer to the idea that
      information might be the ultimate reality.
    - Indeed, we might dismiss this idea for the same reason we
      might dismiss the MUH.
    - We could argue that 'information', like mathematics, is an
      abstraction based on the fundamental properties of the
      _empirical entities_ of the material universe.
    - Quantum particles have properties that we interpret in
      terms of information. But we might conclude that quantum
      information can't exist without quantum properties.
    - Which came first?
        - The PROPERTIES or the INFORMATION?
        - The chicken or the egg?
    - But there is one relationship that might cause us to pause
      before concluding that only physical properties can shape
      physical reality.
    - It lends some credibility to the idea that information
      itself might be considered a _physical thing_.
    - This is the relationship between information and entropy.

    - Jim Baggot 'heats' a Shakespeare soliloquy
        - Suppose we can 'heat' this soliloquy.
        - At first, the passage melts and the words lose their
          places in the structure:  'And syllable but shadow
          a frets sound all …'
        - Eventually, the words come apart and transform into
          a soup of individual letters — 's', 't', 'A', 'n', 'e'…
        - But the letters of the English alphabet can be coded as
          a series of bit strings.
        - With further heating, the bit strings come apart to
          produce a random 'steam' of bits-'0', '0', '1', '0',
          '1' …
        - All the resonance in the soliloquy, all its
          information, hasn't exactly been lost in this process.
            - After all, we still have all the bits.
        - But our ability to recover the information has become
          extremely difficult. Our ignorance has increased.
        - It would take an enormous amount of effort to
          reconstruct the soliloquy from the now scrambled bits,
          just as it'd take much work to reconstruct the cocktail
          glass from the shards picked up from the floor.
        - If the information isn't lost, then it has certainly
          become almost _irretrievably 'hidden'_ (or our
          ignorance of the soliloquy has become very stubborn).
        - This suggests there is a deep _relationship between
          information and entropy_.

### Shannon Entropy
    - What does this mean? Suppose we devise a simple code to
      communicate a particular situation, e.g.
        - code `1`: 'my plane took off on time'
        - code `0`: 'my plane did not take off on time'
    - Knowing airline punctuality, you expect to receive the
      message `0` with 85% probability.
        - If you receive the message `0`, then Shannon's formula
          implies its information content is very low.
    - But if you receive `1`, the information content is high.
    - In this context, information measures the _unexpectedness_
      of the message, or the extent you're surprised by it.
    - So, the entropy of Macbeth's soliloquy is low, and its
      information content is high.
    - The particular sequence of words is of low probability and
      therefore unexpected or 'surprising' (as well as moving).
    - Heat the soliloquy into a steam of bits.
        - The entropy increases and the information content is,
          if not lost, then hidden very, very deeply.

### Information is Physical 1
    - In 1961, this kind of logic led IBM physicist Rolf Landauer
      to declare 'information is physical'.
    - He was interested in processing information in a computer.
    - He concluded that when information is erased during
      a computation, it is actually dumped into the surrounding
      environment, adding to the entropy.
    - This increase in entropy results in an increase in
      temperature: the environment around the processor heats up.
    - Anyone running a complex computation on their laptop
      notices that it gets hot after a while.
    - Landauer's statement requires careful interpretation, but
      it's enough for now to note the direct _connection between
      the information processing and physical quantities such as
      entropy and temperature_.
    - It seems 'information' is not an abstract concept invented
      by the human mind. It is a real, physical thing with real,
      physical consequences.

### Information is Physical 2
    - Take the physical system away, and there can be no
      information. But...
    - The metaphysical interpretation suggests that information
      exists independently of the physical system, that it is
      a primary quality, the ultimate manifestation of an
      independent reality.
    - 'Information is physical' acknowledges that in our
      empirical reality of observation and measurement,
      information becomes dressed in a clothing of physical
      properties.
    - This is like suggesting that heat or temperature are the
      ultimately reality, existing independently but projected
      into our empirical world of experience in terms of the
      motions of physical objects.
    - I have no real issue with this either, so long as we don't
      pretend it is science.


### The Veracity Principle.
    - It's not possible to verify a scientific theory such that
      it provides absolute certainty forever.
    - A theory is instead accepted (or tolerated), on the basis
      of its ability to survive tests and meet other criteria
      such as simplicity, efficacy, utility, explanatory power
      and less rational measures such as 'beauty'.
    - Over time the theory becomes familiar and is accepted as
      'true' or, at least, as possessing a high truth-likeness or
      verisimilitude.
    - The scientists' confidence or degree of belief in the
      theory grows.
    - It is eventually absorbed into the common body of knowledge
      which forms the current 'authorized' version of empirical
      reality.


### Coherence Truth vs Correspondence Truth
    - I've another motive for including the 'Black Hole wars'.
    - This is an area of physics which we struggle to apply
      either `Testability` or `Veracity` Principles.
    - There's no observational or experimental data to compare to
      theory, so this branch of physics seems speculative or
      metaphysical in nature.
    - The notion that the universe is a hologram projected from
      information encoded on its boundary belongs to 'fairy-tale
      physics'.
    - But there's a sense that some scientific progress has been
      made too. Debates have raged over real issues of principle,
      and the debates have been resolved, apparently by arriving
      at the 'right' answer.
    - It does seem that a singular `truth` has been established
      and this isn't something that can be dismissed.
    - How come? If none of this theory is testable, and can't be
      compared to experimental facts, how has resolving the Black
      Hole information paradox helped establish the 'truth'?
    - I think the answer is we have TWO kinds of truth:
        - `CORRESPONDENCE TRUTH`
            - The scientific method is premised on
              a correspondence theory of truth. 
            - This is the 'Veracity Principle'. 
            - A scientific statement is true iff it corresponds
              to facts that we can establish about empirical
              reality. This implies that there can be no
              scientific truth without reference to facts about
              the external world.

        - `COHERENCE TRUTH`
            - We can establish the internal _logical consistency_
              of a collection of sentences or propositions, or
              mathematical structures, independently of the
              facts.
            - In this coherence theory of truth, we try to
              establish right and wrong in relation to the
              theoretical system itself, irrespective of testing
              the theory by empirical facts, and so irrespective
              of whether the theory itself is scientifically
              right or wrong.


### Holographic 'Truth' Verdict
    - The truth is the holographic principle was 'proved' not by
      reference to observed behaviours of bits of quantum
      information or observed physical properties of black holes,
      but by reference to a superstring duality;
        - in other words, by reference to another theoretical
          structure which itself makes no testable ...
    - The truth established in the black hole war is
      a '`coherence` truth', not a '`correspondence` truth', and
      there's a big difference.

### posterior probability
    - IF we have two distinct theories with equal prior
      probabilities, and evidence increases the `posterior
      probability` of one above the other, then we clearly have
      a means to choose between them.
    - We keep the theory with the higher posterior probability
      and reject the other (at least, until we get more
      evidence).
