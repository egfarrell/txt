### Also, some classic unpublished texts:

    - David Malament, Notes on Geometry & Spacetime

    - Rob Clifton, Introductory Notes on QM
        - These are designed to bring the beginning student of QM
          philosophy 'up to scratch' on the maths needed to
          understand elementary finite-dimensional quantum
          theory. There are just three chapters:
            - Ch. 1 Vector Spaces
            - Ch. 2 Inner Product Spaces
            - Ch. 3 Operators on Finite-Dimensional Complex Inner
              Product Spaces
        - The notes are self-contained and presuppose knowledge
          of only high school level algebra.

### Comments
    - I’d suggest maybe Peter Smith’s “Explaining Chaos” if only
      because the above list seems short on Chaos Theory.

    - And while it’s not explicitly philosophy of physics,
      Gillies’ Philosophical Theories of Probability is something
      everyone should be acquainted with.

    - I’d suggest “Every Thing Must Go: Metaphysics Naturalized”
      if you’re interested in the more wide-ranging implications.
      The authors argue (similar to Maudlin in “Metaphysics in
      Physics”) that contemporary metaphysics is too-often
      informed by pseudo-naturalism.
    - Metaphysics is too often conducted, by people who claim to
      be doing it in the name of real science, but who have only
      superficial understandings of scientific concepts.
    - If we want to make serious progress in metaphysics, they
      contend, we need to drop the Humean supervenience-motivated
      appeals to atomic theory and Newtonian mechanics and engage
      with cutting-edge physics on its own terms.
