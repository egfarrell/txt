- George Berkeley's key claim is that physical objects `don't
  exist independently of perception`. 

- When he says esse est percipi ("to be is to be perceived"), he
  means that for something to exist, it must be perceived by
  a mind.

- So, for Berkeley:
    - A tree, for instance, is not some mind-independent object
      out there.
    - Instead, it exists only as a collection of ideas in
      someone's mind.
    - If no human is perceiving the tree—say, it's in a forest
      with no one around—it still exists because God is always
      perceiving everything.

- In this view, material substance (a world existing
  independently of minds) doesn’t exist. What we call the
  "physical world" is really just a coherent set of ideas
  presented to us by God.

- This is why Berkeley is a metaphysical idealist: he claims that
  reality is fundamentally mental or spiritual, not physical.
