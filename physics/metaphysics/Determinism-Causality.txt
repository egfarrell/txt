
### Difference: Causality/Causation and Determinism
    - Causality is often conflated with determinism.
    - Or some consider determinism a _strong form_ of causality.
        - (Cushing calls determinism: "event-by-event causality")
        - $MY_NOTES/physics/metaphysics/Cushing-philosophical-concepts.txt

    - Often the term "Causal Determinism" is used. I maintain
      causality and determinism aren't the same.

    - DETERMINISM
        - given a specified `state` of the world at time `t`, and
          given the laws of nature are fixed, the course of
          future events is _fixed_ as a matter of natural law.

        - ie. Theres only _one possible_ future which is
          determined by the current state of the world and the
          laws of nature.

        - Any earlier complete state of the world entails any
          later state of the world in all its details.

        - The present can also be used to specify past
          conditions. Determinism is _bidirectional_ in time.

    - CAUSALITY
        - the notion that event `E` follows preceding conditions
          `C` with regularity.

        - Whether event `E` _invariably_ results or follows
          condition `C` is another matter.
            - E.g. in QM we know that _apparently_ identical
              conditions `C` may give rise to a variety of
              different events along a stochastic probability
              distribution.

        - The observed events are thus _caused_ by conditions `C`
          but not _determined_ by them.

        - We also know that chaotic and unpredictable behavior
          occurs in complex systems (even those following
          deterministic laws).

        - Causality is not generally considered to be
          _bidirectional_ in time as determinism is.

    - key point
        - The _rejection_ of determinism does not entail
          rejection of causality. (!)

    - summary
        - Determinism: states
        - Causality:   EVENTS

### gpt: Determinism vs. Causality:

1. Determinism is the idea that the state of a system at one time
   uniquely determines its state at all future times.
    - In a deterministic system, given the complete information
      about the _present state_ and the _laws of physics_, the
      future evolution of the system is entirely predictable.
      e.g., classical mechanics (like Newton's laws) is often
      deterministic because, with precise initial conditions, the
      future states can be fully determined.

2. Causality, on the other hand, is the principle that every
   effect has a cause. 
    - It's the idea that events occur as a result of other
      events. Causality is broader and more fundamental than
      determinism because it doesn't necessarily imply the future
      is predictable; it simply implies there is a causal
      relationship between events.

      challenge to determinism is supposed to emerge; the
      challenge is not simply that quantum mechanics is prima
      facie non-deterministic



### Relationship between Indeterminism and Causality:

- An indeterministic system is one in which the `future` is NOT
  entirely determined by the present state. 
    - QM is indeterministic because the outcomes of certain
      measurements (e.g. particle position or momentum) can't be
      precisely predicted; only their probabilities can be.

- However, indeterminism DOESNT necessarily mean that causality
  is absent. Even in indeterministic systems, causality can still
  be present. 
- In QM, although individual events may be probabilistic, they
  still follow causal laws. E.g.
    - In QM, measurement outcomes are not determined in advance,
      but the causal structure is still respected.
    - If an event (like a particle being in a certain state)
      depends on another event (like a measurement made by an
      observer), the cause-effect relationship is still present,
      even if the outcome of the event is probabilistic.
    - (See HN comment below!)

- Thus, indeterminism doesn't imply a lack of causality; it
  merely means that the outcomes are not predetermined.
    - Causality can still operate within an indeterministic
      framework, dictating that EFFECTS FOLLOW CAUSES, even if
      those EFFECTS are not uniquely determined.
