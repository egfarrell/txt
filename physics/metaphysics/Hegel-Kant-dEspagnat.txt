### Answer:
- What your note is picking up on is a three-way contrast between:
    1.  Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel
    2.  Immanuel Kant
    3.  Bernard d'Espagnat

- All agree that what we experience is not reality “as it is in
  itself” in any simple sense — but they diverge sharply on what
  that implies.

### 1) Kant & d’Espagnat: 

- There is a hidden “something” beyond experience Kant (Noumenon)

- Kant’s key move is the distinction between:
    - `Phenomena` → the world as structured by our cognition (space, time, causality)
    - `Noumenon` (“thing-in-itself”) → what exists independently of us, but is unknowable

- So for Immanuel Kant:
    - Reality exists independently
    - But we can never know it directly
    - Science only describes appearances

- d’Espagnat (Veiled Reality)
    - Bernard d'Espagnat updates this with QM:
        - Physics gives correlations between observations, not access to reality itself
        - Quantum phenomena (nonlocality, measurement problem) undermine naive realism
        - So he posits “Veiled Reality”:
            - A mind-independent “something”
            - Necessary to explain why experiments resist us (“something says no”)
            - But structurally inaccessible

- So he’s Kant-like, but motivated by physics rather than pure
  philosophy.

### 2) Hegelian Idealism: 

- No unknowable “beyond” at all. This is where things sharply
  diverge. Hegel’s move: He rejects Kant’s noumenon entirely.

- For Hegel:
    - The idea of an unknowable thing-in-itself is incoherent
    - If something is in principle unknowable, it’s meaningless to assert it
    - Reality is not “hidden behind appearances”

- Instead:
    - Reality = intelligible, rational, and fully knowable (in principle)
    - Reality is a self-developing rational structure (“Absolute Spirit”)
    - What appears (history, nature, thought) is reality unfolding itself
    - There is no gap between:
        - appearance and reality
        - thought and being

- So:
    - Kant: “There is something real beyond appearances, but we can’t know it.”
    - Hegel: “No—what appears is reality coming to know itself.”

### 3) Why d’Espagnat explicitly avoids Hegel

Your note is exactly right here.

- d’Espagnat insists on:
    - There must be a “Something” that resists us. This is crucial.

- Why this matters

If you go Hegelian:

    Reality is fully rational and internally generated

    No need for an independent constraint

    The world becomes (roughly speaking) a self-consistent conceptual system

But d’Espagnat says:

    Experiments fail

    Nature pushes back

    Therefore there must be:

        something independent of our theories

        something not reducible to conceptual structure

👉 That “pushback” is exactly what justifies Veiled Reality
4) The core difference in one sentence

    Kant / d’Espagnat:
    → There is a mind-independent reality we cannot know directly

    Hegel:
    → There is no unknowable beyond; reality is fully expressible through thought

5) Why Hegel looks especially incompatible with quantum philosophy

From a modern physics perspective:

    Hegel implies:

        Reality is ultimately rationally transparent

    But quantum mechanics suggests:

        Limits to representation, locality, and determinacy

So d’Espagnat’s stance is:

    Keep realism (there is something)

    Reject knowability (we can’t access it directly)

    Avoid collapsing into:

        naïve realism (classical physics)

        or idealism (Hegel)

6) A simple analogy

Think of it like this:

    Kant / d’Espagnat
    → You see shadows on a wall.
    → There is a real object casting them, but you’ll never see it directly.

    Hegel
    → The “shadows” are the full reality—properly understood.
    → There is no hidden object behind them.

This is a really sharp question—and you’ve put your finger on the exact place where Hegel is easiest to misunderstand.

The short answer is:
Hegel is not saying “reality is just the physical world as we perceive it.”
He’s saying something much stranger:

    Reality is fundamentally intelligible structure—and what we call “matter” is just one moment in that structure.

1) Why Hegel is still an “idealist”

Your definition of idealism (“reality is fundamentally mental/spiritual”) is basically right, but it needs refinement for Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel.

For Hegel:

    “Ideal” ≠ “in your head”

    “Ideal” = conceptual / rational / intelligible

So his claim is:

👉 What is most real is not matter, but the rational structure that makes anything intelligible at all

He famously says (paraphrasing his core idea):

    The real is rational, and the rational is real.

2) Why he is NOT a materialist

A materialist says:

    Matter is fundamental

    Mind/thought comes out of matter

Hegel flips this:

    Thought (or better: rational structure) is fundamental

    “Matter” is something that only makes sense within that structure

So:

    Materialism: matter → mind

    Hegel: intelligibility → matter

3) The key misunderstanding: “appearance = reality”

When I said Hegel collapses the gap between appearance and reality, that does not mean:

    “What you see with your senses is all there is.”

That would be crude empiricism or physicalism.

Instead, for Hegel:

    What appears (nature, society, history, science, etc.)

    Is reality expressing itself in progressively richer, more self-aware forms

So the “shadow” analogy needs correcting:

    ❌ Not: “the shadow is just a flat surface and that’s all”

    ✅ Rather: “what you first see is only a partial expression of a deeper rational whole, which unfolds through it”

4) What “Spirit” (Geist) really means

This is the core of his idealism.

For Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel:

    Reality is ultimately Geist (Spirit)

But “Spirit” ≠ ghost or mind floating around.

It means:

    Self-developing rationality

    The process by which reality becomes:

        structured → living → conscious → self-conscious → philosophical

So:

    Physics = Spirit at a very abstract level

    Life = Spirit becoming organized

    Human thought = Spirit becoming aware

    Philosophy = Spirit understanding itself

5) Why he rejects Kant’s “hidden reality”

This is the key contrast with Immanuel Kant.

Kant says:

    There is a thing-in-itself beyond thought

Hegel replies:

    That idea is empty

    If you say something exists but is in principle unknowable, you’ve said nothing meaningful about it

So instead:

👉 Reality must be knowable in principle, because reality is intelligibility
6) So why this is still “idealism”

Now we can answer your confusion directly.

You said:

    “Doesn’t this just mean physical reality is all there is?”

Hegel’s answer:

No—because “physical reality” is not self-explanatory.

    Matter by itself is:

        inert

        meaningless

        unintelligible

What makes it a world rather than chaos is:

    laws

    structure

    relations

    concepts

And those are not “material things”—they are rational forms

👉 So for Hegel:

    The true substance of reality is not stuff, but structure

    And structure is what he calls “ideal”

7) A better way to picture the difference

Think of three positions:
Materialism

    Reality = stuff

    Structure is secondary

Kant / d’Espagnat

    Reality = unknown stuff behind appearances

    Structure belongs to our cognition

Hegel

    Reality = self-developing structure

    “Stuff” is just one phase of that structure

8) A modern analogy (optional but helpful)

If you think in terms of physics:

    A materialist says: reality is particles

    A Hegelian view is closer to:
    → reality is laws, relations, and mathematical structure, with particles as expressions

(Not identical to modern physics—but directionally similar.)
9) One-line summary

    Materialist: reality is made of things

    Kant / d’Espagnat: reality is something hidden behind what we know

    Hegel: reality is the process of intelligibility itself unfolding

Great—this is where things get really interesting, because some interpretations of quantum mechanics really do start to look more Hegelian than Kantian/d’Espagnat-like.

Let’s build the bridge carefully.
1) What would a “Hegelian” physics even look like?

From Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel, we’d expect:

    No unknowable reality behind phenomena

    No sharp split between:

        observer vs world

        appearance vs reality

    Reality as a self-consistent, relational whole

    Structure (relations, laws) more fundamental than “things”

👉 So a “Hegelian-friendly” QM interpretation would:

    Treat the wavefunction or structure as the reality itself

    Not posit a hidden, inaccessible “thing-in-itself”

    Emphasize relations, coherence, totality

2) Contrast baseline: d’Espagnat / Kant in QM

Recall Bernard d'Espagnat:

    QM only gives predictive structure

    There must be a Veiled Reality behind it

    Measurement outcomes imply something independent resisting us

👉 This is very anti-Hegelian:

    Keeps a beyond

    Accepts limits in principle

3) Where QM starts to look “Hegelian”
(A) The wavefunction as reality (no hidden layer)

Some interpretations treat the wavefunction as the full story:
Example: Everett / Many-Worlds

    Associated with Hugh Everett III

Key idea:

    The wavefunction never collapses

    It evolves deterministically

    All outcomes exist in a branching structure

👉 Why this is Hegelian-ish:

    No hidden “real state” behind the math

    The mathematical structure is reality

    Apparent randomness = partial perspective within the whole

This matches Hegel’s idea that:

    what seems fragmented or probabilistic is just a limited viewpoint on a fully coherent totality

(B) Relational quantum mechanics

Associated with Carlo Rovelli

Key idea:

    Properties don’t exist absolutely

    They exist only relative to other systems

👉 Why this is Hegelian-ish:

    Reality = network of relations, not independent substances

    No “thing-in-itself” with intrinsic properties

    Being = being-related

That’s extremely close to Hegel’s idea that:

    entities are what they are only through their relations within the whole

(C) Quantum holism & entanglement

Quantum entanglement shows:

    The whole system has properties that cannot be reduced to parts

    Correlations are fundamental (see Bell's Theorem)

👉 Hegelian resonance:

    Reality is fundamentally holistic

    The whole is prior to the parts

    Separation is secondary / derivative

This fits Hegel’s:

    “The true is the whole.”

(D) Structural realism

Some philosophers of physics argue:

    Physics tells us only about structure, not objects

👉 This is almost tailor-made for Hegel:

    Structure = what is real

    Objects = nodes in that structure

    No need for a hidden substrate

This avoids d’Espagnat’s “veiled something” entirely.
4) The big philosophical fork

Now we can frame the deep divide:
d’Espagnat / Kant line

    QM ⇒ limits of knowledge

    Therefore ⇒ there must be something beyond

    Reality = hidden ground

Hegelian-leaning line

    QM ⇒ structure without classical objects

    Therefore ⇒ structure is reality

    No need for a hidden layer

5) Measurement problem: two readings

This is the key battleground.
d’Espagnat-style reading

    Collapse / outcomes show:
    → something beyond theory determines results

    ⇒ Veiled Reality

Hegelian-style reading

    No collapse (Everett), or no absolute state (Rovelli)

    ⇒ No mystery requiring a “beyond”

    ⇒ The theory’s structure is complete

6) Where Hegel might still clash with QM

Important: the fit isn’t perfect.

Hegel expects:

    Full rational transparency

    A kind of ultimate intelligibility

But QM still has:

    Probabilities (Born rule)

    Measurement contextuality

    Interpretational fragmentation

So a strict Hegelian might say:

    QM is not the final form of knowledge yet—it’s an incomplete stage in Spirit’s self-understanding.

7) One clean comparison
View	What is ultimately real?	Hidden reality?
Kant / d’Espagnat	Unknown “something”	✅ Yes
Many-Worlds	Wavefunction	❌ No
Relational QM	Relations	❌ No
Hegel (idealized)	Rational totality	❌ No
8) Intuitive takeaway

    d’Espagnat:
    “QM works, but reality hides behind it.”

    Hegelian-style QM:
    “QM is reality—just not in the classical way we expected.”
