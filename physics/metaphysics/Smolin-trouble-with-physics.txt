### Beauty Myth

- In the history of physics there have been many proposals for
  unified theories that failed. A famous one was that LIGHT and
  SOUND were essentially the same thing: both vibrations in
  matter. 
    - Since sound is vibrations in air, light was proposed to be
      vibrations in a new kind of matter called aether. Just as
      the space around us is filled with air, the universe is
      filled with aether. Einstein killed this particular idea.
      (maybe?)

- Criteria for successful unifications:  
    - surprise, 
    - dramatic new insights and 
    - new predictions confirmed by experiment.

- In the 16th century there were different proposals for
  unification. One cluster of ideas was the older theories of
  Ptolemy and Aristotle whereby the planets were unified with the
  sun and moon as part of the celestial spheres and the earth was
  the centre of the universe. 
    - However Copernicus and Bruno proposed to unify the planets
      with the earth and the stars with our sun. Each side could
      be supported by observation and sometimes even the same
      experiment could be interpreted as evidence for either
      side. (e.g. a ball dropped from the top of a tower)

- In Ptolemy's and Aristotle's theories there was a big
  distinction between MOTION and REST and also between physics in
  the heavens and physics on earth. 
    - Planets change direction between they're attached to
      celestial circles whose nature is to rotate eternally. 
        - This never happens on earth: anything we push or throw
          quickly comes to rest. 
    - Ptolemy also postulated that certain heavenly bodies move
      in epicycles and this allowed predictions accurate to
      1 part in 1000. 
    - Aristotle explained why the earth was the centre of the
      universe: it was composed of earth-stuff whose nature was
      not to move in circles but to seek the centre. This
      world-view powerfully explained what we saw around us.

- Copernicus's proposal that the planets be considered one with
  the earth and Bruno's that the stars be unified with our sun
  must have been unsettling. 
    - If the earth is a planet then it and everything on it must
      be in continuous motion. But this violates Aristotle's law
      that everything not on a celestial circle must come to
      rest. 
    - And if the sun is a star then they must be very far away
      because no parallax is detectable. These proposals violate
      our experience.

- So how come we don't feel the earth moving? The answer was the
  GREATEST UNIFICATION IN ALL SCIENCE: 
    - the unification of _uniform motion_ and rest, also called
      the principle of inertia: 
        - a body at rest or in uniform motion stays in that state
          unless disturbed by forces.

- Being "at rest" merely becomes a special case of uniform
  motion: motion at zero speed (relative to whoever is measuring
  that speed). 
    - The key is to realise that motion has no absolute meaning.
      It is defined only with a respect to an observer, who can
      be moving or not. 
    - But can't observers tell whether they are moving or not? 
    - To Aristotle the answer was obviously yes but Galileo and
      Newton were forced to reply no. If the earth is moving and
      we cannot feel it, that must mean observers moving at
      _constant speed_ don't feel any effects of their motion.

- Hence we cannot tell if we are moving and motion must be
  defined as a purely RELATIVE quantity. Note we are just talking
  about uniform motion here. 
    - When we change speed or direction we do feel it. 
    - Such changes we call acceleration, and acceleration can
      have an absolute meaning.

- Galileo and Newton achieved a beautiful and subtle triumph. To
  others it was obvious motion and rest were completely different
  phenomena, easily distinguished. But the principle of `inertia`
  unifies them. 
    - To explain how they seem different, Galileo invented the
      principle of relativity. This tells us the distinction
      between moving and being at rest is meaningful only
      relative to an observer. 
    - Since different observers move differently, they
      distinguish differently which objects are moving or at
      rest. So the fact that each observer makes a distinction
      between movement and rest is maintained as it must be.

- Thus whether something is moving or not ceases to be
  a phenomenon to be explained. For Aristotle, if anything moved,
  there must be a force acting on it. 
    - For Newton, if the motion is uniform, it will persist
      forever (unless changed by a force); 
        - no force is needed to explain it.

- Kepler's lifelong obsession was the planets. He believed the
  Earth was a planet so he knew of six – Mercury out to Saturn.
  Each planet had an orbital speed and an orbital radius. 
    - The speeds were not uniform; the planets speed up and slow
      down as the move around the sun. 
    - All these numbers seem arbitrary. 
    - Kepler sought his whole life for a principle to unify the
      motions of the planets and explain the data of their
      orbits.

- His first try at unification was based on the ancient tradition
  that cosmological theory only employ the simplest figures. 
    - Kepler searched for beautiful geometrical figures to
      explain the orbit sizes. His unified theory used the five
      platonic solids which explained the orbit diameters in
      a mathematically beautiful way. But it didn't lead
      anywhere. It unified but was too static. 
    - Kepler thought about how to explain the speeds of the
      different planets. Finally he proposed that planets “sing”
      as they travel and the frequencies of the notes are
      proportional to their speed. 
        - The pitches sung by the planets make a harmony of six
          voices he called the "harmony of the spheres".
          Unfortunately there was no unique harmony for six
          voices
    - Also Galileo discovered four moons orbiting Jupiter.
      Kepler's theory should apply to Jupiter's system as well
      but it didn't.

- After years of analysing the data Kepler found three laws, one
  of which was the relationship between the speeds and diameters
  of the orbits. 
    - His three laws also agreed with the observations of
      Jupiter's moons. 
    - Copernicus had said the sun was at (or near) the centre of
      the universe but in his theory the planets would move the
      same way whether the sun was there or not. Its only role
      was to light the scene. 
    - This lead Kepler to ask if the sun's being near the centre
      of each planetary orbit was a coincidence. 
        - Could the sun play some _ROLE_ in causing the orbits,
          exerting a force that might explain their motion? 
    - Kepler had to find a role for the precise position of the
      sun in each orbit. His breakthrough identified the orbits
      as ellipses with the sun at one focus. This is his first
      law. 
    - His second states that the speed of a planet in its orbit
      increased approaching the sun and decreased as it moved
      further away. 
    - His third governed how the speeds of the planets were
      related. These laws point to some DEEP FACT unifying the
      solar system, because they apply to all planets.
    - Math version of his laws:
        - $MY_NOTES/astro/Keplers-Laws.txt

- Newton achieved several unifications at once: 
    - Earth with the planets
    - the Sun with stars
    - Rest and uniform motion, 
    - Gravity on earth with Kepler's sun “force” . 

- Singly none of these ideas would have survived.


### Fields

- Faraday invented the concept of a field to explain how a force
  could be conveyed through empty space from one body to another.
  A field is a quantity, like a number, which lives at each point
  in space. 
    - As you move through space the value of the field changes
      continuously. The value of the field at a single point also
      evolves in time. 
    - EM gives laws about how the field changes as you move in
      space and through time. The laws show the value of the
      field at a particular point is influenced by the value of
      the field at nearby points. 
    - The field at a point can also be influenced by a material
      body at the same point. Thus a field can carry force from
      one body to another – there's no need for ghostly
      action-at-a-distance.

- The electric field isn't a number but a `vector` – imagine an
  arrow at each point in space which can vary its direction and
  length. 
    - Imagine the ends of arrows at nearby points are attached to
      one another by rubber bands. If I pull one, it pulls the
      ones nearby. Electric charges influence the arrows so that
      they point to nearby negative charges and away from nearby
      positive charges.  Faraday also invented the magnetic field
      where the arrows like to point to the poles of magnets.

- Mixed electric and magnetic effects were discovered – e.g.
  a charge moving in a circle generates a magnetic field. 
    - To fully unify the two fields Maxwell had to change the
      equations, simply by adding one term, which then allowed
      electric and magnetic fields to TURN INTO EACH OTHER. 
    - These transmutations give rise to waves of shifting
      patterns moving through space, in which first there is an
      electric field AND THEN a magnetic field. 
        - Such moving patterns can be made by WAVING an electric
          charge back and forth. The ensuing waves carry energy
          form one place to another.

- But even as Maxwell's unification triumphed there was
  a formidable obstacle. Most physicists were “mechanists” and
  believed physics was unified because everything was made of
  matter (in order to satisfy Newton's Laws). 
    - Maxwell's theory made no sense without some stuff bending
      and stretching. The vacuum of space presented a problem
      because light travelled from the sun but there was no
      intervening matter. 
    - Therefore a new form of matter called the aether was
      invented. It was a paradoxical material – extremely dense
      and stiff to allow light to travel through it at high speed
      (essentially as a _sound wave_) but also it had to offer no
      resistance to planets moving through it.
 

### trouble with physics - seers

- Ted Jacobson. 
    - University of Maryland. Special Relativity wrong?
    - _Preferred state of rest_.

- Joao Magueijo 
    - Questions whole framework of relativity. 
    - Speed of light much faster in early universe.

- Robert Laughlin (Nobel Quantum fluid) 
    - Grigori Volovik (Landow Institute, cold liquid helium)
    - Xiao-Gang Wen, James Bjorken (protons, neutrons contain
      quarks) 
    - These men started with the idea that the relativity
      principle is false. Its an approximate emergent phenomenon.

- Holger Bech Nielsen (Bohr Institute)
    - Inventor of string theory. Isolated. Advocates random
      dynamics. Fundamental laws are random, accidental facts
      emergent from an unknowable fundamental theory. 
    - His models are the laws of thermodynamics, which used to be
      based on principles but now understood as the most likely
      way large numbers of atoms in random motion will behave.

- Gerard d'Hooft 
    - QM is wrong. Won Nobel for proving Yang-Mills theories are
      sensible. 
    - Main idea: holographic principle. 
    - There is no space(!). Everything that happens takes place
      on a surface that surrounds what we call space. 
    - The world description of this boundary surface is not QM
      but a deterministic theory that d'Hooft believes will
      replace it.

- Louis Crane (Relational Quantum Theories)
    - Based on the idea that QM is a description of relationships
      between sub-systems of the universe. Further developed by
      Rovelli to show its perfectly consistent with Quantum
      Theory. 
    - Resulted in a new approach to quantum cosmology, by
      Markopoulou. She emphasized that exchanging information
      between sub-systems is the same as the causal structure
      that limits which systems can influence each other. 
    - She thus found that the universe can be described as
      a quantum computer with dynamically generated logic.

- Penrose 
    - Like d'Hooft, he thinks QM is wrong. Argues that the
      incorporation of gravity into quantum theory makes that
      theory non-linear. This resolves the measurement problem
      — quantum gravitational effects cause the quantum state to
      collapse dynamically.

- Barbour 
    - New kind of theory — space and time are nothing but
      a system of relationships. Re-interpreted GR as
      a relational theory — how its usually understood now.

- David Finkelstein. Georgia IT. 
    - Questing for the logic in Nature. 
    - Spin-offs: first person to understand black hole horizon.
      Discovered topological conservation laws. First to study
      quantum groups.

- Anthony Valentini. 
    - Studied under Sciama who founded SISSA — Italian
      Astrophysics group. Made new and important modification of
      hidden-variables QM.
