- ERIC WEINSTEIN: In some ways we’ve been making amazing progress
  for 40 years e.g. the _MATHEMATICS OF FIELD THEORY_, which is
  the underlying geometric structure for both particle theory and
  GR.

- It's been very exciting because this dictionary was opened up
  and it imports all the best insights from physics into
  differential geometry, and from differential geometry back into
  physics.

- So you can't say nothing is happening. The problem is we really
  wanted to _quantize_ the geometry of GR but we've actually
  ended up _geometrizing the quantum_.

- So it’s been disappointing for physicists who hoped they'd be
  living through a golden age of theoretical physics rather than
  the _mathematics of_ theoretical physics.

- The research discipline of particle theory seems to be
  advancing its mathematical underpinnings. But the elaborations
  on the standard model has been stalled from the theory side
  since around 1973-1974.

- So it’s a bit of a paradoxical situation and I think we’ve
  never really been here before.

- There was a period between 1928 until the late 40s when QED
  calculations, the theory of electrons and photons, gave
  infinite answers. The underlying theory seems sound. We just
  didn’t know how to get real contact with experiment.

- And it took a long time to realize we had a technical problem
  rather than a need for an absolutely fundamental revolution of
  the kind that brought us GR and QM.

- So I think we’re a bit stuck and don’t really know how long
  this strange period will last, and this period has been
  dominated by the sort of _quixotic_ hopes that something like
  super-symmetry theory, grand unified theory, technicolor or
  even noncommunicative geometry might work out.

- The problem is all of these speculative theories have remained
  in limbo and many of them have gotten rolled into a very
  strange complex of ideas we call 'string theory' etc...

- And the question is whether it's a a physics-inspired theory or
  really just a sociological phenomenon, where a generation of
  physicists from the baby boom seem to be astounding geometers
  but lacking the ability to make contact with the natural world
  by the standards of previous generations.

- And naturally that’s going to elicit some strong feelings,
  because the idea of maybe 40 years of theoretical physicists
  who didn't make contact with experimental reality is completely
  unprecedented in the modern era.

- This is very interesting and rather disturbing. So I was quite
  inspired by some Arkani-Hamed talks where he points out that
  the three main equations that give us all of theoretical
  physics are all in some sense provably the best possible
  equations in their category:
    - the `Dirac` equation for matter and force equations,
    - the `Yang-Mills` equation,
    - the `Einstein field` equations

- And then we had a question:
    - is there any way to find even better equations? 
    - And can we essentially prove these equations cant be beaten
      in any simple way.

- And I'd say the possible elaborations are now obvious, and
  we’ve tried them all and none have seemed to yield to anything
  that advances our picture...

- So the question is: do we need a radical rethinking? Is there
  something `wrong with the fundamentals`?

- Is Einstein, in fact, wrong to slip in _spacetime_ on the
  ground floor of the theoretical physics which is shared by both
  QFT and GR?

- Or are we in a situation where you’ve searched your apartment
  for missing keys but, in fact, they were hiding in plain sight
  the whole time - You just didn’t think to look in the right
  place?

- And I'd say the jury is really out... but the problem is this
  is the world’s most accomplished intellectual community.
- There's no question that no other group has ever achieved
  anything like the theoretical physics community.
- But the question is, why are they stumped? And if they need
  help where will it come from? 
- It doesn’t seem any chemists or _biologists_ would have enough
  to contribute even though physics has contributed to both of
  those fields.

- So the real hope is it’s going to come from either physicists
  themselves, or from mathematicians who struggle to make any
  kind of contact because the pedagogy in physics is quite
  forbidding, or maybe from some STRANGE SOURCE, like somebody
  who is a _self-teacher_, _off the grid_, that we’ve never heard
  of.

- But we’ve heard from all of the leading lights and I'd say that
  almost no one from the traditional community has a good idea on
  how to make the next progress.

- I think that Einstein’s vision abstractly, will probably be
  proved right in the end - in the abstract. The key question is,
  did he get some particulars wrong?

- He famously said that his equation can be viewed as a mansion
  with two wings, one made from fine _marble_ and the other from
  _cheap wood_ (being the two sides of the equality).

- Now most people have looked at the cheap wood and said, our
  theory of matter and the stress energy tensor is probably what
  needs to be upgraded to make the equation pure marble on both
  sides.

- There’s a more disturbing possibility that the marble is, in
  fact, a premature codification of the geometry and we've been
  so beguiled by the beauty and elegance of the marble side of
  Einstein’s equation that we haven’t put energy into figuring
  out if that’s where the problem is.

- But the problem with taking that route is that Einstein’s
  theory is so locked in at this point through path dependence.

- We’ve built everything on his insights and it’s not really
  clear how you could make a modification to the foundations of
  physics without the whole thing collapsing.

- So even if you're going to do something very heterodox, which
  is to question the bedrock marble of geometry, the question is:
    - can you even get to it given the incredible skyscraper
      built on his solid geometric foundations?

- So this is the route I’ve gone down - to try to think about
  novel approaches.

- If you are going to break with the community it’s very
  difficult to keep up with that level of neural horsepower if
  you have any _other commitments on your time_.
    - So if you choose the path of the dissident, or heterodox,
      or crank, your only hope is to have a really novel idea so
      that you have breathing room from everyone else.

- But of course nobody’s very optimistic about that prospect
  because it’s _very difficult to work on one’s own_ as Einstein
  did in the patent office. In fact we haven’t seen a second
  version of his story since his famous emergence.

- However, the traditional community is also stalled. So you have
  two horses, neither of which seems to be capable of finishing
  the race, and the question is should we be looking more to the
  heterodox - running the risk of craziness and cranks, or should
  we be looking more to the traditional community which seems to
  to be lost in the cul-de-sac we call string theory etc...

- The jury is out, but I think it’s an interesting question
  because traditionally we'd have bet on the experts.

- But the experts have taken more time researching this theory
  than anyone has ever taken to research a theory. And the fact
  that they haven't found anything, means that perhaps the odds
  have changed in that race...
