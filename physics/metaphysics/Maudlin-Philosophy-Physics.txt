### Geometrical structure 1:
    - 3 levels of structure, corresponding to different
      instruments in Euclidean geometry:

        - STRUCTURE            INSTRUMENT     MEASURES:
        - ---------            ----------     ---------

        - Topology:            pencil         Continuity
        > Differentiable       ...            ...
        - Affine structure:    straight-edge  Straight lines
        - Metrical structure:  compass        length, distance

        > Differentiable:
            - Extra level of structure between Topology & Affine
            - distinguish _smooth_ continuous curves from curves
              with CORNERS or sharp BENDS


### Geometrical structure 2:
    - TOPOLOGY
        - (most?) fundamental level
        - determines facts about _continuity_
        - eg. we use a pencil to draw continuous lines
        - if I lift the pencil while drawing, there won't be a single
          connected line
        - to distinguish between a single, continuous line and
          disconnected lines, the points in space must have some
          geometrical organisation
        - rubber sheet geometry:  tearing and pasting not allowed
        - "tearing":
            - separates continuous lines into discontinuous ones
        - "pasting":
            - joins discontinuous lines into continuous wholes
        - if a space had no topology, they'd be no difference
          between drawing a single continuous curve, and several
          disconnected curves, and Euclidian geometry couldn't
          even start

    - AFFINE STRUCTURE
        - Must be a distinction between _straight_ lines and
          other lines
        - Euclids first postulates are:
            - its possible to draw a straight line from any point
              to any point
            - possible to extend any finite straight line
              continuously in a straight line.
        - Not possible without an affine structure ...
        - It's possible to have spaces without this structure.
          Eg.
            - a pair of points might determine no straight line,
              or more than one straight line
            - there might be a limit to how far a straight line
              can be produced

    - METRICAL STRUCTURE
        - lengths of lines
        - distances between points
        - compass draws a circle, locus of points equidistant
          from a given centre
        - Euclids 3rd:
            - a complete, continuous, closed circle can be drawn
              with any given centre and radius
        - again we could imagine spaces where this doesnt hold


### Geometrical TRANSFORMATIONS
    - $MY_IMAGES/physics/metaphysics/geometric-transformations.jpg

    - hierarchical form to transformations too:
        - TOPOLOGICAL TRANSFORMATION:
            - carries continuous lines to continuous lines
            - even though they can be warped or bent... (but not
              torn)

        - DIFFEOMORPHISM
            - preserves differentiable structure and maps smooth
              curves to smooth curves
            - A topological transformation can map a triangle to
              a circle, a diffeomorphism cannot, since a circle
              is smooth but a triangle has corners.


        - AFFINE TRANSFORMATION:
            - map straight lines onto straight lines.
            - It's still affine even if there is a uniform stretching
              that changes distances between points, or _turns
              a circle into an ellipse_...

        - ISOMETRY:
            - preserves distance
            - circles are carried into circles
