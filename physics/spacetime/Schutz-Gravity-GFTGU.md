### Equivalence Galileo:
    - To discover the true properties of gravity we must
      experiment with dense bodies which reduce the effects of
      air resistance. For such objects we find that speed is
      independent of weight.

    - But surely we have to do more work to lift a heavy stone
      than a light one. 
        - Doesn't this mean a heavy stone “wants” to fall more
          than a light one and will do so faster given the
          chance?

    - No said Galileo: heaviness has nothing to do with it. All
      bodies fall with the same the speed. We can prove that by
      measuring it. This was the first step towards the principle
      of equivalence which essentially asserts that: 
        > gravity is indistinguishable from uniform acceleration

### Equivalence Newton:

### Loop-hole in third law:
    - It strictly applies to forces exerted by bodies in
      _constant contact_; touching and not moving relative to
      each other.

    - But some bodies exert forces without being in contact.
      Moving charges exert forces at light speed which weaken
      with distance. Because it takes time for the electric force
      to be transmitted there will be a delay between the time
      charge “A” exerts its force on charge “B” and the time it
      receives the reaction force from “B”.

    - The charges will have changed their relative distances in
      this time and then the two forces will not be equal. This
      imbalance of forces is called _radiation reaction_ on
      accelerating charges which is proportional to the rate of
      change of its _acceleration_. (not velocity?)

    - This force usually opposes its motion and acts as a kind of
      friction. The power delivered to radio antennas goes
      primarily into overcoming the radiation reaction force.

### Newton second law:
    - We hold an object still in our hand. The total force is
      _zero_: our upward force cancels the downward force of
      gravity.

    - If we release the object the force of gravity is still the
      same and it accelerates downwards, i.e. it falls. But what
      is this acceleration in response to the force of gravity?

    - Galileo observed it doesn't depend on its weight, it is the
      same for everything. Now with `a=F/m` the only way we can
      change the force of gravity (weight) without changing the
      acceleration is by changing the mass in proportion to F:
      the force of gravity on an object is proportional to its
      mass.

    - Newtons reasoning leads to a verifiable conclusion. Both
      mass and weight could be measured independently:

        * _weight_ using a scales

        * _mass_ by a combination of measuring the acceleration
          in response to a given horizontal force and `F=ma`. If
          we divide the weight by the mass we should get the
          acceleration of gravity.

### Black bodies (great clarification!)
    - A black body absorbs all the EM radation that falls on it.
    - However black bodies can also radiate light! 
    - Stars are black bodies to a good approximation. 
    - But how can "bright" stars be "black" bodies? 
    - Because the words "bright" and "black" refer to 2 different
      physical processes. 
    - See page 110 for black body explanation. 
    - The two ideas are contradictory because most everyday
      objects are simply not hot enough to be bright (ie to
      radiate) and to be black at the same time.
    - Two different physical processes are at play in a material:
        1. Incoming:
            - How it handles INCOMING LIGHT:
            - either reflecting or absorbing it
            - (or transmission either)
            - if black, then no reflection!
        2. Outgoing:
            - Emission of light by the material itself if it is
              hot (glows)
            - ie the brightness of the materidal, nothing to do
              with its colour from reflecting incoming light...
    - In normal life, if we see something black (absorbing) it is
      not usually hot enough to be _emitting_ at the same time!
