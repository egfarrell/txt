Yes! Gravity Probe B (GP-B) was a famous physics experiment
designed to test two key predictions of Einstein’s general theory
of relativity.

Here’s an overview:


###  Purpose

Gravity Probe B aimed to measure two relativistic effects caused
by Earth’s mass and rotation on spacetime:

1. Geodetic effect

   * The warping (curvature) of spacetime around a massive object
     (Earth).
   * Predicted that the spin axis of a gyroscope orbiting Earth
     would slowly tilt.

2. Frame-dragging effect (Lense–Thirring effect)

   * Earth’s rotation should "drag" spacetime around with it.
   * Predicted a much smaller shift in the gyroscopes’ spin axes.


### The Experiment

* Launched: April 20, 2004, by NASA.
* Orbit: 642 km above Earth in a nearly polar orbit.
* Instruments: Four ultra-precise gyroscopes made of fused quartz
  spheres (among the smoothest objects ever manufactured).
* Method:

  * The gyroscopes were housed inside a superfluid helium Dewar,
    kept at ~1.8 K (near absolute zero) to minimize disturbances.
  * A telescope locked onto a distant "guide star" (IM Pegasi),
    providing a fixed reference direction.
  * If general relativity is correct, the gyroscopes’ spin axes
    would shift very slightly over time, matching Einstein’s
    predictions.


### Results

* After years of careful data analysis (disturbances like trapped
  magnetic flux made things tricky), the final results were
  announced in 2011:

  * Geodetic effect: Measured to 0.28% accuracy — confirmed
    Einstein’s prediction.
  * Frame-dragging effect: Measured to 19% accuracy — also
    consistent with general relativity, though less precise.


### Significance

* Provided direct experimental confirmation of spacetime
  curvature and frame-dragging.
* Technologically groundbreaking: developed advanced gyroscopes,
  superconducting sensors, and precision cryogenics.
* Served as one of the most ambitious and precise tests of
  relativity in Earth orbit.
