# Part 1

### Tennis balls
    - Q:
        - What happens to the speed of the tennis ball when we
          put the cannon on a train?
        - Answer: We have to add the speed of the tennis ball to
          that of the train. (careful here:- once we start
          dealing with SR velocity, addition only works at low
          velocities!)

    - Q: Is the speed the same for all intertial observers?
        - Answer: No, slower for someone on the train.

    - my thoughts:
        - We have to allow for changes in mass?
        - Also this links to Asimovs book and explanation of SR.


### Waves:
    - Q:
        - Is the speed of water, speed of sound waves independent
          of the speed of the source?
        - Answer: Generally yes - the speed of water or sound
          waves RELATIVE TO THE MEDIUM depends on properties of
          the medium and NOT of the source.

    - Q: Is the speed of the wave the same for all inertial
      observers?
        - Answer: NO! Think about the speed of a wave for
          a surfer riding the wave.

    - So light looks A BIT more like water waves than tennis
      balls, but its not really like water waves either.


### Moving Clocks
    - `LIGHT` clocks - time goes "slow" as you move, as observed
      from another reference frame
        - (cos each light beam has further to travel between the
          2 mirrors)

    - But what about other clocks??? Do they go slow as well?
        - Well the principle of relativity tells us that our
          other clocks must tick at the same rate as our (slow)
          light clocks, otherwise we'd be able to tell what speed
          we were travelling at by looking at relative ticking
          rates...

    - hmmmm... very clever and pat...

# Part 2

### Time
    - Are the past, present and future equally real?

    - Presentism:
        - the world consists of what exists right now, reality is
          3D. Past and future not real the way the present is.

    - Eternalism:
        - Past, present, future all equally real... There are
          knowledge differences between the past and the future
          but they still exist, its just you dont know about the
          future yet...

    - Growing block:
        - past and present, but not the future, are real.

### Does time flow?
    - temporal passage / becoming
        - Is flow something to do with us (our perception) or
          something real in the world?
        - notorious question...

    - Flow implies motion, and motion is usually with respect to
      time! Can time move with respect to time? Maybe time flows
      wrt an additional parameter - SUPERTIME! (can get regress
      problems)

    - Moral: Time "flow" is problematic, better to talk about
      "becoming". Time flows gets translated into talk of
      "passage" or "becoming", being an objective feature of
      reality. So we dont have to talk about `rates`.

    - Pooley - two time problem
        - if you have Eternalism, but also need to describe
          change, you need a SECOND SENSE of time in which change
          can happen. (can get regress problems...)

### Is the future open?
    - Be careful about mixing up two things:

        - determinism: ("possible knowledge")
            - specify and initial state - then does the laws of
              nature ensure its state is specified at all future
              times?

        - Openness of the future: ("metaphysical")
            - Are future facts ALREADY true or false?
              (independent of how the laws of nature work
            - i.e. could hold in an indeterministic universe)

### Tense:
    - "what (_1_) exists is what (_2_) exists now"
        - tautology, need another sense of "exists" for (_2_)
        - ...but... presentists deny we can make sense of
          tenseless language.

### McTaggart
    - "A-Series"
    - ----------
        - order events according to whether they are
        1. past
        2. present
        3. future
        - This sequence CAN change 
        - All events will eventually be past.
        - Is this language saying something that the tenseless
          language below cant say...
        - McTaggart claims this type of series is contradictory.
            - All 3 characteristics belong to each event.
            - (although not at the same time)

    - "B-Series"
    - ----------
        - order events according to their `RELATION` to each
          other. This sequence doesnt change.

### McTaggart premises:
    1. Time necessarily involves change.
    2. Change requires the A-Series.
    3. The A-Series is contradictory.
        - Therefore time is unreal.

### Knox:
    - much more helpful to think about
    - the "NATURE" of time
    - rather than its "REALITY"

### Discussion
    - more helpful to think about the third premise:
        - 3. Is the A-Series contradictory?

    - Proponents of `A` deny this, they say tense is primitive
      and non-analysable.

    - `B` people think that `A` (in as far as it has content)
      just reduces to the `B` Series. 
        - Tensed properties are simply INDEXED to times, based on
          my temporal LOCATION.
        - e.g. "The water is here", here is indexed to my spatial
          location.
        - no contradiction, but no extra content

    - Note: we often associate `A` with flow etc... so McTaggart
      has some impact on that idea.

### Time synthesis
    - we naturally group:
        - Presentism, dynamic views, Openess and the A-Series
        - Eternalism, static views, non-Openess and the B-Series

    - but you can have some (funny) combinations of the two
      groups above..
