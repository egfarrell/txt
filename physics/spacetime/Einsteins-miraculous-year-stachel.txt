- (Contradictory views on Maxwell's EM)

- There are some remarkable aspects to Einstein’s relation to
  quantum physics, which border on the _paradoxical_.
    - Earliest and most striking is that Einstein’s revolutionary
      papers on relativity (paper 3) and quantum phenomena (paper
      5) appear to start from mutually contradictory standpoints
      regarding Maxwell’s theory as an explanation of light.
    - In the quantum paper, 
        - Einstein explicitly rejects Maxwell’s equations as
          sufficient to explain the actions of light (as waves in
          the EM ﬁeld) and presents a model in which light
          behaves instead like little particles.
    - Yet, in paper 3, 
        - he develops SR from the starting point that Maxwell’s
          theory does indeed represent fundamental truth.


- One is reminded of Newton’s struggles with basically the same
  problem 300 years earlier, where he proposed a curious hybrid
  of a wave and particle viewpoint to explain conflicting aspects
  of the behavior of light. 
    - In Newton’s case, it's possible to understand his dogged
      adherence to a particle-type picture if one takes the
      reasonable view that he wished to preserve a relativity
      principle.
    - But this argument only holds if the relevant relativity
      principle is that of Galileo (and Newton).
    - In Einstein’s case, this argument will not do, because he
      explicitly puts forward a _different relativity principle_
      (Lorentz), in which Maxwell’s wave theory could survive
      intact.
    - Thus, it's necessary to look more deeply to ﬁnd the
      profound reasons for Einstein’s extraordinary conviction
      that although Maxwell’s wave picture of light was, in some
      sense, "true", it nevertheless needed to be altered to
      something different which, in certain respects, harked back
      _Newton’s hybrid_ "wave-particle" picture of three
      centuries earlier.

- In paper 5, he put this expertise to use by treating EM fields
  in the same way, thereby explaining effects that cannot be
  obtained from the Maxwellian view of light alone.
    - Indeed, Einstein showed that the problem with the classical
      approach was it doesn't make physical sense to have
      a picture in which continuous fields and discrete particles
      coexist, each interacting with the other.
    - Thus, he initiated an important step toward the present-day
      quantum-theoretic viewpoint that: 
        - particles must indeed take on attributes of waves, and 
        - fields must take on attributes of particles.
    - Looked at appropriately in the quantum picture, particles
      and waves actually _turn out to be the same thing_.

- Yet, it is clear that the fundamental advances Einstein made in
  1905 crucially depended on his belief in the actual reality of
  physical entities at the molecular and sub-molecular levels.

- The _central force program_ (?) was shaken around the middle of
  the 19th century when it appeared that, in order to explain EM
  interactions between moving charged molecules, velocity- and
  acceleration-dependent forces had to be assumed.

- But it received the coup de grace when Faraday and Maxwell’s
  concept of the EM ﬁeld began to prevail.
    - According to the field viewpoint, two charged particles do
      not interact DIRECTLY: 
        - each charge creates fields in the space surrounding it,
          and it is these fields which exert forces on the other
          charge.
    - At ﬁrst, these electric and magnetic fields were conceived
      as states of a mechanical medium, the EM ether; 
        - these states were assumed to be ultimately explainable
          on the basis of mechanical models of that ether.
    - Meanwhile, Maxwell’s equations gave a complete description
      of the possible states of the electric and magnetic fields
      at all points of space, and how they change over time.
    - By the turn of the century, the search for mechanical
      explanations of the ether had been largely abandoned in
      favor of _Lorentz’s viewpoint_, frankly DUALISTIC:
        - the electric and magnetic fields were accepted as
          fundamental states of the ether, governed by Maxwell’s
          equations but not in need of further explanation.
        - Charged particles, which Lorentz called electrons
          (others called them molecules or ions), obeyed Newton’s
          mechanical laws of motion under the inﬂuence of forces
          that include the electric and magnetic forces exerted
          by the ether;
            - and in turn the charged particles created these
              fields by their presence in the ether and their
              motion through it.
    - I call Lorentz’s outlook _dualistic_:
        - ... because he accepted the mechanical worldview
          applied to electrons but regarded the ether with its
          electric and magnetic fields as an additional,
          independent element of reality, not MECHANICALLY
          explicable.

- Wien and others suggested another possibility:
    - perhaps the EM field is the true fundamental entity, and
      matter's behavior depends totally on its EM properties.
    - Instead of explaining the behavior of EM fields in terms of
      a mechanical model of the ether, this EM worldview hoped to
      explain the mechanical properties of matter in terms of
      electric and magnetic fields.
    - Even Lorentz flirted with this possibility, though he never
      fully adopted it.

- Einstein hoped his work might help settle the status of

  basis for molecular and gravitational forces, an indication of
  his strong ambition from the outset to pursue theoretical
  UNIFICATION of all the apparently disparate phenomena of
  Physics.

        - This is exactly what happens in Brownian motion, where

        - Even though the second law holds on average, these
          fluctuations briefly create small, temporary reversals
          of entropy—violations that are visible under
          a microscope!
    - What is a 2nd law 'reversal'?
        - A temporary violation of the second law of
          thermodynamics means that, for a brief moment, the
          system becomes more ordered instead of more disordered.
          This can happen due to random fluctuations, and here’s
          what it might look like at different scales:
        1. Atomic Level 
            - At the atomic scale, the liquid molecules
              surrounding a suspended particle are constantly
              moving due to thermal energy. Normally, these
              molecules push the particle in _random directions_,
              leading to Brownian motion. 
            - However, due to statistical chance, there can be
              moments when:
                - More molecules happen to push the particle in
                  one direction instead of randomly. This would
                  momentarily give the particle a small,
                  seemingly "non-random" motion.
                - The surrounding molecules could briefly cluster
                  together in an unusually low-entropy way,
                  forming a temporary "density fluctuation" where
                  some region becomes more ordered.

        2. Visible Microscopic Level
            - If you're looking through a microscope at a large
              suspended particle, a second-law violation might
              look like:
            - The particle suddenly _moving in one direction_ for
              an unusually long time, as if it were
              "self-organizing" rather than moving randomly.
            - Several Brownian particles briefly clustering
              together instead of spreading out, which would be
              a momentary local decrease in entropy.
            - A spontaneous local flow of heat from a colder
              region to a hotter region (extremely rare, but
              possible due to fluctuations).

        - These fluctuations don’t last long and are quickly
          "corrected" by the usual increase in entropy. That’s
          why the second law is statistically valid over time,
          even though temporary reversals can happen.
        - So yes—one way to picture it is particles momentarily
          coming together or organizing in an unexpected way
          before randomness takes over again!


- As a consequence, the Newtonian-Galileian laws of
  transformation between the space and time coordinates of
  different inertial frames must be replaced by a new set of
  transformations: the "Lorentz transformations".
    - Since these transformations are _kinematical_ in nature,
      any acceptable physical theory must be invariant under the
      group of such transformations.
    - Maxwell’s equations, suitably reinterpreted after
      eliminating the concept of the ether, meet this
      requirement; but Newton’s equations of motion needed
      revision.

- His conviction that classical mechanical concepts and those of
  Maxwell’s electrodynamics, as well as any mere modification of
  the two, are _incapable_ of explaining a growing list of newly
  discovered phenomena involving the behavior and interactions
  of _matter and radiation_.

- To obtain this result, Einstein had to assume each quantum has
  an energy proportional to its `frequency`.
    - Emboldened by this result, he took the ﬁnal step, proposing
      his "very revolutionary" hypothesis that _matter and
      radiation can interact only_ through the exchange of such
      energy quanta.

- He showed that the mirror would be unable to carry out such
  a Brownian motion indefinitely, if the fluctuations of
  radiation pressure on its surfaces were solely due to the
  effects of random waves, as predicted by Maxwell’s theory.
    - Only the existence of an additional term, corresponding to
      pressure fluctuations due to the impact of random
      `particles` on the mirror, guarantees its continued
      Brownian motion.
    - Einstein showed that both WAVE and PARTICLE energy
      _fluctuation terms_ are consequences of Planck’s
      distribution law for black-body radiation.
    - He regarded this result as his strongest argument for the
      physical reality of light quanta.

- He was convinced that a satisfactory theory of matter and
  radiation must _construct_ these quanta of electricity and of
  radiation, not simply postulate them.

- In 1909 he made his first attempt to ﬁnd a field theory that
  explains both:
    - the structure of matter (the electron) and

- Paper 5, Einstein’s first paper on the quantum hypothesis, is
  a striking example of his style, mixing the critique of old
  concepts with a search for new ones.
    - It opens by demonstrating that the _equipartition theorem_,
      together with Maxwell’s equations, leads to a definite
      formula for the black-body radiation spectrum, now known as
      the Rayleigh-Jeans distribution.
    - Equipartition Theorem:
        - A result of classical statistical mechanics, where each
          DEGREE OF FREEDOM of a mechanical system in thermal
          equilibrium _receives_, on average, the same share of
          total energy of the system.
