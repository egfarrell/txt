### Is Space a human abstraction or a physical entity?

- From the baseballs perspective, it’s the batter who’s
  approaching at 100 miles per hour(?!)

- According to Newton you are free to consider the world from
  different vantage points, but different perspectives are by no
  means on an equal footing:  ice-skater spinning, and an ant on
  the ice skaters boot: Both points of view seem equally valid,
  both on an equal footing, but they stand diametrically opposed
  to each other.
    - But according to Newton, one of the perspectives really is
      truer than the other: if the skater really is spinning
      their arms will splay out, but if it’s the arena rotating
      they wont.
    - Newtons absolute space means accepting an absolute
      conception of acceleration, in particular, accepting an
      absolute answer regarding who is really spinning.
    - But Brian Greene struggled: only relative motion had
      relevance when considering constant velocity motion, so why
      should acceleration be different?
    - Why shouldn’t relative acceleration, like relative
      velocity, be the only thing that’s relevant when
      considering velocity that isn’t constant.

- Newton searched for something in empty space relative to which
  motion could be defined, and came up with empty space itself.

- So apparently... Absolute space allows us to identify true
  accelerated motion (like the bucket: if something is spinning
  with respect to absolute space then it really is spinning) but
  can't provide a way to identify true constant velocity motion.

- But if absolute space really exists, it should provide
  a benchmark for all motion, not just accelerated motion.

- If Absolute Space  exists we all should have an absolute
  location, no need to reference things in relation to other
  material objects. And if Absolute Space exists, how come it can
  affect us (arms splay when spinning) but we cant affect it.

- And with the bucket experiment, instead of Absolute Space, why
  not choose the ground, or laboratory etc as the “stationary”
  reference point to determine if the water is spinning?
- (Newton jumped too quick from dismissing bucket to introducing
  “Absolute Space”) Maybe referring the spinning water to the
  earth etc, such relative motion can account for the shape of
  the waters surface.

- Mach’s suggestion: in a totally empty universe (no visual
  clues, reference points), standing perfectly motionless and
  spinning uniformly (all body parts in sync) are
  indistinguishable. Actually in an empty universe there is no
  concept of spinning v non spinning.
- But: Mach suggested the force you feel is proportional to the
  amount of matter in the universe. In a universe with a single
  star, you’d feel a minuscule force on your body if you started
  spinning.

- Mach’s ideas imply that the force you feel represents the
  combined influence of all other matter making up the universe.
  You feel acceleration only when you accelerate relative to the
  average distribution of other material inhabiting the cosmos.
- Without benchmarks there would be no way to experience
  acceleration.
- (So it’s a relationist concept of space the same as Leibniz who
  argued that space only exists as a way of encoding where things
  are relative to one another. If all objects were removed from
  space – if space were completely empty – it would be as
  meaningless as an alphabet without letters.)
- Note: sensation of spinning = sensation of accelerated motion.

- So Machs idea puts all perspectives on an equal footing.
  (relative motion/acceleration)

- But Mach never specified _HOW_ the matter content of the
  universe would exert the proposed influence. From modern
  viewpoint, one could guess that it might be gravity that has
  something to do with Machs proposal.

- After Einstein got to work, the question of which is a correct
  view of space – absolutist or relationist – was shattered. The
  corporeality of space is outmoded – question is: Is spacetime
  something?
