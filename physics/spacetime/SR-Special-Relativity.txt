### On shell
    - The phrase “on shell” comes from the mass shell, or more
      precisely the `mass hyperboloid` in Minkowski spacetime.
    - In relativistic mechanics, a free particle with mass *m*
      must satisfy
        - `E^2 - p^2 = m^2`  (using c = 1 units).
    - If you plot energy versus momentum, all the physically
      allowed 4-momenta for that mass lie on a `two-sheeted
      hyperboloid` — a “shell” in momentum space.
    - So a momentum vector on the mass shell satisfies the
      physical dispersion relation, while a vector off the shell
      does not.
    - When early quantum field theorists (Dirac, Feynman, Dyson,
      etc.) began manipulating propagators, they noticed that
      virtual particles in internal lines violate this relation
      — they have “off-shell” four-momenta. 
        - Only the incoming and outgoing real particles in
          a scattering amplitude are “on shell.” 
        - The phrase then broadened: any configuration that
          satisfies the dynamical equations (not just kinematic
          mass relations) is “on shell.”
    - From there, the idiom spread backward into CM and GR. When
      a relativist says *the Hamiltonian is a boundary term
      on-shell*, they mean that once the Einstein equations (the
      gravitational field’s “mass shell”) are imposed, all the
      bulk contributions cancel, leaving only boundary terms.
    - So the genealogy is:
        * 1930s: “mass shell” in relativistic particle physics
        * 1940s–50s: “on/off-shell” in QFT for real vs. virtual
          particles
        * 1960s onward: adopted by field theorists and
          relativists to mean “satisfying the equations of
          motion.”
    - It’s a delightful case of jargon evolving across subfields
      — a fossil from the birth of QFT that now shows up
      everywhere from Feynman diagrams to BH thermodynamics.


### Synchronisation
    - https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09361v1
    - Poincaré’s synchronization and Einstein’s synchronization
      may look similar at the procedural level, but they are
      embedded in fundamentally different conceptual frameworks.
      For Poincaré, synchronization is a convention,
      a _convenient fiction_ layered over an underlying “true
      time” defined by motion through the ether. The procedure
      yields local time, an ”apparent” valuable time for
      observers in uniform motion, but not the fundamental time.
      Poincaré still assumed the ether. The procedure only
      “works” because observers are ignorant of their absolute
      motion through it. Synchronization is a pragmatic patch,
      not a reconstruction of physics. Poincaré considers
      synchronization to be an adjustment rule in the service of
      celestial mechanics and longitude measurements
      — a technical fix that makes dynamics manageable.
    - For Einstein, synchronization is not a convention masking
      reality — _it is what time means_. There is no hidden
      “true” time. By declaring that the ether is superfluous,
      Einstein promoted the synchronization procedure from
      a convention to the operational definition of simultaneity.
      There is no “true” time to compare against. The
      light-signal procedure is definitive, not provisional. The
      relativity of simultaneity emerges not as a correction but
      as the core of the new kinematics.
    - Even if Einstein had known Poincaré’s 1898 paper, the
      conceptual leap from “conventional synchronization under
      the ether” to “synchronization as the very definition of
      time, with no ether” is so radical that appropriation does
      not explain it. Thus, Einstein did not need Poincaré’s 1898
      or 1900 procedure. The procedure was in the air: railway
      clocks, telegraphy, and astronomical longitude. Everyone in
      scientific Europe was aware of signal-based
      synchronization. What Einstein did was to invert its
      meaning: what for Poincaré was a practical fiction became,
      for Einstein, the essence of time itself.

### Check this book
    - Jean-Marc Ginoux’s: Poincaré, Einstein and the Discovery of
      Special Relativity: An End to the Controversy (2024)
    - https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09361v1
    - This book seeks to close the debate over the respective
      roles of Poincaré and Einstein. Yet what is presented as an
      "end" may instead invite a more careful analysis of how
      similar equations can conceal divergent conceptions. 
    - The aim here is not to rehearse priority disputes but to
      show how Einstein's ether-free, principle-based kinematics
      marked out a path that, unlike its contemporaries, became
      the canonical form of special relativity. To this end,
      I reconstruct side by side the 1905 derivations of Poincaré
      and Einstein, tracing their similarities and, more
      importantly, their differences. This paper reconstructs, in
      a novel way, the 1905 derivations of Einstein and Poincaré,
      highlighting their contrasting paths.

    - Interesting comment:
        - Me: Basically accelerating objects CANNOT SHARE the
          same reference frame?
        - Your comment makes the same ASSUMPTION about the
          problem that gives rise to the paradox.
            - It assumes that any two objects that are
              ACCELERATING at the same rate at the same time are
              SHARING a reference frame.
            - They are not.
        - The only objects that can share a reference frame are
          INERTIAL OBJECTS and only when their velocity vectors
          are exactly identical in direction and magnitude at the
          same time.
            - If they're even slightly misaligned in any
              direction or magnitude at a specific time, the two
              inertial objects are officially in TWO DIFFERENT
              REFERENCE frames, even by SR standards.
        - Two spaceships traveling at exactly the same constant
          speed in exactly the same direction (that is, their
          velocity vectors are exactly equivalent) will agree on
          how long they each are and how far apart they each are.
            - They both share the SAME COORDINATE SYSTEM, and so
              will measure each other and their separation on
              that shared coordinate system.
            - A third spaceship observing them, with an unequal
              velocity vector, will disagree, because it is on
              its own _boosted_ coordinate system.
        - And likewise, by SR standards, all objects in
          accelerating frames of reference are in their own
          exclusive little club, regardless whether any other
          objects not already dragged along for the ride have
          otherwise equal acceleration functions.
        - So the two spaceships accelerating "simultaneously" are
          in their own exclusive reference frames and must
          therefore be considered INDEPENDENTLY of one another.
            - To an accelerating frame of reference, time AND
              space translation matter.
            - Two objects sharing the same acceleration function
              MUST have the same position in both time AND space
              in order to share a common reference frame.
            - If the functions are displaced in either term (time
              or space), then the objects are in SEPARATE
              reference frames.


### Bell Spaceship: GPT Analysis 1 
    - Me: What is the difference between
        - the COORDINATE based Lorentz-FitzGerald contraction and
        - the PHYSICAL Lorentz-FitzGerald contraction?
    - key:
        - ChatGPT seems to be saying it's actually two different
          ways of understanding the SAME effect. But from Tim
          Maudlin, I thought they were two separate effects?
    - ChatGPT
        - The Lorentz-FitzGerald contraction, also known as the
          'Lorentz contraction' or 'length contraction', is
          a phenomenon predicted by SR.
        - According to SR, when an object moves at a significant
          fraction of the speed of light relative to an observer,
          its length appears shortened in the direction of
          motion.
        - There are two common ways to describe the
          Lorentz-FitzGerald contraction:
            - the coordinate-based approach and
            - the physical approach.
        - COORDINATE-BASED Lorentz-FitzGerald contraction:
            - In this approach, the Lorentz-FitzGerald
              contraction is described in terms of coordinate
              transformations between different reference frames.
            - When an object is moving relative to an observer,
              the coordinates of its endpoints are transformed
              using the Lorentz transformation equations.
            - These equations incorporate the relativistic
              effects of time dilation and length contraction.
              From the perspective of the observer, the object's
              spatial extent in the direction of motion appears
              contracted.
        - PHYSICAL Lorentz-FitzGerald contraction:
            - The physical approach focuses on the physical
              properties of the moving object itself.
            - In this approach, when an object moves at
              relativistic speeds, its constituent particles
              undergo a physical contraction in the direction of
              motion.
            - This contraction is not merely an artifact of
              coordinate transformations but represents an actual
              physical reduction in size.
            - The physical Lorentz-FitzGerald contraction arises
              from the relativistic effects on the motion of
              _charged particles_ within the object.
                - As an object accelerates, its charged particles
                  experience a _change in momentum and emit
                  electromagnetic radiation_.
                - This radiation exerts a drag force on the
                  particles, causing them to bunch together in
                  the direction of motion and giving rise to the
                  contraction effect.
        - Both the coordinate-based and physical descriptions of
          the Lorentz-FitzGerald contraction lead to the same
          predictions and are consistent with each other.
        - They are two different ways of understanding the SAME
          underlying phenomenon.
        - The coordinate-based approach emphasizes the
          mathematical transformations between reference frames,
          while the physical approach focuses on actual physical
          processes occurring within the moving object.


### Bell Spaceship: GPT Analysis 2 (six months later, GPT 4o)
    - Yes, you are correct. The thread would indeed need to
      expand as seen in the spaceships' frames to combat the
      tension it feels. Here is the corrected explanation: 

    - WHY DOES THE THREAD SNAP?
    - In Bell's spaceship paradox, the thread snaps due to the
      following reason:
        - As the spaceships accelerate, they maintain a constant
          distance in their initial inertial frame. However, due
          to the principles of SR, the distance between them as
          measured in the instantaneous rest frame OF EITHER
          SPACESHIP increases.
        - The Lorentz contraction effect is relative to the rest
          frame of the accelerating objects. Since the spaceships
          are accelerating and thus changing frames, each
          spaceship sees the other moving away more quickly.
        - The constant acceleration leads to a situation where
          the thread, which would need to expand as seen in the
          spaceships' frames to match the increasing distance
          between the spaceships, experiences tension and
          eventually snaps.

    - In essence, from the perspective of the inertial frame in
      which the spaceships were initially at rest, the
      spaceships' simultaneous acceleration keeps the distance
      between them constant. However, in the rest frame of the
      accelerating spaceships, the distance between the
      spaceships increases. The thread, which cannot physically
      expand to accommodate this increasing distance, experiences
      tension and snaps. Corrected Conclusion

    - The thread snaps not because of a split in the types of
      contraction but because of the relativistic effects of
      maintaining a constant distance during simultaneous
      acceleration. The notion of coordinate-based and physical
      Lorentz-FitzGerald contraction, as highlighted by Maudlin,
      helps illustrate the complexities of relativistic motion,
      but the snapping of the thread is fundamentally due to the
      relativistic tension arising from the ships’ constant
      distance in their original rest frame versus the need for
      the thread to expand in their new accelerated frames.
