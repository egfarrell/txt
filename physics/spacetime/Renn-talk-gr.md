
### key point:
    when you have the right field (covariant) field equations you get the
    energy conservation for free... (cf. Noether Theorem)

### initial thought 1907
    Einstein thought gravity was related to inertia.
    Renn: by inertia I mean _INERTIAL FORCES_ eg. what you
    feel when a car is going around a bend.
    Einstein thought he could simulate gravitational 
    effects through accelerated motion.
    By simulating gravity (through accelerated reference
    frames) Einstein could *infer* a lot of properties
    of what later became general relativity.

### use Maxwell as a guide
    previous to Einstein,
    * gravity was considered a _force_
    * inertia was considered a _property_ of space
    Einstein wanted to create
        a 'gravito-inertial' field
    and so followed __the example of EM field__ theory
    (I didnt know that!! )

### field theory: 2 requirements
    * needs an _equation of motion_ - field tells matter how to move
    * needs a _field equation_ - matter (and energy) determine the field

### static gravitational field:
    following EM theory, for a __static__ field:
    (Prague Notebook 1912)
    * `field = - grad (potential)`
    * `acceleration = field` -> equation of motion
    * `div (field) = source` -> field equation (source determines field)

    I prefer:
    * `field = acceleration`  -> field tells matter how to move
    * `source = div (field) ` -> matter & energy (source) determine the field

### equation of motion:  
    (Zurich Notebook, P 39 L, summer 1912)
    * He used _metric tensor_ to describe gravitational potential.
    * _geodesic equation_ 
    (generalisation of the law of inertia to __curved__ space time)

### field equation
    The next 3 years were all about finding a field equation.
    The geodesic equation has _10 components_. 
    Imagine trying to find a (__non-linear__) _differential equation_
    for 10 components.

    He needed a _differential operator_ acting on the metric tensor.

### Entwuf theory
    met the physical requirements but was unclear
    how covariant it was. how did it react to 
    general coordinate transformations?

    Entwuf field equation = (generally covariant equation) 
                            + (coordinate restriction)

### Entwuf coordinate restriction
    Einstein wrote a version of his Entwuf theory
    which he derived from a _variational_ principle.

    The coordinate restriction does __double__ duty:
    * it determines the covariance _group_ of the Entwuf field equations
    * it also guarantees energy-momentum conservation.

    ie the coordinate restriction that allows the Entwuf equations to
    keep their form (remain covariant) __also__ allows energy
    conservation. Here are the seeds of Noethers theorem!

### trouble
    the uniqueness argument for the Entwuf _lagrangian_ theory disappears
    but at least he had a __variational__ formalism now,
    so the Entwuf could serve as a __scaffold__ for the final true theory

### does it match newtonian physics?
    Einstein looked to see if poissons equation can be recovered in the
    limit from his field equations. Poisson just has __one scalar
    gravitational__ potential. 

    From Einsteins equations (in the _weak static_ case) you get extra
    components in the metric tensor along with the scalar potential,
    and this is why Einstein originally thought his equations were
    wrong in 1913. But eventually Einstein found out that these extra
    components are not relevant to the _equation of motion_ for this
    approximation in the static case.

    Note: the newtonian limit isnt simply about reducing the field
    equations to the poisson equation. You have to look at the
    interplay between field equations and equation of motion. 

    How did he learn that the extra components dont matter? From the
    perhelion anomoly of Mercury: he had developed an approximation
    scheme which showed him that he could accept a weak static limit
    that didnt take the expected form.

### the solution
    in the Entwuf theory
    * `gravitational field = - grad (potential)`
    was wrong, it didnt have the cristoffel symbols.
    He switched to:
    * `gravitational field = - grad (potential using CRISTOFFEL SYMBOLS)`
    and inserted this into his existing variational 
    formalism of the Entwuf.
