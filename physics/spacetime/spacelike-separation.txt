# Explanation

- The book’s line is gesturing at a softer idea: not that
  spacelike events are simultaneous, but that they're the only
  kind of pair of events for which simultaneity can be defined at
  all — in some inertial frame.

- Let me unwrap it without turning it into a jungle of indices.

### 1. Three kinds of separation

- Given two events A and B, the spacetime interval is
    - `s² = c² Δt² − Δx² − Δy² − Δz²`

- `s² > 0` → timelike
- `s² = 0` → lightlike
- `s² < 0` → spacelike

- You already know the causal story: timelike and lightlike pairs
  can influence each other; spacelike pairs cannot.

### 2. The relativity-of-simultaneity trick

- Here’s the key: only spacelike-separated events can be made
  simultaneous by choosing an appropriate inertial frame.

- For timelike or lightlike pairs, Δt = 0 is impossible in any
  frame. You can Lorentz-transform all you like; the sign of the
  interval has you locked in.

- But if `s² < 0`, there exists some inertial frame in which `Δt′ = 0`.

- This frame “tilts” your hyperplanes of simultaneity just right
  so that `A` and `B` land on the same `t′`-value.

So:
- spacelike → simultaneity possible in at least one frame
- timelike  → simultaneity impossible in every frame
- lightlike → simultaneity impossible and `Δt′` always `±|Δx′|/c`

- That’s all the book meant by “the closest thing relativity allows to simultaneous.”

- Not simultaneous in absolute terms — relativity forbids that
  notion — but eligible for simultaneity in some coordinate
  system.

### 3. But why “closest thing”?

- Because, geometrically, spacelike separation means the two
  events are both:
    - outside each other’s light cones
    - capable of being placed on the same spacelike slice by
      a suitable choice of frame
    - unable to causally influence one another

- This frame-dependent simultaneity is the last surviving
  fragment of what “simultaneous” used to mean before Einstein
  reworked time into a more flexible fabric.

### 4. And your “nowhen, nowhere” feeling?

- That intuition is good.
- Spacelike-separated events are radically “elsewhere” — they
  cannot communicate, correlate causally, or agree on temporal
  order. In many frames A happens before B; in others B before A;
  in some A and B share the same time coordinate.

- That very freedom of ordering is what makes the “simultaneous
  in some frame” property possible.

### 5. One-sentence takeaway

- Spacelike-separated events aren’t simultaneous, but they are
  the only events that can be made simultaneous in a suitable
  inertial frame — hence the book’s poetic phrasing.
