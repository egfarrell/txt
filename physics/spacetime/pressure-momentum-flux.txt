    - Good—this is exactly the right place to pause and ask
      “pressure against what?” because in everyday life, pressure
      is always against a wall or container.
    - But in GR, pressure means something subtler:
        - it is the `flux of momentum through space`, and it
          gravitates just like `energy density`.


### 1. Stress-energy tensor in plain terms

The stress-energy tensor `T_{mu nu}` is a 4x4 object that encodes:

    * `T_{00}` = energy density (how much energy per unit volume),
    * `T_{0i}` = momentum density (like energy flowing in space),
    * `T_{ij}` = stresses: pressure (diagonal parts) and shear (off-diagonal parts).

- For an isotropic fluid (same in all directions), the diagonal
  spatial terms are just pressure in the fluid’s rest frame:
    - ```
    - T_mu_nu = diag(rho, p, p, p)
    - ```

    - So in GR, “pressure” isn’t relative to a container.
    - It’s an intrinsic property of a distribution of
      matter/energy: the tendency of particles or fields to push
      in all directions.


### 2. Why pressure gravitates

- Einstein’s field equations tell us that spacetime curvature
  couples to the whole tensor `T_{mu nu}`, not just `rho`.

- In the cosmological (Friedmann) equations, the key combination
  that drives *acceleration* of the scale factor is:

```
a_ddot / a = -(4*pi*G/3) * (rho + 3p)
```
    - This equation shows:
        - Energy density `rho` pulls inward (attractive).
        - Pressure contributes threefold, because pressure pushes
          along each of the 3 spatial directions.

    - So:
        - Normal matter: `p ~ 0`, attractive.
        - Radiation: `p = rho/3`, still attractive, even more
          strongly than matter.
        - Vacuum energy: `p = -rho`, so `rho + 3p = rho - 3rho
          = -2rho`, which flips the sign and makes expansion
          accelerate (repulsive).


### 3. Physical picture of pressure as momentum flux

    - Imagine photons in a box. They bounce around, carrying
      momentum. The pressure you measure on the walls is
      literally momentum flux — photons transferring momentum per
      unit area per unit time. Even if there are no walls, the
      same momentum flux exists in the photon gas everywhere.

    - GR doesn’t need walls: it “feels” the stress-energy tensor,
      so the pressure terms contribute to curvature directly.
      Pressure is real, not just a bookkeeping artifact.


### 4. Summary

    - Pressure in the stress-energy tensor is momentum per unit
      area per unit time carried in each direction of space.
      Because Einstein’s equations couple spacetime curvature to
      *all* forms of energy and momentum flux, pressure
      gravitates. Negative pressure is just as real as positive
      pressure, and in the vacuum-energy case it causes repulsion
      instead of attraction.
