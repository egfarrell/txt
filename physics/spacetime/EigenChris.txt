### Geodesics and Christoffels 
    - https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1CuTNveXJRc
    - If you're going 'forward' as _straight_ as possible
      (geodesic), any change in the velocity vector (i.e.
      acceleration) will be in a plane up/down from the
      velocity vector..(i.e. normal to the velocity vector) 
        - but no change in the velocity vector from side-to-side
          (tangentially)!
        - so for a geodesic any acceleration vector will be
          pointing directly out of the surface, always `normal`
          to the surface
        - $MY_IMAGES/physics/GR/EigenChris-geodesic-1.jpg
        - $MY_IMAGES/physics/GR/EigenChris-geodesic-2.jpg
    - To FIND geodesic curves, we need to find curves where the
      acceleration vector is normal to the surface
        - $MY_IMAGES/physics/GR/EigenChris-geodesic-3.jpg
    - ASIDE: 
        - create a curved surface using a map from a 2D
          plane... studied this in Lboro and didn't have a clue
          what this map was about
            - $MY_IMAGES/physics/GR/EigenChris-geodesic-4.jpg
        - and to draw a curve on the curved surface, first draw the
          curve in the `uv-plane`, as a function of some curve
          parameter `λ`, and then put it through the function to
          put it in 3D space...
            - $MY_IMAGES/physics/GR/EigenChris-geodesic-5.jpg
        - Get tangent planes to the surface using partial
          derivatives
            - $MY_IMAGES/physics/GR/EigenChris-geodesic-6.jpg
        - and get tangent vectors along the curve parameterised
          by `λ`
            - $MY_IMAGES/physics/GR/EigenChris-geodesic-7.jpg
    - Get acceleration vector from formula for velocity vector
        - $MY_IMAGES/physics/GR/EigenChris-geodesic-8.jpg
        - $MY_IMAGES/physics/GR/EigenChris-geodesic-9.jpg
        - $MY_IMAGES/physics/GR/EigenChris-geodesic-10.jpg
        - $MY_IMAGES/physics/GR/EigenChris-geodesic-11.jpg
    - After a tidy-up of the formula, we need to figure out what
      part is TANGENT to the velocity vector and what part isn't
        - $MY_IMAGES/physics/GR/EigenChris-geodesic-12.jpg
    - Now the whole concentration shifts to the `2nd derivatives`
      at the end of the formula
        - $MY_IMAGES/physics/GR/EigenChris-geodesic-13.jpg
        - these 2nd derivatives are actually a 3D vector in a 3D
          space, but rather than using `x,y,z` we'll build the 3D
          space as a tangent space to the curve and the 3 basis
          vectors will be the normal, and the two tangent vectors
          to the curve
        - So the three components to this acceleration vector are
          unknown, but let's call them `Γ^1`, `Γ^2`, `L`
            - $MY_IMAGES/physics/GR/EigenChris-geodesic-14.jpg
            - $MY_IMAGES/physics/GR/EigenChris-geodesic-15.jpg
            - $MY_IMAGES/physics/GR/EigenChris-geodesic-16.jpg
        - and the _purple arrow_ is actually the acceleration
          vector, built out of 3 basis vectors, with
          `Christoffel` and `L` components
            - $MY_IMAGES/physics/GR/EigenChris-geodesic-17.jpg
        - Now solve for the Christofel symbols by dotting with
          the tangent vectors
        - But the dot product of the tangent vectors is actually
          the metric tensor!
