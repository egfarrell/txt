- QUESTION: "energy & momentum → 4-momentum" 
    - is that more `SR` or `GR` that created that?


- That’s squarely `SR`:
    * In Newtonian mechanics, energy and momentum are separate
      conserved quantities, but they live in different
      mathematical worlds. Momentum is a 3-vector p = m v. Energy
      is a scalar (kinetic + potential). There’s no single
      unified object that contains both.

    * In special relativity, Einstein rewrote the kinematics so
      that energy and momentum are components of the same
      4-vector:

    - `P^mu = (E/c, p_x, p_y, p_z)`
    - The conservation law is then simple: total 4-momentum before = total 4-momentum after, in any inertial frame.
    - This also gives the beautiful invariant relation:
        - E^2 - (pc)^2 = (mc^2)^2

  which ties together mass, energy, and momentum in a single equation.

* GR builds on that structure, but it doesn’t *create* the
  unification. 
    - In GR, energy-momentum is packaged into the stress-energy
      tensor `T_{mu nu}`, which acts as the "source" for
      spacetime curvature in Einstein’s field equations. 
    - So GR takes the SR concept of unified 4-momentum and
      generalizes it to curved spacetime and continuous
      matter/fields.

So:
    * SR unified energy and momentum.
    * GR elevated that 4-momentum into the bigger framework of
      spacetime geometry.

# More info:

### Einstein’s June 1905 SR paper
    - ("On the Electrodynamics of Moving Bodies")
    - This is where he lays out SR. Space and time become
      spacetime, simultaneity is relative, Lorentz
      transformations are the law of the land. 
    - But in that paper, he didn’t yet write down `E = mc^2`.
      Energy and momentum aren’t explicitly unified into
      a 4-vector there.

### Einstein’s September 1905 follow-up note
    - ("Does the Inertia of a Body Depend Upon Its Energy
      Content?")
    - This is the short, famous one where he derives that if
      a body emits energy L in the form of radiation, its mass
      decreases by L/c^2. That’s the germ of E = mc^2. Still, he
      didn’t yet have the modern formalism of 4-vectors.

- The full unification into the 4-momentum relation 
    - `E^2 (pc)^2 = (mc^2)^2`
    - This came later, in the years after Minkowski’s spacetime
      formalism (1908) and the development of relativistic
      mechanics by people like Planck, Lewis & Tolman. 
    - The invariant relation really crystallized when physicists
      started using 4-vectors consistently.

So:
    - `E = mc^2` (rest energy) was Einstein’s September 1905
      insight.
    - The more general relation `E^2 - (pc)^2 = (mc^2)^2` is the
      later, fully fleshed-out relativistic mechanics, but it’s
      entirely implied by the structure of SR.
    - In other words: the seed was Einstein’s follow-up 1905
      note, but the elegant 4-vector form was a collaborative
      unfolding over the next decade.

- That equation, by the way, is the “Pythagorean theorem of
  relativity”:
    - energy is the “time-like leg,”
    - momentum is the “space-like leg,” and
    - rest mass energy is the invariant “hypotenuse.”

- It’s the geometric heart of why SR unifies them.
