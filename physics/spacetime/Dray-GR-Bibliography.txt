# BIBLIOGRAPHY:

### GENERAL RELATIVITY

    - Exploring Black Holes
        - Edwin Taylor and John Wheeler, (2000)
        - $MY_BOOKS/Physics/Big4/wheeler-black-holes-intro-GR.pdf
        - $MY_BOOKS/Physics/Big4/wheeler-spacetime-physics-2e.pdf
        - Elementary introduction to the relativity of black
          holes, using line elements. Not easy to read, but worth
          it.


    - Gravity
        - James B. Hartle, 2003.
        - $MY_BOOKS/Physics/Spacetime/GR-Basic-Hartle.pdf
        - An "examples first" introduction to GR, discussing
          applications of Einstein's equations before presenting
          the mathematics behind the equations.

     - Introducing Einstein's Relativity, (1991)
        - Ray d'Inverno
        - $MY_BOOKS/Physics/Spacetime/GR-dInverno.pdf
        - An excellent introduction to GR, which also covers some
          topics not usually seen in introductory texts.

     - Relativity Demystified, (2006)
        - David McMahon
        - An abridged treatment of relativity, containing
          a remarkably complete collection of formulas and
          topics, without much derivation. A useful reference.

     - Spacetime and Geometry: An Intro to GR, (2004)
        - Sean Carroll
        - $MY_BOOKS/Physics/Spacetime/GR-Carroll-Summary.pdf
        - $MY_BOOKS/Physics/Spacetime/GR-Carroll.pdf
        - An excellent traditional but somewhat sophisticated
          introduction to GR.

     - A First Course in General Relativity, (1985)
        - Bernard F. Schutz
        - A good, easy introduction to the basics of both tensors
          and general relativity.

    - Gravitation, (1973)
        - $MY_BOOKS/Physics/Big4/1973-Gravitation-MTW.pdf
        - Misner, Thorne, and Wheeler
        - The physicist's bible of GR.
          Exhaustively complete.

     - General Relativity, (1984)
        - Robert M. Wald
        - $MY_BOOKS/Physics/Spacetime/GR-Wald-Basic.pdf
        - $MY_BOOKS/Physics/Spacetime/GR-Wald.pdf
        - The best available introduction to GR for advanced
          students, but uses sophisticated notation (which has
          become the standard for researchers in the field).

     - General Relativity for Mathematicians, (1977)
        - Rainer K. Sachs
        - A very pure mathematical treatment of general
          relativity. Requires a strong background in
          differential geometry.


### DIFFERENTIAL FORMS

    - Differential Forms with Applications Physical Sciences
        - Harley Flanders
        - (1963. Dover 1989)
        - Excellent but somewhat dated intro to differential
          forms; only covers the positive definite case.

    - Tensor Analysis on Manifolds
        - R. Bishop and S. Goldberg
        - 1968; Dover 1980.
        - Somewhat more sophisticated; a good reference for tensors.

    - Tensors and Manifolds
        - Robert H. Wasserman
        - 1992 (2nd edition: 2009).
        - A more readable version of Bishop and Goldberg; but not
          as cheap.

    - David Bachman
        - A Geometric Approach to Differential Forms
        - 2006.
        - Very readable math text.

    - Tensors Differential Forms and Variational Principles
        - David Lovelock and Hanno Rund
        - 1975; Dover 1989.
        - Another inexpensive Dover reprint covering similar
          material from a slightly different point of view.

    - Vector Analysis (Chapter 10 only)
        - Hwei Hsu
        - 1969.
        - A sort-of Schaum outline for differential forms.

    - Div Grad Curl and All That
        - H. M. Schey
        - 1973 (4e: 2005)
        - Excellent informal introduction to the geometry of
          vector calculus.

    - Geometrical Vectors
        - Gabriel Weinreich
        - 1998.
        - A nice (if idiosyncratic) geometrical description of
          differ- ential forms without ever using those words.

    - Elementary Differential Geometry
        - Barrett O'Neill
        - 1966 (2nd edition: 1997).
        - Standard fairly readable introduction to differential
          geometry in ordinary Euclidean 3-space. Uses
          differential forms extensively.

    - Elements of Differential Geometry
        - Richard S. Millman and George D. Parker
        - 1977 (2nd edition: 1997).
        - Good undergraduate text covering similar material to
          the above. Does not use differential forms.

    - Semi-Riemannian Geometry
        - Barrett O'Neill
        - 1983.
        - Graduate math text. The best available treatment of
          differential geometry without the usual assumption that
          the metric is positive definite.

    - Intro to Differentiable Manifolds and Riemannian Geometry
        - William M. Boothby
        - 1986.
        - Fairly readable graduate math text. Good but brief
          treatment of differential forms and integration but
          emphasis is on Lie groups.

    - Riemannian Geometry,
        - do Carmo, 1992
        - Quite readable math text, but no discussion of
          differential forms.

    - A Comprehensive Introduction to Differential Geometry
        - Michael Spivak, 5 Vols, (2nd edition: 1979).
        - Everything you ever wanted to know, and then some, but
          not easy to read.

    - Gravitation
        - Misner, Thorne, and Wheeler, 1973.
        - Don't underestimate this classic!
        - Chapter 4 has a nice introduction to differential
          forms, including great pictures and a discussion of
          electromagnetism.
        - Part III also discusses the differential geometry
          needed for relativity.

    - An Introduction to Spinors and Geometry
        - with Applications to Physics
        - Benn and Tucker, 1988.
        - A very pure mathematical treatment of spinors in the
          language of differential forms.
