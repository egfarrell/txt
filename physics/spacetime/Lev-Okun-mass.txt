### SUMMARY

### 1. Central Thesis: Abandoning Archaic Terms
    - Okun argues "relativistic mass" and "rest mass" are
      misnomers. They create confusion & block the understanding
      of SR. He advocates abandoning these terms.

### 2. The One, True Mass (The Invariant Mass)
    - The paper asserts there is only one concept of mass in
      modern physics, which is the INVARIANT MASS, denoted simply
      by `m`.

    *   Definition: Mass (m) is a Lorentz-invariant scalar
        quantity. This means its value does not change regardless
        of the inertial reference frame or the body's velocity.
    *   Fundamental Relation: The invariant mass is defined by
        the most fundamental equation of relativistic mechanics:
        `m^2*c^4 = E^2 - p^2*c^2` (where `E` is the total energy,
        `p` is the momentum, and `c` is the speed of light).
    *   The "Rest" Property:
            - Mass is a measure of the energy "sleeping" in the
              particle at rest, which is the particle's rest
              energy (`E_0`).

### 3. Correct Mass-Energy Equivalence
    - Okun maintains that the "true" Einstein equation for
      a particle with mass is the relation between mass (`m`) and
      rest energy (`E_0`):
        - `E_0 = m*c^2`
    *   This relation shows that mass is equivalent to the rest
        energy of a body divided by c-squared.
    *   He argues AGAINST the equation `E = m*c^2`, where 'E' is
        the total energy and 'm' is interpreted as
        velocity-dependent mass (the so-called "relativistic
        mass").
            - This is because _TOTAL ENERGY_ (`E`) is dependent
              on the reference frame, and calling `E/c^2` the
              "mass" leads to the confusing and non-standard idea
              that mass changes with velocity.

### 4. Critique of Misnomers
    * "Rest Mass" (`m_0`): This term is redundant. The mass of
      a body *does not* change when it is in motion, so the
      subscript '0' (for rest) is superfluous. The invariant mass
      'm' is simply the mass.
    * "Relativistic Mass" (`m_rel` or `m(v)`):
        - This quantity, defined as E/c^2 (Total Energy / c^2),
          is a measure of inertia only in the non-relativistic
          limit.
        - It is *not* a Lorentz invariant, and it is
          a velocity-dependent component of a four-vector, not
          a fundamental property of the particle.
        - The concept is largely absent from serious monographs
          on theoretical physics, especially high-energy physics.

### 5. Other Relativistic Properties of Mass
    * Non-Additivity:
        - In relativity, the mass of a composite body is not
          equal to the sum of the masses of its constituents. The
          mass of a system of particles depends on their energies
          and momenta. For instance, two massless photons can
          form a system with non-zero mass.
    * Conservation:
        - The mass (invariant mass) of an isolated system of
          bodies is conserved (does not change with time).
    * Massless Particles (e.g., Photons):
        - A vanishing mass (m=0) in the invariant mass framework
          means the "body" must always move at the speed of
          light.


- Okun's rejection of "relativistic mass" and "rest mass," is the
  _overwhelming consensus_ among theoretical physicists,
  high-energy physicists, and experts in modern relativity.


### INSTANCES OF DISAGREEMENT / CRITICISM OF OKUN'S VIEW

- While Okun's position is standard in professional physics, it
  has faced criticism mainly from pedagogical utility and the
  definition of inertia.

### 1. Pedagogical Usefulness (Teaching)
    - The most common defense of "relativistic mass" (`M = E/c^2`) is
      that it simplifies the introduction of relativity for students.

    * Preserving Newtonian Formulas:
            - By defining a velocity-dependent mass `M`,
              a student can maintain a form of the familiar
              Newtonian equations:
            *   Momentum: `p = M * v`
            *   Energy:   `E = M * c^2`
        * This approach may ease the conceptual transition, as
          students feel they are simply replacing a constant mass
          with a variable mass in a known framework.
            - The terms "rest mass" (`m_0`) and "relativistic
              mass" (`M`) are retained to distinguish between the
              invariant and velocity-dependent values.

### 2. "Relativistic Mass" as Physical Quantity
    - Some critics argue that the concept of "relativistic mass"
      is a necessary and physically meaningful quantity that
      reflects experimental facts.

    * Reflecting Inertia/Resistance:
        - It is an experimentally verified fact that a particle's
          resistance to acceleration (inertia) increases as its
          velocity increases.
        - Critics argue that since mass is traditionally
          a measure of this resistance, the concept of
          "relativistic mass" is required to reflect this
          experimental evidence.
    * Time Component of a Four-Vector:
        - The "relativistic mass" is simply the total energy
          (`E`) divided by `c^2`.
        - Since total energy is the time component of the
          fundamental four-momentum vector `(E/c, p_x, p_y,
          p_z)`, rejecting `E/c^2` is seen by some as
          inconsistent with accepting other frame-dependent
          components (like coordinate time `\Delta t`, the time
          component of the displacement four-vector).
        - The argument is that this frame-dependent quantity is
          a real feature of spacetime, even if it is not an
          invariant scalar.

### 3. Criticism of Okun's Rhetoric

    - A few critics have taken issue with Okun's strong rhetoric
      (such as calling "relativistic mass" a "virus"), arguing
      that a formal, defined concept should not be dismissed as
      "inconsistent" or "harmful" when it merely represents
      a variable state definition.

### IS OKUN CORRECT?
    - (The Professional View)
    - YES. Okun's view is the correct and standard view in modern
      professional physics.
    - Okun's perspective represents the standard terminology and
      conceptual framework used by physicists working in particle
      physics, quantum field theory, and advanced relativity
      theory.

    * Why Okun is Correct (and why "Relativistic Mass" is
      Rejected):
        * Inconsistent Inertia:
            - The main argument against "relativistic mass" (`M`)
              is that it is not a consistent measure of inertia.
              The classical formula for force, `F = M * a`, is
              only valid when the force (`F`) is parallel to the
              velocity (`v`).
            - If the force is perpendicular to the velocity
              (e.g., an electron circling in a magnetic field),
              the particle's resistance to acceleration is
              a different value, meaning a single, universal
              velocity-dependent "relativistic mass" does not
              exist to describe inertia IN ALL DIRECTIONS.
        * Lorentz Invariance:
            - Physics seeks to describe nature using quantities
              that are independent of the observer
              (Lorentz-invariant scalars).
            - The invariant mass (`m`) is a Lorentz scalar,
              a fundamental property of the particle.
            - The "relativistic mass" (`M`) is not an invariant;
              it is frame-dependent, making it a much less
              fundamental and clumsier concept to use.
        * Einstein's Preference:
            - As Okun points out, Einstein himself preferred the
              terminology of a single, invariant mass (which he
              initially called "rest mass" `m_0`) and discouraged
              the use of a velocity-dependent mass `M`.
            - In a 1948 letter, Einstein stated, "It is not good
              to introduce the concept of the mass
                - `M = m/(1 v^2/c^2)^1/2`
                - of a moving body for which no clear definition
                  can be given."

- In summary, the term mass (`m`) is reserved for the single,
  invariant scalar (formerly "rest mass" `m_0`), while the
  inertial and energetic properties that *do* vary with velocity
  are collectively and more accurately described by the
  Energy-Momentum Four-Vector `(E, p)`, of which total energy
  (`E`) and momentum (`p`) are components.
    - The quantity `E/c^2` (the rejected "relativistic mass") is
      just a measure of energy, not a fundamental property of the
      particle.
