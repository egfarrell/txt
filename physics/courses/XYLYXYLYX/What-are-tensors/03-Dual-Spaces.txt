### The lecturer's bracket notation:
    - this is different to QM
    - `< map, vector >` 


### Linearity / re-scaling vectors
    - it's enough for a map to re-scale the basis vectors. 
    - Then by the property of linearity, the components can be
      multiplied by the re-scaled vectors.

- where can maps go if you don't want to create another vector
  space?

- well you can map it back onto yourself!
	- $MY_IMAGES/physics/what-are-tensors/dual-maps.jpg

- more specifically, you can map it back onto the "bin" of real
  numbers in your vector space

- and... the real numbers actually are a vector space! 
- a 1D space!

### A single vector space
    - But it seems, for a `map` we always need some other vector
      space.
    - but what if we want to get away from that? 
    - What if we want maps but just "one" vector space

### mathematical "trick"! 
    - The dual space is the set of ALL POSSIBLE MAPS into the
      reals... 
    - and this dual space is actually a vector space
    - and I didn't really have to construct anything else... 
    - by definiing my vector space `V` I get a dual space `V*`
      for free...


### An auto-created space: 
    - I can never just create a single vector space on it's own.
    - Every time you create a vector space, you automatically
      created a dual space, even without adding anything else.


### Dual vector space
    - the elements of this space are FUNCTIONS or MAPPINGS!
    - because the dual is the set of all mappings that bring
      a vector in `V` into the reals...
