- lets choose one set of basis vectors for space `M`. There's an
  infinite choice...

- we'll call all our basis vectors `e` and to distinguish each
  one, we give it a subscript


- Use `A` and `e` and Einstein summation convention
	- $MY_IMAGES/physics/what-are-tensors/basic-notation.jpg

note: 
    - superscript for components
    - subscript   for basis vectors

- interesting: 
    - why superscript / subscript combination? 
    - Does it have to be that way? NO! actually linear algebra
      books often have: `A_μ e_μ` ie both subscripts!
        - $MY_IMAGES/physics/what-are-tensors/linear-algebra-notation.jpg

### mapping basis vectors between spaces 
    - once we know the mapping for the basis vectors, we can map
      any vector between 2 spaces
    - why? because the maps are _linear_ 
        - $MY_IMAGES/physics/what-are-tensors/linear-map-between-2-spaces.jpg

    and now we can map any arbitrary vector between `V` and `W`
