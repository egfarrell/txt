        - Samuel Cramer No. The system does NOT have to be
          orthogonal. The key strength of this formalism is that
          it works for an absolutely arbitrary coordinate
          transformation (uh..... with the condition that the
          transformation functions x’(x) be smooth and
          invertible) which will generally NOT be orthogonal. The
          matrices generated by the \partial x’/ \partial x terms
          (and their inverses) will generally NOT be diagonal!
          I’m glad you are thinking about this. 
        - To see how right you are, study the simple
          transformation from flat space time to a rotating
          coordinate system. Both are flat Minkowski spacetimes
          and represent flat space, but the transformation matrix
          in non-diagonal. 
        - As another example, in a Kerr-space time of a rotating
          black hole, there are several coordinate systems that
          can be used to study the rotating black hole. The
          transformation between these coordinate systems isn’t
          diagonal either. 
        - On the other hand, the transformation between the
          various coordinated systems used to study
          a non-rotating black hole are, usually, diagonal.
