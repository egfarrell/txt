### remember:
    - the COMPONENTS    can be covariant or contravariant
    - the BASIS VECTORS can be covariant or contravariant
    - add a single tensor transformation uses a mixture of both 

    - interesting: if we call a tensor "covariant" or
      "contravariant" it referes to the component part of the
      tensor, rather than the basis vectors

    - philosophical point: we name things by how they
      _transform_. Eg particles are named/grouped by how the
      transform under Lorentz/Poincare transformations

### origin of covariant and contra: 
    - well we started off with our underlying vector space and we
      want to change our basis vectors. And then if we do the
      same in the dual space, we are using an inverse matrix, so
      that is "contra" the way we do it in the original
      underlying vector space.

ah very important: 
    - use the correct "tool" for the correct object. You use
      a matrix to transform the basis vectors, but you use the
      inverse matrix to transform the components...
