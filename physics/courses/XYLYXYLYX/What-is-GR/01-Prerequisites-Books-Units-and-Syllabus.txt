### Units
    - Natural units (important for SR and field theory)
    - Geometrized units (important for GR)
        - Set `c = 1` => [L] = [T]
        - Set `G = 1` => [L] = [M]
        - Implication: `[L] = [M] = [T]`
        - Implication: Length, Mass and Time are all measured in
          the same units! We'll measure all three dimensions in
          _metres_!!
        - Elapsed time will be measured in metres 
        - Mass of a star measured in metres too 
            - $MY_IMAGES/physics/What-is-GR/01-geometrized-units.jpg
        - ASIDE: we're setting `c=1` because we know that's
          fundamental for all observers. 
            - So we're going to make `c` the fundamental
              constant. So we're going to re-jig all our units. 
        - ASIDE: 
            - where does the  `8π` come from in Einsteins
              equation. Well it comes from the Newtonian Limit.
              You've got the gravitational potention, Poissons
              equation and we integrate over spherical volumes...

### Metric Tensor
    - `g_νμ` is a [0,2] tensor field on a manifold
    - Establishes an inner product at every point in spacetime
      for comparing vectors in the tangent spaces...

### Matter tensor
    - Throw-away comment: `T_νμ` : anything that's not a vacuum
      is essentially matter(!)


### Electron Charge (for SR)
    - (note: `G=1` doesn't apply here... it's SR without gravity)
    - we'll set `e` the unit of electron charge `= 1` too...
    - This sets off another cascade of unit changes, just like
      when we set `c=1`
    - and mass ends up being measured in electron volts 
    - momentum gets measured in mass
    - energy gets measured in mass

### Syllabus
    - PATH 1: Use `T_νμ` (Stress-Energy) to calculate `G_νμ`
      (Einstein tensor) and from that calculate the metric.

    - PATH 2: Assume we know the metric. 
        - How does this control matter? What are the geodesics?
        - We do this with test particles that won't actually
          change the spacetime. So no backreaction between
          particle and spacetime.
            - but if you've two NS's orbiting each other, neither
              is a test particle and things get complicated and
              non-linear 
            - note: for basic cases we're going to assume the
              metric stays the same. We're going to remove the
              time dependence from the 4-vectors...
