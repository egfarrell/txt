### Scattering orbits
    - you have high energy and high angular moment
    - you hit the angular moment wall and fly back out to
      infinity 

### flyby orbits
	- $MY_IMAGES/physics/What-is-GR/flyby-orbits.jpg


### - plunging orbits
    - bound plunging orbits
    - unbound plunging orbits
    - if you're energy is too great, bigger than the potential
      curve spike, you get pulled straight into the central body.
      This can't happen in classical physics.
    - $MY_IMAGES/physics/What-is-GR/high-energy-pulled-in.jpg

### - bound orbits
    - they have an inner turning point, and an outer turning point 
    - $MY_IMAGES/physics/What-is-GR/bound-orbits.jpg
    - interesting: to get into a bound orbit, you have to fly in,
      and then _drop_ your kinetic energy, to fall into the
      potential hollow... (but you must retain the _same_ angular
      momentum!)
    - because if you're far away you'll have too much energy to
      naturally be in a bound orbit
