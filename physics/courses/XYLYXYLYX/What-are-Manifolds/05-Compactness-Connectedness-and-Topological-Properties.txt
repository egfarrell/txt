### Homeomorphisms

- homeomorphisms only make sense between topological _spaces_
	- $MY_IMAGES/physics/what-are-manifolds/homeomorphism.jpg

- open sets are mapped to open sets, in both directions... 

- the behaviour of the inverse function `f^-1` will tell us if
  the function `f` is continuous

- a continuous function continuous function maps open sets to
  open sets

- topology is the search and study of things that are invariant
  between homeomorphic spaces

- only need to find _ONE_ function between the two spaces that
  satisfy the requirements of homeomorphism, for two spaces to be
  homeomorphic

- how to prove two spaces arent homeomorphic?
- find a topological property (eg "2nd countable") that one space
  has, that the other space doesn't have

- there are lots of different versions of separability, and
  different versions of compactness too...

- The homeomorphic functions between spaces are just _mutating_
  open sets between spaces, kind of taking one open set in
  a space and _deforming_ it into another open set in a different
  space. 
    - One topology is being "morphed" into another set.
    - and this is the foundation of the point-set topology fact
      that a donut and a coffee cup are topologically equivalent.
    - $MY_IMAGES/physics/what-are-manifolds/topological-donut-coffee-cup.jpg
    - importance of "one-to-one" and "onto": every point on the
      donut has to have a point on the coffee cup, and vice versa


- homomorphism is the weakest notion that _connects_ two sets.
  There's no notion of geometry in here... (no metric, sense of

- increasing levels of specificity (structure?) start growing the deformations
  "shape") allowed

### Compactness
    - open cover
    - infinite cover 
        - infinite sub-cover 
        - finite sub-cover 

    - to be compact you need an infinite cover that has _NO_ finite
      subcover


- path connected
    - confinuous function between x and y 
    - if any `x` and any `y` in a space is path-connected, then
      the space itself is connected

    - but inverse not true: you can have a connected space that
      is NOT path-connected. Legendary example: function `sin(1/x)`

### homotopy
    - how paths can be manipulated (different than homology)
    - two spaces, one with a hole in the middle
    - in the space with a hole, there's path-connections that
      join two points, but the path "loops around" the hole a few
      times
    - if every path can be continuously deformed into another
      path, the sapce is homotopic 
    - simply connected: if you are connected and you don't have
      this problem of different homotopies in the space, they
      you're simply connected

### homology
    - how many times you can cut a space, and still have the
      points simply connected?
