# Different topologies of the line

### 1. usual (standard)

### 2. nested interval line  
        - same set, but different topology
        - makes it profoundly different when you treat it
          mathematically 

### 3. closed interval topology
    - `[-1, 1]`
    - base: two half-open intervals 
        - [-1, a] a > 0
        - [b , 1] b > 0
    - interesting: 
        - you can make open sets from closed or half-open intervals!


### 4. co-finite topology
    - an interval, where we omit some points 

### 5. Lower-limit topology
    - famous


There's a couple of topologies of the plane too...



### T_0 Separability:
    - weakest type
    - can put at least _one_ of two points in an open set

### Euclidean topology
    - I've two points...
    - ie I want an open set, that contains one of the points and
      not the other
    - ie can I find open sets that distinguish between these
      points 
	- $MY_IMAGES/physics/what-are-manifolds/two-separate-points.jpg
    - the Euclidean topology is `T_0`, use open intervals to
      contain one point, and not the other

### nested interval topology... 
        - interval `1 - 1/n`
        - can separate the red and green point in some cases...
        - can define an open set that just contains the red one
        - $MY_IMAGES/physics/what-are-manifolds/t0_separability_nested_interval.jpg
        - but if you have two points less than 1/2... you can't
          separate them!! 
        - so nested interval is not `T_0` 


### closed interval topology
        - can distinguish two points either side of zero
        - $MY_IMAGES/physics/what-are-manifolds/t0_separability_closed_interval.jpg
        - $MY_IMAGES/physics/what-are-manifolds/t0_separability_closed_interval-02.jpg
        - yes it is `T_0` 


### T_1 Separability: (Frechet)
    - put each point in it's own open set
    - Euclidean space is BOTH `T_0` and `T_1` 
    - Co-finite topology is BOTH `T_0` and `T_1` as well


### T_2 Separability (Hausdorff): 
    - you can put any two points in their own open sets
    - ... but the open sets must be disjoint 
	- $MY_IMAGES/physics/what-are-manifolds/T2_seperability.jpg
    - Co-finite topology is `T_1` but not `T_2` 
    - Euclidean is `T_2` as well 

### Types of Hausdorff:
    - you replace 'points' with _closed sets_ and you find
      open sets that separate closed sets from each other
    - regular
    - normal
	- $MY_IMAGES/physics/what-are-manifolds/hausdorff-regular-normal.jpg

### Slotted Disk topology
    - you split a plane into two halves
    - allow open sets on both sides but they open sets can't
      touch the centre line 
    - on the centre line, create loads of a funny open sets, each one only touches
      only one point on the line 
    - you end up with a discrete topology on the centre line 
	- $MY_IMAGES/physics/what-are-manifolds/slotted_disk.jpg

### Subspace Topology
    - `L` is a (subspace) line in the Euclidean plane `ℝ^2`
    - ie `L` is a subset of the Euclidean topology
    - `L` inherits its own topology. The intersection of every
      open set on the plane with `L` will induce a topology on
      `L`. (it will induce the standard open Euclidean topology)
    - Thats a very common property... you cut out a subspace from
      a larger space, and you accept the induced topology on the
      subspace with the intersection of all the open sets of the
      larger space. 

### Cartesian Product Topology
    - if you've two different sets, each with a topology, and you
      cross them... then the new topology is a cross of the two
      individual topologies
	- $MY_IMAGES/physics/what-are-manifolds/cartesian-topology.jpg
