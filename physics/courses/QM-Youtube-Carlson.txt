### When does QM apply?
    - when things are of order `ħ` (10^-34)
        - when angular momentum `L ~ ħ`
        - when uncertanties `∆p ∆x ~ ħ`
        - when uncertanties `∆E ∆t ~ ħ`
        - when action `S ~ ħ`   <--- most fundamental
    - E.g. a speck of dust in a breeze doesn't need QM
        - $MY_IMAGES/physics/Carlson/dont-use-QM-dust-breeze.jpg

### Key concepts:
    - Wavefunction: `Ψ (x, y, z, t)`
        - complex function: when evaluated can have real and
          imaginary parts
        - represents the state of a system
        - gives probabilities

    - Operators
        - they connect `Ψ` to observable quantities
        - usually we use "hats" for operators:  `ã, ū, ṽ`
        - operators act on `Ψ`: 
            - write the operator on the left: `x-hat Ψ`


### Delta functions
    - delta functions make integrals go away!
    - just pick out function value at `f(0)`
        - `∫ δ(x) f(x) dx = f(0)`
    - translated delta function picks value at `f(a)`
        - `∫ δ(x-a) f(x) dx = f(a)`
    - delta function ... of a function
        - `∫ δ(g(x)) f(x) dx = ...`
        - pulls out f(x) value where g(x) = 0
        - the slope of g(x) as it crosses the x-axis is what
          deterimines the effective width of the δ function
        - `δ(g(x)) = ∑ δ(x - x_i)  / | g'(x_i) |`
        - where `x_i` are the zeros of g(x)
        - Steeper Slope `g'(x_i)`:
            - smaller "window" crossing the x-axis, smaller
              weighting and contribution to `δ(g(x))`
        - Shallower Slope `g'(x_i)`:
            - larger "window" crossing the x-axis, larger
              weighting
        - also subtle point:
            - this whole `δ(g(x))` formula is really expressing
              `g(x)` as a SERIES of SHIFTED delta functions
            - See this diagram:
                - $MY_IMAGES/physics/Carlson/delta-gx-stuff.jpg
            - and then the actual integral becomes a summation of
              3 things and you don't have to do any "integration"


### Kets
    - "ket a" `|a>` is a physical state of the system.
        - A ket is more general than representing it as
          a wavefunction in cartesian coords `Ψ` or `Φ` (momentum
          space), or an infinite sum of stationary states, or
          solutions to the Schrodinger eqn. 
        - Those are all different representations of the same
          physical state... and that physical state is what we
          mean by "ket a" `|a>`
            - $MY_IMAGES/physics/Carlson/qm-linear-algebra-link.jpg


### Inner Product
    - Inner products are similar to the "dot product" but
      different as well because we're working with complex
      conjugates
    - `<a | b> = <b | a>*`  if you change the order of the inner
      product you have to take the complex conjugate
    - Don't think of the inner product as just components that
      you're dotting togeter and adding up. 
        - Divorce your thinking from the dot product. 
        - Because we don't neccessarily have components we want
          to consider, we have physical states. 
        - The mathematics of Linear Algebra can tell us about the
          properties of these states and how they interact
          without expressing them in any `basis`.
    - Of course you can do a lot of things when you do express
      them in a basis as well, but the vectors themselves can
      live without the basis.

### Norm
    - Having defined an inner product, it's useful to define
      a norm. (our definition of "length")
    - `|| α || = sqrt(<α|α>)`
    - remember: `<α|α>` will always give a REAL number
