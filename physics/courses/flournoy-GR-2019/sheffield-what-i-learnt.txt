### EM "setup"
    - We use the EM equations to say how SOURCES create -> FIELDS
        - where "sources" are charge distributions `ρ` and
          currents `j`
        - I plug it in everywhere I see it [here] and I solve
          this set of differential equations, and it tells me
          what electric and magnetic fields I get.
      - ie you are given the `ρ`s and `j`s and you solve for `E`
        and `B`
    - but this doesn't tell me what an electron does in an
      electric field! 
    - You have to use the Lorentz force law with some mechanical
      system e.g. Newtons Laws
      - note: you can use Newtons laws to describe an electron if
        its not moving fast. The electric field itself is always
        relativistic.
    - so Lorentz + Newton tells us how the test particles respond
      to the FIELDS created by the SOURCES.
    - It's a type of a "background" <-> "test particle" SPLIT.
        - We assume the fields are very large or fixed. (e.g.
          Parallel plate capacitor)
        - and we ignore the electric field that the test particle
          itself creates
        - ... we dont take the "back reaction" into account
    - So the "background/particle" split is a useful way of doing
      physics. 
    - But for example, if I just take two electrons and put them
      together, then in that case I can't just ignore one and
      focus on the other. I've got a two body problem then.
        - cf. $MY_NOTES/physics/CM/shaggy-steed.txt
    - But the "background/particle" split allows us to reduce
      a lot of things to a one body problems.

### Now the "GR" setup
    - (which is analogous to the EM setup)
    - For GR we're going to have _2 equations_ which are going to
      be very important
    - EINSTEINS EQUATION 
        - is going to essentially replace the top half of
          Maxwells Equations
        - Einsteins equation tells us that sources (energy and
          momentum) will create curved spacetimes
        - e.g. im going to stick a planet here, I'm going to
          stick a gas of relativistic particles. 
        - That's going to be in my space. And Im going to use
          that source to solve for the unknown which is
          "space-time". 
        - Now thats half of it...but we're also going to have
          a Bianchi identity that plays into that as well. (thats
          actually part of finding the spacetime)
    - Now... I have a curved spacetime and I want to figure out
      what a satellite is going to do in that geometry. 
    - What is its motion? 
        - Now in EM at this stage, we go to the Lorentz force law
          and Newtons laws. 
        - (ie for EM, 
            - take the field we found from the sources, 
            - plug it into Lorentz to find the force, 
            - plug it into Newton to find the _motion_)
    - But for GR, we're going to take our curved spacetime
      geometry and find the SHORTEST DISTANCE between 2 points...
      and thats the path an object will take as it moves under
      the influence of gravity. 
    - This is going to be encapsulated in a "geodesic equation"
