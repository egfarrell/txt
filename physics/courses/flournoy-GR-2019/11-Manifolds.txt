### Statement of SR
    - The laws of physics take the same form (invariant) under
      transformations (boosts) connecting intertial frames and
      a few extra assumptions: 
        - space is isotropic and spactime is homogeneous.
    - homogeneous: this means things don't look any different
      when we translate in space and translate in time
    - boosts and spatial isotropy imply spactime isotropy
      - because boosts are just a rotation in a space and time
        axis. The set of symmetries we consider in SR is the
        Poincare group `ISO(1,3) with semi-direct product T^4
        translations`

### Statement of GR
    - The laws of physics are invariant under diffeomorphisms of
      spacetime `(1)`
    - and
    - the connection (or guage field) facilitating this
      invariance is allowed to be dynamical via the introduction
      of an invariant "field strength". `(2)`

- SR is a statment about symmetries. GR is also a statement about
  symmetries but it also says you have a little bit more to do to
  make these good symmetries.

- It turns out that if we defined GR just using statement `(1)`
  we'd come to a theory which describes motion on curved spaces
  ie gravitational influences. 
    - That is, we'd have the tools to say what it looks like for
      a satellite to be moving in the curved geometry created by
      the earth. (Thats not very different from SR which gives us
      the tools to describe motion in a flat geometry)

- But part `(2)` adds something tremendously different and new
  over and above SR. It tells us how curvature arises from
  sources.
