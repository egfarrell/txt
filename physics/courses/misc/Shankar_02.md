### Lecture 04

    ### Picture of 3 charges and superposition at a point p,
        - where a charge of 1 Colomb(?) resides
        - Electric field is defined everywhere,
        - except possibly on the charges themselves where its
          INFINITE. (renormalisation!)

    ### Strength of electric (force) field:
        - Defined by the density of field lines. But not
          a density as in mass per unit volume. But an "area
          density". Take a 1 square meter frame, hold it there,
          and the number of perpendicular field lines through the
          frame gives the density which gives the field strength.

    ### For a single electric charge,
        the field falls off as 1/r^2

    ### For a dipole,
        the field falls off as 1/r^3

    ### Dipole moment
        = 2qa
        (where a is distance between the two charges)

    ### Torque = p X E
        (dipole moment cross electric field) = -pEsin(theta)
        Potential = p.E = -pEcos(theta)

    ### Basically differentiate the Potential
        to get the Torque because
            `Force = -dU/dx`

            electric field force will point straight "up"
            Because of left/right symmetry.
