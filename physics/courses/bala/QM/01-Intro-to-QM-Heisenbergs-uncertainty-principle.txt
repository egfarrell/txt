### Uncertainty principle:
    - it's not about a failure of measurement, in principle it is
      a property of Nature that _canonically conjugate_ variables
      cannot be simultaneously measured
    - Bala question: Does the `x-component` of position and the
      `y-component` of momentum satisfy an uncertainty principle?
        - No. Because they aren't canonically conjugate to each
          other. 
    - This is all a bit like Hamiltonian Mechanics... the
      classical framework is being translated into QM
        - Assumption: you have a Hamiltonian for the system and
          the Hamiltonain framework and you translate to QM

### Squeezed states
    - The uncertantity in the momentum `∆p` is below the
      uncertainty limit, but `∆x` is great..
        - $MY_IMAGES/physics/QM/Bala-QM/01-Uncertainty-squeezed-states.jpg
    - aside 1:
        - Bala answers that simultaneously is the key point. 
        - You can't measure both conjugate variables to arbitrary
          precision AT THE SAME TIME.
    - aside 2:
        - the states of a system can no longer be represented by
          points in phase space (like the way we had phase
          trajectories in phase diagrams in CM). 
        - We have to replace it with something else which
          respects the uncertainty principle.
    - aside 3:
        - so when you study the Hydrogen atom, you can't say the
          electron has an _orbit_. 
        - You can't give the position and momentum of an
          'orbiting' electron.
    - aside 4:
        - ground state of the _Bohr atom_: electron can't be
          'orbiting' because `L` the angular momentum is `0`
        - But if I calculate the 'mean' distance, most probable
          distance, then this is R_0 the Bohr radius... (i.e.
          it's a statistical and quantum mechanical average)

### Bala Philosophy: 
        - The real fact of the matter is an electron doesn't have
          a position and momentum simultaneously. 
        - An electron is not a point particle in the classical
          sense of the word. _It's some kind of crazy extended
          object_.
        - It's described by a "state of the system", or a `state
          vector`. The state vector is a generalisation of the
          wave function.
        - Deep idea: in QM, the words "and" and "or" have
          different meanings.
            - "and" in ordinary language is always about
              'probabilities' and `multiplication` of
              probabilities
            - "or" is always about the addition of probabilities.
              You have two mutually exclusive possibilities. This
              `or` that to happen. So addition.
            - But QM is a change in the rules of classical
              probability. (Quantum probability)
        - CM is an _approximation_ to QM
        - Student: do you need classical mechanics to have QM?
            - Bala: Deep question. For us humans, it looks like
              we need a classical limit, otherwise the theory
              becomes meaningless.
