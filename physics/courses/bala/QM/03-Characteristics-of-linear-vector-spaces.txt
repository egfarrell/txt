BIG POINT!!!    
    In a Real vector space the dot product of a vector with itself
    produces its length squared. You'll only get the null vector if you
    dot the zero vector with itself. This is a nice property.

How do we retain this property if we move into complex vector space.
ie non zero except if dotting the null vector? 

You can retain it by dotting a vector with its complex conjugate!! So
using dirac notation, in a complex vector space, the bra vectors are
the complex conjugate transposes of the ket vectors.

Another interesting point about dot products.
In real vector spaces `a.b = b.a`
But in general thats not true. (ie in complex vector spaces)
Normally its `a.b = b*.a`

Yet another point. 
(Its all opening up today)

The NORM of a vector is DEFINED as the square root of the dot product
of a vector with itself. Because the dot product of a vector with
itself gives its length squared. (see BIG POINT above!)
