### QM:
    1. Operators on vectors of Hilbert Space
    2. Commutators. `[Pi, Qi] = iħ 1`
    3. State vectors
    4. No direct analog to Hamilton Equation. 
        - Schroedinger Equation. or Heisenberg equations of motion.


- Say you have an expectation value of an operator ... `<A>` with eigenfunctions `Φn`...

- The components `C_n` carry the time dependence. The basis `Φ(n)` is fixed.   

- `< Φn|ψ(t) > = Cn(t)`     gives probability amplitude

- The probability the system is in the state `Φn` at time `t` is
  given by `|Cn(t)|^2`. Thats a (main) postulate of QM!

- `<ψ(t) | ψ(t)> = |Cn(t)|^2` 
    - provided the wavefunction is normalised. 
    - (i.e. provided `< ψ(t) | ψ(t) > = 1`)

- If the wavefunction isn't normalised then divide by a normalisation factor.
- Just like the partition function `Z` in Statistical Mechanics.

- When `A` is in state `Φ_n`, its value is `λn`.
- i.e. A |Φn = λn |Φn⟩
- Then the expectation value, or weighted average is simply `<A> = ∑ |Cn(t)|^2`. 
    - λn

- But after a few tricks you can display the expectation value another way:
    - <A>(t) = <ψ(t) |A| ψ(t)> / <ψ(t) | ψ(t)> 
    - This is a fundamental formula.

- `A` looks like a diagonal matrix element (in some weird basis `Φn`). 
- These matrix elements are expectation values.
