Point of tensors:

If they are equal in one coordinate system, then equal in all other coordinate systems (even if a different numerical value)

However, the derivative of a tensor not neccessarily a tensor.
You could have the derivative of a tensor in cartesian coordinates = 0, but in some curvilinear coordinates the derivative componenents keep changing.

So the derivative of V in the x cordinates DONT transform as a tensor. If they were zero in x coordinates they should be zero in y coordinates too.

so we need covariant differentiation

Gamma makes derivatives = tensors.

Note: ordinary derivative of a scalar IS a tensor... equal in all coordinate systems.

Gamma is a "christofel" symbol or a "connection"!! christofel itself isnt a tensor, but it allows you to have a covariant differentiation of a tensor.

susskind defined a vector in polar coords
theta=0, r=1. The components of the vectors are constant but the vectors themselves arent constant, they change as you move around the plane.

In a flat space you can find coordinates where the metric is the kroneckar delta and the christofel symbol is zero. In a curved space you cant find that...
