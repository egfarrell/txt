Yes — the historical astronomical construction is circular in a mild, pragmatic sense, and that’s exactly why philosophers of physics like to separate the empirical practice from the relational justification. Here's the clean distinction:

1. What astronomers actually did (historical practice)

Astronomers in the 18th–20th centuries already accepted Newtonian dynamics as true.
Given that assumption, they wanted a time variable t such that the equations

m a = F

remained accurate when comparing theory with observed planetary motion.

But the Earth’s rotation is irregular, the orbital period is not perfectly stable, sundials drift, etc. So they said, essentially:

We will define a time scale t so that Newton’s equations hold with minimal residuals.

This is circular in the sense that:

• Newton’s laws are used to construct the time variable
• And then the time variable is used to confirm Newton’s laws

BUT the circularity is methodological, not logical:

They already trusted Newton’s laws; the goal was to find a usable clock that makes Nature look Newtonian. They weren’t trying to justify Newton's laws; they were trying to synchronize clocks with planetary dynamics under the assumption that Newton was correct.

So yes: in historical practice, ephemeris time was engineered to make Newton’s laws look simple.

2. Why that circularity didn’t bother them

Because for astronomers:

• Newtonian dynamics was a fixed background assumption
• They weren't deriving time from first principles
• They were calibrating clocks to match the theory they already assumed

As a result, the circularity is like:

We assume the law is correct → we construct time so the law is accurate → we use that time to check the law.

But since the first assumption already fixes the law, the loop is harmless. It’s calibration, not theory building.

3. Contrast with relational dynamics (the non-circular version)

Barbour, Bertotti, Lynden-Bell, and others deliberately remove this assumption:

• No external time
• No assumption that a second-order law in t even exists
• Only relational change and reparametrization invariance

Then they derive that only one parametrization makes the motion satisfy a second-order, force-like law. That emergent time is mathematically identical to ephemeris time, but now justified without assuming Newton’s laws at the outset.

So:

Astronomers:
“We assume Newton → choose t to fit Newton → done.”

Relationists:
“We assume no t → dynamics constrains t → Newton’s form emerges.”

4. Bottom line

Yes: historically, ephemeris time was constructed in a way that is methodologically circular.

But:

• It is not a logical circle, because they were not trying to justify Newton’s laws.
• It is a kind of calibration circle: assume the law → define the clock → check the law.
• The relational program was created precisely to avoid that circle and show that time can be derived from change.
