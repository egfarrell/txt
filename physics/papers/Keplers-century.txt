### Divine Guidance(!)
    - Rather than varying ONE parameter at a time, apparently
      Kepler tried extravagant hypothetical constructions under
      what he claimed was _explicit divine guidance_, since he
      fully believed that God communicated directly with him,
      even leading him along the path of scientific discovery and
      invention.

    - This Kepler, who made bold and imaginative great leaps
      forward, was clearly not a patient classifier of facts,
      however experimental or observational, nor did he use
      a "scientific method" in a way we might advise our students
      to emulate.

### Inertia
    - "inertia" or a fundamental kind of laziness or inertness;
      hence it cannot move unless there is a motive force to keep
      it going.
    - If that force should cease acting, the body would stop
      moving, and would stop moving at once wherever it happened
      to be.
    - Consequently, there could be no "proper place" for
      different kinds of matter, in the sense of the older
      physics and the older astronomy.
    - Newton's inertia is _somewhat different_, for according to
      Newton, inertia will keep a moving body in its uniform
      rectilinear motion after the external moving force has
      ceased to act.
    - Inertia, in other words, keeps a body at rest if it is at
      rest, and keeps a body in motion if it is moving; this
      inertia causes a body to resist any change in its "STATE",
      whether that be a state of rest or of uniform rectilinear
      motion.
    - Newton's concept in fact differs from that attributed to
      him in most elementary textbooks, because in the Principia
      he still thought of inertia as a `FORCE`, a "vis inertiae"
      or "vis insita materiae";
        - such a "force" was different in kind from those
          external forces which produce a change in state, rather
          than maintaining a state or resisting a change in
          state.
        - Thus the 'inertia' of Newtonian physics is quite
          different from the 'inertia' of Keplerian physics.
        - How did Newton arrive at his views concerning "inertia"
          and "vis inertiae" ?
        - I have found that Newton came upon the actual
          expression "inertia" (in the form "natural inertia") by
          reading through Descartes' correspondence.
        - With the greatness of all men of genius, he seized upon
          this concept (without knowing of its Keplerian origin),
          and altered it by the addition of a principle which he
          had also found in Descartes:
            - that motion, like rest, may be a "STATE".
        - I have found some indications that Descartes may have
          derived his notion of a "STATE OF MOTION" from Galileo,
          although indirectly.

- Although Newton knew only of Kepler's notion that the "inertia"
  of bodies tends to keep them at rest or to bring them to rest
  if there is no motive force acting upon them, Kepler was not
  always as limited as Newton supposed, and as many historians of
  science have assumed:
    - In his 'Dream' (posthumously published as 'Somnium'),
      Edward Rosen has shown that Kepler actually went beyond the
      original primitive concept, discussing how "finally the
      bodily mass proceeds towards its destination of its own
      accord".
    - Rosen observes that this statement implies a situation in
      which "a motion, once initiated, continues as a result of
      something internal, and without the help of an external
      force, whether mechanical or not"
