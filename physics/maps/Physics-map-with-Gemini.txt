### The "Hairy Proposition": Locality and Entanglement

- You are correct that assuming both locality and entanglement
  are real is a "fairly hairy proposition." This is the most
  significant point of tension in your belief system and one you
  will need to confront directly in your studies.

Here is the conflict:
- Entanglement is a proven phenomenon where two or more particles
  are linked in such a way that measuring a property of one
  instantaneously influences the corresponding property of the
  other, no matter how far apart they are.
- Locality is the principle that an object is directly influenced
  only by its immediate surroundings. In the context of
  entanglement, this would mean no faster-than-light influence.
- Bell's Theorem has shown mathematically and through experiments
  that you cannot have a theory that is both "local" and
  "realist" (in the sense that particles have definite properties
  before measurement) and still reproduce the predictions of
  quantum mechanics.

- Pilot-wave theory resolves this by being explicitly non-local.
  The guiding wave is a single entity that exists across all of
  space. When two particles are entangled, they are both guided
  by the same, more complex, pilot wave. An interaction with one
  particle (like a measurement) changes the entire pilot wave
  everywhere instantaneously, which in turn immediately alters
  the trajectory of the other particle.

Therefore, you are pointing towards a non-local theory. Your
belief in "locality" may need to be refined. Pilot-wave theory is
non-local in its influence, but it does not allow for
faster-than-light *communication*, which preserves a core aspect
of relativity (causality in the sense of signaling). This is
a subtle but crucial distinction.

- Is there a faster than light influence? (DON'T KNOW #8): Yes.
