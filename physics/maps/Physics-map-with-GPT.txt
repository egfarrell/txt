### York time in brief

- In the 1970s, James York discovered that if you slice spacetime
  into a family of `spatial hypersurfaces` (a foliation), you can
  define a scalar quantity—the mean extrinsic curvature `K` of
  each slice—that measures how that slice is embedded in the
  larger spacetime.
    - York noticed that this curvature behaves monotonically in
      many physically realistic cosmological solutions.
    - So `K`, or more precisely minus one-third of it, can serve
      as a global time parameter.

- It’s not imposed externally, like Newtonian time; it’s
  _constructed from the geometry itself_. That’s why people call
  it intrinsic or dynamical time.
- It’s the universe’s own metronome, ticking according to the
  evolution of its 3-geometry.
