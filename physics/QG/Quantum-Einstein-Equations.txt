- In context, “quantum Einstein equations’’ usually refers to the
  quantum version of the classical Einstein field equations that
  appears in *canonical quantum gravity*. They already exist in
  a precise formal sense, but they are not yet a complete or
  fully solved formulation of quantum gravity.


### 1. Classical starting point
- Classically, gravity is governed by Einstein’s equation
```
Gμν = 8πG Tμν.
```

- In the canonical (Hamiltonian) formulation of GR, this becomes
  a set of constraints on the spatial metric `gᵢⱼ` and its
  conjugate momentum `πᵢⱼ`. 
- The most important are:
    - the Hamiltonian constraint `H = 0`
    - the diffeomorphism constraints `Dᵢ = 0`

2. Quantizing these constraints
Canonical quantization promotes gᵢⱼ and πᵢⱼ to operators acting on a wave functional Ψ[gᵢⱼ].
The classical constraints become operator equations.

The quantum Hamiltonian constraint is the famous Wheeler–DeWitt equation:

Ĥ Ψ = 0.

This equation is often what people mean by the “quantum Einstein equations.’’
It is literally the Einstein field equations rewritten in Hamiltonian form and then quantized.

So they are *not hypothetical*; they exist mathematically.
But they are *not yet a complete or fully controlled theory*, because:

• Ĥ is hard to define rigorously (factor-ordering, regularization).
• The equation is extremely complicated and underdetermined.
• Physical inner products and observables are subtle in a background-free theory.

3. Exact solutions
The authors of the review you quoted were referring to various exact or semi-exact solutions of the Wheeler–DeWitt equation obtained for symmetry-reduced models (minisuperspace), midisuperspace, or simplified canonical quantizations. These solutions provide hints about the possible microstructure of spacetime—suggesting discreteness, non-locality, or other departures from smooth geometry.

4. Relation to other QG formalisms
• Loop Quantum Gravity can be viewed as a re-quantization of the same canonical constraints but in new variables (Ashtekar–Barbero). Its quantum Hamiltonian constraint is another expression of the “quantum Einstein equation.’’
• Spin foams, path integrals, and covariant LQG can be interpreted as providing solutions to these constraints.
• In string theory, the analog of “quantum Einstein equations’’ arises as β-function conditions on background fields, which reduce to Einstein’s equations at low energy.

Summary
They are not separate hypothetical equations—rather, they are the canonical quantization of Einstein’s equations, mainly embodied in the Wheeler–DeWitt equation. They exist, but they are not fully solved or understood, and they are a key piece of ongoing research in quantum gravity.
