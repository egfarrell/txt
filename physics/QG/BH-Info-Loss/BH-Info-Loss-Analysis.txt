### (Information) Paradox Regained?
    - A Brief Comment on Maudlin on Black Hole Information Loss
    - JB Manchak, James Owen Weatherall
    - $MY_BOOKS/Physics/QG/BH-info-paradox-Maudlin/Comment-Manchack-Weatherall-2018.pdf

### Notes:
    - RST
        - Relativistic Spacetime Thesis
        - ie “spacetime is a smooth Lorentzian manifold”
        - (the default GR framework).
        - ie that spacetime is modeled as a smooth 4D manifold,
          equipped with a Lorentzian metric field (which defines
          causal structure and geometry), and that this manifold
          is well-behaved (Hausdorff and paracompact, meaning
          roughly “no points glued together” and “every patch
          sits neatly within a finite cover”).

### summary
    - Tim Maudlin had claimed that the BH information loss
      problem is not a genuine paradox—that physicists only
      misdescribed what happens during evaporation.
    - Manchak and Weatherall disagree. They argue that, on
      standard general-relativistic assumptions, the problem is
      a paradox in the literal sense:
        - a set of seemingly true propositions that cannot all be
          true together.

- They isolate two core propositions:

    - `CCH`
        - All physically reasonable relativistic spacetimes are
          globally hyperbolic.
        - This expresses the cosmic censorship hypothesis: no
          naked singularities, determinism holds, unitary
          evolution possible.

    - `BHE`
        - Some evaporation spacetimes (those representing black
          holes that fully evaporate) are physically reasonable.
        - QFT in curved spacetime predicts Hawking radiation and
          thus black hole evaporation.

- From Kodama (1979) and Wald (1984):
    - No evaporation spacetime is globally hyperbolic.

- Therefore, `CCH` and `BHE` are inconsistent.
    - Something must give.

- Most physicists reject either `BHE` (deny full evaporation) or
  `CCH` (accept loss of global hyperbolicity and hence
  determinism/unitarity).

- Maudlin, however, claims both can be true. He argues that once
  an “evaporation event” is properly included—by adding a point
  at the top of the Penrose diagram—the spacetime becomes
  globally hyperbolic again.

- Manchak and Weatherall reply: this maneuver secretly rejects
  a third, implicit premise assumed by both Kodama and Wald:

- `RST` – Any physically reasonable spacetime can be represented
  by a smooth, Hausdorff, paracompact Lorentzian manifold.

- Maudlin’s “evaporation event” requires abandoning this. His
  resulting structure is not a smooth manifold; the metric cannot
  be continuously extended to the added point.
    - This breaks the causal structure that makes physics
      work—Maxwell’s equations and other hyperbolic systems can’t
      even be defined there.

- The authors show, with examples, that adding or removing
  a single point can radically alter spacetime structure and
  causal coherence. The “added evaporation point” leads to
  discontinuities in light-cone structure, meaning no
  well-defined causal cones and hence no meaningful laws at that
  event.

- They then demonstrate that even if one tries to stretch the
  definition of “global hyperbolicity” to include Maudlin’s
  structure, it loses the features that make global hyperbolicity
  valuable—causal continuity, determinism, and closure properties
  like `J+(p)` being closed.
- The resulting “globally hyperbolic” spacetime is causally
  pathological: it contains “gaps” and fails basic lemmas of
  causal theory.

- Thus, Maudlin’s supposed “solution” does not dissolve the
  paradox; it merely hides it by abandoning the standard
  mathematical framework of spacetime. If the laws of physics
  cannot be defined everywhere, determinism and retrodictability
  still fail.

- In short: the paradox remains. One cannot keep `CCH`, `BHE`,
  and `RST` simultaneously. Maudlin implicitly sacrifices `RST`,
  at a steep conceptual cost.

- The paper closes by reframing Maudlin’s victory as “pyrrhic”:
  keeping both global hyperbolicity and evaporation only by
  giving up the very spacetime structure on which relativity and
  field theory depend.
