- What *BHG* (the string theorist) is doing here is using the
  holographic dictionary of AdS/CFT to deliver a one-two punch
  against Maudlin’s “baby universe” move.

### 1. The setup

- AdS/CFT says: Every physical state of a gravitational spacetime
  in Anti-de Sitter (AdS) space corresponds exactly to a state of
  a conformal field theory (CFT) that lives on the boundary of
  that space.

- So: bulk gravity ↔ boundary field theory.

- Crucially, the *energy* of the gravitational configuration is
  encoded entirely by data on the boundary.

- This isn’t just a cute slogan; in GR with a negative
  cosmological constant, the Hamiltonian (the generator of time
  translations, i.e. energy) is a boundary integral.

- No boundary ⇒ no definition of total energy.


### 2. What Maudlin proposed (as BHG reads it)

- Maudlin’s picture has the final state of the universe defined
  not just on the exterior slice `Σ`₂ (the one after evaporation)
  but on the union of that slice with a disconnected interior
  piece `Σ₂_in`behind the horizon.
- That union is, topologically, a disconnected Cauchy surface.
- The state on `Σ₂_out` looks mixed, but the full state on
  `Σ₂_out` ∪ `Σ₂_in` is pure.

- In ordinary quantum theory terms, he’s saying:
    - `H_total = H_out ⊗ H_in`
    - and the full wavefunction lives in H_total, though outside
      observers only have access to H_out.


### 3. BHG’s counterargument in slow motion

- BHG grants that this decomposition might make sense
  kinematically, but he says it’s incompatible with the
  holographic dual CFT because of how energy works.

- Here’s his reasoning chain:

1. Energy in gravity is a boundary charge. The bulk Hamiltonian
   is a surface term at infinity (Brown–York, ADM, etc.).
    - That means that all measurable energy is defined by what’s
      happening at the AdS boundary.

2. Therefore the CFT Hamiltonian equals the boundary energy
   operator. The CFT’s notion of energy is the bulk’s boundary
   energy.

3. Now suppose the bulk Hilbert space factorizes into H_out
   ⊗ H_in (outside plus baby universe interior). The “inside”
   region (`Σ₂_in`) is disconnected from the boundary, so its
   degrees of freedom don’t contribute to the boundary energy.
    - Hence: every possible internal state `|ψ_in>` has zero
      energy as measured from the boundary.

4. Combine this with the “outside” vacuum. Take the exterior to
   be vacuum `|0_out>` and pair it with arbitrary `|ψ_in>`.
    - You then have infinitely many states `|0_out> ⊗ |ψ_in>`,
      all of which have total energy zero.

5. That’s a problem. On the CFT side, we know there is exactly
   one zero-energy state: the vacuum.
    - If the bulk contained infinitely many zero-energy states,
      the CFT would have to mirror that degeneracy—but it
      doesn’t.
    - Therefore, a disconnected “baby universe” sector cannot
      exist in any true AdS/CFT duality.

- In short: baby universes → infinite degeneracy of zero-energy
  states → contradicts unique CFT vacuum → ruled out.


### 4. The takeaway

- BHG’s argument is a version of what holographers call
  factorization failure: in QG, the Hilbert space does not
  generally factorize into “inside” and “outside” regions,
  precisely because of the way constraints tie the entire
  gravitational system to its boundary.

- So from the AdS/CFT perspective, Maudlin’s split Cauchy
  surface—and hence his way of making the evolution pure-to-mixed
  only by ignoring the interior—violates this fundamental
  non-factorization.
    - The CFT, being an ordinary unitary QFT with a single
      vacuum, simply can’t represent a bulk with extra hidden
      baby universes that cost no energy.


- The interesting philosophical punchline is that BHG isn’t
  refuting Maudlin’s logic within GR; he’s saying: “Sure, if GR
  were the whole story, maybe your baby universe picture works.
    - But in any QG theory consistent with AdS/CFT, it
      can’t—because the boundary theory can’t represent it.”

- That’s the physicist’s way of saying: the map between “bulk”
  and “boundary” forbids the extra interior Hilbert space.
    - The universe can’t have invisible information pockets
      without the boundary seeing an infinite vacuum degeneracy.
