# Chasing the Weyl quote

- The ringing defense of `Λ` is Eddington, not Weyl
    - The line “I would as soon think of reverting to Newtonian
      theory as of dropping the cosmical constant” is by
      Eddington in his book "The Expanding Universe" (early 1930s
      popular exposition). 
    - In the same discussion he praises Weyl’s unification
      attempt and says `Λ` has “a secure position” in Weyl’s
      theory. 
    - You can see both the sentence and the surrounding paragraph
      in a digitized copy; it explicitly attributes the line to
      Eddington and, in a footnote, cites Weyl for a related
      remark (see below). 

- The “cosmological factor … part of ours from the very
  beginning” is Weyl, in Space–Time–Matter Eddington’s page
  includes a footnote: 
    - “The cosmological factor which Einstein added to his theory
      later is part of ours from the very beginning.” and cites
      Raum. Zeit. Materie, p. 297 (English edition). 
    - That is: Eddington is quoting (or paraphrasing) Weyl’s
      Space–Time–Matter to say `Λ` arises naturally in Weyl’s
      geometric framework rather than being appended ad hoc. 
    - So your snippet’s second quotation traces to Weyl via
      Eddington’s citation. 

- About “`λ` intrinsically dependent on the EM potential”

- Here the waters get murkier. Weyl’s 1918 unification builds EM
  into geometry through a 1-form (often written `Qμ` or `Aμ`)
  that controls scale (length) transport, and he identifies this
  with the EM potential—hence the famous gauge idea. 

- That is a genuine gravity–EM link in Weyl geometry. But I still
  don’t see a primary-source where Weyl says `λ` depends on the

    - Eddington saying Weyl’s theory “unifies the gravitational

    - Later literature (modern Weyl-geometric gravity) where

    - For historical overview and Weyl’s stance on cosmology

# More background from GPT

### 1. What Weyl’s 1918 theory was

- Weyl’s ambition in 1918 was to unify gravitation and EM by
  generalising Riemannian geometry to what became called Weyl
  geometry (or “purely infinitesimal geometry”).

- In standard Riemannian geometry (used in Einstein’s GR), the
  metric `g_μν` defines lengths and angles, and the Levi-Civita
  connection is metric-compatible:

```
 ∇_λ g_μν = 0
```

- Weyl replaced this by a connection in which the length of vectors under parallel transport can change:

```
∇_λ g_μν = − Q_λ g_μν
```

Here `Q_λ` is a new 1-form (often identified with the electromagnetic potential `A_λ`).

- Weyl identified this 1-form `Q_μ` with the electromagnetic
  potential. The “gauge” (scale) invariance he introduced
  corresponded to electromagnetic gauge freedom: 
    - changing the length scale at each point corresponds to
      a gauge transformation `A_μ → A_μ + ∂_μφ`.

- The idea: the length-scale of measuring rods might change from
  point to point, and that change is governed by the same
  potential that gives the electromagnetic field. Thus the EM
  field becomes part of spacetime geometry.

- This unified gravitation and electromagnetism conceptually,
  though it ran into Einstein’s famous “second-clock effect”
  objection (physical clocks’ rates would depend on their
  history), which made the proposal untenable experimentally.


### 2. The cosmological constant (Λ or λ) in Weyl’s theory

- Einstein added Λ to his field equations in 1917 to allow
  a static universe. Weyl’s 1918 geometry naturally includes
  extra geometric terms, and later researchers noticed that its
  structure can produce an effective cosmological-constant-like
  term even without adding one by hand.

- In Weyl geometry, local scale transformations and the field
  `Q_μ` can generate contributions resembling a cosmological
  constant in the gravitational field equations. Modern
  “Weyl-geometric gravity” theories explicitly show that such
  Λ-type terms can arise from the Weyl field itself.

- However, I could not find a primary Weyl text that says
  literally “λ is dependent on the electromagnetic potential.”
  That exact dependence is more a modern reinterpretation than
  a phrase from Weyl’s own 1918–1920 writings.

### 3. Comparing to your note snippet

- Your note said:

- “The cosmological factor which Einstein added to his theory is part of ours from the very beginning.”

- That is indeed something Eddington later quoted from Weyl’s Space-Time-Matter. Weyl meant that what Einstein had to add by hand (Λ) appears naturally in his more general geometry.

- The next line in your note,
    - “lambda was a factor that was intrinsically dependent on
      the electromagnetic potential,”
    - is a stronger statement. It reflects the spirit of Weyl’s
      geometry—where the electromagnetic potential is built into
      spacetime structure—but not a direct Weyl quotation.
      Historically, `λ(A_μ)` as an explicit functional dependence
      isn’t found in his papers.

### 4. Why this isn’t in standard textbooks

    - Weyl’s 1918 model predicted physically unobserved effects
      (length and time scales depending on past history), so it
      was quickly abandoned in that form.
    - Einstein’s `Λ` served a very different purpose (cosmic
      staticity).
    - Later quantum field and gauge theory developments subsumed
      Weyl’s geometric unification into other frameworks.
    - Thus the idea that Λ is driven by electromagnetism is
      historically marginal and survives mostly in
      modified-gravity research, not in mainstream GR or
      cosmology.

### 5. Modern reinterpretations

- Weyl’s geometry keeps resurfacing. In scalar-tensor and
  conformal gravity theories, the Weyl vector or associated
  scalar field can produce effective cosmological-constant terms.

- Recent papers on Weyl-geometric gravity or Geometric Proca–Weyl
  fields show models where the vacuum energy (`Λ_eff`) arises
  from the dynamics of the Weyl field. In these, the cosmological
  constant is emergent rather than inserted. This is conceptually
  aligned with Weyl’s original unification spirit, though not
  historically identical to his equations.

### 6. Working conclusion

- Weyl genuinely unified gravity and electromagnetism through geometry:
    -   `∇_λ g_μν = − Q_λ g_μν`, with `Q_μ ≡ electromagnetic potential`

- The cosmological constant appears naturally in his framework,
  so he could claim Λ was “there from the beginning.”

• The assertion that λ explicitly depends on `A_μ` is a later or
interpretive embellishment, not a clear Weyl statement.

- • To verify details, check Raum-Zeit-Materie (especially around
  p. 297 of the English edition cited by Eddington) and
  Eddington’s The Expanding Universe, which contains the “I would
  as soon think of reverting to Newtonian theory…” line.
