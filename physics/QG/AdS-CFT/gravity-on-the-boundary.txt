### 1. Why gravity doesn’t have a local energy density

In a normal field theory (like electromagnetism), we can define
an energy density `T^00(x)`—part of the stress-energy tensor—and
integrate it over space to get total energy.

But in GR, spacetime geometry itself *is* the dynamical field.
The coordinate system you use can always be warped to make
gravitational potential energy disappear locally. That’s why we
can’t write down a generally covariant, local expression for
gravitational energy density—it’s not an invariant concept.

In effect, Einstein’s equivalence principle kills the notion of
“gravitational energy here.” There’s only “gravitational energy
relative to infinity.”


### 2. How we define total energy in gravity

To rescue something like “energy,” one instead defines it at
spatial infinity (for asymptotically flat spacetimes) or on the
conformal boundary (for AdS).

The standard constructions are:

* ADM energy for asymptotically flat spacetime: it’s a surface
  integral over the metric and its derivatives at infinity.
* Brown–York or holographic stress tensor for AdS spacetime:
  again, it’s a boundary integral, giving you conserved charges
  like energy and angular momentum.

These are the only *gauge-invariant* notions of total energy.
They measure how the metric deviates from the background at
infinity.

So when we say *“the Hamiltonian of gravity is a boundary term”*,
we mean that the volume integral over constraints vanishes on
physical states (the Einstein constraints equal zero), leaving
only a surface term at infinity as the actual generator of time
translations.


### 3. Why that matters for AdS/CFT

In AdS/CFT, this geometric fact maps beautifully onto the field
theory:

* The bulk Hamiltonian, which generates time evolution, is
  entirely equivalent to a boundary operator (the CFT
  Hamiltonian).
* Therefore, every bit of bulk energy must be “visible” at the
  boundary, because that’s literally how the dual theory defines
  it.

A region of the bulk that is causally disconnected from the
boundary—like Maudlin’s baby universe tucked behind an evaporated
black hole horizon—would have no associated boundary energy. From
the boundary’s perspective, it’s invisible and costs nothing to
excite.

That’s what BHG is pointing out: such disconnected interior
degrees of freedom would correspond to *zero-energy excitations*.
And if there are many of them, you’d get an infinite degeneracy
of zero-energy states—something the boundary CFT, which has
a unique vacuum, absolutely does not have.


### 4. Philosophical resonance

This boundary-centric nature of energy means gravity is
inherently *nonlocal*. The whole state of the universe is, in
a deep sense, encoded in its edges.

That’s the root of holography: the dynamics of the “inside” are
constrained by what happens at infinity. You can’t just split the
universe into an “outside” and an “inside” the way you do in
local quantum field theory, because the gravitational constraints
tie them together.
