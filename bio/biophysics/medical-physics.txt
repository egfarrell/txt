    - Lecture 16 - Crystallography
        - Vector direction: `[0-1-1]` <--- no commas, and the
          minus we put on top as a "macron"
        - all "face" diagonals: `<011>` = [011], [01-1], [0-11]
          etc 
        - Planes `(011)`: use Miller indices which is the
          reciprocal of where the plane cuts the x,y,z axes.
          (Advantage of the reciprocal is if you have something
          paralell to the z axis say, then you don't have
          a component of infinity `∞`)
        - all planes (or all "faces"): `{011}` = (001) (010)
          (011) etc
        - hkl plane is perpendicular to the hkl direction
            - `(hkl)` is perpendicular to `[hkl]`
    - He moves on to X-RAYS now...
    - X-Rays: short `λ` EM radiation
    - between 0.01A < λ < 10Å. on average `1Å` (ie atomic
      dimensions)
    - energy given by: `E = hν = hc/λ = hcν-bar`
    - typical energy of 1Å X-Ray = 12,400 eV per photon!!!
    - it takes 648 eV to strip the most tightly bound electron
      from Nitrogen. X-Rays are ionizing radiation writ large!!!
    - Discovery: Röntgen used gas discharge tubes: high voltage,
      low pressure (vacuum)
    - we're talking about 1000's of eV and Rontgens cathode ray
      setup had a voltage of 35,000 volts, so we're not talking
      about emission from an excited electron in a gas molecule
      - the gas binding energies are way lower than that. This
        must come from the solid. The electron leaves the cathode
        and crashes into the anode. There's the smoking gun.
        There's something going on when the electron crashes into
        the anode and gives rise to the radiation.
    - Shells: K, L, M, N
    - emission intervals: from 2 -> 1 = α (∆n = 1, fall 1 level)
    - emission intervals: from 3 -> 1 = β (∆n = 2, fall 2 levels)
    - emission intervals: from 4 -> 1 = γ (∆n = 3, fall 3 levels)

    - "intensity reflects the relative FREQUENCY of emission" - I higher intensity for
      a particular wavelength means this emission occured more
      often, or is more probable (I had it wrong - I thought
      higher "intensity" was higher energy somehow). Instead the
      wavelength `λ` reflects the energy!

    - Copper and zink α lines: the relative intesity of each
      line is related to the relative concentrations of each
      element: copper and zinc. So now I have a tool for chemical
      analysis of the composition of the anode.

    - Mosley corrected Mendelev: Mendelev thought atomic MASS was
      the key, whereas Mosley said atomic NUMBER was key. Atomic
      number is the (positive) charge in the central nucleus.

### Youtube Talk: Overview of Radiation Oncology
    - before 1960's X-Rays didn't go deep, lots of skin reactions
    - 1960's: Linac invented - high energy rays were produced
    - 1970s/1980s: computers introduced to assist wtih dose
      measurement and treatment planning
    - Radiation Forms
        - photons: high energy X-Ray, no different to
          a diagnostic X-Ray, just more energy
        - electrons: for an electron beam, just remove the
          tungsten target, and shower down electrons on the
          patient
        - protons: ("bragg peak") minimally interacts when it
          goes into the body, and then at the energy the proton
          stops, it deposits all its energy there. (not like
          X-Rays which start to interact straight away and go out
          the other side)
        - radioactive sources
    - photon beam: flame torches
    - proton beam: fire cracker - throw it in and it deposits all
      its energy


### What Is Quality Assurance?
    - AAPM definition of QA: "To assure that machine
      characteristics do not deviate significantly from their
      baseline values acquired at the time of testing and
      commissioning."
    - Linac measurements are taken during acceptance and
      commissioning.
    - The AAPM requires daily, monthly, and annual QA tests to
      ensure that linacs are able to deliver treatment as
      planned.
    - TG-40 set standards for basic linacs without MLCs, on-board
      imaging, or respiratory gating.
    - TG-142 adds standards for MLCs, IMRT, SBRT, on-board
      imaging and respiratory gating.

### 5 things not to do with a particle accelerator
    - theres 26,000 particle accelerators (not just CERN!)
    - dont put a neutron in an linac: it's not charged!
    - demo: _uncharged_ ball spinning around in a bowl. There was
      different metal stips in the bowl, charged and grounded.
      The ball picked up charge, but then dumped in on a grounded
      strip. Then picked up charge again and dumped it. It ended
      up spinning around and around.
        - so it was a basic "dummy" accelerator
        - but the charge was changing all the time
        - a real accelerator would have a constant charge so the
          voltage would change instead
    - beam dump: massive long blocks of graphite to stop the beam
      (mainly CERN?)
    - Russian PHD student put his head in a beam and survived.
      Paralysed on one side of his face, that side stopped aging!
    - Bragg peak: protons stopping in water?
    - beam power: more and moe particles? so particle count is
      "power"?
    - nuclear power: use very powerful proton accelerators in
      a nuclear power generator: the beam creates neutrons which
      drive a critcal reaction. But need 10 times the current
      accelerator max power.
    - 2 types of radiation: ionising (alpha, beta, gamma)
      / non-ionsing
    - irradiated food! (electron beams)

### Glossary
    - EPS         : Engineering Physical Sciences
    - MHRA        : Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency
    - CMG         : clincial management group
    - GTV         : tumour volume: gross tumour volume
    - CTV         : tumour volume: clincal target volume
    - ITV         : tumour volume: internal target volume
    - PTV         : tumour volume: planning target volume
    - IMRT        : Intensity Modulated Radiation Therapy: less dose delivered to surrounding areas (not "treating" them better)
    - IGRT        : Image Guided Radio Therapy
    - VMAT        : Volumetric Modulated Arc Therapy (beam changes as it moves)
    - SABR        : Stereotatic ablative radiotherapy
    - SGRT        : Surface Guided Radio Therapy (uses camera to line up the beam, no need for tatoo markers)
    - NEOADJUVANT : radiation: treatment before surgery (to reduce tumour size)
    - ADJUVANT    : treatment: used after surgery to mop up cancer cells
    - cyber knife : very fine beams (more targeted than a linac)
