# Histopathology 
    - is a bridge between biology and medicine. 
    - It delves into the microscopic world of tissues, revealing
      stories about diseases, especially cancer. 
    - Understanding its concepts requires diving into both the
      normal and abnormal structures within the body.

### Cancer :
    - At its core, cancer is UNCONTROLLED CELL GROWTH. 
    - Normally, cells grow, divide, and die in a regulated
      manner. Cancer disrupts this process, leading to excessive
      growth and division. These cells can invade nearby tissues
      and spread to other body parts (metastasis).

# Stages of Cancer Development:

    - Pre-cancerous: 
        - Cells are abnormal but not yet cancerous. They have the
          potential to become cancer.
    - Early-stage Cancer: 
        - Cancer has formed but is confined to its origin. For
          instance, in DCIS, cancer cells are in the breast ducts
          but haven't invaded surrounding tissues.
    - Invasive Cancer: 
        - Cancer cells have spread from their original site to
          adjacent tissues. IDC is an example, where cells from
          breast ducts invade surrounding breast tissues.
    - Metastatic Cancer: 
        - Cancer has spread to distant body parts.

# Histopathology Terms:

### TDLU 
    - Terminal Duct Lobular Unit: 
    - Found in breast tissue, it's a functional unit consisting
      of ducts and lobules. Lobules produce milk; ducts transport
      it. Changes in TDLU can indicate benign conditions or
      potential for cancer.

### HELU 
    - Hyperplastic Enlarged Lobular Unit: 
    - This is when TDLUs are enlarged with increased cellularity.
      It's not cancer but can signal a higher risk. It's
      identified by more cells than normal, but these cells
      appear typical under a microscope.

### UDH 
    - Usual Ductal Hyperplasia: 
    - This benign (non-cancerous) condition involves an increased
      NUMBER of cells within the breast ducts. The cells look
      fairly normal, and the condition slightly elevates the risk
      of developing breast cancer.

### ADH 
    - (Atypical Ductal Hyperplasia): Here, the cell growth in the
      breast ducts is not only excessive but also abnormal. Cells
      appear irregular. ADH is not cancer, but is a significant
      risk factor for developing breast cancer.

### DCIS (Ductal Carcinoma In Situ): 
    - Early form of breast cancer where the cells lining the
      milk ducts become cancerous, but haven't spread beyond the
      ducts. It's often treatable and considered non-invasive.

### IDC (Invasive Ductal Carcinoma): 
    - The most common form of breast cancer. Begins in the milk
      _ducts_ and then invades surrounding breast tissue. From
      there, it can metastasize to other body parts.

- Histopathologists study these conditions by examining tissue
  samples under a microscope. They look for cell size, shape,
  arrangement, and other characteristics to diagnose diseases.
