    - VARIABLE GROUPS
        - aka: "side chain"
        - aka: "R Group"
        - All Amino Acids have a variable group
        - Variable group determines if acid is hydrophilic or
          hydrophobic. Important for protein folding.
        - Based on variable group, amino acids are classified:
            - nonpolar,
            - polar,
            - negatively charged,
            - positively charged.
        - nonessential amino acids: Of the 20 amino acids, 11 are
          made naturally by the body
        - essential amino acids: Amino acids not made naturally
          by the body


    - PEPTIDE BONDS:
        - type of covalent bond that links 2 amino acids
        - created via _condensation_ reaction. This reaction
          removes a water molecule in the making of a bond.
        - Removal of water links a carbon from the carboxyl group
          to the nitrogen of the amino group.
        - "Dipeptide": two amino acids linked together
        - "Polypeptide": > 3 amino acids

    - PROTEIN STRUCTURE (4 levels)
        - _Primary_
            - order of amino acids
            - peptide bonds

        - _Secondary_
            - Shape a segment of the polypeptide takes on (not 3D
              shape of entire polypeptide)
            - hydrogen bonds
            - α-helix: segment forms helical structure
            - β-pleated sheet: chain of amino acids folds over on
              itself (folded sheet)

        - _Tertiary_
            - 3D shape of entire Polypeptide chain
            - how all secondary structures fold over on each
              other to become this globular 3D shape
            - hydrogen bonds
            - ionic bonds  (acid/base interaction)
            - Van der Waals interactions
            - disulfide bridges (special type of covalent bond)

        - _Quaternary_
            - > 1 Polypeptide chain making up a particular
              protein
            - note: not ALL proteins have a Quaternary structure

        - DIFFERENCE Polypeptide <-> protein
            - POLYPEPTIDE: chain of 3 or more amino acids that is
              not yet folded (its a strand of amino acids)
            - PROTEIN: Polypeptide which has taken on full 3D
              shape
            - "Think of a Polypeptide as a straight piece of yarn
              and a protein as a knitted sweater". The protein
              has now taken on a shape

        - CHAPERONINS
            - all cells have chaperonins to help fold proteins
            - large complex structure, itself made up of proteins
            - When a Polypeptide is being made/folded, it goes
              inside a cavity or chamber in the Chaperonin. 
            - This provides a good environment for all the amino
              acids to have the proper interaction and create the
              proper bonds.

        - DENATURED
            - Sometimes a protein can "unfold"
            - Can happen in hot temperatures; the hydrogen bonds that
              hold the protein together can be broken.
            - Or in acidic or basic environments the ionic bonds can
              be disrupted. (ie the acid/base interactions are
              affected)
            - This can be dangerous as the shape of a protein is
              crticial for its function
