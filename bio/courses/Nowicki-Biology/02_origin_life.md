
### key idea: 
    biological information in living systems

### millers experiment 
    - organic molecules

### polymerization 
    - complex organic molecules like proteins or DNA 
    * are long chains of polymers 
    * i.e. long chains of simple building blocks called monomers

    - polymers are essential

### enzymes
    - specialised proteins called enzymes are responsible for
      building polymers out of monomer building blocks

### nucleotides
    - make up polymers which build DNA and RNA

### mystery
    - could polymers have arisen abiotically in the early earth
      even without the specialised protein machinery (enzymes) to
      build the polymers?

### protein
    * is a polymer of amino acids!
    * or a polypeptide chain! because the molecular bond for
      the amino acids is a peptide bond

### Catalyst
    - increase the rate of a chemical reaction

### Sidney Fox
    - showed polymerisation could be possible? 
    - He took Millers experiment and got a few kinds of amino
      acids to start joining together into short polypeptide
      chains. 
    - But this could only happen under a very narrow range of
      conditions (unlikely in the early Earth).

    - Fox speculated you could increase the hit rate if you had
      a non-biological Catalyst. e.g. Shoreside clay with lattice
      like structures and weak electrical charges, could increase
      the probability of polymerization.

### Ferris
    - showed that showed short stretches of polypeptides could be
      produced like this? 
    - But they're __non-functional__ polymers... they don't make
      any sense or do anything.

### primoridial soup
    - ocean brimming with complex (organic) polymers. 
    - But they're not approaching life, they're not in highly
      ordered packages.

### fundamental package
    All living things come in units of a cell. It's like
    a barrier between the living and non-living.

### spontaneous cells - Protobionts
    could arise from the primoridial soup? Lab experiments
    suggest yes! (Early life studies) You can get
    aggregations of abiotically produced complex molecules
    that spontaneously form themselves into cell like
    structures. 

### lipids
    put them in water and they spontaneously form a package
    with a membrane that encloses a central space.

### Coacervate
    colloidal aggregation. Self assembles into a cell-like
    object (ingredients: polypeptides, nucleic acids,
    polysaccharides)

    If you introduce enzymes, the Coacervates will take them
    up and also take up the substates produced by the
    enzymes.

    "We gave the Coacervate the hammer and it started to hit
    something."

### Coacervate
    Oparin believed that life developed from 'coacervates'.
    microscopic spherical aggregates of lipid molecules
    (spontaneously formed) that are held together by
    electrostatic forces and that may have been precursors
    of cells.

### How did we get 
    from simple probionts to complex modern cells? Well we
    don't really know but part of the answer may be
    reproduction.

### Reproduction    
    1. How it arose (tricky problem, currently researched)
    2. How it works

### Protobiont
    comes up with a new property/trait. e.g. new _molecule_
    that makes it more durable, or molecule that makes it
    more efficient.

    But problem: theres only one of them and this individual
    wont last forever!

    We need the improvements to accumulate over time
    somehow!

    Our problem would be solved if the Protobiont could
    reproduce itself.

### reproduction
    by itself isn't sufficient. e.g. the cell splits into
    two. Which of the daughter cells gets the new molecule?
    Or even if theres a few molecules, when the daughter
    cells divide, the new molecule gets further diluted.

### solution
    the probionts need the ability to make new and accurate
    copies of themselves, or parts of themselves. They have
    to be able to store __information__ about the structure
    of the "new molecule" and transmit that stored
    information about building the molecule to its
    offspring.

    How such a mechanism for transmiting information
    developed is the most unresolved question about the
    origin of life.

### DNA
    now we all know that such a molecule exists now 
    (but how did it develop?)

    _DNA_ -> (transcription) _RNA_ -> (translated) _protein_

    DNA is a kind of blueprint and proteins are the product
    of this blueprint.

### Catch 22
    DNA could not have been the storage molecule that arose
    in early life! The reason: DNA can't replicate itself!

    DNA requires a huge number of other proteins (enzymes)
    to replicate itself.

    We would need to postulate __simultaneously__ DNA to
    store information about proteins and proteins that could
    replicate that DNA.

    Which came first - the chicken or egg? Well neither
    because DNA and proteins can't functionally exist
    without each other in modern cells.

### solution
    we needed some sort of  molecule that can
    * replicate itself
    * and do other useful things in the cell

    We think some early version of RNA fits the bill here.
    RNA might have acted as the first blueprint or genetic
    material.

    Under certain conditions on a limited scale if you put
    in RNA and nucleotides its possible for RNA to replicate
    itself

### Thomas Cech
    Discovered that there are some types of RNA that do act
    as catalysts just like some protein enzymes do. That is
    they perform some biochemical tasks in the cell.
    These are called __ribozymes__.

    Hypothesis so RNA might have got things rolling in the
    early Earth but eventually DNA took over the job of
    being the information bearing molecule and proteins took
    over the job of doing all the catalytic work of the
    cell.

    RNA became relegated to being an intermediate in the
    process.

### phasing out of RNA?
    Proteins are amazingly versatile. They come in many
    different shapes. The _chemical properties_ of RNA are
    such that it can only take on a __limited__ number of
    shapes (and therefore only assume a limited number of
    catalytic functions).

    DNA assumes a particular type of chemical configuration
    that makes it particularly good at storing and
    replicating information (much better than RNA).

### definition of information
    storing the structure of biologically useful molecules

### the replication
    of information leads to another key property of living
    systems. They change over time.
