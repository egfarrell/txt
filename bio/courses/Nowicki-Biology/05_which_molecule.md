### Chromosomal theory of Inheritence
    Boveri and Sutton noticed that the peculiar the way
    chromosomes move when a cell is dividing (to create
    a gamate) corresponded to patterns of transmission of
    traits between parents and their offspring.
    (they also knew about Mendels work)
    So... the way chromosomes move is somehow related
    to inheritence and is therefore related to how
    information is stored (the information has to be
    on the chromosomes)


### Frederick Griffith
    first insight into the chemical nature of genetic
    information. He studied Strep Pneumonia. He wanted to
    create a vaccine. He worked with two strains of the
    bacteria:

    1. S strain - virulent
    2. R strain - non-virulent. (rough)

    Both strains "breed true" - so the difference between
    S and R must be genetically encoded.


### Vaccines - Pasteur approach
    Take the organism you wanted to create a vaccine for and
    kill it (heat it up). It couldn't harm you but might
    induce an immune response if injected. Good idea but
    doesnt always work. E.g. inject a mouse with dead S
    strain bacteria, then live S-strain, the mouse would
    die, it didn't become immune.

### Vaccines - another approach
    Inject the dead virulent strain, but also a related
    strain that was less virulent and still alive.
    The theory is the live strain will induce a full
    immune response but won't kill the patient, and they
    will also develop immunity to the dead virulent strain.

### Griffith experiment
    Inject the mouse with __DEAD__ S-strain and __LIVE__
    R-strain. Neither should kill the mouse. But the mouse
    died! When Griffith disected the mouse he found LIVING
    S-strain bacteria!

    He conclued: the living R-strain bacteria had taken up
    something from the dead S-strain bacteria. The R-strain
    had been transformed into the virulent deadly S-strain.
    And whatever the R-strain had picked up it must be
    genetic material (have information in it) because how
    else could the R-strain become virulent like the S-strain.

    Actually you don't even need the mouse, you can put dead
    S and live R in a beaker and some of the live R would
    become transformed into live S.

    So the R bacteria were incorporating material from the
    dead S and "thats an interesting process on its own".
    Griffith said he had discovered "_transforming
    principle_".


### genetic material "must be" a "protein"
    - Why did they think that? Well nearly everything important
      in the cell is done by a protein. 
    - Also they asked what are chromosomes made of? (cos they new
      about the role of chromosomes in trait transmission). Well
      they found chromosomes are actually made of proteins (and
      a small amount of nucleic acids!) They found 5 to 10 times
      more protein than DNA.

    - Also proteins were made up of 20 different amino acids
      while DNA came from only 4 nucleotides. So DNA looked
      simple and didnt seem to be doing anything.

### nagging evidence against proteins
    - if you heat proteins up they break down. The complex bonds
      in a protein breaks apart and "de-natures".
    - So when Griffith heated up the S-strain to kill them, he
      probably de-natured all the proteins.

### Rockfeller - Avery et al.
    1940's biochemists who had techniques to _selectively_
    break down different classes of biological molecules.

    1. proteins
    2. nucleic acids
    3. carbohydrates (sugars)
    4. lipids

    So which class when broken down stops the "transforming
    principle" from working? They found that the key class
    was nucleic acids but their result wasn't accepted.
    Protein biochemists thought their techniques hadn't
    really zapped all the proteins.

### Chase and Hershey (1950's)
    Used viruses. Viruses are not real cells, they're an
    outer coat of protein and an inner core of nucleic
    acids. Viruses can't replicate themselves, they have to
    take over another cell. Clearly then the viruses must be
    injecting "information" into the cell in order to take
    it over.

    Chase and Hershey asked: what are these phage virsues
    injecting into the cells? Well theres only two candidates.

    They radioactively labelled the proteins:
        They grew T2 viruses in radioactive sulfur (S 35).
        They viruses as they reproduce would incorporate
        sulfur into their protein coats.

    They radioactively labelled the DNA:
        They grew T2 in radioactive phosphorous (p 32).
        Phosphorous is not found in proteins but is a major
        part of DNA.

    Experiment:
        Allow virus to inject their "information" then
        agitate the solution to break off the virus coats
        from the host bacteria cells. Then use a centrifuge
        to separate the heavier bacterial cells into pellets
        from the virus coats and solutions.

    Then question:
        Is the radioactivity in the fluid (super-natent)? or
        in the pellet? Well whenever they used the
        radioactive DNA they found that the pellet was
        radioactive. Published their results in 1952 and had
        widespread interest.

### nucleic acids
    Their building blocks are nucleotides.

    - made up of:
        - centre: pentose-sugar forming a ring (5 carbons numbered clockwise)
        - left: phosephate group (P with four O's around it)
        - right: nitrogenous base

        - Pyrimidine - 6 element ring
            - Pyrimidine - Cytosine (C)
            - Pyrimidine - Thymine (T)

        - Purines - 6 element ring and 5 element ring
            - Purine - Adenine (A)
            - Purine - Guanine (G)

### nucleic acid string
    - goes: sugar - phosphate - sugar - phosphate - sugar - phosphate - ...
    - is polarised with
        - 3-Carbon at one end
        - 5-Carbon at another end
