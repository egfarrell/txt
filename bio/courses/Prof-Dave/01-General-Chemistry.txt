### 008 The Mole Avogadros Number and Stoichiometry
    - $MY_VIDEOS/bio/prof-dave/chem/008-The-Mole-Avogadros-Number-and-Stoichiometry.mp4
    - the mole is the way to go between AMU's and GRAMS
        - $MY_IMAGES/bio/Prof-Dave/chem/from-amus-to-grams.jpg
    - molar mass: 
        - same as its atomic mass, but expressed in grams instead
          of AMU's
        - H2O is 18 amu
            - 16 amu for oxygen
            - 2  amu for the 2 hydrogens
            - so a mole of H2O is 18g
        - How many grams of water to we get from 20g of propane
          (C3H8)?
            - $MY_IMAGES/bio/Prof-Dave/chem/grams-water-from-propane.jpg
            - there's a 4 to 1 ratio
                - if    one molecule of propane makes 4 molecules of water 
                - then  one mole     of propane makes 4 moles of water

### 011 Kinetic Molecular Theory and the Ideal Gas Laws
    - $MY_VIDEOS/bio/prof-dave/chem/011-Kinetic-Molecular-Theory-and-the-Ideal-Gas-Laws.mp4
    - Ideal gas - good aproximation
        - We're interested in:
            - Pressure
            - Temperature
            - Volume
            - Moles (how MANY particles are in the container)
    - These four variables depend on each other in interesting
      ways, that have been turned into laws
    - pressure volume
        - $MY_IMAGES/bio/Prof-Dave/chem/boyles-law.jpg
    - volume and temperature are directly proportional
        - if one doubles so must the other
        - $MY_IMAGES/bio/Prof-Dave/chem/temperature-volume-pressure-relationship.jpg
        - balloon expands to keep the pressure the same
    - Avogadros Law
        - equal volumes of gas at the same temperature and
          pressure contain the same _NUMBERS_ of molecules
        - 1 mole of ideal gas occupies a volume of 22.4
          L , regardless of the gas
    - Ideal Gas Law: a combination of Boyle/Charles/Avogadro
        - handy if we know 3 out of the 4 variables
        - ie we know the pressure, temperature and volume, so we
          could quickly calculate how many moles of particles
          must be in the sample
