        - A simple gradient descent algorithm based on a learned

### Conference question 3

      Transformers?
        - that is correct - transformers and attention operators


### Conference question 4
    - There are many algorithms to generate MSA alignments, each
      one of them trying to detect a different property or
      enancing a particular feature. There would be any advantage
      on feeding different MSA generated with different algorithm
      or there will be just a MSA that will work better than
      others?
        - it is clear that MSA methods really aiming for aligning
          structurally equivalent parts (rather than reflecting
          evolutionary history) is more suitable for being used
          in alphafold. As the number of sequences available for
          many families is large, the challenges are different
          than when for example clustalw was developed. I have
          not seen analyses of how sensitive alphafold is on the
          accuracy of the MSA, this would be interesting.


### Conference question 5
    - Have we already learned anything about HOW proteins fold
      from AlphaFold?
        - I believe the consensus is that AF2/RF are able to
          predict the final, folded 3D structures for protein
          domains and some whole proteins when there are many,
          60-100 or more, homologous proteins in the same protein
          (super)family. AF2/RF does NOT explain how the unfolded
          to folded process takes place, or HOW proteins fold.
          Btw the methods are also not good, currently, at de
          novo prediction. You cannot use a random sequence, not
          related to anything in the sequence databases, and
          expect to get a good model.


### Conference question 6
    - And related to my question: I am not sure I understood how
      AlphaFold v2 differs from this, i.e. does v2 still use this
      gradient descent method?
        - Simply put: the difference is that AF2 learns
          end-to-end (sequence in, structure out), whereas AF1
          learns a potential from which it then gets a structure
          after gradient descent.
        - Less simply put: AF2 has some later stages that do some
          non-physical optimisation on a “triangle gas” to get
          a good structure and it can do postprocessing that
          involves an amber relaxation process. All explained in
          detail in the appendices of the AF2 paper.


### Conference question 7
    - Great talk Inge. While AlphaFold2 predicts brilliantly,
      could you comment on the prospects for this type of
      technology to give us insight on the actual folding
      process?
        - Thanks, Rein! Alphafold as such is not targeted to this
          problem. In order to use a similar approach to learn
          about the folding process, one would need to have
          example data informative for this process. You may know
          more than me about data available - I am not aware of
          large datasets on protein folding trajectories.

### Conference question 9
    - Is it possible or will it be possible to predict whether
      the protein has monomeric or multimeric character?
        - Quite sure this is currently not in AF2. Possibly in
          some of the Rosetta-related tools. However, this is
          also a type of prediction that will benefit a lot from
          AI and the people developing these tools will learn
          from AF2/RF.


### Conference question 10
    - Are there 5 predictions from AF2 really different to each
      other? What if you can make available only a matrix of
      differences respect to the AF1 model?
        - For the few cases I have looked into, the 5 predictions
          are very similar. Much more similar than what
          “last-year ab initio structure prediction” often
          produced, where different models often were
          very/completely different

### Conference question 11
    - I wonder how AF performs for soluble proteins vs membrane
      proteins. Is there already some information whether AF
      performs well for membrane proteins, too?
        - I think AF performs quite well for TM proteins. All
          three case studies in the AF human proteome database
          paper are TM proteins, at least partially.


### Conference question 12
    - Very interesting talk. I was wondering how good is method
      in interpreting structures for the complexes for eg.
      antigen-antibody complex.
        - I believe the consensus is that AF2/RF are able to
          predict the final, folded 3D structures for proteins,
          protein domains and segments when there are many,
          60-100 or more, homologous proteins in the same protein
          (super)family with the same structure. This is
          unfortunatly not the case for antibodies, so I think
          the methods as they are now are not optimal here


### Conference question 13
    - What about proteins with native ligands/co-factors?
        - I think the situation here is that AF2, possibly RF,
          does not know anything about ligands, PTMs, metal ions
          etc., but still is in some cases able to make good
          predictions for these proteins, including side chain
          conformations for the residues contacting the
          ligands/metals/etc. See f.ex. Fig 1C in the AF2 methods
          paper

### Conference question 14
    - How pratically do you use the estimate errors of the
      alphafold2 model for successful molecular replacement? Are
      the estimate errors reflected in the B-factors in the PDB
      of the model?
        - Yes, it turns out that if you assume a 3D Gaussian
          error distribution, you can smear an atom out by that
          probability distribution by adding 8 Pi^2/3 times the
          rmsd-squared to the B-factor.

### Conference question 15
    - @Béatrice: “AlphaFold produces a per-residue estimate of
      its confidence on a scale from 0 - 100 .
    - This confidence measure is called pLDDT and corresponds to
      the model’s predicted score on the lDDT-Cα metric. It is
      stored in the B-factor fields of the mmCIF and PDB files
      available for download (although unlike a B-factor, higher
      pLDDT is better).”


### AF2 Method: "transformers"
    - AF2 uses an 'attention' approach
    - One big difference between Alphafold1 and Alphafold2 is the
      use of transformers. These are very, very effective at
      sequence problems (such as language, where they
      originated).
        - very good point - I should have emphasized that during
          my talk


### importance of metagenomics
    - excluding the metagenomics database gives a big reduction in
      performance


### Misc Notes
    - having a model is great, but know where you can trust your
      model is as important (ie having a measure of uncertainty)

    - RMSD: "evaluation unit" , individual domain, AF team were
      looking for the full RMSD

    - Support for multimers is experimental
        - no option to specify molecular symmetry-

    - Only proteins!
        - no ligands, RNA, DNA,
