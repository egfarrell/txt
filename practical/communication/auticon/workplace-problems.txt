E. Farrell – Workplace Analysis

I’ve analysed some problems I always face in the workplace.
Maybe it’d help Auticon decide if I'd be a suitable candidate over the long term.

Habitual Problems:

-	Uneven regulation of work effort: I work at 100% maximum for
    months but then "burn out" and find it difficult to continue.
-	I often ask many pedantic questions to fully understand
    a task. (irritates work colleagues)
-	It's hard to understand spoken instructions. e.g. When
    someone explains a task verbally or when tasks are discussed
    at meetings. I need time to process what someone is saying.
-	Problems with prioritising: I tend to work slowly as I'm
    over-meticulous on each aspect of a task. This leads to
    stress as my work colleagues finish their work quicker. (They
    use judgement to prioritise essential items)
-	I often wait a day before contacting a colleague with
    a question. (I get anxious in case I don't express myself
    clearly or the colleague will get annoyed)
-	For creative tasks such as coding a new system, I cannot
    "switch off" and think about it non-stop. I feel stress
    because I must find the most elegant solution. I nearly
    always find a good solution but the stress cost is very high.

An ideal IT environment would be:

-	if task instructions were written down.
-	if technical queries could be answered via email. (reduced
    amount of face-to-face meetings)
-	if the task is "non-creative" in the sense that it involves
    meticulously checking something, rather than coding something
    new. (e.g. Testing, Compliance or Security Penetration
    Testing)
-	if the task was (somewhat) repetitive. I never get bored with
    repetitive IT work. I can carry out a basic process/approach
    again and again until it's perfect.


Examples of IT work that didn't cause stress:

-	At Derby University, I converted hundreds of hand-written
    lecture notes into electronic documents. It took many hours
    of meticulous typing and translating formulas into
    a type-setting language. Although it was repetitive and
    demanding work I enjoyed it. (No uncertainty, no stress)

-	I wrote hundreds of software tests for a Loughborough
    University web-site. This was repetitive meticulous work but
    I found it satisfying. (I could "switch off" at the end of
    every day)
