- There are several major modern theological views on
  Christology, each offering a unique perspective on how to
  understand the nature of Jesus Christ.

### 1. Chalcedonian Christology:
    - rooted in the Chalcedonian Creed (451 AD) and is the
      predominant Christological position in mainstream Christian
      traditions such as Catholicism, Eastern Orthodoxy, and many
      Protestant denominations.
    - It affirms the belief in the "hypostatic union" – the idea
      that Jesus Christ is fully divine and fully human, without
      mixing or confusing the natures. He is one person with two
      distinct natures, divine and human, united in one
      hypostasis (individual existence or reality).

### 2. Kenotic Christology:
    - Kenotic Christology emphasizes the self-emptying (kenosis)
      of Christ as described in Philippians 2:5-8.
    - Proponents of this view suggest that, in the incarnation,
      Christ voluntarily laid aside some aspects of his divine
      attributes to fully experience humanity. This view seeks to
      explain how the divine and human natures of Christ interact
      during his earthly life.

### 3. Neo-Chalcedonian Christology:
    - Building upon Chalcedonian Christology, this view seeks to
      address some of the criticisms and challenges posed to the
      traditional formulation.
    - It emphasizes the importance of maintaining a _dynamic
      relationship_ between the two natures of Christ while
      avoiding any separation or division. This view is often
      associated with theologians like Karl Rahner and von
      Balthasar.

### 4. Social Christology:
    - Social Christology focuses on the communal aspect of
      Christ's identity, emphasizing how Christ is present and
      active within the Christian community.
    - This view highlights Christ's role in creating and
      sustaining a community of believers, often in the context
      of liberation theology and social justice concerns.

### 5. Narrative Christology:
    - Narrative theology views Christology through the lens of
      the Gospel narratives and the overall biblical story. It
      emphasizes the significance of the stories and accounts
      that shape our understanding of Christ, his teachings, and
      his mission.

### 6. Mystical Christology:
    - Mystical theologians explore the experiential and
      contemplative aspects of Christology. They emphasize the
      personal and intimate encounter with Christ, often seeking
      union with the divine through prayer and spiritual
      practices.

### 7. Feminist Christology:
    - Feminist theologians focus on reinterpreting Christology
      from a gender-inclusive and feminist perspective. This
      approach challenges traditional male-centric language and
      imagery used to describe Christ and seeks to highlight the
      importance of women's experiences and perspectives.

### 8. Process Christology:
    - Process theology offers a unique perspective by emphasizing
      the dynamic and relational nature of reality, including the
      divine. Process Christology suggests that Christ's divinity
      is not fixed but emerges from the ongoing relationship
      between God and the world. This view is often associated
      with theologians like Charles Hartshorne and John Cobb Jr.

- These are just a few examples of modern Christological views.
  Each perspective seeks to engage with the mystery of Christ's
  nature and its implications for Christian faith and practice.
  It's important to note that these views are not exhaustive, and
  there are many variations and nuanced positions within each
  category.
