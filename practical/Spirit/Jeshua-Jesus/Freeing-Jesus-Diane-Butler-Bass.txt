### The “Get me out of here” experience

- At the start of Freeing Jesus, Diana Butler Bass describes
  praying in Washington National Cathedral, standing before
  a monumental image of Jesus, when she hears an interior voice
  say, three times, “Get me out of here.”

- I agree with you that the sentence is metaphorical rather than
  a claim of literal auditory revelation. Read theologically and
  psychologically together, several layers are at work.

- First, the voice functions as a critique of confinement. The
  Jesus in the painting is monumental, institutionalized, fixed
  in stone, glass, and authority. 
    - The phrase “get me out of here” names the tension between
      a living, relational Jesus and the ways churches,
      doctrines, and power structures can immobilize him. 
    - The experience dramatizes the book’s core thesis before it
      is argued: Jesus has been captured by systems that claim to
      represent him.

- Second, the repetition matters. Three times evokes biblical
  resonance (Peter’s denial, Samuel’s call, resurrection motifs)
  but also signals insistence. 
    - This is not a fleeting thought but a pressure on
      conscience. Bass is experiencing a moment where her
      inherited religious framework can no longer contain her
      lived faith, and the voice externalizes that inner rupture.

- Third, the sentence is not “get you out of here,” but “get me
  out of here.” That reversal is crucial. 
    - It shifts responsibility to the pray-er. Jesus is not the
      object of devotion but the subject making a demand.
    - Interpreted metaphorically, Bass is recognizing that her
      task is not to defend Christianity as she received it, but
      to participate in freeing the meaning of Jesus from
      cultural captivity.

- Finally, the scene is mystical in the classic sense without
  being supernaturalist. Mystical experiences often arise when
  symbol and self suddenly realign. 
    - The cathedral, the image, her prayer, and her long
      intellectual struggle converge into a single symbolic
      utterance. 
    - The experience authorizes the journey of the book without
      claiming infallibility. Bass never asks the reader to
      believe the voice was literally Jesus; she asks the reader
      to take seriously what it revealed.

- In short: the episode names a vocational turning point. Jesus
  becomes less an object of belief and more a living question
  pressing against inherited forms.


### Introduction: Freeing Jesus
    - The intro establishes the problem: many people feel they
      must choose between Jesus and the church, belief and
      integrity, faith and freedom. Bass argues that the problem
      is not Jesus himself but narrow, frozen images of him.
    - Faith matures by releasing those images rather than
      defending them.

### Chapter 1: Jesus as Friend
    - Faith often begins in intimacy, not doctrine. Bass reflects
      on childhood and early faith, where Jesus is experienced as
      companion and presence. 
    - This chapter affirms emotional connection as a legitimate
      theological starting point rather than something to be
      outgrown.

### Chapter 2: Jesus as Teacher
    - Jesus’s authority lies in wisdom that transforms how we see
      and live. Bass emphasizes parables, ethics, and social
      reversal. 
    - Jesus is not primarily a rule-giver but a wisdom figure
      whose teachings disrupt religious complacency and invite
      moral imagination.

### Chapter 3: Jesus as Savior
    - Salvation is broader than rescue from sin or afterlife
      reward. Bass reframes salvation as healing, liberation, and
      becoming whole. 
    - Jesus saves by restoring people to life, dignity, and
      community here and now, not merely by solving
      a metaphysical problem.

### Chapter 4: Jesus as Lord
    - Commitment does not require domination. Bass wrestles
      honestly with the language of lordship, acknowledging how
      it has been used to enforce hierarchy and control. 
    - She reinterprets lordship as freely chosen allegiance to
      love, justice, and truth rather than submission to
      authoritarian power.

### Chapter 5: Jesus as Way
    - Christianity is a path, not a possession. Drawing on
      pilgrimage and practice, Bass argues that Jesus is known by
      walking with him, not by mastering belief systems. 
    - Faith is dynamic, relational, and shaped through movement,
      doubt, and growth.

### Chapter 6: Jesus as Presence
    - Jesus is not confined to the past or to institutions. In
      this culminating chapter, Bass speaks of Jesus as a living
      presence encountered in prayer, community, compassion, and
      the world itself. This image integrates the others without
      freezing them.

### Conclusion
    - The final message is not to choose one image of Jesus over
      the others, but to let them coexist. A mature faith is
      spacious. Jesus remains alive precisely because no single
      description exhausts him.
