- In modern times, the vast majority of Christian theologians
  adhere to the traditional orthodox view that Jesus is fully
  divine, being the second person of the Trinity and co-equal
  with God the Father.
- However, there have been some individuals / groups who hold
  non-traditional views about the divinity of Jesus. It's
  important to note that these views are still considered outside
  the mainstream of orthodox Christian theology:

### 1. Unitarian Universalists:
    - Unitarian Universalism is a liberal religious movement that
      emphasizes individual spiritual exploration and
      inclusivity. While not a single theology, some Unitarian
      Universalists may hold beliefs that are non-traditional
      within mainstream Christianity, including views that do not
      uphold the traditional understanding of Jesus' divinity.

### 2. John Hick (1922–2012):
    - John Hick was a philosopher of religion who proposed
      a pluralistic understanding of religions. He believed that
      different religious traditions are diverse ways of
      perceiving and responding to the transcendent reality. In
      his later works, Hick challenged the traditional Christian
      understanding of Jesus' unique divinity, suggesting a more
      inclusive perspective.

### 3. Marcus Borg (1942–2015):
    - Marcus Borg was a prominent figure in the Jesus Seminar,
      a group of scholars that aimed to critically assess the
      historicity of the Gospels. Borg's views often leaned
      toward a more metaphorical or symbolic interpretation of
      Jesus' divinity, rather than a literal one.

### 4. John Shelby Spong:
    - John Shelby Spong is a retired Episcopal bishop known for
      his controversial and progressive views on Christianity. He
      has questioned traditional beliefs about Jesus' divinity,
      virgin birth, and resurrection, proposing metaphorical
      interpretations instead.

### 5. Paul Tillich (1886–1965):
    - Tillich was a Christian existentialist theologian who
      explored various aspects of Christian doctrine. While not
      outright denying Jesus' divinity, Tillich's understanding
      of Christology incorporated existential and symbolic
      elements that some may interpret as a departure from
      traditional orthodox views.

- These examples represent a small minority, and it's important
  to emphasize they do not represent the mainstream consensus
  within Christian theology. The vast majority of Christian
  theologians and denominations affirm the orthodox belief in the
  full divinity of Jesus as a central tenet of their faith.


- If you're looking for theologians or scholars who share similar
  perspectives to Marcus Borg on a more metaphorical or symbolic
  interpretation of Jesus and Christianity, you might find
  resonance with the following figures:

### 1. Dominic Crossan:
    - John Dominic Crossan is another prominent member of the
      Jesus Seminar. He's known for his historical Jesus research
      and emphasis on understanding Jesus within his
      socio-cultural context. Crossan often emphasizes the
      symbolic and social justice aspects of Jesus' teachings.

### 2. Karen Armstrong:
    - Karen Armstrong is a religious historian and writer who has
      explored various aspects of religious history, including
      Christianity. She emphasizes the importance of
      understanding religious symbols and metaphors as ways to
      convey deeper truths, rather than focusing solely on
      literal interpretations.

### 3. Don Cupitt:
    - Don Cupitt is a British theologian and philosopher who
      advocates for a non-realist, non-literal interpretation of
      Christianity. He has written extensively about the concept
      of "non-realism" and focuses on the ethical and
      experiential aspects of religious traditions.

### 4. Lloyd Geering:
    - Lloyd Geering is a New Zealand theologian who has expressed
      views that challenge traditional Christian doctrines. He
      advocates for a non-literal, symbolic interpretation of
      religious language and concepts, emphasizing the evolving
      nature of human understanding.

### 5. Harvey Cox:
    - Harvey Cox is a theologian who has explored the
      intersection of religion, culture, and society. His book
      "The Future of Faith" reflects on the changing nature of
      religious belief and emphasizes the importance of adapting
      religious language and concepts to modern contexts.
