# THE TRUTH WILL MAKE US FREE
- Jose Antonio Pagola


- In the first days of January 2008, various texts commenting on
  and judging my book « Jesus. Historical Approach », PPC, Madrid
  2007. Specifically, it is a Letter from Monsignor Demetrio
  Fernández with the title: « Pagola's book will do harm »;
  a brief note from Luis J. Argüello, Vicar of the city of
  Valladolid, entitled " About Jesus of Pagola "; a letter from
  José Rico Pavés, director of the Secretariat of the Episcopal
  Commission for the Doctrine of the Faith (CEE) with the title,
  " An unrecognizable Jesus "; a review by the theologian José
  María Iraburu entitled « Pagola. historical approximation» and
  an extensive work by the theologian José Antonio Sayés,
  « Jesus, historical approximation » by José Antonio Pagola,
  which had already appeared on an Internet space in November
  2007.

- Although there are five texts signed by different authors, in
  reality a similar voice resonates in all of them and they have
  been presented by the diocese of Tarazona forming a block. Not
  only is the approach and treatment of the various issues
  practically identical, but the same expressions and phrases are
  used on various occasions almost literally. The Bishop of
  Tarazona presents them as a set of texts whose content he
  identifies with, approves, and recommends to his faithful.

- This fact allows us to jointly study his position, perfectly
  defined within the current theological pluralism, considering,
  at least, the most important and decisive questions. This is my
  purpose in this writing. The important thing for me is not to
  defend my book. After all, it is a minor and insignificant
  event in the history of our Church. What I am looking for is
  that we not be the theologians or the hierarchy who close the
  doors to simple people for a vital and renewing encounter with
  Jesus and with his Gospel. I do not want to judge the authors
  of these writings or those who strive to spread them. I want to
  hear in these moments the firm call of Jesus to his followers:
  « Do not judge anyone... Do not condemn anyone. sorry» (Luke 6,
  37 – 38). I will limit myself to stating some facts and asking
  some questions. In any case, my disposition now and in the
  future will be to be willing to improve my book taking into
  account the criticisms and suggestions that may be made to me.
  I just want to help people to know, love and follow Jesus
  Christ more faithfully.

### 2. THE TRUE NATURE OF A HISTORICAL STUDY OF JESUS
    - How is it explained that these authors attribute to a book
      that, from its very title, clarifies that it is
      a " historical approach " to Jesus, a whole set of
      doctrines contrary to the Church and even heresies? It
      seems to me that things begin to become clearer when we
      consider what the true nature of historical research on
      Jesus consists of.
    - These authors do not distinguish between "historical
      research" and "Christology"
    - Catholic theology has always sought to differentiate
      between the study of the human dimension of Jesus
      (jesuology) and the study of the Christian faith in Jesus
      Christ, the Son of God made man for our salvation
      (christology). But we have to thank in a special way the
      precisions carried out by John. P. Meier , the most eminent
      Catholic researcher on the historical Jesus in his work
      « A marginal Jew: new vision of the historical Jesus ».
      Divine Word, Estella (1998-2003), p.47-57. This clarifying
      effort by Meier has been accepted very positively by most
      Catholic exegetes (although with some qualifications) and
      his work has been considered by Benedict XVIas
      a "historical-critical exegesis model in which both the
      importance and the limits of this discipline are revealed"
      (Jesús de Nazareth, p. 144). Following Meier above all
      , Catholic researchers today clearly distinguish between
      a " historical study on Jesus ", carried out according to
      the criteria proposed by the Pontifical Biblical Commission
      (Interpretation of the Bible in the Church) in 1993, and
      " Christology ". » which is the dogmatic theology treatise
      that studies and exposes the content of faith in Jesus
      Christ as it is confessed by the Catholic Church.
    - This elementary distinction is not taken into account by
      this group of authors who demand from a study of
      "historical approach" to Jesus what only has to be asked of
      "Christology". Thus, Sayés raises the study of him in this
      way: «So, we will continue the search for Pagola asking
      ourselves what he thinks of Jesus: is he an itinerant
      prophet who speaks to us of God as Father or the Son of God
      in person? And we will deal with it by entering the
      decisive themes of his theology». Later, in a moment of his
      study, he affirms: «what a theologian has to do is not
      eliminate the data of Scripture and Tradition. This is not
      how theology is done. What a theologian has to do is
      understand, as far as possible, the mystery that is
      revealed in them». Dragged, perhaps, by Sayés 's error
      , the rest of the authors analyze my historical research
      book as if it were a treatise on Christology. Thus Iraburu
      comes to say as a conclusion of his work that "Pagola tries
      a historical approach to Jesus, dispensing with it by
      system of everything that the Old Testament, the New
      Testament, Tradition and the apostolic Magisterium have
      taught about Jesus until today " . Rico Paves, at the
      beginning of his analysis he says: «Through the use of
      historical research , the author outlines a comprehensive
      program of revision of the Church 's teaching on Jesus ».
    - This confusion, difficult to describe in a theologian,
      leads these authors to incredible consequences. I only
      point to two. On the one hand, they attribute to me all the
      heresies and doctrinal errors that are not exposed by me in
      my historical research book: denial of the divinity of
      Jesus, denial of the Incarnation of the Word, elimination
      of the virginal conception of Jesus, denial of the
      character supernatural nature of miracles, denial of the
      redemptive character of Jesus' death... and other
      "innumerable doctrines of dogmatic and moral theology...
      that are irreconcilable with the Catholic faith" ( Iraburu
      ). On the other hand, Monsignor FernándezTaking his
      ignorance of what a historical study of Jesus is to
      surprising extremes, he reproaches me for the fact that in
      my book "there is total silence about the reflection that
      the Church has carried out throughout history, particularly
      in the seven ecumenical councils of the undivided Church
      throughout the first millennium».
    - What Pagola says
    - In my book I do not intend, of course, to present
      a Christology. I simply try to articulate a historical
      approach to Jesus taking into account the criteria issued
      by the Pontifical Biblical Commission. The interpretation
      of the Bible in the Church (PPC. Madrid, April 2001). This
      document, when presenting the principles of the
      historical-critical method, affirms that "it is a critical
      method that operates with the help of scientific criteria
      that are as objective as possible in each of its steps"
      (p.36), that is, it does not it operates with the Christian
      faith as an instrument of investigation. It is added that
      "it is an analytical methodthat studies the biblical text
      in the same way as any other text of antiquity and comments
      on it as human language» (p.36). For this reason, what Pope
      John Paul II says in the presentation of the Document is
      very important: «Catholic exegesis does not have its own
      and exclusive method of interpretation but, starting from
      the historical-critical basis, without philosophical or
      other contrary assumptions to the truth of our faith, take
      advantage of all current methods, seeking in each of them
      the seed of the Word» (p.16).
    - This explains why no one has condemned or attributed
      doctrinal errors or heresies to important Catholic
      researchers on Jesus. I am going to give four examples of
      eminent Catholic researchers whose works can be read in
      their Spanish translation and who, along with many others,
      have served as a reference in my work: Meier JP, A marginal
      Jew. New Vision of the Historical Jesus . Estella, Divine
      Word, 2001-2003. This author, professor of New Testament at
      the Catholic University of Washington, has been president
      of the North American Catholic Biblical Association and is
      the Catholic researcher most praised by Pope Benedict XVI.
      2) Gnilka Joachim, Jesus of Nazareth. message and story.
      Barcelona, Herder, 1993. This internationally
      renowned biblical scholar is a professor of New Testament
      exegesis at the Catholic University of Munich. The Pope
      considers his work as one of the "most important and
      recent" on Jesus. 3) Schlosser Jacques, Jesus the Prophet
      of Galilee . Salamanca, Follow me, 2005. He is a professor
      at the Catholic University of Strasbourg and one of the
      most renowned specialists in Jesus and the New Testament.
      4) Barbaglio Giuseppe. Hebrew Jesus of Galilee. Historical
      research . Salamanca, Trinitarian Secretariat, 2003.
      Recently deceased, he was an eminent Italian specialist,
      author of a high-quality biblical production.
    - None of these authors studies the divinity of Jesus, the
      incarnation of the Word, the supernatural nature of
      miracles, the salvific value of Christ's death, the
      sacramental theology of the Eucharist... and yet, no one
      has thought of condemning them. . So that Spanish readers
      know the nature of the investigation of these eminent
      Catholic exegetes, not only not condemned by Rome but
      praised by the Pope, I would like to quote them on some
      issues:
    - On the virginal conception of Jesus: «On its own,
      historical-critical research simply lacks the necessary
      sources and means to reach a definitive conclusion about
      the historicity of the virginal conception as narrated by
      Matthew and Luke. The acceptance or rejection of the
      doctrine will be conditioned by the philosophical and
      theological ideas from which one starts, as well as by the
      weight that is given to the teaching of the Church» ( John
      P. Meier , oc, I, 236). «To say that Jesus was or was not
      conceived in a virginal way is something that is outside
      the field of historical investigation» ( Barbaglio , oc,
      126).
    - On the supernatural nature of miracles . "Did God act
      directly in Jesus' ministry to cause miracles to occur?
      I am not saying that this question is illegal, but simply
      that it is beyond the specific field of the historian or
      exegete» ( Meier , II/2, p.605). "From the testimonies
      analyzed, it cannot be deduced that the healer of Nazareth,
      once again unlike Elijah, tried to legitimize himself in
      this way as a prophet or divine envoy" ( Barbaglio , oc,
      p.247). «The authority of the mission of Jesus, in its
      singular and unique character, cannot be deduced
      historically» ( Gnilka , oc, 322).
    - Questions
    - How is one to explain such a serious error in the analysis
      and condemnation of my book by this group of authors? Is it
      because, according to what they tell me, none of them are
      exegetes or biblical scholars? Is it due to a hasty
      approach?
    - But, then, how can one explain such a resounding, unanimous
      and certain public condemnation, without listening to the
      author and without debating it among theologians? Is not
      the intervention of a bishop particularly unusual? Is it
      pastorally appropriate for the bishop of a diocesan Church
      to dispense with the Episcopal Conference and the author's
      bishop, in order, after an individual reading, to publicly
      condemn a book, three months after its publication, stating
      that "it does not conform to the faith of the Church" and
      that "the Arian temptation" looms over him? Is this the
      best path to evangelically guide believers?

### 3. FORGETTING THE TEACHING OF THE PONTIFICAL BIBLICAL COMMISSION ON THE HISTORICAL-CRITICAL METHOD
    - Perhaps what is most surprising in the criticism of these
      authors is that, to analyze my book, they do not resort at
      any time to the principles and criteria emanating from Rome
      precisely to guide Catholic exegetes in their research.
    - These authors do not take into account the teaching of the
      Pontifical Biblical Commission
    - By not taking into account the historical-critical nature
      of my book, these authors analyze it ignoring the criteria
      of the PCB of Rome on historical exegesis and its
      evaluation. iraburuHe even adopts a suspicious and
      pejorative attitude. It goes like this: «Pagola, therefore,
      attempts a "historical approach" to Jesus, twenty centuries
      away, using only the historical-critical method, with other
      complementary methods – the sociological approach, cultural
      anthropology, some keys to the theology of liberation and
      feminism. I don't know if he realizes that what he calls
      "complementary methods" are precisely some of the ones that
      the PCB offers and values so that we Catholic
      exegetes can use them correctly: Historical-critical method
      (p.33-39 ); The sociological approach (p.55-57); Approach
      through cultural anthropology (57 – 58); Liberationist
      approach (61-64); Feminist approach (61-66).
    - On the other hand, they also do not indicate any historical
      research work on Jesus, carried out by Catholic authors and
      that can be presented as a correct application of the
      principles of the PCB, in contrast to my book. I do not
      quite understand why these authors condemn my work and do
      not do the same with the works of John P. Meier, J. Gnilka,
      J. Schlosser, G. Barbaglio. Does my book distance itself
      from these works, forgetting the teaching of Rome?
    - Finally, I have to say that I do not find in these authors
      the enormously positive and grateful assessment that
      Benedict XVI makes of the historical-critical method to
      approach the figure of Jesus. The Pope's words are these:"
      The historical method... is and remains a dimension of
      exegetical work that cannot be renounced " (p.11). «The
      history of the factual is an essential part of the
      Christian faith , it must face the historical method. Faith
      itself demands it ” (p.11). Finally, speaking of his book,
      he says: «This book is not written against modern exegesis
      , but rather with great gratitude for how much it has given
      us and gives us. It has provided us with a large amount of
      material and knowledge through which the figure of Jesus
      can be made present to us with vivacity and depth.that
      a few decades ago we could not even imagine» (p.19-20).
    - Naturally, the Pope as a good theologian says that the
      historical-critical method is "indispensable", but it has
      its limits . The Christian faith "cannot arise from the
      mere historical method." For this reason, J. Ratzinger
      humbly explains his work, not easy but undoubtedly
      necessary, with these words: «I have only tried, beyond the
      merely historical-critical interpretation, to apply new
      methodological criteria that allow us to make a properly
      theological interpretation . of the Bible, which require
      faith, without wanting or being able in any way to renounce
      historical seriousness» (p.20).
    - Confusing a "historical-critical investigation" with
      a "Christology" and not taking into account the principles
      and criteria of the PCB, can have serious consequences of
      a pastoral nature that we theologians should avoid. This is
      what, in my opinion, can happen with the letter from
      Monsignor Fernández. He writes because he considers that
      "many of the readers will not have the evidence" to read my
      book correctly. But in his letter he does not offer his
      faithful any "elements of judgement" but puts them on their
      guard, alerting them to the dangers that may lurk his faith
      ". Even more, I think that it can confuse them, since it
      guides them to read as "Christology" a book that is only
      a "historical approach" to Jesus, and presents it as a work
      written "according to the technique of demythologization
      promoted by Bultman" Today In today's theology, "Bultmann's
      demythologizing exegesis" is perfectly distinguished from
      a work of "historical approximation" to the human dimension
      of Jesus in which we Christians discover and confess the
      incarnate Son of God.
    - What Pagola says
    - When in the prologue of my book I notice that "I am writing
      this book from the Catholic Church", I am not saying it
      just to say it. For this reason, from the beginning I took
      care to study the Roman document of the PCB « The
      interpretation of the Bible in the Church » in order to
      adjust my work to its guidelines. For the same reason,
      I thought that I should offer my readers, in ANNEX 2, these
      general criteria of Catholic interpretation, even before
      the technical criteria of historicity (ANNEX 5).
    - Perhaps, today I would add what the Roman document says in
      the section « Exegesis and dogmatic theology ». On the one
      hand, “in order to interpret Scripture with scientific
      accuracy and precision, theologians need the work of
      exegetes. For their part, exegetes must direct their
      investigations in such a way that the study of Sacred
      Scripture can effectively be like "the soul of theology."
      Then it is added «Exegetes can help theologians to avoid
      two extremes: on the one hand, dualism , which completely
      separates a doctrinal truth from its linguistic expression,
      considered as unimportant; and on the other,
      fundamentalism, who, confusing the human and the divine,
      considers as revealed truth even the aspects of human
      expressions».
    - I believe that the position of Jose María Rovira Belloso
      , a seasoned theologian and not at all suspected of
      frivolity, is very clarifying when he presents my book.
      Rovira Belloso speaksIn the first place, from books about
      Jesus that "have been born from the need of the believer
      and theologian to express his faith in Jesus because, after
      reading the sources of his life, the conviction arises that
      the best interpretation of his figure is the believer:
      Jesus, Son of God. He then speaks of books written by
      authors «who have been moved by the desire to rigorously
      present the reliable historical elements that allow us to
      contemplate the "biographical" elements of Jesus, who, by
      added grace, tends to deliver those who fix their gaze on
      he, the secret of his most divine mystery. Both go from
      history to faith, following the only Jesus, the Christ,
      since only one is the person of the historical Jesus and
      the Christ of faith. Such diverse books do not harm each
      other, since each one fills a peculiar area..., Thus,J.
      Ratzinger does not hinder my once again laudatory
      recommendation of José Antonio Pagola 's book » (Vida
      Nueva, November 3-9, 2007. Nº 2587, p.46).
    - Questions
    - Is a historical investigation of Jesus that respects and
      remains within its own limits legitimate, or must it also
      be required to make explicit the dogmatic faith, typical of
      a treatise on Christology? Can a "historical approach" to
      Jesus be publicly condemned without resorting to the
      guidelines of Rome at all? From what other criteria? Why is
      my book condemned and not other works of Catholic exegetes
      that circulate among us? Is this hasty, theologically
      ill-founded, public condemnation, without any notification
      to its author, the best way to make truth in a climate of
      dialogue and communion?

### 4. BREAK BETWEEN THE HISTORICAL JESUS AND THE CHRIST OF FAITH?
    - What the authors say
    - Two of these authors generically and forcefully accuse me
      of introducing into my work a break between the historical
      Jesus and the Christ of faith. Rico Pavés says: «The great
      difficulty offered by Pagola's approach lies in the rupture
      marked between the Jesus of history and the Christ of
      faith». Argüello, for his part, affirms: "As the title
      already states, it is a historical approximation and to
      carry it out the author establishes a break between
      historical research and faith." This accusation is possible
      when historical study is asked to exceed its limits and
      come to establish and found the Christian faith.
    - What Pagola says
    - There is something worrisome in the discourse of these
      authors. His way of reflecting seems to presuppose that the
      Christian faith has sprung directly and immediately from
      the preaching and actions of Jesus, that is, from the
      historical Jesus, without the need to resort to the fact of
      the Resurrection and the consequent Easter experience.
      I think that at no point in their speech do they remember
      that the resurrection of Christ is a fundamental and
      decisive fact in the origin of the Christian faith.
      However, it is a serious Christological error to base the
      Christological faith only on the pre-Easter Jesus. I find
      it surprising to see that Rico Pavésadmits that I affirm
      that "Jesus is the incarnation of God" or that Jesus is
      "the man in whom God has become incarnate", but points out
      that these affirmations appear "by exposing what the
      followers of Jesus, once resurrected , expose about Jesus”?
    - I actually do it like this. And I can't do it any other
      way. Only after talking about the resurrection of Jesus and
      studying the paschal experience, do I expose the
      Christology that emerges in the Gospels and also in the
      Christological titles attributed to Jesus in Christian
      communities. That is the moment. That is his place. Not
      before the resurrection.
    - Indeed, in its strictest sense, a study of the Jesus story
      should end when the Jesus story ends: at the execution on
      Calvary in the year 30. That is why most Jesus researchers
      end their books with the Crucifixion chapter. Thus the
      Catholic Gnilka , after studying the execution of Jesus,
      ends his research with a brief epilogue where he simply
      says that "the story of the resurrection of the Crucified
      from the dead no longer belongs to the earthly history of
      Jesus of Nazareth" (p. .389). The same does Schlosser, who
      says thus: «the event of the resurrection, since the
      sources present it as something totally different from
      a return to a previous life, deserves to be called
      metahistorical or transhistorical. It escapes, therefore,
      from the hands of the historian (p.280).
    - Despite all this, I have not wanted to finish my book on
      Calvary. I have added two other chapters that go beyond the
      earthly history of Jesus: chapter 14 on the risen Jesus and
      chapter 15 on the first names and title that begins to be
      attributed to Jesus. I do it for pedagogical reasons, so as
      not to introduce a break between the historical Jesus and
      the Christ of faith, and to invite Christians and
      non-Christians to take a stand, not simply before the Jesus
      who lived in Galilee, but before Jesus Christ, it is that
      is, before Jesus as he was lived by his followers after the
      paschal experience.
    - Naturally, let no one claim to find here a systematic
      Christology. Not even in these chapters do I abandon the
      historical perspective. Chapter 14 is not a theology on the
      resurrection of Jesus, but a historical approach, to
      approach three questions: 1) what do the first followers
      mean when they start talking about the resurrection of
      Jesus; 2) what can we say historically about the process
      that led them to believe in the risen Jesus; 3) what
      consequences they were drawing from the resurrection on the
      person of Jesus they had met in Galilee. Nor is chapter 15
      a systematic Christology,

    - Questions
    - Are these authors unaware that all Catholic research
      affirms the same thing that J. Ratzinger has stated with
      the precision of an informed theologian: "Christological
      hermeneutics... presupposes a decision of faith and cannot
      arise from the mere historical-critical method" (p. 15)? .

    - What Pagola says
    - My exposition on the attitude of Jesus before his death is

      as he has always understood his life: a service to the

      feature that defines him from the beginning to the end, the
      one that inspires and gives ultimate meaning to his living
      and his dying. This is probably his basic attitude when
      facing his death »(p.351).
    - Salvific value of his death . «What salvific value did
      Jesus attribute to his death? Could he intuit what his
      violent and painful death would bring to the kingdom of
      God? He had lived offering "salvation" to those who lived
      suffering from evil and disease, giving "welcome" to those
      who were excluded by society and religion, giving God's
      free "forgiveness" to sinners and lost people, unable to
      return to their friendship. He not only proclaimed the life
      and salvation of God. At the same time he offered them. He
      did it out of his trust in God's incredible love for all.
      He lived his service healing, welcoming, blessing, offering
      free forgiveness and God's salvation. Everything points to
      thinking that he died as he had lived.His death was the
      ultimate and supreme service to God's project, his greatest
      contribution to the salvation of all ” (p.351-352).
    - In the context of the last supper . «In that cup that Jesus
      is passing and offering to everyone, Jesus sees something
      "new" and peculiar that he wants to explain: " This cup is
      the New Covenant in my blood . My death will open a new
      future for you and for all". Jesus does not think only of
      his closest disciples. At that decisive and crucial moment,
      the horizon of his gaze becomes universal: the New
      Covenant, the definitive kingdom of God will be for many,
      "for all"... These words express what his death will now
      be: he has "delivered " for offering everyone, in the name
      of God, welcome, healing, hope and forgiveness. He now
      gives his life to death offering everyone the salvation of
      the Father » (p.366-367).
    - Theology on the Crucifixion . «Apparently, Jesus did not
      elaborate any theory about his death, he did not theology
      about his crucifixion. He saw it as a logical consequence
      of his unconditional dedication to God's project »(p.350).
      "There is no certain saying that Jesus attributes to his
      death the meaning of a sacrifice of expiation" (p.351, note
      45). Jesus did not interpret his death from a sacrificial
      perspective. He did not understand it as an atonement
      sacrifice offered to the Father. It was not his language.
      He had never linked the kingdom of God to the cultic
      practices of the temple; he had never understood his
      service to God's project as a cultic sacrifice. It would
      have been strange if, in order to give meaning to his
      death, he resorted at the end of his life to categories
      from the world of atonement »(p.350).
    - H. Schürmann , a German Catholic theologian, praised by J.
      Ratzinger in various writings, «has coined the term
      " pro-existence " to evoke this existential attitude of
      Jesus in his life and death, prior to all theology
      elaborated later in the Christian communities » (p.352,
      note 48).

      in his New Testament.
    - A confused theological view
    - The first thing that is observed in the analysis of these
      authors is a confused and disorienting theological vision:
    - There is no difference between what is a historical study
      of Jesus and what is a treatise on Christology. That is why
      my historical research book is required what can only be
      asked of Christology.
    - Precisely for this reason, historical-critical exegesis
      is confused with "canonical exegesis" which is the
      interpretation that, inspired by faith, studies a text
      using the entire set of the Catholic Bible. In fact,
      Argüello asks me to use «canonical exegesis» in this
      historical work, forgetting that the PCB warns that this
      approach «is not intended to replace the
      historical-critical method, but rather to complete it»,
      when it comes to «leading to good term a theological task
      of interpretation .
    - The historical Jesus and the Christ of faith are
      confused, without differentiating the faith of the
      disciples in Galilee and the new faith that originates and
      develops from the fact of Jesus' resurrection. Even J.
      Ratzinger , who in his book uses "canonical exegesis"
      referring to all the texts of the New Testament, when in
      chapter 8 he studies "the images of the Gospel of John (not
      the images of Jesus), says that he is going to focus the
      focus "on the image of Jesus that the Fourth Gospel gives
      us, which in several aspects is very different from that
      offered by the Synoptics" (p.261).
    - Lastly, they do not precisely qualify their sentence.
      They indistinctly affirm that my book goes against "the
      doctrine of the Church", against "dogma", against "the
      Catholic faith", against "the teaching of the Church",
      against "the Catechism of the Catholic Church"... It would
      be expected, at least, a precise distinction between
      "dogmatic doctrine", "certain teaching of the Church" and
      "theological interpretation".
    - Reading prone to fundamentalism
    - At the bottom of the theological vision of these authors,
      it seems to me that I discover a reading that does not
      avoid the risks of fundamentalism, so rejected by the
      Pontifical Biblical Commission of Rome.
    - According to this important Roman Document, the
      fundamentalist reading "opposes the use of the
      historical-critical method, as well as any other scientific
      method for the interpretation of Scripture" (p.67).
    - This fundamentalist reading, "rejecting the historical
      character of biblical revelation, becomes incapable of
      fully accepting the truth of the Incarnation itself" (p.68)
    - «As far as the gospels are concerned, fundamentalism does
      not take into account the growth of the evangelical
      tradition, but naively confuses the final study of this
      tradition (what the evangelists have written) with the
      initial study (the actions and words of Jesus of history»
      (p.69)
    - «The fundamentalist approach is dangerous, because it
      seduces people who are looking for biblical answers to
      their vital problems. It can deceive them, offering pious
      but illusory interpretations... Fundamentalism tacitly
      invites a form of thought suicide» (p.69-70).

### 10. BY WAY OF CONCLUSION
    - A wish. 
        - I would like bishops and theologians, biblical scholars
          and exegetes, in spite of our reasonable differences,
          to take into account, in approaching Jesus, this
          important criterion of the PCB: «A correct knowledge of
          the biblical text is only accessible to those who have
          an affinity lived with that of which the text speaks».
          We will all know Jesus better if we follow him every
          day with more passion and fidelity.
    - A call. 
        - I ask all those who want to defend me, not to do so by
          attacking or condemning anyone. You can be with me
          without being against anyone. We have to purify our
          mood, our attitudes and our very little evangelical
          language many times. Only a healthy Church can announce
          the Gospel of Jesus in a cordial and clean way.
    - A question. 
        - Those of us who think we are wise and knowledgeable,
          should we not be more attentive to what the simple
          understand? Let us remember that, according to Jesus,
          the Father likes to hide these things from the wise and
          understanding and make them known to the simple.
    - An approach. 
        - Always trying to find what can lead us to a more
          faithful knowledge and adherence to that Jesus in whom
          the Mystery of God has been incarnated and revealed,
          I am considering the advisability of writing a NOTE in
          the prologue of my work: "FOR CHRISTIANS", that
          explains and guides believers about the nature of my
          book, its purpose, its virtues and its limits.
    - An availability. 
        - No reviews or studies of my book have yet appeared in
          specialized theological or biblical exegesis magazines.
          Naturally, I will be very attentive to the assessment
          that is made of my work and, above all, to criticism
          and suggestions. This way I will be able to improve the
          book and, furthermore, it will be able to be felt as
          more of everyone. The important thing is to help us
          know Jesus more.

- Jose Antonio Pagola
- San Sebastian, January 15, 2008


### Important bits
    - The resurrection is something that has happened to Jesus.
      Something that has occurred in the crucified, not in the
      imagination of his followers. This is everyone's
      conviction. 
    - The resurrection is a real fact, not the product of fantasy
      or the result of his reflection on him. Nor is it a way of
      saying that your faith in Jesus has been reawakened. It is
      true that a new faith in Jesus has sprung up in the hearts
      of the disciples, but his resurrection is a previous fact,
      which precedes everything his followers have been able to
      live after. 
    - It is precisely the event that has ripped them out of their
      bewilderment and frustration, transforming their adherence
      to Jesus at the root» (p.416). 
    - «For the first Christians, above any other representation
      or mental scheme, the resurrection of Jesus is an act of
      God who, with his creative force, rescues him from death to
      introduce him to the fullness of his own life. 
    - This is how the first Christian confessions and the first
      preachers repeat it over and over again. To put it in some
      way, God welcomes Jesus into the very interior of death,
      introducing him to all his creative force »(p.418).
