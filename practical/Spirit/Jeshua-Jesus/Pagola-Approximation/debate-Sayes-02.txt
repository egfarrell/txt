# THE RENEWED EDITION OF PAGOLA'S BOOK ABOUT JESUS (PPC 2008/9)
    - We are facing the renewed edition of the book by JA Pagola
      Jesús, aproximación histórico , which bears the nihil
      obstat of the Bishop of San Sebastián JM Uriarte and which
      presents some corrections with respect to the first. The
      bishops have not yet ruled on him.
    - For my part, I am interested in verifying if Pagola has
      changed his idea about Jesus or if he continues to maintain
      the same idea. This is the same posture she had. Actually
      the new book provides only a longer presentation; The rest
      of the text, except for some exceptions that we will point
      out, brings us the same content. The corrections have been
      made to respond to the criticisms he had received of the
      first edition.
    - In the presentation, the author tells us about the method
      he has followed, which is the historical-critical one. The
      Jesus of history is not the Christ of faith. And the
      historical-critical method is necessary and essential if we
      really want to know what Jesus was, although it does not
      allow us to know the total reality of him. This method is
      autonomous and dispenses with faith, although it does not
      deny it. Furthermore, faith can influence research to be
      done better. Historical research cannot, by itself, awaken
      faith in Jesus (8). Now, this historical-critical study
      would have to end with the death of Jesus. The
      resurrection, he says, does not belong to the life of
      Jesus, because it is passing into the life of God (17).
      However, "something" happened and it is difficult to
      explain: an experience that the apostles had that Jesus was
      still alive after death (17) and,
    - We have to come back to Jesus in a direct and critical way.
      "Jesus invites us to follow his experience of a God the
      Father more human and greater than all our theories:
      a saving God and friend" (16). «We begin to find ourselves
      with Jesus when we begin to trust God as he trusted, when
      we believe in love as he believed...» (20).

### The baptism of Jesus
    - If we start with the public life of Jesus, we find the
      scene of the baptism on the banks of the Jordan by John the
      Baptist. Jesus at that time did not have a project of his
      own, but he made John's project his own: all the people
      must confess their sins and radically convert to God. John
      preached a conversion baptism for the forgiveness of sins.
      And there is no doubt that Jesus was baptized by John. But
      at this moment a voice from heaven is heard: "You are my
      beloved Son", when the heavens were opened and he saw the
      Spirit descending on him in the form of a dove (Mc 1,9-10).
    - Now, according to Pagola, this is not a revelation from God
      that expresses the identity of Jesus and his consecration
      for the mission, but what Mark intends is to compensate for
      the fact that Jesus was baptized by John with an indication
      that Jesus he lived a strange religious experience with
      those words, so that he comes to be "the strongest" that
      the Baptist spoke of (84). Therefore, it is not an
      objective revelation, but Jesus lives a religious
      experience (84). From here, Jesus will make his own
      project, presenting God not as a judge, but as a Father.
      But Pagola forgets, in relation to the objectivity of the
      revelation, that in Saint John there appears a witness of
      the voice who is John the Baptist (Jn 1,32); the same
      qualified witness of other realities in the prologue (Jn
      1,15.30.34). And it happens too

### God's kingdom
    - Indeed, the preaching of Jesus according to all the
      exegetes is the arrival of the Kingdom of God. With the
      Kingdom, God is in our midst as a Father who loves, who
      forgives, who saves, who frees the poor and oppressed. It
      is about a God who enjoys forgiving and who with his love
      and his forgiveness breaks the logic of the Pharisees.
    - For Pagola, the Kingdom is reduced exclusively to social
      justice. Pagola rebels against those who make the Kingdom
      of God something private and spiritual that occurs in the
      depths of the person when he opens himself to the love of
      God (105). No, the Kingdom is a liberating force that tries
      to cure suffering, disease and poverty. The enemy to fight
      is the evil that reigns in the world. Jesus proclaims God's
      salvation by healing. God is a friend of life and wants to
      generate a healthier society: heal, free from evil, get rid
      of depression, heal religion. That is the Kingdom (108).
      God comes to abolish misery, so that men can recover their
      dignity. God does not tolerate the suffering of the poor.
      And things have to change.
    - In this context, Jesus speaks of the devil, "symbol of
      evil." He stands in favor of those who suffer and against
      evil, since the Kingdom of God consists in freeing everyone
      from what prevents them from living in a dignified and
      happy way (108). In Pagola it does not appear that the
      Kingdom is identified with the person of Christ. "It is not
      his religion or explicit adherence to Jesus that leads to
      the Kingdom of God, but his help to those in need" (203).
      He does not speak of grace either, since if Christ is not
      God (as we shall see) he cannot deify us. The first
      dimension of the Kingdom is reduced to healing and fighting
      against injustice: "God defends those whom no one defends"
      (133).
    - Nor does it speak of the reality of repentance to enter the
      Kingdom, as we see in the parable of the prodigal son:
      «Father, I have sinned against heaven and against you! I do
      not deserve to be called your son anymore. Treat me like
      one of your hired hands" (Lk 15:21). Something that we also
      see in the parable of the Pharisee and the publican: the
      publican was justified because he asked for forgiveness (Lk
      18,9-14). He forgives the good thief because he has asked
      for forgiveness (Lk 23,19). "The surprising thing," says
      our author, "is that Jesus welcomes sinners without
      previously demanding their repentance" (217). And neither
      does he warn, when speaking of the Kingdom, of the
      possibility of damnation for those who reject it (Mt
      11,22-29; Jn 8,4; 3,16-21). God's forgiveness is
      unconditional, it is not conditioned on a subsequent
      positive response. These are his words:
    - «This forgiveness that Jesus offers has no conditions. His
      therapeutic action does not follow the paths of the law:
      define guilt, call for repentance, achieve change, and
      offer conditional forgiveness to a subsequent positive
      response. Jesus follows the ways of the Kingdom: he offers
      welcome and friendship, gives God's forgiveness and trusts
      in his mercy, that he will know how to recover his lost
      sons and daughters. He approaches, welcomes them and begins
      with them a path towards God that is only sustained by his
      infinite compassion. No one on this earth has made a sign
      more full of hope, a more gratuitous and more absolute sign
      of God's forgiveness.
    - Jesus places everyone, sinners and just, before the
      bottomless abyss of God's forgiveness. There are no longer
      just with rights against sinners without rights. From the
      compassion of God, Jesus poses everything differently: the
      Kingdom of God is offered to all; only those who do not
      accept his mercy are excluded» (218).
    - If I understand correctly, Pagola means that God forgives
      without conditions, without the commitment of a subsequent
      positive response. The Kingdom is offered to all. Only
      those who do not accept his mercy are condemned. Therefore,
      it is possible to take advantage of his mercy without
      a commitment to change. But what regret is that? How can
      you accept God's mercy without repenting and making the
      resolution to change your life?

### Miracles and exorcisms
    - Pagola doesn't talk about miracles, he prefers to talk
      about cures. Now, what do his cures consist of? Christ,
      with them, wants to show the compassionate love of the
      Father. Also other prophets such as Elisha and Elijah had
      made them, and Jesus makes them as a sign of the arrival of
      the Kingdom of God. In reality, what Christ does is heal by
      the force of his word and the gestures of his hands: he
      touches and transmits trust (176) and thus Christ awakens
      trust in God, rouses the sick from isolation and
      hopelessness, and it is that trust in God that Jesus
      transmits the one who heals (177). "His power of him to
      awaken unknown energies in the human being created the
      conditions that made possible the recovery of health"
      (175). Faith belongs, therefore, to the same healing
      process. When trust is awakened in a sick person, healing
      takes place. It is faith that awakens the unknown
      possibilities. Jesus worked in the hearts of the sick so
      that they would trust in God (173).
    - Jesus also performs exorcisms. Those people believed in
      diabolical possession, but "possession was a complex
      strategy used in an unhealthy way by oppressed people to
      defend themselves from an unbearable situation" (180). It
      was a sick way to rebel against Roman subjugation and
      domination by the powerful (180). And logically the Kingdom
      of God has to cure the evil that manifests itself in this
      world. Miracles, in any case, are not proof of God's power.

### The identity of Jesus
    - Pagola dedicates chapter 11 to the identity of Jesus. And
      he says of him that he is "a faithful believer" (313). But
      in the new edition he brings two notes in which he affirms:
      «Jesus acted moved by his experience of God and invited the
      people and his followers to believe and welcome God with
      the same confidence with which he did. Jesus' relationship
      with God made a deep impression” (313, n. 1). And then he
      adds: «naturally, our work of historical approximation to
      Jesus does not prejudge in any way what the doctrine of the
      Church affirms or Christology studies about the filial and
      messianic consciousness of Jesus, nor about the peculiar
      relationship that the Son of God incarnate lives with his
      Father in an unrepeatable singularity, nor on the
      legitimacy or non-legitimacy of the attribution of faith to
      Jesus Christ and in what sense.
    - In other words, historical research must leave the study of
      the divine consciousness of Jesus for dogmatics. And so,
      from the historical-critical point of view, Jesus «does not
      intend at any time to replace the traditional doctrine of
      God with a new one. His God is the God of Israel: the only
      God, the creator of heaven and earth, the savior of the
      beloved people, the close God of the alliance in whom the
      Israelites believe »(314). He experiences this God as
      Father (Abba) and Jesus makes him the center of his life.
      The entire life of Jesus exudes this trust. Jesus lives
      abandoning himself to God (321). Thus, we think that when
      the evangelists call Jesus Son of man, Lord, Incarnate
      Word, Son of God (chap. 15) they confess something that has
      no support in the history of Jesus.
    - Pagola thus consecrates the radical distinction between the
      Jesus of history and the Christ of faith. We believe, on
      the contrary, that the divinity of Christ can be proven
      from the historical-critical point of view, studying the
      lodges in which he is placed as the center of religion and
      the key to salvation. Likewise, there is his dominion over
      the Sabbath, the law and his superiority over the Temple
      and his claim to forgive sins in his name (the implicit
      Christology, of which Pagola does not speak). And,
      likewise, the use of the title of Son of man (which the
      evangelists do not invent) and the condition of his only
      Son.
    - Curiously, in this new edition he maintains as possibly
      historical the scene in Mc 2,5-10 in which Jesus speaks of
      the forgiveness of sins (216, n. 65), when in the previous
      edition he had denied its historicity (which gives us the
      idea of the arbitrariness with which Pagola
      uses the historical-critical method). But he continues to
      forget that Jesus is therefore accused of blaspheming, as
      on many other occasions (Jn 5,18; 10,33; 8,58; 19,7). The
      primitive community would not have invented these
      accusations if they did not know Jesus' confession of his
      divinity (discontinuity criterion).
    - In this reduction, Jesus is no more than a faithful
      believer, although the earlier words of the first edition
      (379) saying that Jesus had never claimed of himself that
      he was God in a transcendent sense have been removed.

### The passion
    - In any case, Pagola comes to say that Jesus ends up on the
      cross not by the will of the Father or by making
      a sacrifice of expiation. He did not come to repair a God
      offended by sin, but to give himself totally for the
      Kingdom of God (362). Jesus died as he lived. The Father
      does not demand reparation. The Father does not want his
      beloved Son to be killed and what he does is accompany him
      to the cross. The Father does not seek the ignominious
      death of his Son, nor does Jesus offer his blood to the
      Father knowing that it will be pleasing to him (446-447).
      The Father and the Son in the crucifixion are united facing
      evil together until the last consequences, so that, in the
      Resurrection, God has shown that he was with the Crucified.
      It is not, then, a just God who does not forgive if the
      offended honor is not returned to him. No atonement
      sacrifice.
    - As we can see, in this way the whole mystery of Christ's
      redemption disappears. Everything is explained naturally.
      But the point is that Scripture constantly tells us that it
      was the Father's will that Christ go to the cross. I will
      only cite three texts of the many that appear. Christ asks
      the Father in the garden to take away the chalice of the
      Passion and adds: "but not my will but yours be done" (Mt
      26,39). In Jn 12,27 we read: «Father, deliver me from this
      hour, but for this I have come». We also read in Flp 2,6-8
      that Christ, even though he was divine, lowered himself by
      obeying to the point of death and death on the cross. There
      are many more texts that we could have cited.
    - In the context of the Passion, we have to talk about the
      Eucharist, since in it Christ sacramentally advanced the
      sacrifice of the cross to leave the Church the memorial of
      his death and resurrection. This is a disappointing point
      in Pagola, as it reduces the Eucharist to a farewell
      dinner, which suggests the final banquet of the Kingdom. In
      it Jesus wanted to signify that his death was not going to
      destroy anyone's death, that his death was not going to
      prevent the arrival of the Kingdom. And at the time of
      breaking the bread, what Jesus wants to imply is that we
      must see him in the pieces of that bread delivered to the
      end. That bread and that wine will remind them of the total
      dedication of Jesus until death and will evoke the final
      feast of the Kingdom (379).
    - It is therefore a memory and an evocation. It says nothing
      of its sacrificial sense. How is he going to say it if he
      doesn't admit that the death of Christ had it? Nor does it
      say anything of what St. Paul affirms regarding the real
      presence: that the cup is communion with the blood of
      Christ and that the bread is communion with his body (1Cor
      10,16) to the point of affirming that the one who eats the
      bread or drinks the cup of the Lord unworthily he becomes
      guilty of the body and blood of the Lord (1Cor 11,27). He
      also forgets the words of Christ in the Gospel of John,
      when he affirms that if we do not eat the flesh of the Son
      and drink his blood, we have no life in us (Jn 6,53-54).
      Pagola even affirms that the framework of the Eucharistic
      celebration was not that of the Jewish paschal dinner.

### The resurrection
    - Pagola had already said in the prologue that the
      Resurrection does not belong to the life of Jesus, because
      he had entered into the life of God, but that "something"
      had happened to the apostles: they had had an experience
      that he was still alive. Pagola maintains that the
      Resurrection is real but not historical, that is, it has
      not taken place in history, because it is a reality that
      transcends it (430). We agree that it is not a Resurrection
      like that of Lazarus who returns to earthly life and death.
      The Resurrection of Christ is transcendent because with his
      glorious body he has definitively defeated death. But he
      has left traces in history: empty tomb and apparitions.
    - He pays part of the apparitions to speak after the empty
      tomb. Why? Because he understands that everything comes
      down to an "experience" of faith (that's how he interprets
      the apparitions) and the tomb is a reality that basically
      can be dispensed with. "Something" has happened to the
      apostles (432): "they have gone through a process that has
      not only revived the faith they had in Jesus, but has
      opened them to a new and unexpected experience of his
      presence among them" (432). That was "a grace of God"
      (438). They feel forgiven and accepted by the Father.
    - And let us go to the empty tomb. Speaking about him, Pagola
      says: "we do not know if (Jesus) ended up in a common grave
      like so many of the executed or if José de Arimathea could
      have done something to bury him in a tomb in the
      surrounding area" (443). But the discovery of the empty
      tomb is not decisive. What is decisive is not finding him
      but the revelation that is made about him: "Jesus of
      Nazareth, the crucified, has been raised by God" (444).
      What matters is that Jesus' disciples experienced him as
      alive from faith.
    - Speaking of all these accounts, Pagola says: “more than
      historical information, what we find in these accounts is
      early Christian preaching about the resurrection of Jesus.
      Everything suggests that it was not his empty tomb that
      generated faith in the risen Christ, but the "encounter"
      that his followers experienced, who experienced him full of
      life after death »(444).
    - The first Christian creed coined these words: "Christ died
      for our sins according to the Scriptures" (1Cor 15,3). But
      God the Father could not ask for the blood of his Son to
      forgive sins, nor can we think of a sacrifice of expiation.
      What God the Father did was accompany Christ in his pain
      (447), he was with Jesus, behind his pain was the saving
      force of God. Jesus died trusting in the Father and the
      Father welcomed him into his unfathomable life. God does
      not demand the suffering of his Son in order to forgive. It
      is not an atonement sacrifice and in death you are not
      suffering any punishment from God. Jesus bore the suffering
      unjustly inflicted on him by men and the Father bore the
      suffering suffered by his beloved Son (450).
    - conclusion
    - The Jesus of Pagola was a faithful believer in God the
      Father in whom he deposited and abandoned his life. His God
      is the God of Israel that he experiences as a Father. From
      historical research it cannot be concluded that he was
      aware of being God, and his divinity belongs to the first
      confession of the evangelists. Thus, he establishes
      a radical break between the Jesus of history and the Christ
      of faith, which means that it was the Church that invented
      the divinity of Christ. It would have been the evangelists
      who confessed him as Lord, Son of Man, transcendent Son of
      God, Incarnate Word, etc. (457 et seq.).
    - The historical-critical method is used in a reductive and
      frequently arbitrary way, ignoring everything that can
      manifest in the story of Jesus the confession of his divine
      transcendence. With the historical-critical method, used
      without prejudice, it is possible to arrive at the
      confession of divinity by Jesus. As one more example of the
      arbitrariness of his use of the historical-critical method,
      he goes on to say that, according to many researchers, the
      primitive community surpassed the confession of Jesus as
      the son of man (which only meant that he was a human
      being), with the vision of Son of man (which appears in
      a transcendent way in Dn) (476-477), when the primitive
      community never called Jesus Son of man in any of their
      confessions (discontinuity criterion).

- Jose Antonio Sayes
