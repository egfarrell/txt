# Legion of Mary
    - The Legion of Mary is a Catholic lay apostolic organization
      dedicated to evangelization and spiritual works of mercy.
    - It was founded in Dublin, Ireland, on September 7, 1921, by
      Frank Duff, a devout layman who was inspired by the model
      of St. Louis de Montfort’s Marian spirituality and
      devotion.

### Origins and Purpose

- Frank Duff established the Legion to promote deeper personal
  sanctification among Catholics and to encourage active lay
  participation in the Church’s mission. The organization is
  modeled after military-style discipline and structure, but its
  "battle" is a spiritual one—bringing souls to Christ through
  the intercession of the Blessed Virgin Mary.

### Structure and Activities

- The Legion operates through `praesidia` (local groups), which
  consist of active and auxiliary members.

    - Active members engage in works such as door-to-door
      evangelization, visiting the sick and elderly, catechesis,
      and assisting in parish activities.
    - Auxiliary members support the mission through prayer,
      especially the Rosary.

### Spirituality and Influence

- The Legion emphasizes total consecration to Mary as a means of
  drawing closer to Christ. Its influence has spread globally,
  and it remains one of the largest lay movements in the Church,
  active in over 170 countries.

### Legacy and Recognition

- Frank Duff's vision anticipated the role of the laity in the
  Church, which was later affirmed by the Second Vatican Council.
  Though he has not been canonized, his cause for beatification
  was officially opened in 1996.


### Frank Duff, founder of the Legion.

### Early Life and Career

    - Born: June 7, 1889, in Dublin, Ireland.
    - He was raised in a devout Catholic family and received
      a strong religious education.
    - In 1908, he joined the Civil Service, working for the
      Department of Finance.

### Spiritual Awakening and Apostolic Work

    - In his early twenties, he joined the Society of St. Vincent
      de Paul, where he developed a deep concern for the poor and
      marginalized.
    - A pivotal moment in his spiritual life was his discovery of
      St. Louis de Montfort’s treatise True Devotion to Mary,
      which inspired him to deepen his Marian devotion.

### Founding of the Legion of Mary

    - On September 7, 1921, he founded the Legion of Mary in
      Dublin with a small group of Catholics, dedicated to
      evangelization and charitable works under the patronage of
      the Blessed Virgin Mary.
    - The movement expanded rapidly, becoming a worldwide lay
      apostolic organization.

### Later Life and Legacy

    - Duff devoted his life entirely to the Legion, eventually
      resigning from his government job in 1934.
    - He attended the Second Vatican Council in 1965 as a lay
      observer—one of the few laymen given such an honor.
    - He continued his work tirelessly until his death on
      November 7, 1980, at the age of 91.

### Recognition and Cause for Beatification

    - His cause for beatification was officially opened in 1996,
      recognizing his life of holiness and dedication to the lay
      apostolate.

- Duff’s legacy lives on through the Legion of Mary, which
  continues to inspire millions worldwide.


### Structure

- The Legion of Mary has a structured hierarchy with different
  titles and roles for its members.

### Main Titles and Roles

    - `Active Members` – Those who participate in weekly meetings
      and carry out apostolic works (e.g., visiting the sick,
      evangelization).

    - `Auxiliary Members` – Support the Legion through prayer,
      especially the Rosary.

    - Praesidium Officers – Each local praesidium (small group)
      has four officers:
        -  `President` – Leads the meetings and overall
           direction.
        -  `Vice President` – Assists the President and
           substitutes when needed.
        -  `Secretary` – Handles records and correspondence.
        -  `Treasurer` – Manages financial matters (though the
           Legion operates without fees).

    - Higher Councils – Oversee multiple praesidia, including:
        - `Curia` – Supervises praesidia in a parish or district.
        - `Comitium` – Governs multiple curiae in larger areas.
        - `Regia` – Oversees a diocese or several dioceses.
        - `Senatus` – Supervises a country or major region.
        - `Concilium` – Highest governing body, based in Dublin.

### Who Are the 'Correspondents'?

- `Correspondents` are experienced Legion members assigned by
  a higher council (e.g., Senatus or Regia) to maintain
  communication and oversight of distant or newly established
  Legion councils. Their duties include:

    - Ensuring the group follows Legion rules and spirituality.
    - Receiving reports from the group they oversee.
    - Offering guidance and encouragement.

- This role is crucial for the global coordination of the Legion
  of Mary, especially in regions where councils are still
  developing.

### International

- The Legion of Mary has a strong presence worldwide,
  particularly in countries with large Catholic populations.

- The Legion of Mary is active in over 170 countries, but it has
  particularly strong membership in:

### 1. Ireland

    - The birthplace of the Legion (founded in Dublin in 1921).
    - Although numbers have declined in recent years, it remains
      an important lay movement.

### 2. The Philippines

    - One of the strongest Legion countries, with thousands of
      praesidia (local groups).
    - Plays a major role in parish missions, catechesis, and
      evangelization.

### 3. Nigeria

    - A leading country for Legion membership in Africa.
    - Actively involved in church ministries, outreach, and
      missionary work.

### 4. South Korea

    - Has one of the highest numbers of Legion members in Asia.
    - Strong lay participation in evangelization efforts.

### 5. Brazil

    - One of the biggest Catholic nations in the world, with
      a strong Legion presence.
    - Active in faith formation and community outreach.

### 6. The United States

    - Present in many dioceses, though smaller compared to other
      movements.
    - Engaged in door-to-door evangelization and outreach
      programs.

### 7. India

    - The Legion is growing in various states, especially among
      Catholic communities in Kerala and Tamil Nadu.

### 8. Democratic Republic of Congo

    - A major hub for the Legion in Central Africa.
    - Very active in missionary work and church-based services.

### Estimated Membership Numbers

    - The Legion of Mary has an estimated 3 to 4 million active
      members worldwide.
    - There are millions more auxiliary members (who pray for the
      Legion's mission).
    - Membership fluctuates, with some areas experiencing growth
      while others face decline.
