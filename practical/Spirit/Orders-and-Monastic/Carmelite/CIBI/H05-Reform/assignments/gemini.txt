- Change was seen as vital to the Church's mission during Teresa's time due to
  perceived corruption and a need to reconnect with original religious
  principles (Source-text-03). The Church needed to reform from within, to
  counter the Reformation, which saw people leaving the Catholic Church in
  droves (Source-text-01). Personal piety and spiritual engagement became more
  important than traditional rituals (Source-text-05). Teresa’s reform focused
  on strict community rules and mental prayer (Source-text-05). A significant
  transformation to the communities was the allowance of personal prayer in the
  new Carmelite monasteries (Source-text-04).

- Traditional reform movements, like those of the "observants," aimed to return
  to the original spirit of their founders (Source-text-03). This meant
  simplifying practices, emphasizing poverty (Source-text-05), and prioritizing
  prayer (Source-text-04). Reformers such as Fransiscans also abandoned costly
  shoes for sandals (Source-text-03). The central theme of all reforms in the
  context of Teresa of Avila was to strive toward perfection (Source-text-06).

- The "life-cycle" of a religious community can begin with initial fervour and
  strict adherence to rules (Source-text-03). Over time, wealth and comfort can
  lead to a decline in observance (Source-text-03). The community can adapt to
  the external environment and begin to show conformity (Source-text-05). A
  phase of potential decline may be followed by a renewed push for reform,
  attempting to restore the original ideals (Source-text-03).

- The Carmelites adapted to life in Europe by moving away from their original
  eremitical lifestyle (Source-text-06). They established monasteries in urban
  areas, becoming less secluded. Furthermore, emphasis was placed on devotion to
  the Blessed Virgin, and Divine Office ceremonies were developed
  (Source-text-06). There was an introduction of fasting and abstinence and the
  maintaining of silence for prayer (Source-text-06).

- The decline of fervour among Carmelites manifested in several ways. There was
  an increased wealth in monasteries and nuns going in and out of convents
  (Source-text-03). Rules were mitigated to allow for greater comfort
  (Source-text-03). Some Carmelites sought privileges and exemptions for
  superiors and those with degrees (Source-text-03). In fact there was no
  designated time set aside for mental prayer (Source-text-06). The order was
  not fully enclosed (Source-text-06).
