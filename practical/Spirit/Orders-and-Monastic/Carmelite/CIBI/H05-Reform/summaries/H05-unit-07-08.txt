### Unit 7: An Expanding Vision

- Teresa founded San José on August 24, 1562, and became prioress
  in January 1563.

- She adapted the Constitutions for this community and wrote the
  second redaction of "The Book of Her Life" by spring 1565.

- Teresa promoted interior prayer; faced opposition from Domingo
  Bañez, leading her to write "The Way of Perfection."

- "The Way of Perfection" is a guide on prayer and community
  living, emphasizing a joyful, supportive environment.

- Teresa's emphasis was on relationships and a relational
  mysticism, placing prayer as intimate friendship with Christ.

- Teresa was inspired by the need for a spiritual response to the
  Reformation, leading her to emphasize prayer for church
  defenders.

- Her mission expanded after a visit from Franciscan Alonso
  Maldonado, motivating her to consider more foundations.

- The catalyst for her new foundations was Carmelite General John
  Baptist Rossi, who supported her vision.

- Rossi provided Teresa with permission and support to establish
  additional monasteries.

- Teresa's foundations became models for new ways of being church
  and society, highlighting evangelical friendship and equality.

- Teresa faced numerous challenges but was driven by a mission,
  emphasizing love in action, despite criticism.


### Unit 7: Teresa and the General (Rossi)

- Teresa anticipated the visit of Carmelite General John Baptist
  Rossi and feared potential conflict.

- Rossi supported Teresa's endeavours and provided extensive
  letters and permissions for more monasteries.

- Despite challenges, she founded monasteries of Discalced
  friars, the idea likely pre-discussed.

- Teresa's determination was bolstered by divine encouragement
  and her desire for religious reform.

- Her foundation was later moved from Duruelo to Mancera for
  pragmatic reasons.

- Teresa's interpersonal skills were crucial in founding new
  communities, including navigating ecclesiastical resistance.

- Her boldness and charm were key, exemplified in situations like
  her confrontation in Toledo.

- Teresa's critics questioned her activities, but she saw
  a divine mandate for her outward, active mission.

- She advocated for interior prayer, stressing its importance,
  even as societal norms challenged this view.

- Teresian foundations served as spiritual oases in a turbulent
  time, promoting human flourishing and spiritual growth.


### Unit 7: Discalced Friars

- John of St Mathias (later John of the Cross) was pivotal to
  Teresa's friar project.

- Anthony of Jesus joined Teresa's initiative, showing her
  capability in recruitment.

- Duruelo became the first site for Discalced friars, though
  later relocated.

- Anthony of Jesus characterized himself as a founder of the
  Discalced Carmelites, showing occasional tension with Teresa's
  role.

- Teresa faced numerous frustrations in establishing missions but
  remained committed.

- Both her critics and allies shaped her journey; she relied on
  divine guidance.

- Her sense of mission led her to make numerous travels and face
  challenges head-on, selling a message of love in action.

### Unit 8: John of the Cross

- John of the Cross, the first Discalced Carmelite friar, worked
  closely with Teresa.

- Born in 1542, John's life of poverty led to his entrance into
  the Carmelites and collaboration with Teresa.

- Under her influence, John stayed with the Carmelite order and
  became a leading figure in the reform.

- Through their partnership, Teresa and John encouraged spiritual
  growth and mutual inspiration.

- John regarded Teresa as a spiritual mother and founder,
  acknowledging her profound influence.

- John's writings, among the greatest in Spanish literature, grew
  from his poetic insights and reflective spirituality.

- The "Dark Night" of the soul and his approach to mystical
  theology were innovative and enduring.

### Unit 8: Teresa as Writer & Teacher


- Teresa, an avid reader, used her writings to share her
  spiritual experiences and teachings.

- Her account of prayer, using personal experiences, became
  a resource and inspiration for others.

- Teresa's writings like "Life," "The Way of Perfection," and
  "Interior Castle" guide spiritual autonomy and prayer.

- Her focus on communion, prayer, and community enriched
  spiritual practice and emphasized simplicity and shared
  humanity.

- Teresa's spirituality was not about isolated mystical
  experiences but about transformation and practical love in
  action.

- Her metaphor of a spiritual marriage described ultimate union
  with God through friendship, love, and service.

- Teresa's writings reveal a dynamic personality, affecting
  potential for spiritual growth and community support.
