### Unit 5: Teresa of Ávila

- Teresa and Her Times:
  - Known for Carmelite reform during the 16th century.
  - An important figure in the Catholic Counter Reformation.

- Family Background:
  - St. Teresa of Ávila descended from a converso family.
  - Her grandfather was prosecuted by the Inquisition for being
    a judaizante.

- Conversion and Criticism:
  - Critiqued the concept of honour, emphasizing social
    legitimacy as fragile.
  - Founded convents focused on women's religious empowerment.
  - Emphasized the importance of interior prayer over
    institutional dictates.

- Social Criticism and Influence:
  - Teresa was outspoken against the treatment of women in
    society and the Church.
  - She criticized the reliance on coercive state force in
    spiritual disputes.

- Intellectual and Spiritual Influence:
  - Influenced by Augustinian professors and Jesuits in her
    approach to spirituality.
  - Emphasized personal spiritual experience over rigid
    orthodoxy.

- Historical Context:
  - Lived through a period of significant social, political, and
    religious transformation.
  - Influenced by events like the Reformation and the rise of
    early modern capitalism.

- New Vision of Religious Life:
  - Advocated for reforms creating a distinct style of monastic
    living promoting equality and spiritual experience.

- Personal and Religious Transformation:
  - Teresa's conversion was a turning point emphasizing personal
    relationship with Christ and transformative creativity.
  - Advocated for a religious life that transcended clerical
    constraints and focused on personal faith.


### Unit 6: Founding San José

- New Foundation Initiative:
  - Motivated by conversation and personal spiritual desire to
    return to a purer form of religious life.
  - Inspired by discalced Franciscan nuns' model.

- Supporting Figures:
  - Supported by spiritual director St. Peter of Alcántara and
    colleagues like Doña Guiomar.

- Practical Steps and Challenges:
  - Received approval and encouragement from various religious
    and secular authorities.
  - Overcame significant opposition and logistical challenges to
    establish the new community.

- Philosophy of the New Community:
  - Focused on simplicity, poverty, and equality within the
    monastic life.
  - Rejected aristocratic privileges in favour of community and
    spiritual equality.

- Development of Community Rules:
  - Drafted short and focused Constitutions, emphasizing personal
    spiritual growth over strict legalism.
  - Allowed laymen significant spiritual roles, reflecting trust
    and openness in spiritual guidance.

- Broader Impact:
  - Teresa's reforms critiqued the larger religious system and
    societal values, promoting an interior-focused devotion
    compatible with church needs post-Reformation.
  - San José served as a model for other communities desiring
    meaningful spiritual engagement.
