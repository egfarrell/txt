# MODULE INTRO
    - This module has 16 units and introduces one of the most
      important saints in Carmel, a mystic and a doctor of the
      Church.
    - By the end of the module, you will:
        - come to know this fascinating woman.
        - be introduced to her major works.
        - see the 16th century Carmelite Reform from her
          perspective.
        - see her pedagogy and teaching on prayer.
        - consider her relevance as a spiritual teacher today.

    - You'll be invited to reflect on various issues as well as
      texts from St. Teresa.
    - You'll be able to check your progress by answering some
      questionnaires based on what you read.


# READING
    - 1st recommendation: Something short (`GPT`?)
    - 2nd recommendation: Biography or general intro
    - 3rd Recommendation: Works of St. Teresa   (`K-R`)
    - 4th Recommendation: Studies on St. Teresa (`K-R`, intro & notes)

    - The bibliography on St. Teresa of Avila is enormous. The
      annual Carmelite bibliography in Carmelus shows that in
      1979 there were 75 books and articles in various languages,
      237 items in 2001, and 146 in 2005.
    - In this module the aim is to help students to read Teresa
      of Avila, so that they'll have a grasp of her life,
      personality and teaching that will serve as a basis for any
      future study of reflection.
    - Three words can summarise its aim: academic, reflective and
      enjoyable.
    - Hence the overwhelming concern of the module is to help
      students begin reading Teresa’s texts.
    - The principle adopted is that it is better to read with
      understanding and concentration a small amount of secondary
      literature (= articles and books on Teresa as opposed to
      primary literature which comprises her writings).
    - If a book is not available, there need be no anxiety.
      Accordingly, there are four recommendations.

    - 1st recommendation:  Find something short
        - Start with something short in a dictionary of the
          saints, an encyclopaedia, a dictionary of spirituality,
          Butler’s Lives of the Saints (even an old edition), the
          entry in Wikipedia,  etc.
        - The CTS pamphlet by Jennifer Moorcroft, Teresa of Avila
          (London: CTS, 64 pages) is highly recommended.
        - This reading will give you a sense of time and place;
          you will remember some of it.  Don’t bother taking
          notes at this stage.  What you find will come again in
          various guises.

    - 2nd recommendation: Biography or general introduction
        - There are many biographies of Teresa. They will vary in
          quality.  Don’t worry about details.
        - Scholars will have reservations about all of them.
          Don’t think that any author will necessarily be fully
          accurate in fact or interpretation.
            - In time “the self-correcting process of learning”
              will begin to operate. The more you read the more
              you will correct your existing knowledge; you pick
              up errors in authors arising from their date, the
              slant of an author, the mistakes authors make or
              from their prejudices or idiosyncrasies.
        - Some well-known studies, not reliable in every detail,
          however, are:

            - Auclair, G., Teresa of Avila: The Vivid Dramatic Life Story of a Great Woman, Organiser, Foundress, Mystic, Saint.  New York: Pantheon, 1953.
            - Bielecki, T., Teresa of Avila: An Introduction to her Life and Writings. London: Darton, Longman and Todd, 1994.
            - Du Doulay, S., Teresa of Avila. London: Hodder and Stoughton, 1991.
            - Lincoln, V., Teresa a Woman: A Biography of Teresa of Avila. New York: Albany, 1984.
            - Luti, J.M., Teresa of Avila: A Way. Collegeville: Liturgical Press—Glazier 1991.
            - Medwick, C., Teresa of Avila: The Progress of a Soul. London: Duckworth, 1999.
            - Nevin, W., Teresa of Avila: The Woman. Milwaukee: Bruce, 1954.
            - O’Brien, K., Teresa of Avila. London: Parrish, 1951 frequently reprinted.
            - Obbard, R., La Madre: The Life and Spirituality of Teresa of Avila. Slough: St. Paul’s, 1994.
            - Papasologio, G., St. Teresa of Avila. New York: St. Paul, 1958, 1990.
            - Peers, E. A., Mother of Carmel London: SCM, 1945.
            - Walsh, W.T., St. Teresa of Avila: A Biography. Milwaukee: Bruce, 1943.
            - McCaffrey, Eugene, Let Nothing Trouble you, - Teresa: The Woman, The Guide and the Storyteller. The Columba Press 2015
            - McCaffrey, Eugene, Journey of Love (Teresian Press, Carmelite Priory, Boars Hill, 2015).
            - Tomas Alvarez: Teresa of Avila: 100 themes on her Life and Work translated by Kieran Kavanaugh OCD (Washington DC: ICS 2011).

        - There are many good books not on this list.  Try to get
          your hands on ONE biography or general introduction.

    - 3rd Recommendation: works of St. Teresa of Avila
        - There are many `translations` of St. Teresa of Avila.
          Each one will have its own strong points and none will
          be fully satisfactory in every respect.
        - Even a good knowledge of modern Spanish will not
          suffice for a perfect grasp of her text in the
          original, though it will help.
            - As in biblical studies checking with another
              translation on some word or phrase can be helpful.
              But these considerations are way beyond an
              introductory course.
        - You'll have to make choices about texts depending on
          what you can find!
        - The two most important texts are Teresa’s `Life` and
          `The Interior Castle`.
        - Extensive quotations in the module make the other two
          texts less vital to have at hand:
            - `The Way of Perfection`
            - `The Book of Her Foundations`

        - All things considered, the best translation is by the
          Discalced Carmelites, `Kavanaugh and Rodriguez`, The
          Collected Works of St. Teresa of Avila. 3 vols.
            - Translated by Kieran Kavanaugh and Otilio Rodriguez
              (2nd editions from 1987).
            - This translation has excellent introductions to
              Teresa, to each of her works and splendid concise
              notes to the text.
            - It is the one used in this module, with the
              abbreviations `K-R` [for Kavanaugh-Rodriguez]
              followed by the volume and page. If you are using
              the first editions, you may not find the page
              agreeing, but a little searching will find the
              reference.
        - An older text, in its time ground-breaking and still
          valuable, is the translation by Edgar Allison Peers:
            - St. Teresa of Avila’s Complete Works 3. vols. (1946
              with subsequent reprints).

        - The earlier translations by David Lewis (1870-1871) are
          not really recommended, as the Spanish text from which
          they taken is not reliable and the translation is not
          into current English.
            - But if these are the only ones available, you
              should of course use them—better a deficient
              translation than none at all.

        - There are several modern translations of the life of
          Teresa, such as The Life of Teresa of Avila by Herself.
          Tr. J.M. Cohen (London: Penguin Books, 1957),
            - But these don't have introductions or notes on par
              with Kavanaugh-Rodriguez et al...

        - There are other editions of various works of Teresa,
          some new, but mostly by the Benedictines of Stanbrook.

    - 4th Recommendation: studies on St. Teresa

        - The 'Introductions' and 'notes' of the
          `Kavanaugh-Rodriguez texts` are the prime
          recommendation.
            - ../Misc/Kavanaugh-Rodriguez-Vol1-intro.txt

        - If only one study is recommended, then it'd have to be
          the excellent, but at times dense study by Archbishop
          Rowan Williams:
            - Teresa of Avila (1991)

        - One will find in various libraries booklets in “The
          Living Flame” series, originally published by the
          Discalced Carmelites at Avila, Dublin, and mostly out
          of print.  There are some excellent studies on Teresa
          in that series.
