
- The text reflects on Teresa's major tract on prayer,
  specifically her "Life chapters 11-22," focusing on the `Four
  Waters` allegory.

- The chapters on prayer were added later to her Life at Fr.
  García's suggestion.

- Teresa's writings will be studied separately from her other
  work, "The Interior Castle," though there are parallels.

- The treatment in 'Life' is more aimed at beginners in prayer.

- Teresa uses an allegory of four ways to water a garden to
  explain prayer progression:

  - Buckets from a well - initial mediation and recollection.

  - Waterwheel - prayer of recollection and quiet with less
    effort.

  - Stream diversion - prayer of the sleep of the faculties.

  - Rain - prayer of union, a mystical state.

- Key ideas: understanding the distinction between normal and
  mystical prayer, focusing on God's activity versus human
  efforts.

- Teresa emphasizes the importance of humility and a "determined
  determination," especially in the beginning stages.

- The "Well" stage involves beginners actively working, like
  drawing water, and advises on perseverance and understanding
  during spiritual dryness.

- Teresa warns against striving for mystical experiences
  prematurely and stresses `discernment` in spiritual
  experiences.

- The unit discusses the ongoing need for self-awareness,
  humility, and the reality of joys and temptations in spiritual
  practice.

- She outlines criteria to discern if spiritual experiences are
  genuine, focusing on detachment, humility, and the desire to
  help through challenges.

- The transition from the Well to the Waterwheel stage involves
  less effort as prayer becomes more about God's work in the
  soul.

- Discernment is crucial in higher stages of prayer to determine
  whether the experiences stem from God, self, or the devil.
