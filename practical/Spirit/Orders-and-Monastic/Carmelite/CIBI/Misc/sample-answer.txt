# Sample Question:
    - What is the appropriate spiritual disposition towards God’s
      unconditional love demonstrated with the parable of the
      Prodigal Son (Luke 15: 11-32)?

# Sample answer 1 (needs improvement):

    - The story of the prodigal son is part of a shocking
      response given by Jesus to those who gathered around him,
      in addressing the objections of the Pharisees and Scribes
      who were complaining that Jesus welcomes sinners and eats
      with them (Luke 15: 11-32).
    - The story is so stunning to the extent that some consider
      it a tragedy, an unfair story of partiality especially from
      the perspective of the elder son who felt that he got a raw
      deal and that his younger brother should have been punished
      to teach him a lesson, because he did not only squander his
      inheritance, but indirectly wished his father’s death by
      demanding his inheritance.[1]
    - Instead of being punished, the action of his father
      revealed to him that his father had never stopped loving
      him, even in his sinfulness (Luke 15: 20). This is the core
      of the story that reveals something about the heart of God.
      That no matter how terrible what one has done, God
      continues to shower his sustaining grace and blessings upon
      the person.[2]
    - This teaches a deep lesson of God’s unconditional love
      which is founded on the principle of mercy and not
      judgement.[3] That divine justice is about the essence of
      the human person and not a remunerative kind of justice.
      The only condition is for the human person to acknowledge
      his condition of sin and desire to return to his true
      essence. It is an invitation to stop wallowing in grief and
      turn the page to be clothed in the robe that is a sign of
      God’s unconditional love.[4] It can them be concluded that
      the parable of the prodigal son teaches a lesson of God’s
      unconditional love for all, including sinners, even when
      they were stuck in their pattern of sin. (Word count, 365).

### Comment:
    - The draft appears so succinct and well researched that
      a student may think that nothing can be removed from it
      without missing the main points made or impoverishing the
      work. 
        - But the opposite is the case, because it can be
          paraphrased and shortened in a way that brings out the
          answer to question about “the appropriate spiritual
          disposition” more clearly. 
        - There are four paragraphs in the sample and it can be
          reduced to two by further proofreading, summarizing and
          paraphrasing the draft, while retaining the main points
          made and the other essential components of the draft,
          including the citations, to achieve the 200 word count
          or even less, as in the example below.


# Sample answer 2 (improved):

    - The story of the prodigal son is Jesus’ shocking response
      to the objection of welcoming and eating with sinners (Luke
      15: 11-32). The story sounds unfair to the elder son who
      wallowed in grief,[5] but it reveals unconditional love
      (Luke 15: 20), of God for those who adopt the appropriate
      spiritual disposition.[6]

    - This teaches the principle of God’s mercy, which seeks the
      essence of the human person and not a remunerative kind of
      justice.[7] The required appropriate disposition is to
      acknowledge one’s sin and desire return to one’s true
      essence.[8]  In conclusion, the appropriate spiritual
      disposition is to recognize and seek God’s unconditional
      love by returning to our true essence as beings made in the
      image and likeness of God. (Word count, 191).
