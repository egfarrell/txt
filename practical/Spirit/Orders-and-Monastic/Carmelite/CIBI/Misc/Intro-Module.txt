        - The lists provide useful background material for each module.
          At least one book from the list should be read. The

    - Briefly set out three components of a high standard
      questionnaire answer.
        - https://moodle.cibi.ie/mod/lesson/view.php?id=1908

        - Focused/Relevant:
            - Each answer should directly answer the question and
              not be over-complicated. It should be concise, and
              not too long or detailed. It is the content that
              matters, not the length.

        - Good Structure:
            - Theanswer should have a logical flow, perhaps with
              a brief intro, followed by main content, and
              finishing with a summary or final remark. It should
              be formatted neatly, with numbering, word count,
              and any required referencing (e.g., footnotes).

        - Proper Referencing:
            - All quotations or material from other sources
              should be referenced using the Turabian style. This
              shows that extra reading that has been done and it
              makes sure all plagiarism is avoided.

    - What is a bibliography?
        - It's a list (in alphabetical order) of all sources used
          in an essay.
        - It comes at the end of an essay and matches any sources
          mentioned in the footnotes of the main essay text.

    - Define plagiarism in your own words.
        - Plagiarism is taking the work of other people (i.e.
          their ideas, thoughts, writing) and presenting them as
          your own work. A common example is copying text from
          another writer and not referencing it.

    - How do you avoid plagiarism?
        - By correctly referencing all quotes, summaries and
          paraphrasing from any work that isn't your own. It's
          also possible to check your work using Turnitin.

    - What is the difference between the “mark” and the “grade”
      for your assignments?
        - Marks: the percentage you get for an assignment (e.g. 70%)
        - Grade: a letter giving the level you received. e.g. A, B, C etc

    - Why is it important to carefully read the feedback you
      receive on your assignments?
        - It's important because it helps you make changes to
          improve your academic writing in the future. If
          feedback is ignored, it could affect the marks/grades
          received in future questionnaires.

### Intro to Student Support Centre

### Aims:

    - To introduce you to the tools and resources that are
      available in the "Student Support Centre" and to help you
      prepare by increasing your familiarity with these tools.
    - By the end of this unit you'll be able to use the online
      tools to answer any queries you might have about the course
      or modules you are undertaking, and be able to use the
      referencing and citation guidelines in your submitted work.
    - Many students will be familiar with online learning, but it
      is useful to complete this section as each course and
      institution has different procedures and guidelines.

### Structure and Questionnaires:

- In this unit there will be sections of short questions (“Check
  your Reading”) that you should use to check you have read the
  text and understood it. You do not need to submit these “Check
  you Reading” questionnaires.

- At the end of this unit are ten questions that must be
  submitted to CIBI. This questionnaire will not be marked but
  you will be awarded a “Pass” or “Fail” grade.
    - If you receive a pass grade you may continue to your
      registered modules.
    - If you receive a fail grade your tutor will contact you to
      work through difficulties that arose before you can
      continue. This process is to ensure that students are fully
      prepared to start online study and to minimise problems
      that may arise.

- The structure of this section will roughly follow the info for
  students on the “Student Support Centre” section.

### 1.   Intro

    - a)    What is `Moodle`?
        - The CIBI website bases its online course around the
          Moodle system (www.moodle.com). You will notice that
          when you log on to the website, you are redirected to
          the moodle.cibi.ie website. Many colleges and
          universities use Moodle (there are over 68 million
          users around the world) as a way of connecting with the
          students (even those who do not study online) as it is
          a very efficient system for sharing files (documents,
          links, files, videos etc) and it is easy to change and
          update.

    - b)    What is the `Student Support Centre`
        - We will focus on the content contained in the “Student
          Support Centre”.
        - It's a set of resources that have been designed to
          contain most of the information you will need to begin
          studying, and should serve as a reference section for
          all of your modules. You must become familiar with the
          content of this section before you begin to study. Take
          some time now to find the section and read through the
          different headings. You can then come back to this
          Introduction unit at any time.



### 2.   Semester Calendar

- The semester calendar is very important as it gives you an
  overview of the important dates and deadlines that you will
  need to keep to when working through each module. The calendar
  is divided in two, one section for each semester. You will note
  that each module is divided into 16 units. After you have
  studied four units, you will have to complete a questionnaire.
  After you have completed all the units and corresponding
  questionnaires you will be asked to submit an essay. The essay
  titles are given at the end of the last unit and the date that
  they are available is displayed.

    - Complete Units 1 - 4         Do Questionnaire 1
    - Complete Units 5 - 8         Do Questionnaire 2
    - Complete Units 9 - 12        Do Questionnaire 3
    - Complete Units 13 – 16       Do Questionnaire 4
    - Complete all units above     Do Essay (one essay from a choice of three)

    - The column “Published On” shows the date that the units are
      published online on Moodle, and you will have access to the
      content from this date. The content will remain online so
      that you can view it until the date in the column headed
      “Published Until” (this is usually the end of the
      semester).
    - A very important date is the column headed “Assignment Due
      By”. This is the `last day` you can submit that particular
      assignment.
        - As you have been given the due date very far in advance
          it is presumed that unless very exceptional
          circumstances exist, students will have submitted their
          assignment on or before this date. Questionnaires and
          essays will not be accepted after this date.

    - In exceptional circumstances an extension to the deadline
      date may be granted, and this can be requested by email to
      admin@cibi.ie outling the reason for the request and
      expected timeline for submission. All such requests must be
      received and approved prior to the deadline date.

### 3.   Reading Lists

- If you click on the Reading Lists link in the Student Support
  Centre, you'll see a list of all the courses available through
  CIBI. You will not be doing all of the modules, but take some
  time to find the modules that you will be taking. Each module
  has a code and a module name.

- The reading list is a guide provided by the module author. It
  is not a definitive list and normally includes some general
  works for reference or more specific works what will help you
  with particular aspects of the course.

- The Reading List is not a list of books that you must read,
  rather it is a helpful guide to texts that will be useful for
  the course. It will be made clear if there is a set textbook or
  text that should be studied in depth for a particular module.

### 7.   Other Information: Exemption Policy, Marks and Grades, Feedback

- Exemption Policy: this covers the exemption policy of CIBI with
  regard to students who are exempt from completing certain
  modules on the BTh programme.
- Marks and Grades: all marks (the percentage that you get for
  your questionnaire or essay) and grades (the “title” and level)
  are explained here. Your tutor uses the rubric (or explanation
  for each grade) when they are marking and grading your paper so
  you should study these for what is required in your answer.

- Feedback: You will receive feedback for each piece of work you
  submit to CIBI. This will always be in written form and is
  written by the person who corrected your work (not necessarily
  the person who wrote the module you are studying). You should
  read this feedback very carefully as it’s designed to highlight
  what you did well (and therefore should continue to do) as well
  areas for improvement that will help you to improve your grade.

### 8. Using Turnitin

- For `BTh` Long Essays CIBI uses Turnitin's Originality Check
  for detecting potential plagiarism. The service matches text
  from student work against extensive databases of websites,
  academic reference materials, and previously submitted student
  papers, producing both an originality score and detailed
  originality report.

- This service allows us to systematically check for similarity,
  thus ensuring that all students' work is original and graded
  accordingly and also helps students to improve their academic
  skills by providing feedback on citation and referencing.

- The website is www.turnitin.com and relevant students will be
  provided with log on details. Once you have logged on to the
  website, you will see your student homepage.
    - This shows a list of the modules (or classes as the website
      terms them). If you click on a module, you can see the
      sections where you can upload your work in the assignment
      inbox (very detailed instructions are given at the top of
      the inbox and if you require further help, the help section
      can be accessed at the top right-hand side of the page).

- If you need additional assistance, the CIBI office will email
  you a step-by-step visual guide to getting started with
  Turnitin. Simply email `admin@cibi.ie` and request Turnitin
  assistance.
