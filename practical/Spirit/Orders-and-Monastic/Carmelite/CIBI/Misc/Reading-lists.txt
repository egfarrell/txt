### H08: The Compiègne Martyrs. Died by Guillotine July 17th 1794.
    - Tutor: Therese Marie of the Holy Face OCD
    - The only book you will need for this module is To Quell the
      Terror: The True Story of the Carmelite Martyrs of
      Compiègne by William Bush published by ICS. All references
      to additional reading in the module are taken from this
      book. It is the only full length book in print on the
      Compiègne Martyrs in English.

    - Much of William Bush’s work is based on the monumental work
      of Fr. Bruno a French Discalced Carmelite friar who spent
      many years researching the martyrs. His book Le Sang du
      Carmel (The Blood of Carmel) was published by Plon, Paris
      in 1954.

    - An earlier book in English, The Compiègne Martyrs was
      compiled by Eleanor Mary Wilson and published by Art & Book
      Company Ltd. London in 1907. It may be available in
      libraries or second hand.

    - These three books have been used in preparing this module.
      I have also used two obituaries. Those of Sr. Charlotte of
      the Resurrection and Sr. Teresa of the Heart of Mary.

    - There are articles on various Catholic websites about the
      Compiègne Martyrs, most of them are reliable as they are
      nearly all based on William Bush’s book.

### S04: St. Thérèse of Lisieux

          Schmidt, FSC.
            - Both are published in the USA by Word Among Us
              Press and are companion volumes to Story of a Soul.
            - They contain some basic background materials as
              well as some “between-the-lines” reading of Story
              of a Soul.

    - For your own personal study, curiosity and sheer enjoyment,
      you might wish to access the Theresian Archives on the web:
      http://www.archives-carmel-lisieux.fr/english/carmel/
    - It is constantly updated and there seems to be no end to
      all the interesting memorabilia and facts about Thérèse."

### T01: An Introduction to the New Testament

    - Recommended text-books, one of the following:
        - Raymond E. Brown, An Introduction to the New Testament,
          The Anchor Bible Reference Library, New York etc.:
          Doubleday, 1997. 878 pages.
            - This is an excellent book to have and to study,
              written by one of the big stars within NT studies.
              It is clear and entertaining, and includes many
              intriguing and unanswered questions. It has several
              tables and maps. The first part of the book deals
              with preliminaries, such as the formation and the
              text of the NT, and the political, social and
              religious background. Part two is about the
              Gospels, Acts and Johannine letters. The third part
              deals with Pauline literature, as well as Paul’s
              life and importance, and letter-genres. The final
              part deals with the rest of the NT writings, and
              the appendixes include information on other early
              Christian literature and a chapter on the
              historical Jesus.
        - Bart D. Ehrman, The New Testament: A Historical
          Introduction to the Early Christian Writings, New York:
          Oxford University Press, 2000. 437 pages.
            - This is also a very good book containing several
              maps, timelines and photos. Ehrman deals with the
              Greco-Roman world and with Judaism, with each of
              the NT books, with the historical Jesus and the
              historical Paul, textual criticism and much more.
              Ehrman, who believes that no clear distinction can
              be drawn between canonical and non-canonical books
              during the first three centuries, also treats other
              early Christian works.

    - In addition, if you wish:
        - Raymond E. Brown, Joseph A. Fitzmyer and Roland E.
          Murphy (eds.), The New Jerome Biblical Commentary:
          Student Edition. London: Geoffrey Chapman, 1990. 1475
          pages.
            - This book covers not only the NT, but also the OT
              and almost any other related topic, such as
              intertestamental literature, history, archaeology,
              canon and inspiration, exegetical methods, OT and
              NT theology and much more. It also gives a comment
              to each of the biblical books; the comments are too
              short to cover what you need to know if you plan to
              do a course on a specific book of the Bible, but
              are handy if you only want a quick overview. The
              book is big and fairly expensive, but a good
              investment.

### T15: The Pentateuch

    - Recommended: Suitable text books are a problem for this
      course. If you want to read only one book, you need a book
      which contains both chapters on the formation of the
      Pentateuch, and which analyses at least the texts analysed
      in this course.
    - Such books exist, but their analyses are so short that they
      do not go sufficiently in depth on any topic.
    - I would therefore advise you to rather read at least one
      book dealing with introductory questions, and to read other
      books for textual analysis. The more books, the better.
    - If you have limited funds available for buying books, then
      try to get to a good library. If you are unable to go to
      a library, then know that many university libraries (eg the
      Pontifical Biblical Institute in Rome, or Manchester in
      England) will send you photocopies of what you request for
      a small fee.
    - A list of recommended books is included with each lecture;
      in addition, the textual commentaries of the commentary
      series listed above are good.

    - If you wish to buy books, I would recommend buying one book
      on background information, and one commentary on Genesis,
      since most of the texts analysed in this course are from
      Genesis. Books dealing with background information, such as
      the formation of the Pentateuch, are:

        - Blenkinsopp, Joseph, The Pentateuch: An Introduction to
          the First Five Books of the Bible, Anchor Bible
          Reference Library. New York: Doubleday, 1992. Probably
          the best book available.
        - Whybray, R. Norman, The Making of the Pentateuch:
          A Methodological Study, Journal for the Study of the
          Old Testament Supplement Series 53. Sheffield:
          Sheffield Academic Press, 1987 and 1994. A relatively
          short book; very good, in spite of some
          self-contradictions and rash judgements.

    - Several good commentaries on Genesis exist, though some of
      them are beginning to be a bit old. Perhaps the best one
      available is:

        - Brueggemann, Walter, Genesis, Interpretation. Atlanta:
          WJK, 1982.
