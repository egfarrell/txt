    - Our Lady
    - Of course, she did ask how it would be possible since she
      was a virgin, and the angel explained it to her. But
      I think she mainly acted out of complete trust that
      whatever God asks, He loves her, and it must be good. So,
      she was willing to do it. In today's reading from the
      Hebrews, we heard that God doesn't want sacrifice; He just
      wants us to follow His will. This idea was reflected in the
      Responsorial Psalm, too. We don't need to come up with
      anything spectacular for God or try to take control of our
      lives. We just need to trust God's love and accept His will
      for us.

    - It's funny because I wrote this before our meeting today,
      where we talked about the future of Holy Hill. This is what
      it's all about—simply trusting God's love. We know He loves
      us, and we just follow what He wants. So, there's no need
      to worry because we are loved. While writing this,
      I thought of a great line from Abraham Heschel, a Jewish
      rabbi, that I love to use. He says that surely, God will
      always be surprised by a handful of fools who don't fail.
      They will always be a spiritual underground where brave
      minds continue to fight.

    - I love this idea. We often feel out of step with the
      culture and society, but being a contemplative is like
      being part of the spiritual underground. After reading
      Marilynne Robinson, I realized how important it is. There
      are always not many people truly in tune with God and
      trying to follow His will, but it's crucial that such
      people exist because that's how God reaches humanity and
      spreads His love, making the world flourish.

        - The _scapegoat_ / 'victim'
        - The simplest solution to the problem of violence for
          early human communities was to polarize blame and
          hostility onto _one member_ of the group who would be
          killed and interpreted as the source of conflict and
          hostility within the group. 
        - The transition from the violent conflict of
          all-against-all would be transformed into the unifying
          and pacifying violence of all-except-one whose death
          would reconcile the community together. 
        - The victim who was persecuted as the source of disorder
          would then become venerated as the source of order and
          meaning for the community and seen as a god. 
        - This process of engendering and making possible human
          community through arbitrary victimization is called,
          within mimetic theory, the scapegoat mechanism.

### Dear Friends of Carmel,
    - A way to face current news with good cheer I am discovering
      is to go deeper.  Is God really dead?  Has our experience
      of covid sobered us?  Where is true joy? What values can we
      count on? Base our life on?  All adults in our time need to
      rethink meaning.  The Christianity upon which we were
      reared is not holding up.  Secularization has not ruined
      religion; it has purified it.
    - The Enlightenment—Age of Reason and Science-- got rid of
      our Christian enchantment ,wanting to judge everything by
      reason alone.  The result became a very flat understanding
      of our story with the need to go deeper.
    - Secularization tore down our superficial answers, our weak
      practice of witness.  Secularization demands more
      authenticity.  We need to become more porous to more
      sublime levels of being, to the Celtic sensibility.
    - We will never get rid of religion.  Notice how religious
      patterns emerge in our politics, our language, in how we
      celebrate together.  Religion creeps into psychoanalysis.
      Philosophy now speaks more in religious terms. We long to
      pay attention together to something that transcends us.
    - As Friends of Carmel at Holy Hill we want to be alert to
      our cultural crisis of meaning.  We want to go forward with
      confidence and hope into a new era.  We trust in our
      Carmelite roots that reach deep into Mystery and the
      divine.  Faith is the courage to live in the Mystery.
      Friends of Carmel invites those seeking for deeper meaning
      to gather with us so that we can share insights and dream
      a new future and together draw up courage to be
      transformed.
    - Zoom served us well in the past year, however because of
      connectivity issues,we have to discontinue.  Now we move
      forward, encouraging you to either join us here in person
      for our next gathering on March 12th or make time later to
      check our friendsofcarmel.ie web site with talks and
      descriptions of the day.
    - Enclosed is our flyer inviting us into transformation.  We
      hope you will join us.
    - We are also planning future events this year for June 11th
      and Oct 8th.

        - this little heaven of my soul where the maker of heaven
          and earth is present.

        - Your Kingdom come within me. Your will be done on earth

### Carmelite life
    - Our life cannot be judged by its apparent accomplishments,
      only by its depth, by the degree to which we penetrate into
      and through the Dark _Nights_ and are transformed into love
      by the ascent of Mt. Carmel.
        - (Multiple Dark Nights?)

    - Purity of Heart means to will one thing. It applies not
      only to sexual desire, but will and intellect as well. Our
      vow of chastity includes emotional and _intellectual
      chastity_. (What is that?)

    - The goal of obedience is to unite our wills with God’s
      will.
        - Obedience literally means to _listen_. Obedience is
          self-actualizing surrender. It means freedom from the
          narrow-minded willfulness that deafens us to the
          commands of God. It implies a swift and cheerful
          willingness to act on what we hear. It requires mature
          interdependence, and counters obsequious dependence and
          selfish autonomy.

    - Each hermit spends a minimum of two hours each day in
      prayer. This includes the Divine Office, celebration of
      Mass, and meditative reading that enhances prayer. In
      addition, we do at least one additional hour of spiritual
      reading _as a preparation_ for prayer. During days and
      weeks of solitude, the amount of time for prayer increases.

    - Leisure and learning
        - Our life should be lived in leisure, which is not the
          absence of work. It is a personal, passionate,
          presence, characterized by stillness, recollection, and
          receptivity even in the midst of hard work and heavy
          responsibility. Without it, we cannot receive
          inspiration or vision.

        - We are a school in the most real sense of the word, for
          like the original Greek schools (_schole meaning
          leisure_), our goal is the non-utilitarian but most
          human action of contemplation. Towards that end, we
          encourage the study of Scripture, philosophy and
          theology, the classics, and the best contemporary and
          spiritual books. Our model for the intellectual life is
          Christ. His intelligence served his primary end: love.
          But we cannot love what we do not know. ‘Learning is
          a great help for shedding light upon every matter’.
