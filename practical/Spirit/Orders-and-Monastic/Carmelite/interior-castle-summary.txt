        - light on

              according to God's plan. 
            - In Teresa's case , its function seems to have been

              that this gift, puts a person in a class apart;

            - When she tried to use this knowledge universally,

    - Recollection: avoid wasted energy
        - If we abstract from the unimportant bodily reaction
          , we see that it is a gathering up of the powers within
          the self in order to surren der the whole self to God.
          We are scattered beings, disintegra ted , torn with
          conflicting desires, drawn hither and thither. God's
          loving encounter has the effect of drawing us together,
          making us whole. Love is born of it and this love
          begins to dominate our _wandering desires_, calling
          them within to the one sole Good, ordering everything
          to the service of love . 
        - Gradually it masters them all and so one is
          recollected, gathered up, living always from one's
          centre, always directed to God and choosing him. This
          is the end of _dissipation_ and _wasted time_ and
          energy.
    - Eremetical vocation (very important)
        - Teresa grasped that, in order to be thus exposed one
          must be both purified and mature. For this WE NEED
          OTHERS absolutely. There is no other means of
          purification than learning to live with others. 
        - lt calls for continual sacrifice and provides
          opportunities for seeing the bad in us and for
          overcoming it. It keeps us wholly in touch with
          reality, coping with real people in real situations and
          this helps us to maturity. 
        - We could live in physical solitude and be exposed, not
          to God but to ourselves. We could remain immersed in
          selfishness, blind to our defects, undeveloped
          intellectually and emotionally. Is it ever allowed,
        - I wonder, considering the absolute need we have of
          others, to take the initiative and choose the
          eremitical vocation? 
            - It could be presumptuous, a 'tempting God' in the
              old sense, expecting him to provide miraculously
              while we deliberately forego essential means to
              human and spiritual growth. 
            - The vocation to the purely eremitical life must, of
              necessity, be very rare, and, in my opinion, MUST
              IN SOME WAY BE FORCED UPON US. 
            - Who of us would presume we were sufficiently
              purified and sufficiently mature on the per sonal
              level, to dispense with the society of others?



### GPT Summary
    - Teresa of Ávila wrote about the "seven mansions" (or "seven
      dwelling places") in her renowned work The Interior Castle
      (El Castillo Interior), which describes the soul's mystical
      journey toward union with God.
    - Teresa uses the image of a castle with many rooms or
      "mansions" to represent the stages of spiritual development
      as the soul moves deeper into a relationship with God.


### 1. First Mansion (Awakening or Self-Knowledge)
- (Nullabour)
- Description: This is the initial stage where the soul becomes
  aware of God's presence but remains entangled in worldly
  distractions, sin, and attachment to material things.
- Spiritual State: The soul begins to reflect on its spiritual
  condition but is still heavily influenced by the outside world.
  There is a desire for God, but the soul struggles to overcome
  sin and temptations.
- Challenges: The soul encounters difficulties in prayer and may
  be drawn away by external temptations. Progress is slow and
  filled with inner conflicts.

### 2. Second Mansion (Practice of Prayer and Humility)
- Simon Community Years
- Description: The soul is more committed to prayer and
  self-reflection. It recognizes the need for deeper conversion
  and begins to take its spiritual life more seriously.
- Spiritual State: The soul starts to detach from the
  distractions of the world and grows in humility. It is more
  conscious of God’s grace and desires a closer relationship with
  Him.
- Challenges: There is still a significant struggle between the
  soul’s attachment to the world and its desire for God. The soul
  may face periods of spiritual dryness or doubt.

### 3. Third Mansion (Illumination and Active Devotion)
- Simon Years in Dublin, the 19th Annotation
- Description: Here, the soul experiences a period of stability
  and spiritual discipline. It is actively practicing virtue and
  has developed a solid prayer life.
- Spiritual State: The soul has grown in self-discipline and
  piety, following a regular practice of prayer, fasting, and
  good works. It experiences a sense of spiritual fulfillment and
  peace.
- Challenges: The soul may face a subtle form of pride and
  self-satisfaction in its accomplishments. There can be
  a temptation to rest on its spiritual laurels, forgetting that
  deeper levels of surrender are still needed.

### 4. Fourth Mansion (Supernatural Consolation and Quiet Prayer)
- The L'Arche disaster. Try for Zambia, switch to PHysics,
  Cliffony
- Description: In the fourth mansion, the soul begins to
  experience more profound encounters with God through
  contemplative prayer. God starts to take a more active role,
  bestowing spiritual consolations.
- Spiritual State: The soul moves from active meditation into the
  realm of contemplative prayer. It begins to receive divine
  gifts, such as a sense of interior peace and spiritual
  sweetness.
- Challenges: The soul must learn not to become overly attached
  to these spiritual consolations. It is a time of balancing joy
  in receiving these graces with the humility to realize they
  come from God, not personal effort.

### 5. Fifth Mansion (Union with God and Spiritual Betrothal)
- Description: The soul enters into a deeper union with God,
  often described as spiritual "betrothal." The presence of God
  is felt much more intimately, and the soul experiences
  transformative graces.
- Spiritual State: The soul has now achieved a deep level of
  union with God and experiences profound mystical experiences.
  It feels as though the will is united with God's will in a much
  more intimate way.
- Challenges: The soul may experience periods of intense
  suffering or purification as God prepares it for even deeper
  levels of union. There is a growing detachment from worldly
  concerns.

### 6. Sixth Mansion (Spiritual Marriage and Deep Suffering)
- Description: In this stage, the soul experiences "spiritual
  marriage," which is an even deeper and more permanent union
  with God. However, it is also a period of intense suffering,
  trials, and purification.
- Spiritual State: The soul enjoys an intimate and near-constant
  awareness of God’s presence. It also experiences mystical
  phenomena, such as visions, ecstasies, or divine revelations.
  At the same time, the soul may undergo severe trials, doubts,
  or spiritual darkness, testing its love and trust in God.
- Challenges: The soul undergoes intense purification. God allows
  suffering to detach the soul entirely from self-love and the
  world, so that it can be fully united with Him. The soul may
  feel _abandoned_, yet these trials are meant to perfect its
  love.

### 7. Seventh Mansion (Union with God: Spiritual Marriage in Fullness)
- Description: The seventh mansion represents the complete and
  perfect union of the soul with God, often described as the
  "spiritual marriage" in its fullness. The soul lives in God,
  and God in the soul.
- Spiritual State: The soul experiences continuous, intimate
  communion with God. It is completely transformed by divine love
  and is at peace, no longer subject to the trials and struggles
  of earlier mansions. The will of the soul is fully aligned with
  God’s will.
- Challenges: There are fewer struggles in this mansion, but the
  soul may still experience suffering in the form of compassion
  for others. The soul's life is now entirely centered on serving
  God and others out of pure love, without self-interest.

---

The Interior Castle reflects Teresa’s own mystical experiences
and emphasizes the importance of prayer, humility, and
self-surrender to God. The progression through these mansions is
a journey toward increasing intimacy with God, leading to
complete transformation in divine love.
