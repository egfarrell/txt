### BrianBr: Monastery wisdom
    - When you're going to a monastery, first advice, prepare for
      war
    - First two years you'll experience deep graces, that'll set
      you up for the rest of your life. Very important to pay
      attention to those graces. But after that you get weaned,
      because you've been fed on warm milk.
        - The time has come then to be a soldier.
    - When you enter the monastery, satan goes in with you.
    - Your work is going a long way to helping the kingdom of G-d
      on this Earth. So s...n is very invested in stopping that.
    - Br Brian realised: satan isn't with the evil people
      (they're in his pocket already... he's with the good
      people)
        - stn will offer little suggestions, little whispers, try
          this, try that
    - But remember, if stn is entering the monastery with you, so
      is a massive army of angels. But the angels are coming to
      fight, they're very good at it. But the battleground is
      your mind, your passions... you have to make yourself
      available... through prayer, quiet suffering, holiness of
      life... You don't join a religious community to relax for
      the rest of your life... You're there to _fight_.
    - Advice: before you start, lay your vocation to the
      religious life at the feet of Our Holy Mother.
        - He can't promise, but he believes at all the key
          moments, the decisive times, she will be there to
          intervene, to advise you profoundly, and to strengthen
          you when you really need it.
        - Lay your vocation at Her feet, and ask Her to accompany
          you. And She will.
        - Don't forget you're being called to fight till your
          last breath. It's not going to be an easy fight.

### BrianBr: Monasteries closing
    - Dear Br Brian, thank you for sharing that. I enjoy your
      thoughtful reflective honest videos. Please what is your
      perspective on the Benedictine vs Cistercian charisms. I am
      62 and have the opportunity of being a live in Cistercian
      oblate in Nth Ireland. I also can start in a Benedictine
      Abbey in England with a view to becoming a monk. I have
      tried being a postulant 3 times with Benedictine abbeys in
      England and left after about 9 months thinking the
      community was not right for me. This Benedictine monastery
      looks very humble and good and the Trappist one is more
      spartan but I love the Trappists and their straightforward
      ascetical simplified life. Plenty of time for lectio, more
      emphasis on silence and contemplation, the physical work,
      the early rising. The Bens do the Gregorian chant which
      I love but will it help me grow closer to Christ? I think
      sometimes the Bens put too much emphasis on liturgical
      traditions and academic concerns which are nothing to do
      with becoming more like Jesus and finding a glimpse of God
      in meditation and silence, for example.  This is my last
      chance. I dont want to get it wrong! Over the years I have
      been an avid reader of Mertons books, Andre Louf, and many
      many more books on monastic life, even buddhist and hindu
      autobiographies. I love the wisdom of the sages and stories
      of saints, particularly contemplatives and hermits. I think
      I am seeking freedom to seek God in monastic life. Am
      I mistaken in thinking the Trappist life is more
      contemplative? I value your advice.
        - I would say that the No.1 thing a candidate would need
          to ask themselves, regarding monastic communities, in
          Ireland and the UK is, "What future does this community
          have?"  All the Cistercian communities in Ireland are
          currently in a process of discernment about closing.
          This is also true for many Benedictine communities in
          the UK.
        - If I am 62 years of age I ask myself, 'Does this
          community have another 20 years for me to live monastic
          life?"  I know it sounds brutal looking at it in
          a purely mathematical way but you have to.
        - Regarding Benedictine or Cistercian vocation I would
          suggest looking at it this way.  At the beginning, all
          monastic communities will feed you spiritually, with
          its distinctive lifestyle, but the question to ask
          yourself (and play out in your imagination) is, "Will
          this particular community be able to continue feeding
          my spiritual life in 5 years time?"  "Will it drive me
          crazy?" "Will I find peace here?"  Don't shy away from
          these questions.  Wishing you blessings in your
          seeking.
