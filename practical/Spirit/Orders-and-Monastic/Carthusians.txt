### Vocation?
    - Determining a vocation to the Carthusian order is a deeply
      personal and spiritual process that unfolds over time. The
      Carthusians emphasize solitude, silence, and asceticism
      more than many other monastic communities. Signs of an
      vocation:
    1. Drawn to Solitude:
        - You feel spiritually alive when alone. Solitude isn't
          loneliness for you; it's a space for deep communion
          with God.
    2. Love for Liturgy and Prayer:
        - The Carthusian life revolves around liturgical prayers,
          personal meditation, and the sacraments. A strong
          attraction to these can indicate a vocation.
    3. Ascetic Aspirations:
        - The Carthusian lifestyle is austere—simple food, basic
          clothing, limited social interactions. If you find such
          ascetic practices appealing, it might be a clue.
    4. Resilience:
        - A Carthusian's life isn't easy. It requires
          psychological strength and _emotional stability_. If
          you naturally have or are developing these traits, it
          could be a sign.
    5. An Inexplicable Pull:
        - Sometimes a vocation isn't logical. You just know deep
          down God is calling you to this particular way of life.
    6. Spiritual Affirmation:
        - Other devout people have noticed and affirmed your
          spiritual inclinations. This external confirmation can
          often be a powerful indicator.
    7. Alignment with Carthusian Values:
        - A strong resonance with Carthusian principles, like the
          contemplative life, silence, and work, might indicate
          a good fit.
    8. Desire to Live for God Alone:
        - The Carthusian motto is "Stat crux dum volvitur orbis"
          ("The Cross stands firm as the world turns"). If you
          find yourself increasingly disinterested in worldly
          pursuits and more focused on living solely for God,
          take note.
    9. Spiritual Counsel:
        - A spiritual director or confessor senses that your
          calling aligns with Carthusian spirituality.
    10. Trial and Discernment:
        - Many monastic vocations get clarified during a trial
          period at a monastery. If you find peace and spiritual
          growth during such a period, it's a strong indicator.
          If these signs resonate with you, the next step is
          usually to engage in a serious process of discernment,
          often involving spiritual direction, retreats, and
          possibly even a visit to a Carthusian charterhouse for
          a more in-depth experience of their way of life.

    - 11:45 Matins and Lauds—Angelus

    - 2:15 Sleep
    - 5:50 AM Rise
    - 6:00 Primes
    - 6:30 Angelus, meditation
    - 7:15 Litany of the Saints, Conventual Mass
    - About 8:00 in the chapels: Terce, Private Masses, Communion
    - 9:00 Terce (BVM), a little relaxation in private
    - 9:30 Theological Studies
    - 10:15 Manual Work
    - 11:00 Sexts, dinner, recreation in private
    - 12:00 PM Spiritual reading, theological studies
    - 1:30 Angelus, Nones
    - 2:00 Spiritual reading
    - 2:30 Vespers of BVM

    - 3:15 Study
    - 4:30 Manual work

    - 7:00 Angelus, Complines

### Timetable
    - Night
        - 23:30: Rise. Matins of the Virgin Mary. Personal prayer in cell.
        - 00:15: In church, Office of Matins followed by Lauds. Angelus.
        - 02:15: Return to cell and sleep after Lauds of the Blessed Virgin.

    - Morning:
        - 06:30: Rise.
        - 07:00: Office of Prime, followed by the Angelus. Prayer or readings for Mass.
        - 08:00: Conventual Mass in the church. Solitude Mass for fathers, work for brothers.
        - 10:00: Office of Terce in cell. Lectio divina and study.

    - Afternoon:
        - 12:00: Angelus. Office of Sext. Meal.
        - 14:00: Office of None. Manual work in cell for fathers, other work for brothers.

    - Evening:
        - 16:00: Vespers of the Virgin Mary in cell.
        - 16:15: Vespers in church, return to cell.
        - 17:00: Spiritual reading.
        - 18:00: Supper.
        - 18:45: Angelus and office of Compline.
        - 19:30: Bedtime.
