### Distinguished poet with an unconventional life
    - She was a devout Roman Catholic, and religious faith was
      a central theme in much of her poetry.
    - She studied English at St Anne's College, Oxford, from 1944
      to 1947.
    - She became associated with The Movement, the influential
      group of post-war British poets that included Philip Larkin
      and Kingsley Amis.
    - She won the Somerset Maugham Award in 1955 for her
      collection A Way of Looking.
    - She struggled with periods of poverty and mental illness,
      yet continued writing poetry throughout her life.

### “bag-lady” nickname?
    - In later life, Jennings was often seen around Oxford
      carrying plastic bags stuffed with papers, wearing
      practical, rather unglamorous clothes. When she received
      a CBE in 1992, her appearance attracted unkind press
      attention, and the nickname stuck. It obscured the fact
      that she was a respected, award-winning poet whose work had
      a substantial readership.


### Her connection with Oxford
    - Jennings moved to Oxford with her family when she was six
      and spent almost all her life there. She worked at Oxford
      City Library, lived in various rented rooms and bedsits,
      and became a familiar figure in local cafés, where she
      would sit with her papers and observe the people around
      her.
    - In her later years, she lived in Old Headington, including
      at Unity House, 8 St Andrew's Lane. She died in 2001, aged
      75, and is buried in Wolvercote Cemetery.


### Biographies
    - Elizabeth Jennings: The Inward War by Dana Greene 
        - a full biography published in 2018.
    - “Clarify Me, Please, God of the Galaxies” — First Things
        - an appreciation of her poetry, Catholic faith and
          difficult later life.

- She is a particularly interesting figure because the apparent
  eccentricity of her everyday life sits alongside poetry of
  considerable technical discipline, emotional subtlety and
  religious seriousness.

### 1. One Flesh — the loneliness inside a marriage
    - This is one of her best-known poems. She observes her
      elderly parents sitting together, apparently united by
      habit but emotionally distant from one another. The title
      evokes both the Christian idea of marriage and the strange
      separation that can develop within it.
    - The striking thing about Jennings is that she doesn't need
      melodrama. She looks at an ordinary domestic scene and
      finds something quietly devastating beneath it.
    - What makes it characteristic: her ability to make restraint
      carry enormous emotional weight. She is compassionate, but
      she doesn't sentimentalise the people she describes.

### 2. Delay — the strange experience of waiting
    - Jennings frequently writes about the moments when life
      seems suspended: when a person is waiting for something to
      happen, unable to force an answer or resolution.
    - In Delay, that experience becomes an opportunity to explore
      uncertainty and the limits of human control. Her poetry
      often has this quality of inward stillness — an awareness
      that the most important events in our lives may not be the
      ones we can arrange or explain.
    - What makes it characteristic: she treats uncertainty not
      merely as frustration but as a condition in which we
      discover something about ourselves.

### 3. The Enemies — the struggle within the self
    - This poem gives a sense of her more searching,
      psychologically intense side. Jennings was interested in
      the divisions within human beings: the conflict between
      what we want and what we believe, between our outward lives
      and our inward selves.
    - Her Catholicism was important here, but her poems are not
      simply expressions of settled religious certainty. They
      often contain doubt, vulnerability and a desire for
      clarity.
    - What makes it characteristic: she approaches the spiritual
      life as something lived through struggle, rather than as
      a set of easy answers.
