earth not to trust life.

I: But tell me — were there moments in your own life when you
lost Fundamental Trust in Life, even briefly?

D: It is challenged again and again, again and again. Even by
small things it is challenged.

I: For example?

D: Through pain. And also through the ways human beings behave
— that such things are possible at all.

I: Mhm.

D: Fundamental Trust in Life is repeatedly challenged.

But when I imagine no longer trusting life — what then should
I trust? Then everything loses meaning.

I: Mhm.

D: It simply comes down to recognising: “One cannot live that
way. Without Fundamental Trust in Life, one cannot live.”

We know this. We can experience it.

So we try to nourish Fundamental Trust in Life, to build it up,
to cultivate it. To cultivate trust in life within oneself.

I: As a final question before we end this conversation:

What would you recommend or advise people who are trying to
discover what the right and important path in their life is? How
can they learn Fundamental Trust in Life?

D: Mhm. Through gratitude, because gratitude is an expression of
Fundamental Trust in Life. (Lebensvertrauen)

In every moment, looking closely: “What is life giving me now?”

The simple fact that I can breathe, that I am alive — I did not
cause that, buy it, or somehow choose it for myself. Life Lives
Us.

So how could I not trust it?

I: And with that we now bring this conversation to a close. Thank
you very much, Brother David Steindl-Rast.

D: You spoke earlier about death. And I would still like to add
something.

I: Mhm.

D: It is a sentence I do not wish to elaborate on further, but it
comes from Otto Mauer.

He was a very famous preacher in Vienna during my youth, who held
artists’ masses. One could see all the famous artists there. Some
people only went to church there because they wanted to see the
famous artists.

And he coined a very profound saying:

(gold)
“A human being does not die from death, but from ripened love.”

I: Mhm.

D: And that is really something one should not flatten by
over-explaining, but rather quietly reflect upon.

I: Then we have now added something more to life — and to this
conversation. Thank you very much.

D: Thank you, Olivia.
