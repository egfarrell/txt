    - The combination of priest and scientist is nothing new; but
      in his case what was really astonishing was his closeness
      to the earth and his deep feeling for the value of matter.
    - People who were shocked by him never realised how deep lay
      the roots of this simultaneous love of God and of the
      world. 
    - Throughout my whole life,' he wrote, during every moment
      I have lived, the world has gradually been taking on light
      and fire for me, until it has come to envelop me in one
      mass of luminosity, glowing from within... The purple Hush
      of matter fading imperceptibly into the gold of spirit, to
      be lost finally in the incandescence of a _personal
      universe_ ...

    - From the smallest individual detail to the vastest
      aggregations, our living universe (in common with our
      inorganic universe) has a structure, and this structure can
      owe its nature only to a phenomenon of growth.'

    - Thus the slow progress of energies must reach a peak, from
      which life will never slip back'. 
    - To overcome every obstacle, to unite our beings without
      loss of individual personality, there is a single force
      which nothing can replace and nothing destroy, a force
      which urges us forwards and draws us upwards: 
        - this is the force of love. 
    - We can thus appreciate the central position in Teilhard's
      whole philosophy, of Christ, prototype of Man-Love;
      'God-Love reaching self-fulfilment only in love.
    - Christianity,' he tells us, ' is nothing more nor less than
      a " phylum of love" within nature.'

    - Now every genesis presupposes inter-connections, mutual or
      reciprocal dependence, with no breach. It implies in the
      being that is forming itself a kinship between the
      composing elements; thus a static cosmos, fragmented in
      make-up, is unthinkable. If everything forms itself,
      everything must hold together. Matter and spirit, then, as
      we know them in our universe, are not two separate
      substances, set side by side and differing in nature.
        - They are two distinct aspects of one single cosmic
          stuff and there is between them no conflict to baffle
          our intelligence. 
        - Physical energy contains in itself something of the
          spiritual, and since the upward trend of energy is
          a fact we can observe and verify with the increasing
          complexity of organisms, the law of the universe must
          surely be a continually progressing, irreversible,
          spiritualisation. 
        - Matter has now lost its former attraction for Pere
          Teilhard: 'the felicity that I had sought in iron,
          I can find now only in Spirit.' 
        - It was during these decisive years that he was ordained
          priest. Marked now with the priestly character, freed
          from the commitments involved in theological studies,
          and intellectually awake to the consequences of
          a generalised theory of evolution, he set about
          building up the structure of his own interior universe.
        - This was now the pivot on which turned all his
          activities, his mental attitudes and his thought. 
        - He resolved in future to collaborate with all his

    - It is easy to see how deeply Pere Teilhard was imbued with
      his vision of working to build the future. Nothing else had
      the power to impress itself on him. 
    - And by the future he meant more than the building up of the

    - In addition to the originality of his thought and personal

        - he could listen to others and seem really interested in

    - Many have been struck by Pere Teilhard's great optimism. He
      was indeed an optimist, in his attribution to the universe

        - but in the daily life that concerned him personally, he

        - During that period he was at times prostrated by fits

        - But, calling on all the resources of his will, he
          abandoned himself to the supremely Great, to his
          Christ, as the only purpose of his being; and so hid
          his suffering and took up his work again, if not with
          joy, at least in the hope that his own personal
          vocation might be fulfilled.

    - Since 1912, when he had completed his theological studies,
      Pere Teilhard's aspirations were quite definitely
      formulated. 
    - He was a priest and a religious, and his first duty was
      therefore to Christ. At the same time he was resolved, as
      we have seen, to 'participate' in the world, not to live in
      isolation from it. 
    - It was these seemingly `contradictory principles`, the
      service of Christ and participation in the world, that had
      to be reconciled. 
    - The evangelical doctrine of the _Redemption through the
      Cross_ had to be reconciled with the salvation of the world
      through active co-operation in the building up of the
      universe.

    - Pere Teilhard was fully alive to the danger that might lie
      in such statements. Wrongly interpreted they might engulf
      the Christian in a type of pantheism that denied to the
      supernatural its pre-eminent position. 
    - He himself, firm in his faith in the universal value of
      creation, was in no danger of falling into this error. 
    - 'I am not speaking metaphorically,' he wrote, 'when I say
      that it is throughout the length and breadth and depth of
      the world in movement that man can attain the experience
      and vision of God.'

          - impassioned and a little frightening (without going

    - In his own self the integration of life had been achieved;
      if he loved God, it was through the world, and if he loved
      the world it was as a function of God, the animator of all
      things. 
        - 'The joy and strength of my life,' he wrote a month
          before his death, 'will have lain in the realisation
          that when the two ingredients - `God` and the `world`
          were brought together they set up an endless mutual
          reaction, producing a sudden blaze of such _intense
          brilliance_ that all the depths of the world were lit
          up for me.
    - There was no contradiction in his soul, no ambiguity
      between his humble loyalty as a son of the Church and the
      boldness of his philosophical views. 
    - But in the depths of his being there raged the excruciating
      torment of reconciling his complete submission to the
      Church with the integrity of his thought.
    - Consciousness of the progression and synthesis of all
      things in Xristo Jem, I have found an extraordinarily rich
      and inexhaustible source of clarity and interior strength,
      and an atmosphere outside of which, it is now impossible
      for me to breathe, to worship, to believe.
    - What might have been taken in my attitude during the last
      thirty years for obstinacy or disrespect, is simply the
      result of my absolute inability to contain my own feeling
      of _wonderment_. 
    - Everything stems from that basic psychological condition,
      and I can no more change it than I can change my age or the
      colour of my eyes.
    - Let me repeat that, as I see it, this letter is simply an
      exposition of conscience and calls for no answer from you.
    - Look on it simply as a proof that you can count on me
      un-reservedly to work for the kingdom of God, which is the
      one thing I keep before my eyes and the one goal to which
      science leads me. 
        - Your most respectful in Xto filius

### Divine Milieu summary
    - De Chardin’s *The Divine Milieu* presents a revolutionary
      vision of the relationship between science, faith, and
      spirituality, reframing traditional Christian theology
      through an `evolutionary` lens. 
    - As a French Jesuit, paleontologist, and philosopher,
      Teilhard wrote during the early 20th century, at a time
      when science and religion were often seen as opposing
      fields. 
    - His work sought to integrate the two, exploring the cosmic
      and personal dimensions of faith in a universe that he saw
      as in a constant state of spiritual evolution toward
      ultimate unity with God.

    ### Vision and Purpose of the Divine Milieu
        - The central theme is the belief that everything in
          existence, including matter, energy, and consciousness,
          has a divine orientation and is actively involved in
          a journey toward union with God. 
        - For Teilhard, the world is more than a passive
          creation; it is a dynamic milieu where the Divine is
          present and active, drawing all things to itself. 
        - He envisions God as not only present within the
          universe but also as its ultimate destiny or *Omega
          Point*, a concept Teilhard uses to describe the
          culmination of evolutionary processes. 
        - This divine milieu represents a field in which God
          works with all created things, and in which human
          beings can actively participate in God’s plan.

        - Teilhard’s aim is to inspire readers to recognize that
          everyday experiences, both the joys and sufferings of
          life, are meaningful contributions to a larger
          spiritual process. 
        - By living consciously and lovingly within the divine
          milieu, each individual participates in this cosmic
          evolution and draws closer to God. 
        - He encourages readers to live in a way that is both
          active and contemplative, engaging with the world while
          maintaining a deep awareness of God’s presence within
          it.

    ### The Personal and Cosmic Dimensions of Union with God
        - Teilhard divides *The Divine Milieu* into two primary
          parts: the _personal_ and the _cosmic_ aspects of unity
          with God. 
        - In the _personal_ dimension, Teilhard emphasizes the
          individual's relationship with God, asserting that this
          relationship can be enriched by embracing both activity
          and passivity. 
        - For Teilhard, activity means working creatively,
          lovingly, and with purpose, contributing to the
          advancement of humanity and the earth as part of God’s
          plan. 
        - He sees such endeavors as acts of cooperation with God,
          adding value to the world through personal efforts and
          aspirations.
        - On the other hand, Teilhard introduces the concept of
          “passivity” as a necessary component of spiritual
          growth. 
        - By accepting life’s hardships, setbacks, and
          limitations with a spirit of surrender, individuals
          grow closer to God. 
        - Rather than viewing suffering and loss as meaningless,
          Teilhard reframes them as integral aspects of spiritual
          evolution. 
        - When people embrace both joy and suffering as
          opportunities for growth, they not only find deeper
          personal connection with the divine but also contribute
          to the cosmic process of evolution toward unity with
          God.

    ### The Cosmic Christ and the Omega Point
        - One of the most profound aspects of *The Divine Milieu*
          is Teilhard’s exploration of the cosmic Christ. 
        - He argues that Christ’s incarnation and resurrection
          represent more than isolated historical events; 
            - they are metaphysical symbols of God’s unity with
              all of creation. 
        - Teilhard envisions Christ as the driving force behind
          evolution, working through the physical and spiritual
          processes to bring about the eventual divine
          culmination of all things.
        - Central to Teilhard’s vision is the idea of the `Omega
          Point`, a term he uses to describe God as both the
          origin and the ultimate goal of creation. 
        - The Omega Point represents the convergence of all
          individual and collective efforts, culminating in
          a final, cosmic union with God. 
        - In Teilhard’s view, human progress, scientific
          discovery, and spiritual practices are all parts of
          a single process moving toward this point of divine
          fulfillment. 
        - By drawing closer to God individually and as a species,
          humanity actively participates in this evolutionary
          process, contributing to the development of a world
          where all things are unified in love and knowledge of
          God.

    ### The Role of Love as a Unifying Force
        - A fundamental aspect of Teilhard’s spirituality is his
          belief in `love` as the primary force driving the
          universe toward its divine destiny. 
        - He sees love not only as a personal emotion or action
          but as a cosmic energy that links all beings and
          propels them toward unity. 
        - This love is exemplified in the teachings of Jesus,
          whom Teilhard sees as the embodiment of divine love.
          Love, according to Teilhard, is what makes life
          purposeful and transformative, enabling individuals and
          communities to overcome division and work toward common
          goals.
        - Teilhard’s concept of love is expansive, including the
          love between people, the love for creation, and even
          the love that exists within the evolutionary processes
          of the universe. 
        - For Teilhard, love is the force that binds all things
          together, giving rise to cooperation, compassion, and
          progress. 
        - As humanity grows in love, it brings the world closer
          to the Omega Point, fostering a deeper connection with
          God and with one another.

    ### Faith in an Evolutionary Universe
        - One of the most challenging yet inspiring elements of
          the book is Teilhard’s insistence that `faith` should
          adapt to the reality of an `evolving universe`. He
          encourages readers to see the relationship between
          science and religion as complementary rather than
          conflicting. 
        - For Teilhard, scientific discoveries about the origins
          and development of the universe reveal a deeper
          understanding of God’s creativity and purpose.
        - Evolution, in his view, is not a threat to faith but
          rather a path that leads to greater insight into God’s
          work in the world.

        - Teilhard believes that human beings are co-creators
          with God, actively participating in the ongoing process
          of evolution through their thoughts, actions, and
          relationships. 
        - He calls for a spirituality that is compatible with
          scientific knowledge, open to change, and responsive to
          `new insights` about the universe. 
        - Teilhard’s vision is thus both profoundly mystical and
          deeply practical, urging believers to engage fully with
          the world as an expression of their faith.

    ### Conclusion
        - *The Divine Milieu* represents Teilhard’s attempt to
          bridge the gap between science and spirituality,
          offering a vision of faith that is expansive,
          integrative, and responsive to the challenges of
          modernity. 
        - He encourages readers to embrace both activity and
          passivity in their spiritual lives, viewing all aspects
          of existence, joy, suffering, work, and rest, as part
          of a larger divine plan. 
        - Teilhard’s vision of the Omega Point and the cosmic
          Christ provides a hopeful perspective on humanity’s
          place in the universe, presenting love as the ultimate
          force that drives all things toward union with God.

    - In sum, *The Divine Milieu* is a work that invites
      believers to find God in the world around them, recognizing
      that every act of love and every moment of faith
      contributes to the cosmic journey toward unity with the
      divine. 
    - Through this dynamic vision, Teilhard redefines
      spirituality for an age of scientific advancement, urging
      humanity to see the divine potential in all things.
