### Background
- Jean Sulivan was a French Catholic priest who treated faith less as a system
  of answers than as a restless search. He spent much of his life on the edges
  of institutional Catholicism while never really leaving it. ([Commonweal][1])
- His recurring theme: God is found not in certainty, power, or religious
  respectability, but in vulnerability, doubt, freedom, and human encounter.
  ([Commonweal][1])
- He distrusted slogans, ideologies, and clerical self-importance. For Sulivan,
  the Gospel was an invitation to liberation rather than control.
  ([Commonweal][1])
- Stylistically, he is lyrical, introspective, and existential—think of a
  meeting point between French literary modernism and Christian mysticism.
  ([Gerard Manley Hopkins][2])
- One of his best-known lines about writing was: 
    - “I write in order to lie a little less.” 
    - That captures his whole project: stripping away pretence to get closer to
      reality, God, and the self. ([Commonweal][1])
- If figures like Thomas Merton or Simone Weil appeal to you, Sulivan has a
  similar intensity, but with a more rebellious and distinctly French
  sensibility. ([America Magazine][3])

- A one-sentence flavour: 
    - **Sulivan is the patron saint of believers who cannot stop questioning,
      and questioners who cannot quite stop believing.**
