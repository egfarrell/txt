- The philosopher argues that much of our suffering comes from
  seeking approval from others and living according to others’
  expectations. True freedom and happiness come from having the
  courage to be disliked—that is, the courage to live in
  accordance with one’s own values rather than conforming to
  societal pressures.

### Key Takeaways:

1. Separation of Tasks
   - Recognize what is within your control and what belongs to
     others. You only need to concern yourself with your own
     tasks and not interfere with others’ tasks or worry about
     how others perceive you.

2. The past does not determine your future
   - Unlike deterministic psychology, Adlerian psychology
     suggests that your past experiences don’t dictate your life.
     You can choose the meaning you assign to past events and
     decide how to act moving forward.

3. People seek recognition (or “strokes”), but seeking it often
   leads to unhappiness
   - Chasing approval from others creates dependence and anxiety.
     True freedom comes from internal validation, not external
     approval.

4. All problems are interpersonal relationship problems
   - Many personal issues arise because of fears surrounding how
     others perceive us. Overcoming this requires courage to be
     disliked and authentic in relationships.

5. Encourage contribution and community feeling
   - Feel a sense of belonging through contributing to others and
     your community rather than competing or feeling superior.

6. Self-acceptance and self-responsibility
   - Accept yourself as you are without self-pity. Take full
     responsibility for your life and choices, knowing you have
     the power to change.

7. Live in the “here and now”
   - Don’t be burdened by past traumas or future worries. Focus
     on what you can do today to move toward your goals and
     happiness.
