### Ruth Carmel Book: "The Standard Bearers"
    - $MY_BOOKS/Spirit/Ruth-Burrows-Carmel-Tradition.pdf
    - Really struck by this passage:
        - Far more frequently we find her extolling the
          privilege, the holiness, the fruitfulness of the
          enclosed, hidden life of her daughters. 
        - In their deep seclusion they were in the forefront of
          the fighting line. She assigns to them the office of
          standard bearer.
        - They _wield no weapons_, strike no blows, but remain
          passive, holding aloft the standard, prepared to be cut
          to pieces rather than let it fall. 
        - We recall the significance of the standard. It
          symbolised that cause, those values for which the
          combatants were fighting and prepared to die. 
        - So, her nuns not merely symbolised the heart of the
          Church, her nature, the reason for her existence, but,
          hidden away in their cloisters, actually lived out this
          central reality. 
        - If the standard falls, all is lost, the combatants lose
          heart and collapse in disarray. 

### Recommended Book
    - (Dom Św. Jakuba ul. Szkolna 1, 42-595 Sączów)
    - "47 lat życia"
    - Maksymiliana Kolbe – męczennik z Oświęcimia i Rudolf Höss
      – założyciel oraz komendant obozu zagłady...
    - Maksymilian Kolbe, a martyr from Oświęcim and 
    - Rudolf Höss founder and commander of the extermination camp
      ...
    - Their life begins similarly. They get to know the world and
      grow up. They serve as altar boys. They have their dreams.
      As young boys, they choose life paths. 
        - One: taking the name of Maksymilian, he enters the
          convent. He becomes a priest, goes on missions. He puts
          on Niepokalanów and entrusts himself completely to
          Mary. 
        - Second...
    - They meet at the Auschwitz-Birkenau camp. They will both
      live for 47 years. Same time, same period in history. But
      how completely different characters. And completely
      different ideas they serve.
    - An efficiently written story that confronts love and
      humility, bestiality and the lack of human mercy.
      Addictive, but at the same time very demanding reading.
