### Breathing Under Water 
- “I built my house by the sea.
- Not on the sands, mind you;
- not on the shifting sand.
- And I built it of rock.
- A strong house
- by a strong sea.
- And we got well acquainted, the sea and I.
- Good neighbors.
- Not that we spoke much.
- We met in silences.
- Respectful, keeping our distance,
- but looking our thoughts across the fence of sand.
- Always, the fence of sand our barrier,
- always, the sand between.
- And then one day,
- -and I still don’t know how it happened -
- the sea came.
- Without warning.
- Without welcome, even
- Not sudden and swift, but a shifting across the sand like wine,
- less like the flow of water than the flow of blood.
- Slow, but coming.
- Slow, but flowing like an open wound.
- And I thought of flight and I thought of drowning and I thought of death.
- And while I thought the sea crept higher, till it reached my door.
- And I knew, then, there was neither flight, nor death, nor drowning.
- That when the sea comes calling, you stop being neighbors,
- Well acquainted, friendly-at-a-distance neighbors,
- And you give your house for a coral castle,
- And you learn to breathe underwater.”
- (Sr. Carol Bialock, RSCJ)

### Poema XX
Glosa a lo divino
“For All the Beauty”
John of the Cross
––––––––––

For all created beauty
I shall never lose myself,
but only for that I-know-not-what
that comes unbidden.

I
The savor of all finite good,
at most, can only go so far:
it wearies appetite
and spoils the palate’s taste.
So not for all the sweetness here
shall I ever lose myself,
but only for that I-know-not-what
that comes to me unbidden.

II
A generous heart will never rest
where passage still is easy;
it presses on toward harder ground.
Nothing ever fills it full.
Its faith ascends so very high
that it tastes an I-know-not-what
given when it is given.

III
The one who suffers love’s own wound,
touched by the divine itself,
finds every taste so overturned
that earthly pleasures fail.
Like one with fever who rejects all food,
he longs for an I-know-not-what
bestowed beyond all seeking.

IV
Do not be startled by this change,
that taste should thus fall away:
the cause of this affliction
is unlike all other ills.
So every creature fades from view,
and the soul delights in an I-know-not-what
which God grants unbidden.

V
For once the will is touched by God,
it cannot be content
with anything but God alone.
Yet since His beauty can be seen
only by the eye of faith,
the will tastes Him as an I-know-not-what
which is found unbidden.

VI
Then tell me, would you pity one
so utterly in love
that nothing in creation brings him joy?
Alone, without all form or figure,
finding neither foothold nor support,
he tastes an I-know-not-what
that comes to him unbidden.

VII
Do not imagine that the inward soul,
whose life is of far greater worth,
finds joy in what delights the senses here;
rather, beyond all beauty,
beyond all that was, is, or will be,
it savors an I-know-not-what
given when it is given.

VIII
Whoever seeks to rise more high
gives greater care to what remains to gain
than to what is already won.
So toward still greater heights
I ever bend my longing,
beyond all things, toward an I-know-not-what
bestowed beyond all seeking.

IX
For nothing grasped by sense or thought,
nor all the mind can comprehend,
however lofty it may be,
nor grace, nor beauty, nor delight,
will ever make me lose myself —
but only for that I-know-not-what
which God grants unbidden.

### The monk and the Quantum
    There once was a lad named Enda,
    From Leitrim, he hailed, full of awe.
    His love for all Physics, oh what a geek,
    With dreams of a theory, oh so unique!

    In the County of Sligo, he yearned for true peace,
    A Carmelite brother, his heart would release.
    A mixed monk community, oh what a sight,
    His quirks and much more, they'd surely ignite!

    Quantum Gravity, his mind, long did explore,
    In equations and charts, he'd ponder and more.
    From atoms to stars, to space he'd fly deep,
    Calculations flowed, while he struggled to sleep.

    With a robe and a laptop, he'd sure proudly lope,
    To a monastery hill, clutching hard a great hope.
    Brother Enda they'd call him, laughing with fun,
    A physicist monk, an oddity to see!

    In the tiny old chapel, he'd meditate and pray,
    Contemplating those particles, he'd sure find his way.
    "God is the master... of Quantum's embrace,"
    Whispered he to the stars, with a curious grace.

    The other dear monks, would sure scratch their heads,
    As Enda talked long, of visions ahead.
    "Brother, focus on prayers... and stillness" they'd say,
    While Enda dreamed on... of a quantum ballet.

    His fellow wise monks, mused he'd gone astray,
    While Enda searched onwards, for quanta at play.
    He'd calculate mysteries, in soft candlelight,
    Unraveling God's plan with his might through the night.

    One day as he pondered, the old monastery gate,
    A theory just struck him, a moment so great!
    Quantum Gravity he just saw it, ever so clear,
    His dream fusing faith, and science, so dear.

    In excitement he shared, his new revelation,
    To those who had wondered, his endless fixation.
    But Enda, undaunted, embraced all the fears,
    For deep in his heart, a truth had appeared.

    And so this odd monk, researcher in glens,
    Kept studying those particles, again and again.
    His quest for true knowledge, his spirit aligned,
    A path from our faith to the Cosmos combined.

    His journey, an ode, to wonder and mirth,
    This wandering pilgrim, who long walked the Earth,
    A blend of the mystical, and science and grace,
    With faith in the Cosmos, he'd sure find his place.

### A Student's Evening Hymn
    - by James Clerk Maxwell

    - I.
    - Now no more the slanting rays
    - With the mountain summits dally,
    - Now no more in crimson blaze
    - Evening’s fleecy cloudless rally,
    - Soon shall Night front off the valley
    - Sweep that bright yet earthly haze,
    - And the stars most musically
    - Move in endless rounds of praise.


    - II.
    - While the world is growing dim,
    - And the Sun is slow descending
    - Past the far horizon’s rim,
    - Earth's low sky to heaven extending,
    - Let my feeble earth-notes, blending
    - With the songs of cherubim,
    - Through the same expanse ascending,
    - Thus renew my _evening hymn_.


    - III.
    - Thou that fill’st our waiting eyes
    - With the food of contemplation,
    - Setting in thy darkened skies
    - Signs of infinite creation,
    - Grant to nightly meditation
    - What the toilsome day denies—
    - Teach me in this _earthly station_
    - Heavenly Truth to realise.


    - IV.
    - Give me wisdom so to use
    - These brief hours of thoughtful leisure,
    - That I may _no instant lose_
    - In mere meditative pleasure,
    - But with strictest justice measure
    - All the ends my life pursues,
    - Lies to crush and truths to treasure,
    - Wrong to shun and Right to choose.


    - V.
    - Then, when unexpected Sleep,
    - O’er my long-closed eyelids stealing,
    - Opens up that lower deep
    - Where Existence has no feeling,
    - May sweet Calm, my languor healing,
    - Lend note strength at dawn _to reap_
    - All that Shadows, world-concealing,
    - For the bold enquirer keep.


    - VI.
    - Through the creatures Thou hast made
    - Show the brightness of Thy glory,
    - Be eternal Truth displayed
    - In their substance transitory,
    - Till green Earth and Ocean hoary,
    - _Massy rock and tender blade_
    - Tell the same unending story—
    - "We are Truth in Form arrayed."

    - VII.
    - When to study I retire,
    - And from books of ancient sages
    - Glean _fresh sparks of buried fire_
    - Lurking in their ample pages—
    - While the task my mind engages
    - Let _old words new truths inspire-_—
    - Truths that to all after-ages
    - Prompt the Thoughts that never tire.

    - VIII.
    - Yet if, led by shadows fair
    - I have uttered words of folly,
    - Let the kind absorbing air
    - Stifle every sound unholy.
    - So when Saints with Angels lowly
    - Join in heaven’s _unceasing prayer_,
    - Mine as certainly, though slowly,
    - May ascend and mingle there.

    - IX.
    - Teach me so Thy works to read
    - That my faith, —new strength accruing,—
    - May from world to world proceed,
    - Wisdom's fruitful search pursuing;
    - Till, thy truth my mind imbuing,
    - I proclaim the Eternal Creed,
    - Oft the glorious theme renewing
    - God our Lord is God indeed.

    - X.
    - Give me love aright to trace
    - Thine to everything created,
    - Preaching to a ransomed race
    - By Thy mercy renovated,
    - Till with all thy fulness sated
    - I behold thee face to face
    - And with Ardour unabated
    - Sing the glories of thy grace.


### The Song of Wandering Aengus (Sligo 2022)
    - (Original title: "A Mad Song")
    - I went out to the hazel wood,
    - Because a fire was in my head,
    - And cut and peeled a hazel wand,
    - And hooked a berry to a thread;
    - And when white moths were on the wing,
    - And moth-like stars were flickering out,
    - I dropped the berry in a stream
    - And caught a little silver trout.

    - When I had laid it on the floor
    - I went to blow the fire a-flame,
    - But something rustled on the floor,
    - And someone called me by my name:
    - It had become a glimmering girl
    - With apple blossom in her hair
    - Who called me by my name and ran
    - And faded through the brightening air.

    - Though I am old with wandering
    - Through hollow lands and hilly lands,
    - I will find out where she has gone,
    - And kiss her lips and take her hands;
    - And walk among long dappled grass,
    - And pluck till time and times are done,
    - The silver apples of the moon,
    - The golden apples of the sun.
        - By William Butler Yeats

### Spirit of God, Descend upon My Heart
    - George Croly

    Spirit of God, who dwells within my heart,
    wean it from sin, through all its pulses move.
    Stoop to my weakness, mighty as you are,
    and help me love you as I ought to love.

### The Secret of the Universe
    SPIN, I spin, around, around,
      And close my eyes,
      And let the bile arise
    From the sacred region of the soul’s Profound;
    Then gaze upon the world; how strange! how new!
      The earth and heaven are one,
      The horizon-line is gone,
    The sky how green! the land how fair and blue!
    Perplexing items fade from my large view,
    And thought which vexed me with its false and true
    Is swallowed up in Intuition; this,
      This is the sole true mode
      Of reaching God,
    And gaining the universal synthesis
    Which makes All—One; while fools with peering eyes
    Dissect, divide, and vainly analyse.
    So round, and round, and round again!
    How the whole globe swells within my brain,
    The stars inside my lids appear,
    The murmur of the spheres I hear
    Throbbing and beating in each ear;
    Right in my navel I can feel
    The centre of the world’s great wheel.
    Ah peace divine, bliss dear and deep,
      No stay, no stop,
      Like any top
    Whirling with swiftest speed, I sleep.
    O ye devout ones round me coming,
    Listen! I think that I am humming;
      No utterance of the servile mind
    With poor chop-logic rules agreeing
      Here shall ye find,
    But inarticulate burr of man’s unsundered being.
    Ah, could we but devise some plan,
    Some patent jack by which a man
    Might hold himself ever in harmony
    With the great whole, and spin perpetually,
      As all things spin
      Without, within,
    As Time spins off into Eternity,
    And Space into the inane Immensity,
    And the Finite into God’s Infinity,
      Spin, spin, spin, spin.

    - by Edward Dowden (1843-1913)

### It Really Happened - Getting Back
    - "Shelly" - Sheila Hudson
    - (Simon Voices Issue 1 Winter 2008)

    I am sitting in rehab, in the women’s house, happy to be
    here and getting my life together and OH! BOY! What
    a life. I went into Detox on the 8th of November 2006,
    a Wednesday and what a relief to get in. I was so sick
    you would not believe it. It was the lowest I ever went,
    my energy was gone, I was seven stone and could not
    walk. The pain I experienced through my whole body was
    a nightmare. I did not think I would make it; my mind
    was like a black cloud, Jeshua! I kept saying, HELP ME!
    I could not think further than my nose, my mind was
    disturbed, my words were slurred, my eyes blurred. And
    my legs were numb. I was a total wreck.

    Like a child that had lost everything I cried with pain,
    my heart ached, my head had many thoughts of hurt and
    loneliness, insecurity of being on the streets. I wanted
    my DAD who had recently died, I wanted my CHILDREN and
    my PARTNER and my JOB which I lost through drink.
    I prayed to get better. The hospital said if I went on
    the way I was going I would be dead in a year.

    It’s amazing what drink can do to you and the lack of
    food. I drank for 6 months, heavy with barely any food.
    I had to learn to eat again. The drink controlled me,
    I had lost my FATHER, PARTNER, CHILDREN, AND JOB, and
    all I had was PRAYER.

    I am now supported in REHAB on the Quays (DUBLIN). They
    put me back together again, both DETOX and REHAB. The
    staff are all excellent, I found it hard to adapt at
    first but they kept at me and I gradually came around to
    the proper way to do things and accept my mistakes.
    I found it hard to deal with change, due to insecurity,
    anxiety and nerves, and the confusion of living a normal
    life. They put me back on track and I thank them all
    very much. When the drink hits the nervous system you
    know you are in trouble.

    DETOX, REHAB and AFTERCARE are so important, we don’t
    realize how important, they save lives. I am just
    4 months sober now and doing a C.E. Scheme and am on the
    way to getting accommodation and a job and THANK GOD!
    The get up and go to want all this and leave the drink
    behind me. Learning the simple things in life are nice
    again and so easy. We make it so hard on ourselves with
    abusing drink. I wrote this so others might see and
    learn how bad I got and all I had left was a PRAYER! And
    people that cared!


### SOLITUDE (Alexander Pope)
    Happy the man, whose wish and care
    A few paternal acres bound,
    Content to breath his native air
        In his own ground.

    Whose herds with milk, whose fields with bread,
    Whose flocks supply him with attire;
    Whose trees in summer yield him shade,
        In winter, fire.

    Blest, who can unconcernedly find
    Hours, days, and years slide soft away
    In health of body, peace of mind;
        Quiet by day.

    Sound sleep by night; study and ease
    Together mixed, sweet recreation,
    And innocence, which most does please
        With meditation.

    Thus let me live, unseen, unknown;
    Thus unlamented let me die,
    Steal from the world, and not a stone
        Tell where I lie.
