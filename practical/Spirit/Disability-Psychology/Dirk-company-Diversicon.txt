### Diversicon

- He successfully placed autistic individuals in IT jobs – this
  is his second project
- April 5, 2017 | Georg Räth

- Many people with autism are employable in the job market – but
  face social hurdles. Dirk Müller-Remus is familiar with these
  problems; he has a son with Asperger’s syndrome and has just
  launched Diversicon, a company aimed at helping autistic
  individuals return to work.
- Müller-Remus, 59, founded the non-profit organization in Berlin
  alongside Rene Kuhlemann. Prior to this, he started Auticon
  GmbH in 2011, an IT consulting firm that also focuses on
  employing autistic people. Many DAX companies are reportedly
  among its clients, with Virgin founder and billionaire Richard
  Branson being one of the investors.
- In contrast, Diversicon, which launched in 2017, is still in
  the early stages and has recently started raising funds on the
  crowdfunding platform Companisto. So far, around €300,000 from
  400 supporters has been raised. The founder forecasts stronger
  growth for the company from 2019, and by 2020 they aim to also
  assist people with other disabilities, such as ADHD. In an
  interview with Dirk Müller-Remus: "Dirk, you want to take on
  social responsibility while also addressing the skills
  shortage. What is your main focus?"
- Both. Autistic people are employable in the job market thanks
  to their various strengths and special interests. However, due
  to challenges in social interaction and communication, they
  stand no chance in a normal job application process. For
  instance, they may take sentences literally and struggle to
  empathize with situations. This is why we want to build
  a bridge for them into the mainstream job market.
- But you can’t place every autistic person, due to a lack of job
  skills or psychosocial issues. What percentage of autistic
  people can you reach?
- There are about 800,000 autistic people in Germany. About half
  of those are aged between 18 and 55. Due to a lack of
  professional skills, the number is even smaller. Additionally,
  only a few are psychosocially able to take up a job – for
  example, due to a lack of stress resistance or depression. And
  then they also need to be activatable and motivated, leaving
  around 24,000 autistic people we can target.
- Isn’t that a very small target group?
- For every autistic person we get into work, the state saves
  around €20,000 per year, as recipients of benefits become
  contributors. If, for example, we were to create 1,000 jobs
  over seven years, that would save society around €40 million.
  What specific jobs are suitable for your target group?
- We don’t focus on specific jobs because autistic individuals
  can excel in a wide variety of fields. Anything related to
  structuring and optimization, consistency checks, quality
  management, development, design, research, or analysis are
  ideal tasks for autistic people. And what challenges do you
  face?
- It’s especially difficult to find the right people. To help
  with that, we rely on contacts with the Autism Association,
  self-help groups, and organizations for the gifted. And once
  you’ve found the right people?
- We train them for two months in technical and social skills
  tailored to the work environment. After that, we build a bridge
  to the mainstream job market through temporary employment, with
  the goal of having the company take on the autistic individual
  after the temporary phase. That’s the end goal. How do
  companies react to the subject of autism?
- There is a strong willingness to accept and respect people with
  autism. It just needs to be communicated properly. That’s why
  we don’t approach it from a social angle but focus on the
  economic benefits – this is a language companies understand.
  A few years ago, you founded Auticon, which hires autistic
  individuals as full-time IT consultants. Why are you broadening
  your focus now?
- Around 10% of autistic people have strengths in IT, but 90% do
  not. With Auticon, we proved that it’s possible to place
  autistic individuals in the mainstream job market and integrate
  them. Diversicon is the next logical step to expand this
  effort. Do you plan to include people with other developmental
  disorders as well?
- There are other people who are well-suited for the job market,
  but whose weaknesses are often the focus, for example, people
  with ADHD, burnout, or other mental health issues. But for now,
  we are focusing on autistic people, starting in Berlin, and if
  things go well, we will scale up.
- You’re currently raising money from the crowd on Companisto.
  What do you plan to do with the funds?
- We’re still in our first year and have many expenses. We need
  to build a team, obtain certifications, and create the
  infrastructure. And of course, in the first year, we barely
  have any revenue, but we aim to change that in 2017, with
  training that will be paid for by the Federal Employment
  Agency. And after that, you plan to generate revenue through
  temporary employment?
- Exactly, we will bill companies for temporary employment
  services. We aim to make a profit for the first time in 2019.
  With Auticon, you had a prominent investor in Richard Branson.
  Is there any chance he will get involved in your current
  company?
- No, Diversicon is a non-profit organization, while Auticon is
  a for-profit business. So, the two companies are entirely
  separate.
