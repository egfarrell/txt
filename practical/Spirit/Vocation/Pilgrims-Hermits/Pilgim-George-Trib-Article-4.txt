# Reporter describes George's 'boundless faith' Rob Amen

It's not often you meet a man likes George Walter.

A man of profound depth. Of immense devotion. Of boundless faith.

It's not often you write a story that makes you examine the way
you live your life. I've been fortunate to have two: an obituary
on John Lorenz, a Kiski Area wrestler who died in a tubing
accident on the Allegheny River shortly after graduating in 1999,
and this week's feature on Pilgrim George.

After speaking with George, it's difficult not to look in the
mirror and question if you are taking stock in what matters most.
It's nothing he says in particular; it's the feeling you get from
being around him. Justin Merriman, the former Valley News
Dispatch photographer who first came up with the idea of
following George on his pilgrimage, will concur.

George told me in April -- before I got to know him -- that it's
not uncommon for strangers to invite him into their homes to
sleep, to eat, to talk with their children. I couldn't image who
would do such a thing. I still couldn't until the Gay family of
Massillon, Ohio, stopped George on Route 172. Thank God they did.

Finding George at St. Mary of the Assumption church in Hampton
was easy. Tracking him on the road with nothing more than a rough
sketch of where he would be proved to be more vexing.

Justin and I had driven to Ohio to spend time with George and,
after an hour of searching, were losing faith when finally we
spotted him along the road. We were so thrilled to find him that
we parked the car and started walking, figuring we would hail
a taxi to drive us back at night.

Wrong.

There are no taxis in Massillon. At least we didn't see any. But
Kyle Gay was nice enough or crazy enough -- take your pick -- to
leave his family behind at the house with George and drive us 10
miles back to our car.

Have faith, George always told us. Justin and I were starting to
understand what he meant.

A month later, we flew to Chicago to talk with George as he
reached his destination, Marytown, in Libertyville, Ill. The
plane was almost full and an empty seat remained beside us when
a priest who happened to know George sat down.

What are the odds?

We landed in Chicago and drove 30 miles north to find George. No
luck. After combing a 10-mile stretch of highway for more than
three hours, searching behind churches for his tent and
helplessly yelling his name into empty fields, dusk had fallen,
and we called it a night.

The next morning, George called Justin's cell phone. He had
changed his route. He wound up sleeping under a bridge on a golf
course that night, a golf course Justin and I swear we passed at
least once.

Still, that Chicago trip ran much smoother than our trip to
Massillon. Rather than hoping to find a taxi, Justin and I took
turns driving ahead, then walking back to George, then walking
with him several miles before repeating it all over again.

In total, we figure we walked 27 miles with George. No wonder my
black Sperry Top-Siders need new soles.

His pilgrimage -- and ours -- is over for now. He will continue
to walk, and we'll continue to find him. Somehow.

Call it optimistic. Call it naive.

We call it having faith.

About the reporter

Rob Amen joined the Valley News Dispatch in December 1997 after
graduating from Penn State University with a bachelor's degree in
journalism.

He covered local high school sports for two years before becoming
the Valley News Dispatch's Steelers beat writer for three years.

Since 2003, he has served as an investigative/enterprise
reporter.

Amen has won three Golden Quill awards (one in 2000, two in 2002)
and two Keystone Press Awards (in 2002 and 2003) for his work at
the newspaper.

He currently is pursuing a master's degree in journalism and mass
communication at Point Park University.

Justin Merriman/Valley News Dispatch



# Photographer: George 'is faith' Justin Merriman

I first saw George Walter a few years ago along Greensburg Road
just outside of New Kensington. My reaction, I'm sure, was much
like the many who passed by him this summer during his three
million steps to Chicago.

He seemed different. Beyond the obvious -- the long beard, the
patchwork robe and the staff adorned with a crucifix -- he drew
my attention and set my mind to wander.

I wanted to pull over and photograph him but my daily assignments
required me to be elsewhere, so I decided I'd catch up with him
later. What I never realized was that later wouldn't come for
another three years.

When Rob Amen, a staff reporter with whom I worked on this story,
and I met up with George for the first time, we were intrigued by
his story.

He captivated us and we could hardly wait for his journey -- and
our own -- to begin.

George set off in April and so did we. Unsure where the road
would take us, we were fortunate enough to witness the life of
Pilgrim George on the road as he headed across the country to
Chicago.

Perhaps the greatest part of being a photographer is the fact
that our profession relies on us living the lives of our
subjects. The camera allows us to witness life. We are fortunate
whenever we come across a story as rare as George's. When I took
my first steps with George, I never realized how I would be
changed as I took my last steps with him.

George has an uncanny ability to make you feel at peace. He makes
everyone question his or her own faith, sometimes without even
speaking a word. He is faith. He puts his entire life into the
hands of God, and, through all the hardships, he still smiles. To
George, the goal isn't the destination but the journey and he
reminds us that we should cherish each step in our own journeys.

My job as a photographer is to neither condone nor indict but
rather bear witness. For those of you who may never meet George,
I hope the images from this pilgrimage bring you closer to him
and take you on your own journey.

About the photographer

Justin Merriman joined the Valley News Dispatch as a photographer
in 2000 after graduating from the University of Pittsburgh at
Greensburg with a bachelor's degree in English writing.

In his four years at the newspaper, Merriman won several local,
state, regional and national awards, and, in 2002, he was invited
to attend the prestigious Eddie Adams Workshop in New York City.

President of the News Photographers Association of Greater
Pittsburgh, Merriman recently joined the Valley News Dispatch's
sister paper, the Pittsburgh Tribune-Review.

He resides in Greensburg.

Justin Merriman can be reached at jmerriman@tribweb.com .
