'Amt' and 'Sendung' in von Balthasar's theology

### 1. "Amt": What von Balthasar means

- For von Balthasar, `Amt` means ecclesial office:
    - bishop, priest, pope, superior — any role defined by
      juridical authority, sacramental power, and institutional
      continuity.

- `Amt` has three essential features.
    - It is objective.
        - Office does not depend on the holiness, insight, or
          intelligence of the person who holds it. A bishop
          teaches with authority not because he is personally
          gifted, but because the Church has entrusted him with
          a function.
    - It is stable and public.
        - Office is visible, transmissible, and regulated by law.
          It protects the Church from chaos by ensuring
          continuity across time, personalities, and cultures.
    - It is protective.
        - Amt exists to safeguard the deposit of faith. It limits
          innovation, tests new impulses, and slows things down.
          From this perspective, caution and even resistance are
          virtues, not vices.

- Von Balthasar strongly rejected any romantic opposition between
  office and “spirit.” Without `Amt`, the Church dissolves into
  competing inspirations.

### 2) "Sendung": What he means

- Sendung means mission in the strict sense: a personal vocation
  given by God to a concrete individual, often before any office
  is assigned.

- Its features are almost the mirror image of Amt.
    - It is personal and interior.
        - A mission is not primarily recognized by law but
          discerned in `conscience`. It often begins invisibly
          and without authorization.
    - It is concrete and unrepeatable.
        - Two people can hold the same office, but no two
          missions are identical. Mission is tied to a person’s
          history, wounds, gifts, and limits.
    - It is risky.
        - Because it precedes institutional recognition, Sendung
          always appears suspect. It can be mistaken,
          exaggerated, or even pathological. But without such
          risks, the Church would never renew itself.

- For von Balthasar, the Church lives not only from sacramental
  office but from missions that are initially fragile and
  exposed.

### 3) Why tension between them is inevitable

- Von Balthasar argued that tension between office and mission is
  not an accident caused by sin alone. It belongs to the
  structure of the Church in history.

- Why? Because:
    - `Amt` looks backward, guarding what has been handed down
    - `Sendung` looks forward, responding to what God is doing
      now

- Office must ask: is this safe? is this faithful?
- Mission must ask: is this obedient? is this necessary?

- Neither question can eliminate the other.

- Importantly, von Balthasar denied there is a simple rule for
  resolving conflicts. Sometimes office is right to suppress
  a mission. Sometimes mission must endure rejection without
  immediate justification. There is no algorithm, only
  discernment lived through time.

### 4) The Marian and Petrine principles

- Here von Balthasar introduces a deeper symbolic layer.

- He associates `Amt` primarily with the Petrine principle:
    - Peter’s authority, juridical leadership, visible unity.

- `Sendung`, however, ultimately roots in the Marian principle:
    - Mary’s prior, receptive, non-official “yes” to God, without
      which no office could exist.

- This does not mean Mary “outranks” Peter in authority. It means
  that mission precedes office ontologically. Before there is
  structure, there is response. Before there is law, there is
  obedience of faith.

- This was a controversial claim, but it allowed von Balthasar to
  say something subtle: the Church is most herself not when she
  governs, but when she receives.

### 5) How his own life fits the theory

- Von Balthasar saw his departure from the Jesuits as a case
  where `Sendung` and `Amt` could not be reconciled without loss.

- He did not claim that his mission gave him authority over the
  Church. Instead, he believed that his task was to carry the
  mission even when office could not confirm it.

- That is the key point: `Sendung` does not overthrow `Amt`. It
  accepts being crucified by it, if necessary, while trusting
  that fruitfulness does not depend on immediate recognition.

- This is why he never framed his story as injustice corrected by
  later vindication. The suffering was not an error in the
  system; it was part of how mission and office coexist in
  a fallen but graced Church.

### In one sentence

- For von Balthasar, Amt gives the Church stability and truth,
  Sendung gives her life and freshness — and their unresolved
  tension is not a problem to be solved, but a cross to be
  carried.
