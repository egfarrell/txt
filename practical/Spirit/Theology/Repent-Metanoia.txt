# “Repent” in Mark 1:15 — Greek, Hebrew, and English Layers

### Mark 1:15
    - “The time has come,” he said. “The kingdom of God has come
      near. Repent and believe the good news!”

## Greek (New Testament)
- *metanoeō* (verb), *metanoia* (noun)
- Literal: “change one’s mind”
  - *meta* = after/beyond
  - *noeō* = to think, perceive
- Sense:
  - inner transformation
  - shift in perception and understanding
  - reorientation of life

## Hebrew (Old Testament)
- Primary word: *shuv* (שׁוּב)
  - meaning: “turn,” “return,” “go back”
  - emphasis: action, direction, relationship (returning to God)

- Secondary word: *nacham* (נָחַם)
  - meaning: “to feel regret,” “to be sorry,” “to be moved”
  - emphasis: emotion (less central to repentance)

## Combined Biblical Sense
- Not just feeling sorry
- Not just thinking differently
- But:
  - change your mind (Greek)
  - turn your life around (Hebrew)
  - return to God (relational)

## Early English (“Repent”)
- In Early Modern English (e.g. 1500s–1600s):
  - “repent” included:
    - regret
    - change of mind
    - turning away from sin
    - amendment of life
- Much broader than modern usage

## Historical Translation Path
- Greek: *metanoeō*
- Latin (Vulgate): *paenitentiam agere* (“do penance”)
- English (Tyndale, KJV): “repent”
  - chosen to avoid narrow “penance” meaning
  - intended to capture inner + outer change

## Modern Shift in Meaning
- Today “repent” ≈ feel sorry / guilty
- Lost much of:
  - transformation
  - reorientation
  - return to God

## Expanded Sense (Paraphrase)
- “Change your mind, turn your life around, and return to God”
