# PBS: From Jesus to Christ: The Frist Christians

### Notes on Part 2

### Gospels
    - Quelle "Q" presents Jeshua as a more apocalyptic figure,
      BUT "Q" was probably written before the First Jewish-Roman
      War AD 70
    - Mark presents Jeshua as a more anguished figure, dying
      mocked and in agony... like some of his community were in
      the AD 70s
    - Some people thought the says "Q" were enough about the
      meaning of Jeshua, but others thought his life, death and
      ressurection were the most important. You get both strands
      put together in Matthew and Luke
        - $MY_IMAGES/misc/1st-century-christian-communities.jpg

### - MATTHEW: 
        - most Jewish of all gospels
        - Jeshua goes up the mountain like Moses
        - Jeshua delivers 5 different sermons like the 5 books of
          the Torah
        - IN Matthew Jeshua is a proponent of Torah piety, just
          like the Pharisees
        - Pharisees were actually a marginal group when Jeshua
          was alive... but after the War in Matthews community
          they had risen to prominence
        - Matthew alters Marks ending... Jeshua meets the
          Apostles on a mountain in Galilee and they're told to
          go out and preach to the world
    - Gospels are "religious advertisements"

### - LUKE
        - Luke was a gentile, travelling companion of Paul
        - Also wrote the "Acts"... really Luke is a 2-volume work
        - High literary culture... like an ancient "romance"
          / adventure
        - Jeshua is a very powerful figure in Luke
        - Also Jeshua is a "scholar", finds his way around
          a synagoge scroll... just like Luke...
        - Chritians can be good Roman citizens... (LUke is more
          antagonistic towards the Jewish community)
        - When the gospel gets to Rome, that's the end of the
          story in Luke

### - JOHN
        - Relationship between Christians and Jews has become
          more virulent
        - Jeshua is a more 'serene' figure here...
        - The 'Spiritual Gospel'
        - Different approaches: compare the agony in the Garden:
          Mark v John 
            - aside: in Mark no garden, in John no agony
            - Mark writing for a persecuted community: Jeshua the
              night before he dies rolls on the ground, feels
              abandoned by G-d
            - In John, Jeshua is not on the ground. The Romon
              cohort come out, and they end up with their faces
              on the ground in John
            - Crossan: you've a Jeshua almost 'out of control' in
              Mark, totally in control in John
        - Crossan: neither historical, I think neither of them
          know exactly what happened
