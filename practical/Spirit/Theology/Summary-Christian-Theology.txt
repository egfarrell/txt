- Christian theology is traditionally divided into several key
  subsections or sub-disciplines, each focusing on a specific
  aspect of Christian belief and doctrine.
- These subdivisions help systematically explore different areas
  of theological study. Main divisions:

### 1. Systematic Theology
   - Systematic theology seeks to organize and synthesize the
     entire body of Christian doctrine into a coherent system,
     often addressing the Bible's teachings on various topics in
     a structured way.
   - Examples: Christology, soteriology, ecclesiology, etc.

### 2. Biblical Theology
   - This area focuses on understanding the Bible within its
     historical and literary context. It studies how specific
     themes, doctrines, and ideas develop throughout the Bible.
   - Key Themes: The Kingdom of God, covenant theology, etc.

### 3. Historical Theology
   - Historical theology investigates how Christian doctrines and
     practices have developed over time. It explores the history
     of the church, theological movements, creeds, and councils.
   - Key Areas: Early church history, Reformation theology,
     medieval theology, etc.

### 4. Practical Theology
   - Practical theology focuses on the application of theological
     principles to everyday Christian life and ministry. It deals
     with the lived experience of faith.
   - Key Areas: Pastoral theology, liturgics, Christian
     education, ethics, and homiletics (preaching).

### 5. Philosophical Theology
   - Philosophical theology involves the rational investigation
     of theological concepts, often using philosophical methods.
     It engages with questions about the nature of God,
     existence, and the relationship between faith and reason.
   - Key Areas: The existence of God, the problem of evil, free
     will, etc.

### Key Subsections within Systematic Theology:

1. Theology Proper (or "The Doctrine of God")
   - Focuses on the nature and attributes of God.
   - Key Topics: The Trinity, God's omnipotence, omniscience,
     omnipresence, and sovereignty.

2. Christology
   - The study of the person and work of Jesus Christ.
   - Key Topics: The divinity and humanity of Christ, the
     Incarnation, the Atonement, Resurrection.

3. Pneumatology
   - The study of the Holy Spirit.
   - Key Topics: The nature of the Holy Spirit, the Spirit’s role
     in salvation, gifts of the Spirit.

4. Soteriology
   - The study of `salvation` and how it is applied to humanity.
   - Key Topics: Grace, justification, sanctification, atonement,
     election, predestination.

5. Eschatology
   - The study of the "last things" or the end times.
   - Key Topics: The Second Coming of Christ, the resurrection of
     the dead, heaven, hell, the final judgment, the Kingdom of
     God.

6. Ecclesiology
   - The study of the church.
   - Key Topics: The nature and mission of the church, church
     government, sacraments, the relationship between church and
     state.

7. Anthropology (Theological Anthropology)
   - The study of human beings from a theological perspective.
   - Key Topics: The nature of humanity, the image of God,
     original sin, free will.

8. Hamartiology
   - The study of sin.
   - Key Topics: The origin of sin, the fall of man, the nature
     and effects of sin.

9. Bibliology
   - The study of the nature and authority of the Bible.
   - Key Topics: Inspiration, inerrancy, canonicity,
     interpretation of Scripture.

10. Theodicy
    - The problem of evil and suffering in relation to a good and
      all-powerful God.
    - Key Topics: Why evil exists, the nature of suffering,
      divine justice.

### Other Subsections or Themes

- Creation Theology: 
    - God as Creator and the theological implications of the
      created order.
- Covenant Theology: 
    - A framework that understands God's dealings with humanity
      as unfolding through covenants.
- Sacramental Theology: 
    - The study of sacraments (e.g., Baptism, Eucharist) and
      their significance.
- Missiology: 
    - The study of Christian mission and evangelism.
- Christian Ethics: 
    - The application of Christian moral principles in personal
      and societal contexts.
