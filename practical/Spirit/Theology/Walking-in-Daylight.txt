
### Setup
    - The Raising of Lazarus 
    - 11 A man named Lazarus was sick. He lived in Bethany with
      his sisters, Mary and Martha. 
    - 2 This is the Mary who later poured the expensive perfume
      on the Lord’s feet and wiped them with her hair.[a] Her
      brother, Lazarus, was sick. 
    - 3 So the two sisters sent a message to Jesus telling him,
      “Lord, your dear friend is very sick.”
    - 4 But when Jesus heard about it he said, “Lazarus’s
      sickness will not end in death. No, it happened for the
      glory of God so that the Son of God will receive glory from
      this.” 
    - 5 So although Jesus loved Martha, Mary, and Lazarus,
    - 6 he stayed where he was for the next two days. 
    - 7 Finally, he said to his disciples, “Let’s go back to
      Judea.” 
    - 8 But his disciples objected. “Rabbi,” they said, “only
      a few days ago the people[b] in Judea were trying to stone
      you. Are you going there again?” 
    - 9 Jesus replied, “There are twelve hours of daylight every
      day. During the day people can walk safely. They can see
      because they have the light of this world. 
    - 10 But at night there is danger of stumbling because they
      have no light.” 
    - 11 Then he said, “Our friend Lazarus has fallen asleep, but
      now I will go and wake him up.”

### Summary
- In short Jesus is saying:
    - My life is governed by God’s timing, not human threats
    - Acting within God’s purpose is “daylight,” even when it
      looks dangerous
    - What follows (death → resurrection) will occur in the
      light, not in chaos


### Discussion
It is cryptic at first glance—but in context it’s doing several things at once. John’s Gospel often layers a practical meaning with a symbolic one.

First, the immediate, practical level
In Gospel of John 11, the disciples are afraid to return to Judea because Jesus was nearly killed there. Jesus answers their fear indirectly.

- “Day” here means the time appointed by God. In the ancient Jewish world, “twelve hours of daylight” was a way of saying: a fixed, allotted period. Jesus is saying, in effect:

- As long as my appointed time (“day”) has not ended, nothing can happen to me. One does not stumble when walking during the time given for work.

- So on this level, it reassures them: It is still safe for me to go; my hour has not yet come.

Second, the symbolic / theological level
John consistently uses light and darkness symbolically.

Light → God’s will, truth, divine guidance, life
Darkness → danger, confusion, opposition, death

So “walking in the day” means acting in alignment with God’s purpose. When someone walks in that light, their path is secure—not because nothing bad can happen, but because their life is ordered by God’s intention.

“Walking at night” is acting outside that light—self-directed, fearful, or opposed to God—where stumbling is inevitable.

This connects tightly with earlier themes in John:
– “The light shines in the darkness” (John 1)
– “I am the light of the world” (John 8)

Third, how it prepares the Lazarus sign
Right after this saying, Jesus speaks of Lazarus being “asleep.” Death itself is being reframed. If Jesus is the light, then even death is not ultimate darkness anymore—it is something navigable, reversible, within God’s “day.”

So the saying quietly signals:
This journey toward danger, death, and the tomb is still taking place in the light. Even what looks like night is under God’s control.

In short
Jesus is saying:
– My life is governed by God’s timing, not human threats
– Acting within God’s purpose is “daylight,” even when it looks dangerous
– What follows (death → resurrection) will occur in the light, not in chaos
