
# Original
- BENEDICT LABRE (1748-1783)
In God’s presence, he is very straightforward and simple,
somewhat de plein pied [self —assured]. He has a sort of
overflowing prayer and a longing for the vision of God, which
eliminates every obstacle in prayer. He prays in such
a matter-of-fact and straightforward manner, just as a healthy
person sleeps or eats. He only has trouble with his fellowmen,
for he is not able to summon the simplicity he has in relation to
God when he is with them. He cannot approach them the way he
approaches God. And yet at the same time he feels approached by
them as if by God himself. To stand before God is no problem for
him, any more than it is that God gives preference to him in
spite of his unworthiness. Or any more than it is that God wants
him to be with other people. But it costs him a great deal of
self-overcoming in order somehow to forget how unworthy he is of
the other people. He is so thoroughly penetrated by the grace of
God that his unworthiness in Holy Communion does not worry him
any more. He knows it has been overcome in God. But in his
interaction with other people it is not the same. Nevertheless,
he would like to bring them all to God; and if he travels to so
many holy sites with them, then it is because he would like to
give each person to God: and since he is not able to give the
person he possesses to God very well, since he is not sure how to
manage it, he tries to bring them closer to God in an acceptable
way: to transpose them into a state of more elevated joy of
acceptance through pilgrimage. It is as if he lifted people up
and lowered God—not in an objectionable sense, but in the sense
of rapprochement, as he imagined it and as it flowed out
immediately from his standing in God’s presence.



# BENEDICT LABRE (1748–1783)
    - (Book of All Saints)

- In God’s presence he is very simple and direct, in a way de
  plein pied [self-assured]. His prayer overflows, and his
  longing for the vision of God removes every obstacle to prayer.
  He prays in a plain, matter-of-fact way, as naturally as
  a healthy person sleeps or eats.

- He has difficulty only with other people. With them he cannot
  summon the same simplicity he has before God. He cannot
  approach them as he approaches God. And yet, at the same time,
  he feels approached by them as though by God himself.

- Standing before God causes him no difficulty — nor does the
  fact that God gives him preference in spite of his
  unworthiness, or that God wants him to be with other people.
  But it costs him great self-overcoming to forget, even for
  a moment, how unworthy he feels in relation to others.

- He is so deeply penetrated by God’s grace that his unworthiness
  in Holy Communion no longer troubles him. He knows that it has
  been overcome in God. But this is not the case in his dealings
  with other people.

- Still, he wants to bring everyone to God. And if he travels
  with them to so many holy places, it is because he wants to
  give each person to God. Since he does not know how to give the
  person entrusted to him directly to God, and is unsure how to
  manage this, he tries instead to bring them closer to God in
  a way he can accept: by leading them, through pilgrimage, into
  a higher joy of acceptance.

- It is as though he raises people up and lowers God — not in
  a mistaken sense, but in a movement of drawing closer together
  — as he understands it, and as it flows immediately from the
  way he stands in God’s presence.


# Analysis


- “In God’s presence he is very simple and direct, in a way de
  plein pied.”
    - She is naming his basic spiritual stance. This is not about
      feeling confident or bold, but about standing before God
      without inner hesitation, preparation, or retreat.


- “His prayer overflows, and his longing for the vision of God
  removes every obstacle to prayer.”
    - His desire for God is so strong and uncomplicated that
      prayer does not meet resistance. Nothing inside him
      interrupts or blocks it.


- “He prays … as naturally as a healthy person sleeps or eats.”
    - Prayer is not an activity he initiates or manages. It
      happens with the same lack of self-conscious effort as
      bodily functions in a healthy person.

- “He has difficulty only with other people.”
    - The contrast begins here. What is effortless with God
      becomes difficult with human beings.


- “He cannot approach them as he approaches God.”
    - His mode of presence before God does not translate into
      social or interpersonal situations. The same simplicity
      cannot be carried over.

- “And yet … he feels approached by them as though by God
  himself.”
    - Even though he cannot approach others rightly, he
      experiences their approach to him as something given,
      almost sacramental, rather than chosen.

- “Standing before God causes him no difficulty — nor does the
  fact that God gives him preference in spite of his
  unworthiness.”
    - - His sense of unworthiness does not disturb his relation
      to God. He does not argue with God’s freedom to give grace.

- “But it costs him great self-overcoming to forget … how
  unworthy he feels in relation to others.”
    - With people, unworthiness becomes a psychological and moral
      obstacle. He cannot simply accept being received by them.

- “He knows that it has been overcome in God.”
    - In the divine sphere, the problem of unworthiness is
      already resolved and therefore no longer active for him.

- “But this is not the case in his dealings with other people.”
    - The resolution that exists before God does not
      automatically carry over into human relationships.

- “Still, he wants to bring everyone to God.”
    - Despite the difficulty, his basic orientation toward others
      remains positive and outward.

- “Since he does not know how to give the person entrusted to him
  directly to God …”
    - He lacks an interior or relational method for offering
      another person to God as a person.

- “… he tries instead to bring them closer to God … through
  pilgrimage.”
    - Pilgrimage becomes a concrete substitute for a spiritual
      act he cannot perform directly.

- “It is as though he raises people up and lowers God … in
  a movement of drawing closer together.”
    - This is an image, not a theory. She is describing his
      intuitive way of reducing distance, not redefining God or
      humanity.


- “… as it flows immediately from the way he stands in God’s
  presence.”
    - The entire pattern of his behavior with others is
      a secondary consequence of his original, simple stance
      before God.
