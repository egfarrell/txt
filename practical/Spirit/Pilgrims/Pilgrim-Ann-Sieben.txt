### Awakin org article:
    - https://www.awakin.org/v2/calls/241/ann-sieben/bio
    - https://www.awakin.org/v2/calls/241/ann-sieben/recording?check=1
    - From Nuclear Engineer to Adventure Pilgrim to Spiritual
      Pilgrim to Servant Pilgrim
    - Ann Sieben, the "Winter Pilgrim," has been a life-dedicated
      mendicant pilgrim since 2007.  A mendicant pilgrim is one
      who travels on foot along ancient paths to significant
      destinations. She carries nothing of material value; no
      money, cell phone, camera, GPS unit, nothing anyone would
      be tempted to steal. Her pilgrim days are divided in two
      parts.  One part is communing with nature, sensitive to the
      world surrounding her, as she walks village to village. The
      other part is communing with humanity; engaging with people
      and their communities to take care of her basic needs, like
      food and shelter. In these daily acts she is actively
      building trust in the world which is the foundation of
      peace.
    - A recent news article sums up, "The 52-year-old gave up
      a career in nuclear engineering to take long journeys on
      foot to holy destinations. In the 9 years that she's been
      a pilgrim, she has walked in 44 countries."
    - Ann began her journey as a touristic pilgrim with a taste
      for adventure.  Slowly, she evolved into a spiritual
      pilgrim when she began to rely on humanity more and more.
      “Over the pilgrimages,” she says, “I went from the
      spiritual side, the Catholic side, not being very
      important, to now where the Catholic side is very
      important. The adventure part, it's there and it's fun, it
      is adventurous to cross through a river and see
      a crocodile, but the meaning of the pilgrimages has become
      Catholic.”  "As a Catholic person I have faith in God, of
      course," she says, "but to have faith in humanity — it was
      a beautiful transition."
    - Ultimately, she progressed further, becoming a servant
      pilgrim -- one who serves humanity not only by loving her
      neighbor as herself, but also by actively loving her
      enemies as well, and thereby building trust and
      establishing peace.  As she beautifully recounts, "I have
      been with narco-traffickers in the Mexican desert;
      religious extremists in North Africa and elsewhere;
      Marxists in high country of Peru; wife beaters and family
      abusers in many different countries. They’ve all hosted me
      for an evening.  For those nights, those moments, those
      evenings, there was peace."
    - Amid connecting and building trust with faces of humanity
      in all languages, skin tones, and cultures, Ann's
      pilgrimages cultivate not only a faith in the human spirit,
      but also, an inner resilience and humility. Part of this
      search manifests in her not carrying money. She reflects,
      “To go with no money is just a whole different experience,
      to build trust, the trust when somebody invites you into
      their home. That trust is the foundation of peace. So
      that's very important for me. To fall back into the arms of
      humanity and know that somebody will catch you and no harm
      will come to you. That's what I do as a pilgrim.”
    - In her most recent pilgrimage, she left Denver, Colorado
      (her base when she's in the U.S. and not on a pilgrimage)
      in November to trek more than 2,000 miles, from Colorado to
      the Shrine of Sainte-Anne-de-Beaupré in Quebec, Canada by
      Easter this past March. With little more than the clothes
      on her back, including her bright green jacket for
      visibility, she walks with a handful of items in a small
      pack, and has received the countless hospitality of
      strangers-turned-family.
    - “People are good,” she reminds us. “It's universal, simple,
      fundamental."


### National Catholic article
    - Ann Sieben, former nuclear engineer from Denver, left her
      career 18 years ago to live as a full-time pilgrim.
    - She’s walked 55,000 miles through 56 countries, spreading
      faith through personal encounters.
    - Her current mission: visit all 305 French towns named for
      St. Martin of Tours; 53 remain before Nov. 11, St. Martin’s
      feast day.
    - First walked from Canterbury to Rome in 2007; realized
      pilgrimage was her life’s calling.
    - Devoted to St. Martin, the 4th-century
      soldier-turned-bishop known for sharing his cloak with
      a beggar.
    - Project planned during COVID: mapped all St. Martin towns
      into five loops from Tours, France.
    - Walks ~22 miles daily, relying on hospitality; welcomed by
      locals, mayors, and parishes.
    - Sees pilgrimage as trust-building and peace-making:
      “Pilgrims have no ulterior motive.”
    - Views uncertainty as essential to faith: “Every day is
      a new day in Pilgrim Land.”
    - Formerly a nuclear engineer, her fifth pilgrimage (Spain to
      Jerusalem during Arab Spring) confirmed her vocation.
    - Aims to “re-humanize the world” through encounters beyond
      digital isolation.
    - Promotes the lesser-known Via Sancti Martini over the
      crowded Way of St. James.
    - Next journey (after 2025): follow St. Martin’s influence in
      Scotland and northern England.
    - Hopes to reach 100,000 km by 2028 but values spirit over
      statistics: “The world needs more pilgrims.”
