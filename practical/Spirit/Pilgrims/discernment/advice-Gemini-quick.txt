### The Influence of Benedict Joseph Labre and George Walter
    - Your admiration for Benedict Joseph Labre is particularly
      relevant. He was an 18th-century French Franciscan tertiary
      who, after being unable to join monastic life, chose a life
      of poverty and pilgrimage, traveling to major shrines
      across Europe and subsisting by begging. He was known for
      his extreme asceticism, devotion to prayer, and generosity
      to others despite his own poverty. He embraced the world as
      his cloister, living as a "poor pilgrim serving God."

    - Similarly, George Walter, often called "Pilgrim George,"
      walked over 40,000 miles across many countries on Christian
      pilgrimages, living off the kindness of strangers and
      spreading a message of peace and love. He embraced a life
      without worldly possessions, carrying a staff with
      a crucifix and trusting God for his needs. He also penned
      two books about his experiences. These figures exemplify
      a radical trust in divine providence and a commitment to
      their unique calling as wandering pilgrims.
