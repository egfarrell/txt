
### Discernment tension

  * You feel a repeated, persistent inner pull toward a long
    pilgrimage.
  * External spiritual authorities are cautious — possibly due to
    concerns about safety, finances, or social norms.
  * This creates a classic tension: interior conviction vs.
    exterior prudence.

### Signs of authenticity (drawn from spiritual tradition)

  * Inspiration is consistent over years, not fleeting.
  * It brings peace, awe, and prayer rather than agitation or
    vanity.
  * It doesn’t contradict Scripture or faith.
  * There is willingness to embrace humility and hardship.

### Cautions to weigh

  * Your funds are far below your previous comfort level — risk
    of hardship is much higher.
  * Your previous 7-month pilgrimage was long, but this is more
    than double that duration.
  * Lack of endorsement from guides may indicate genuine pastoral
    concern rather than disbelief in your call.

### Possible interpretations

  * The call is real but the *timing or method* may need
    adjusting (e.g., staged pilgrimages).
  * God may be calling you to radical trust, but that does not
    automatically mean reckless abandonment of prudence.
  * The resistance you face may be part of the “testing” of the
    call — requiring patience before acting.

### Practical discernment steps

  * Test the call with a shorter “trial” pilgrimage under the
    same financial constraints you’d face for 1.5 years.
  * Seek one experienced pilgrim or priest who has walked similar
    paths for candid, practical counsel.
  * In prayer, specifically ask not “Should I go?” but “If this
    is Your will, make the way unmistakably clear and provide the
    means.”

- It’s possible the lack of encouragement is not meant to
  *cancel* your call, but to ensure it’s purified of
  impulsiveness so that, if you go, you go in both faith and
  wisdom.
