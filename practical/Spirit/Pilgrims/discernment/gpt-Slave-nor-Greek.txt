Prayer-Meditation: "Clothed in Christ" (from Galatians 3)

- Begin with stillness. Sit or walk slowly. Breathe in... and out.

- Opening
    - God of promise,
    - God of journey,
    - God who called Abraham without a map,
    - and clothed us in Christ without conditions—
    - I turn to you now.
    - Not to earn, not to prove,
    - but to listen.

1. Receiving, not achieving

    - You ask me not what I have done,
    - but whether I will trust you.

    - You are not keeping score.
    - You are opening a path.

    - Pause. Where in my life am I still trying to earn love or justify myself?
    - What if I laid that down?

2. Living from the promise

    - Before I knew law,
    - before I had plans,
    - you whispered promise:
    - "You are mine."

    - Even now, that promise still holds.

    - Pause. Can I recall a moment—perhaps obscure, perhaps bright—where I sensed God’s call,
    - not as a task, but as love drawing me forward?
    - Rest in that moment.

3. Inheriting freedom

    - I am no longer a servant chasing instructions.
    - I am your child, _walking with you_.
    - I wear Christ like a robe, like skin.

    - Even when I shift from monastery to street,
    - from spreadsheet to sandals—
    - still, I am clothed in Christ.

    - Pause. How might God be calling me to live as fully myself
      in all these places; not fragmented, but whole?

4. A prayer of surrender

    - God, I may not know the next step.
    - I may feel the tension of many paths.

    - But I choose to trust your promise,
    - not my perfection.

    - I choose to follow—not with fear,
    - but with faith.

    - Clothe me again in Christ.
    - Clothe me in clarity, if you will—
    - but also in courage when clarity does not come.

    - Amen.

### GPT Advice
    - Let me offer a reflective reading of Galatians 3 with
      special attention to how it might "speak to" vocational
      searching.

### 1. Faith as the Foundation (Galatians 3:1–9)
    - Paul opens by confronting the Galatians for turning back to
      a life of rule-following—"Did you receive the Spirit by
      doing the works of the law or by believing what you heard?"
      (v. 2). He then points to Abraham as the example: it was
      faith that made him righteous, not what he did.
    - For a vocation-seeker, this can be liberating. The deepest
      calling is not necessarily the most impressive work or the
      most ascetic life. Rather, vocation begins in trusting
      God—leaning into a relationship where your identity and
      direction are received rather than achieved. You've
      explored paths of service and pilgrimage, but your truest
      compass is not the worthiness of those actions; it is God's
      ongoing invitation spoken into your life.

### 2. The Trap of the Law (Galatians 3:10–14)
    - Paul warns that relying on "works of the law" brings
      a curse. Why? Because no one can fulfill them perfectly.
      The law is not bad, but it's not what saves. Christ "became
      a curse for us" (v. 13) to free us for the life of the
      Spirit.
    - In terms of vocation, this might speak to the temptation of
      self-justification. There’s a kind of inner law many
      seekers feel—"Am I doing enough? Am I choosing the right
      path? Should I be suffering more, or achieving more?" This
      passage says: you are not defined by what you manage to do
      or prove. You're already loved. Vocation, then, becomes
      a response of freedom, not pressure.

### 3. The Promise Precedes the Law (Galatians 3:15–22)
    - Paul argues that the promise made to Abraham came before
      the law. The law was a kind of temporary guardian. But the
      promise—the relational covenant—was always primary.
    - If you've walked long pilgrimages or spent time in monastic
      stillness, you may have felt moments where the path didn't
      come from logic or duty but from something deeper—promise.
      Vocation is rooted in this promise: that God desires
      communion with you and calls you to participate in life not
      because of obligation, but as a beloved child.

### 4. From Slavery to Sonship (Galatians 3:23–29)
    - Paul concludes that, before faith, "we were imprisoned and
      guarded under the law," but now that faith has come, "you
      are all children of God through faith." There is no Jew or
      Greek, slave or free—only those clothed in Christ.
    - For you, who have moved between vastly different worlds
      (monastery, street, office), this is a powerful
      affirmation. Your identity does not depend on whether
      you're doing care work or solving algorithms. Your vocation
      is to live as a child of God, wherever that takes you.
    - And more than that—your past is not fragmented. It's
      unified in Christ. Every stage of your journey has been
      part of your clothing in Christ, and your call now may not
      be a choice between those paths, but a synthesis of them.

### A Summary Reflection

- Galatians 3 teaches us:
    - Vocation is rooted in faith, not in performance.
    - The journey is not about becoming worthy, but about
      trusting.
    - All that is asked of us is to receive the Spirit, walk in
      the promise, and live as children of God.
    - Your varied experiences—pilgrimage, care, contemplation,
      intellect—are not contradictions but facets of your
      God-given identity.

- You might ask yourself:
    - Where do I feel most free in the Spirit?
    - What work (or rhythm) invites me to rely on God’s promise
      more than on my own strength?
    - What part of my past do I feel I need to "justify"—and can
      I instead allow God to integrate it?
