### The Enduring Tradition of Pilgrimage

- Your desire for a long, prayerful journey to the Marian shrines
  of Europe resonates with a rich history of Christian
  pilgrimage. For centuries, individuals have embarked on such
  journeys, seeking a deeper connection with God, offering
  penance, or simply responding to an inner call. These
  pilgrimages are not merely physical treks; they are profound
  spiritual exercises. The silent prayer, praise for creation,
  and sense of peace you describe are hallmarks of this ancient
  practice.

- Your admiration for St. Benedict Joseph Labre and George Walter
  places you in a specific stream of this tradition: the
  mendicant pilgrim, one who travels with radical dependence on
  God's providence.

- St. Benedict Joseph Labre (1748-1783), after being rejected by
  several monastic orders, embraced a life of perpetual
  pilgrimage. He journeyed to the major shrines of Europe with
  only a rosary, a crucifix, and a few spiritual books, living
  off alms and sharing any surplus with the poor. He was often
  misunderstood and seen as a fool, yet his profound holiness was
  recognized by the people of Rome, who proclaimed him a saint
  immediately after his death. His life testifies to the idea
  that a profound love for God can be lived out in a way that
  diverges from conventional religious life.

- George Walter (1941-2023), a modern-day pilgrim, walked over
  40,000 miles across the globe to "lift up Jesus." A former
  seminarian, he felt a call to a different path and embarked on
  his first pilgrimage from Barcelona to Jerusalem with little
  money. He wrote two books about his experiences, inspiring
  others with his life of faith and dependence on providence.

- These figures demonstrate that your calling, while unusual in
  this day and age, is not without precedent. It is a path that
  has been walked by saints and devout individuals throughout
  Christian history.

### The Role of Spiritual Guidance

- The caution from your professional religious guides is also
  understandable and should be received with respect and careful
  consideration. Their role is to help you discern God's will and
  to protect you from potential harm, both spiritual and
  physical. Long-distance pilgrimages, especially with limited
  funds, do carry inherent risks, including physical injury,
  illness, and vulnerability.

- However, it is also important to understand the nature of
  spiritual direction. A spiritual director's role is not to make
  decisions for you, but to help you listen to God's voice in
  your life, to challenge you, and to offer an objective
  perspective. Ultimately, the decision of what path to follow
  rests with you and your conscience, in your direct relationship
  with God.

- It's possible that your guides' caution stems from a framework
  that prioritizes stability and community life, which is the
  norm for most religious vocations. Your calling seems to align
  more with the less common but equally valid tradition of the
  "Fool-for-Christ" or the mendicant, a path they may be less
  familiar with.

### Navigating Your Discernment

- So, what can you make of all this? Here are a few points for
  your continued prayer and reflection:

- Continue in Prayer: The most crucial element of your
  discernment is your ongoing conversation with God. Continue to
  bring this desire to Him in prayer, asking for clarity, wisdom,
  and the courage to follow His will. Time in front of the
  Blessed Sacrament, as was central to St. Benedict Joseph
  Labre's life, could be particularly fruitful.
- Reflect on Your Motivations: Your description of what
  pilgrimage means to you—silent prayer, praise, awe, and
  peace—is beautiful and spiritually sound. It does not seem to
  be a journey born of delusion or false inspiration. It appears
  to be a genuine desire for a deeper union with God.
- Acknowledge the Practical Realities: Your previous 7-month
  pilgrimage with €8,000 in savings provided a degree of comfort.
  A 1.5-year journey with €3,000 will be a different experience,
  demanding a greater level of trust and dependence on God's
  providence, much like St. Benedict Joseph Labre and George
  Walter. Your ability to play the flute and your strength to
  work are gifts that can be part of this providence.
- Seek Further Counsel (Wisely): You might consider seeking out
  a spiritual director who has experience with or a deeper
  understanding of the eremitical or pilgrim life. Perhaps a wise
  monk in one of the monasteries you've visited could offer
  a different perspective.
- Trust in Your Experience: You are not a novice pilgrim. You
  have walked thousands of miles and have spent significant time
  in monasteries. You know the rigors and the graces of this way
  of life. Trust the spiritual senses that you have honed over
  years of prayer and pilgrimage.

The fact that this call has persisted for four years is
