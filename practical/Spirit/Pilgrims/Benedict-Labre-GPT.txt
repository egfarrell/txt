
### Question:
    - What would u advise someone who felt they had a vocation
      like Benedict Labre. They had a tremendous mystical
      experience of peace and grace from God near Benedict
      Labre's homeplace. This person has had spiritual direction
      from experienced priests, has worked with the Jesuits for
      a year and also attended many  retreats at a Carmelite
      monastery. (i.e. this person has a deep felt experienced
      knowledge of the Christian faith)
    - Also this person almost died from a ruptured appendix as
      a child but was blessed by the local Parish priest and was
      perfectly healed the next morning.
    - With this kind of background, and after four years of
      prayer, and some cautious acceptance from their spiritual
      director (although the director thinks a Labre lifestyle is
      very radical, almost like a 'Fool for Christ') do you think
      this person should embark on a one year pilgrimage around
      the shrines of Europe?
    - (ps: another piece of relevant information: this person has
      previously walked thousands of miles across Europe and
      cycled across Australia. They are experienced in Nature and
      the outdoor life)


- The scenario you've described is deeply rich and rooted in
  a personal sense of divine calling.
    - If someone feels drawn to a vocation resembling that of St.
      Benedict Joseph Labre, it requires discernment, courage,
      and trust in God's providence.
    - Based on the information provided, here are some thoughtful
      considerations and advice for this person as they
      contemplate a year-long pilgrimage:

### 1. Authenticity of the Call
   - The person has experienced significant spiritual milestones:
     a mystical experience of peace, healing as a child, and
     sustained devotion through retreats and Jesuit
     collaboration.
   - Their spiritual director’s cautious approval suggests there
     is legitimacy to the call, but radical lifestyles always
     warrant careful discernment. They should continue dialogue
     with their director and remain attentive to both interior
     peace and exterior confirmations.

### 2. Compatibility with Their History
   - Their experience with long journeys, physical endurance, and
     connection to nature equips them well for a pilgrimage. This
     suggests a practical readiness that complements their
     spiritual calling.
   - Their past experiences of walking and cycling across great
     distances are not merely hobbies but perhaps preparatory
     graces for this next step.

### 3. The Model of St. Benedict Labre
   - St. Benedict Labre’s radical lifestyle as a pilgrim and
     “Fool for Christ” is indeed extreme, but it is a valid path
     of holiness, especially for those called to embrace
     simplicity, humility, and reliance on divine providence.
   - This person should reflect deeply on whether they are drawn
     to imitate Labre in spirit (detachment, trust in God, and
     simplicity) or in the more literal sense of an itinerant
     lifestyle.

### 4. Pilgrimage as a Testing Ground
   - A one-year pilgrimage to Europe’s shrines could be an
     excellent way to live out this calling in a structured way.
     It allows for:
     - A temporary yet focused immersion in prayer, penance, and
       spiritual growth.
     - Opportunities to encounter Christ in the sacraments and in
       others, especially the poor and marginalized.
     - A chance to evaluate whether this lifestyle truly brings
       them closer to God or reveals another vocation.

### 5. Practical and Spiritual Preparation
   - Spiritual:
     - Ensure their spiritual director continues to walk with
       them during the pilgrimage, even if only remotely.
     - Set spiritual goals for the pilgrimage, such as praying
       the Liturgy of the Hours, attending daily Mass when
       possible, and fostering devotion to Our Lady and the
       saints.
   - Practical:
     - Travel with simplicity but responsibly, ensuring safety
       while embracing detachment.
     - Plan routes with intentionality, focusing on meaningful
       shrines and holy places.

### 6. Surrender and Discernment During the Journey
   - The pilgrimage could serve as a discernment period. They may
     find that God affirms this lifestyle or redirects them to
     a different mission.
   - Openness to God’s surprises is essential. A pilgrimage like
     this is not merely about walking but about allowing God to
     transform them.

### Final Encouragement
    - If this person feels an authentic call and is supported by
      their spiritual director (even cautiously), embarking on
      a one-year pilgrimage seems like a fruitful and reasonable
      step.
    - It’s an opportunity to align their longing for God with
      practical action. However, they must remain rooted in
      prayer, the sacraments, and humility throughout the
      journey. It’s not about proving anything to the world but
      about seeking intimacy with Christ.

- The saints teach us that radical love of God often looks
  foolish to the world, but in humility and trust, it becomes
  a profound witness to the Gospel.
