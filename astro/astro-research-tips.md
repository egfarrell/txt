
### Research Tips: (SARA talk)
    - Start out simple. Do something where you know the results
      and verify them.
    - Set realistic goals
    - Then refine and improve
    - Build on others work.
    - Don't try to be a master of all things. We're not all
      mathematicians, programmers, astrophysicists or microwave
      electrical engineers.
    - Continuous improvement is the ideal path to enlightenment.


### Astro Referees
    - Earlier Adrian Price-Whelan (Flatiron) called to discuss
      his latest referee response. Referee reports are extremely
      valuable; the paper will be much better for every change we
      make in response. We discussed our philosophies about
      responding. My approach is: 

        - Every referee comment, confusion, or question is a real
          problem with the paper. The huge value of a referee
          report or submitting to a refereed journal is that you
          have called in a close read of your paper by
          a professional astronomer.
        - That's amazingly valuable. Any place where that
          referee's close read led to a confusion or a comment or
          a question is a place where (by example!)
          a professional astronomer might have a comment or
          a question or a confusion! 
        - Even if you think the referee misunderstands, the
          misunderstanding is, at least at some level, because
          you didn't explain something well enough in the paper.
        - So I recommend making a change in the paper text in
          response to every referee comment. Every comment is
          a real issue with the paper.

### planning a research project:
    - what is the question you want to answer? Start off with
      a well defined question you want answered. (amazing how
      often this isnt the case)
    - what has been done in this area before?
    - would a negative result, or partial progress towards an
      answer be of interest? 
        - (if the answer isnt in doubt, its not real research)
    - At all stages of the project, keep a scientific question in
      mind. 
        - (the question may evolve, but you should always know
          what it is)
    - Be prepared to ask questions (of your supervisor). 
        - No such thing as a stupid question (well slightly
          simplified) Asking questions clarifies your thinking.
