Holiday Message 2020 | The Screwy Universe – Sean Carroll
CMB temperature (colors) and polarization (lines), as measured by the Planck satellite.

- Welcome to the third annual Mindscape Holiday Message! Just
  a chance for me to be a little more chatty and informal than
  usual, although as it turned out this isn’t all that different
  from a conventional solo episode. With the difference that what
  I’m talking about a phenomenon called “cosmic birefringence”
  which has played a big part in my personal scientific career,
  so I get to be a bit autobiographical.
- Every photon has a direction of polarization, which generally
  remains fixed as the photon travels through space.
- Birefringence is an effect by which the polarization rotates
  rather than staying fixed. It can happen in materials, but
  generally not in outer space. But there are exotic physics
  ideas that could cause such a rotation, including the dynamical
  dark energy candidate known as quintessence. People have put
  limits on such cosmic birefringence for a while now, but
  recently there was a claim that there might be a nonzero amount
  of birefringence visible in the polarization of the cosmic
  microwave background! Still very tentative, but if this hint
  turns into real evidence, it would big extremely big news for
  our understanding of physics and cosmology, possibly helping us
  pinpoint the nature of dark energy.

- Let me give some pointers to some of the papers and resources
  I talk about in the episode. Here is my first paper, with
  George Field and Roman Jackiw, where we talk about Lorentz
  invariance, Chern-Simons theory, and rotating polarizations,
  and a follow-up where we talk about pseudoscalar fields:
-     Carroll, Field, and Jackiw, 1990, Limits on a Lorentz and
      Parity Violating Modification of Electrodynamics (pdf).
-     Carroll and Field, 1991, The Einstein Equivalence Principle
      and the Polarization of Radio Galaxies (pdf).
- Here are a few papers we wrote about using this kind of model
  for early-universe magnetogenesis:
-     Garretson, Field, and Carroll, 1992, Primordial Magnetic
      Fields from Pseudo-Goldstone Bosons.
-     Field and Carroll, 1998, Cosmological Magnetic Fields from
      Primordial Helicity.
- Here is the paper by Borge Nodland and John Ralston claiming
  a preferred direction in space, and our response to it:
-     Nodland and Ralston, 1997, Indication of Anisotropy in
      Electromagnetic Propagation over Cosmological Distances.
-     Carroll and Field, 1997, Is there evidence for cosmic
      anisotropy in the polarization of distant radio sources?
- Here is my paper on quintessence, suggesting that it might also
  lead to birefringence:
-     Carroll, 1998, Quintessence and the Rest of the World.
- Here are early papers showing that birefringence can be
  constrained using the cosmic microwave background, and a recent
  survey of experimental prospects:
-     Lue, Wang, and Kamionkowski, 1998, Cosmological Signature
      of New Parity-Violating Interactions.
-     Lepora, 1998, Cosmological Birefringence and the Microwave
      Background.
-     Pegosian, Shimon, Mewes, and Keating, 2019, Future CMB
      constraints on cosmic birefringence and implications for
      fundamental physics.
- And here is the new paper by Minami and Komatsu, with the hint
  of a nonzero amount of birefringence in the CMB:
-     Minami and Komatsu, 2020, New Extraction of the Cosmic
      Birefringence from the Planck 2018 Polarization Data.
- Finally, there is a recent seminar on the Minami and Komatsu
  result, an interview I recently did with Brian Keating about
  this new result, and an old blog post talking about the
  Nodland/Ralston paper.
