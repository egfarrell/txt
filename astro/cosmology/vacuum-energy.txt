### 1. Normal energy vs. vacuum energy

* In GR, the source of gravity isn’t just mass, it’s the
  stress-energy tensor (energy, pressure, and momentum).
* For normal matter (stars, gas, dark matter), energy density is
  positive, and pressure is usually small or positive. This gives
  attractive gravity.
* For radiation (like photons), pressure is also positive, still
  giving an attractive effect overall.

But vacuum energy — the "energy of empty space" — is special.


### 2. Vacuum energy has negative pressure

The vacuum acts like a fluid with:

* energy density: `rho_vac > 0`
* pressure: `p_vac = -rho_vac`

That minus sign is the kicker. In Einstein’s field equations:

`G_mu_nu = 8*pi*G * T_mu_nu`

the stress-energy tensor of the vacuum looks like:

`T_mu_nu (vac) = -rho_vac * g_mu_nu`

This minus sign leads to repulsive gravity. Instead of pulling
things together, it accelerates the expansion of space.


### 3. The cosmological constant and repulsion

If you take the Friedmann equation for the expansion of the
universe:

`(a_dot/a)^2 = (8*pi*G/3) * rho - (k/a^2) + Lambda/3`

where:

* `a(t)` is the scale factor,
* `rho` is normal matter/radiation density,
* `Lambda` is the cosmological constant.

A positive `Lambda` acts like positive vacuum energy and makes
`a(t)` accelerate — that’s repulsive gravity. This is literally
what we observe as dark energy today.


### 4. Why Feynman said what he did

He was pointing out a contradiction: quantum field theory
predicts that the vacuum energy density should be huge (like
10^120 times bigger than what we infer from cosmic acceleration).
If that were true, the repulsive gravity from vacuum energy would
be so strong that the universe would have expanded so fast that
galaxies could never have formed. Yet galaxies exist, so the
vacuum energy must be much smaller than naive theory predicts.

So his statement “energy in empty space leads to repulsive
gravity” is exactly a paraphrase of “a positive cosmological
constant accelerates expansion.”


### 5. Intuition

Why does negative pressure repel? In GR, both energy density and
pressure gravitate. Positive pressure increases attractive
gravity, but negative pressure acts like tension — like
a stretched rubber band pushing everything apart. Vacuum energy
is like that tension filling all space. Instead of clumping, it
dilutes nothing and makes expansion accelerate.


This whole thing is the heart of one of the biggest unsolved
problems in physics: why the vacuum energy isn’t absurdly huge.
It’s not obvious from Newtonian thinking at all — it’s baked into
the math of Einstein’s equations.
