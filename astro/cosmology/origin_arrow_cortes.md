- Jump to QM problem:
    - Theorists like to think of a pristine Hamiltonian evolution
      of the wave function. Its supposed to be unitary.
    - But as soon as you measure it, (perturb it) you break the
      unitarity of it.
    - So how do you preserve the evolution:
    * reversal of the measurement
    * stochastic step in the collapse of the wave function
    - So the measurement is a nuisance. But this measurement is
      all there is! You can't find out anything about the wave
      function if you don't measure it! So why are we sticking to
      the perfect time symmetric evolution if everything we can
      learn about the system is by perturbing it and spoiling the
      time symmetry. Although many worlds preserves the unitary
      by saying all outcomes exist.

- Smolin Comment: (limit cycles)
    - (inaudible) ... discrete set of states and a deterministic
      evolution rule. So if you're in the "n" state the next
      state will be the "n+1" state.
    - But its not generically reversible. The prior state to "n"

    - This is a system I learnt about from Stu, which biologists
      have studied a great deal. They have a generic behaviour.
      You start at some point and you go to a limit cycle. Why do
      you go to a limit cycle?
    - Because since the number of states is finite you eventually
      have to repeat a state, but once you repeat a state,
      because its deterministic you keep going around in a loop.
    - So consider such a system in its mature stage. Consider an
      ensemble of such systems. You're going to find a lot of
      systems running around loops. Now when they're running
      around loops they have a unique predecessor as well as
      a unique follower. So you could say I'm looking at a bunch
      of finite _reversible_ systems. Because they're all going
      some way around their limit cycle but they might have gone
      the other way. So you could fool yourself into thinking you
      live in a world where the dynamical law is reversible. But
      in reality you live in a world where your dynamical law is
      _irreversible_, you're just in a late stage of it, where
      you've collapsed to the limit cycle.
    - We've developed a kind of dynamical system where I've just
      described its essential feature: it starts off with
      a dynamics that is irreversible and thats the fundamental
      dynamics. But has a late time evolution which looks
      reversible.
