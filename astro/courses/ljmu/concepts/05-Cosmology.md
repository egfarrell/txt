### Lecture 01

History
    - Aristotles system: egg-shell universe (Earth is the
      centre of the universe and heavenly bodies are
      attached to concentric spheres which rotate around the
      Earth.)

    - Hubbles discovery of the expansion of the universe
      along with Einsteins GR marked the beginning of
      modern Cosmology

Assumptions:
    - _Homogeneous_: Structure (eg galaxies, Stars) are
      negligble on large enough scales. We'll try to
      understand what scales later on. From the assumption
      that structure is negligble, we have the notion that
      the Universe is Homogeneous (invariant under
      _translation_). Also its consistent in: density,
      composition anywhere in space.

    - _Isoptropic_: The universe is Isoptropic (invariant
      under _rotation_). If we look in different directions
      in the universe, it should look the same.

    - Note: If the universe is isotropic around every point
      then ⇒ its Homogeneous! (this is in the tutorial)

Example 1:
    - We're in the centre of a universe thats like a brick
      wall.
        - Is it Homogeneous? _Yes_. Its an infinite
          distributin of bricks, and however far out we go
          we see the same distribution of bricks.
        - Is it Isotropic? _No_. We see the cement lines of
          the bricks in one direction (and the bricks look
          longer) compared to the other direction.

Example 2:
    - In the centre of a universe of concentric circles
      separated by gaps:
        - Homogeneous? No. Because some regions have gaps
          and some regions are filled with circles.
        - Isotropic? Yes. If you look in any direction
          around the Earth you see the same pattern.

Conclusion:
        - you can have different combinations of Homogeneity
          and Isotropy.

Our Universe:
    - The HST took deep field images in North / South
      hemispheres. They were very similar statistically.
      This gives us strong evidence that the universe is
      isotropic (on average).
    - Also at _different wavelengths_: plotted the
      distribution of radio sources (at cosmological
      distances) within 15 degrees of the north celestial
      pole. There was no difference in the distribution in
      any part of the area... (could also plot the
      distribution of _x-ray_ sources etc...)

Now Einstein/Curvature
    - From principle of equivalence (and firing laser beams
      across accelerating rockets!) we predict that gravity
      light (_light falls_!)
    - the force a person feels standing stationary in
      a gravitational field (eg on the Earth) can be
      _replaced_ by that of someone accelerating in the
      absence of any gravitational field.
    - now gravity bends light, and from that it must be the
      case, that _the universe must be curved_. (???) In
      other words the passage of light doesnt travel in
      a (Euclidean) straight line. It travels in curves.

### Scale Factor
    - we can think about the expansion of the universe in
      a simple way if we think about a particular distance
      at a given time, called the co-moving distance, then
      the expansion of the universe can be represented by
      a simple scale factor that scales that co-moving
      distance.

    - so if the distance between two points is `s` we can
      think of that as: `s = x ∙ a(t)` where `x` is fixed.
      - `x` is the comoving distance
      - `a(t)` is the scale factor

### Recession Velocity
    - now find the recession (expansion) velocity: just
      differentiate the distance wrt time:
        ```
        v  = (ds/dt)
           = x ∙ da/dt
           = s/a ∙ da/dt
           = ȧ/a ∙ s
           = H_0 ∙ s
        ```

    - where `H_0` is the Hubble "constant" that actually
      varies with time! Note: H_0 refers to the Hubble
      constant at the present time.

    - The equation above demonstrates the _linear_ nature of
      Hubbles Law.


### Redshift
    - The redshift of an object (eg Galaxy) has a direct
      physical relationship to the scale factor of the
      universe.

    - The redshift is _not a velocity in a conventional_
      sense. (You can think of it as a velocity on small
      scales). So Hubbles original diagram hes plotting
      velocity and not redshift, because the galaxies are
      very local (need to check this)

    - The formula:
        ```
        1 + z = λ / λ_0
              = a_0 / a(t)
              = 1 / a(t)
        ```

### consequences of expanding universe
    - if the universe was expanding at a constant rate, the
      age of the universe would be a simple `1/H_0`
    - however the mass in the universe tends to slow down
      the expansion, therefore `1/H_0` will be the upper
      limit to the age.
    - the rate of change of the scale factor `a(t)` is
      governed by the average density of the universe. 
    - If the _density_ is low, the universe will expand
      forever.
    - If the _density_ is high, the expansion will
      eventually be stopped and reversed.
    - The cosmological parameter that refers to this
      critical density is: `Ω` and its the ratio: `Ω
      = ρ / ρ_c` where `ρ` is the current density and `ρ_c`
      is the critical density.
    - for a universe only containing mass we have:
       - `Ω < 1`: infinite expansion = Open Universe 
       - `Ω = 1`: balance point = Flat Universe
       - `Ω > 1`: re-collapse will occur = Closed Universe 

### How do we measure "Ω"
    - Add up all the mass we can see, and divide by the
      volume!
    - starlight from galaxies gives `Ω_stars ~ 0.003`. If
      this was everything, then universe would expand
      forever!
    - other types of matter eg hot gas in galaxy clusters
      account for `Ω_m = 0.03`
    - however there is evidence for a lot of matter we cant
      see making `Ω_m ~ 1`. This is so-called _Dark Mater_.
    - Dark Matter: its matter which acts gravitationally but
      we cant see it because it doesnt seem to radiate.

### Evidence for Dark Matter
    - the idea that most of the mass in the universe is
      something we cant see is a bold one!
    - However we can see the gravitational effects of dark
      matter.
    - eg the rotation curves of galaxies
    - _important_! look at the image of the rotation curve
      equations (i saved using vlc). Theres 2 predictions
      depending on if its a point mass or an extend mass
      object
    - a similar mechanism operates when observing clusters
      of galaxies. The velocities of individual galaxies
      tell us about the mass interior in the the cluster.
      But the galaxies are observed to be moving much more
      rapidly that can be explained by bright matter.
    - most dark matter is believed to be non-baryonic

### Lecture 03: Big Bang and CMB
    - if the universe is expanding, then by going backwards
      in time we reach a moment when the universe is
      infinitely dense
    - important to remember: if we're imposing the ideas of
      Homogeneity and Isotropy on the universe, then the
      universe must be very dense at all points in space and
      time. So although the universe appears to be expanding
      away from us, its also expanding away from every other
      point in space.

### Big Bang assumptions
    - the universe is Homogeneous and Isoptropic
    - the cosmological principle
    - gravity dominates at large scales (related: GR is
      correct)
    - the same laws of physics apply through all time and
      space. (no easy way of testing that the laws at
      cosmological distance are the same as those nearby)

    Without these assumptions, no theory can even get
    started.

### Big Bang tests
    - the big bang model explains 3 very important
      observational facts (tests)
        - the expansion of the universe
        - the abundance of light elements (H, He ... all the
          up to Beryllium) are all synthesised in the big
          bang and we can make tests on the abundance of
          those elements
        - the CMB

### Primordial Nucleosynthesis

    - in the first 10-15 mins, the temp of the universe was
      high enough to proudce light elements by nuclear
      fusion

    - the theory BBN combines nuclear physics with the big
      bang model to predict relative abundances of the light
      elements:

      - Deuterium
      - Helium-3
      - Helium-4
      - Lithium-7

    - the abundances are tested by studying (old) metal-poor
      galaxies and intergalatic clouds (where stellar
      processing has had very little effect)

### CMB

    - in the early universe all matter was fully ionised.
      Matter and radiation was in equilibrium.

    - After about 300,000 years (z=1,100) the temp dropped
      to 3,000K and eutral Hydrogen could form. Then the
      number of free electrons dramatically dropped and
      photons were free to move independently of matter.
      This is the era of decoupling.

    - so the universe was filled with (blackbody) radiation
      of about 3,000K. Since then the expansion has cooled
      the radiation

    - so BB theory predicts a sea of photons filling the
      universe with a blackbody temp of ~3K, visible in all
      directions

    - as the universe expands, the matter scales as
      `(1+z)^3`. As we look back in time, the matter is in
      a smaller volume, and the energy density increases.

    - now photon energy density scales as `(1+z)^4`. Because
      the photon _redshifts_ and as the universe expands it
      loses more energy
      
### COBE blackbody plot
    - the error bars are 400 sigma! (normally you quote 1 or
      2 sigma error bars)
    - on cobe the bars are blown up to show how accurate the
      data fits the model
    - its the most perfect blackbody yet found. No other
      spectrum is so close to a blackbody. Thats important,
      because in other theories of the universe, its very
      difficult to construct such a natural blackbody within
      it. (if it wasnt from a hot origin at the start of the
      universe)
    - the temperature of the CMB goes as `1+z`, cos
      a blackbody goes as `T^4` and the photon energy
      density goes as `(1+z)^4`.
    - so divide `3000/ 1100 ≈ 3` which is roughly the
      temperature

### WMAP - fluctuation spectrum
    - if we take the WMAP results and take a fourier
      transform of the _fluctuations_ what we see is
      a spectrum of the moments of distribution against the
      amplitude. (the angular scale is along the top)
    - what you see is an enormous increase in the
      fluctuations of the CMB at the scale size of 1º.
      (which apparently indicates `Ω = 1`)
    - and thats no coincidence. Because that peak is called
      the _first doppler peak_. It represents the scale of
      the universe when the sound speed changed. 
    - The sound speed change when the universe went from
      being radiation dominated to matter dominated. And
      that happened at a specific time in the universe, and
      the size of the universe at that time is essentially
      represented now by a scale size of about 1º in the
      sky. 
    - the fluctuation spectrum at smaller scales is given by
      the RHS of the plot which shows the harmonics of the
      1st doppler peak. And the amplitude of the 1st doppler
      peak is really telling us about the amount of matter
      in the universe - its actually a good measure of the
      overall matter density `Ω`
    - The fit to these higher harmonic fluctuations (on
      smaller scales than 1º) they depend on the combination
      between bayronic matter and cold dark matter (CDM). So
      the proportions of those go into the predictions of
      the small scale fluctuations.
    - _damping_: you can see a very strong decline in the
      fluctuation spectrum: basically the baryons "free
      stream", have enough momentum, or enough velocity, to
      stream out of these dense regions on this particular
      scale (???? check this)
    - we can actually measure the effect of the
      _cosmological constant_ too. Remember `λ` is a large
      scale effect and only operates significantly at late
      times. So in the very early universe we dont see much
      of a signal. Except on the very largest scales, and
      these are photons that are changed by the geometry of
      the universe due to `λ` at very late times before they
      enter our telescopes.
    - the red line is our best fit based on the Lambda CDM
      model (and its a great fit)
