mercury:
In the 50s people thought that Mercury was orbitally locked
to the sun. They thought it had one fiery side and one cold
side with aliens living in the thin "equatorial" region
in-between.

But in fact, Mercury's rotational period is 2/3s of its
orbital period. This is not a coincidence. It stems from
_spin-orbit coupling_ which is similar to _tidal resonance_
(synchronous rotation?)

Spin orbit coupling is locally stable and is a favourable
configuration. Full tidal coupling is a more desirable
global configuuration but moving away from a 3-to-2
resonance means going though a less stable confiuration.
This won't happen without a sudden "kick" to the system.

Not this spin orbit coupling comes from:
- a slightly asymmetric planet
- a slight elliptical orbit
and the asymmetry matches on alternate orbits of the
perhelion.
