### Gaia tutorial:
    - Precision of Photometry: Choose the 3 radii (Aperture,
      2 Annulus) so you maximise the S/N. 
        - ie you maximise the `(count on the star) / (Error on
          the count on the star)`
    - discussion about PSFs. All objects are effectively point
      sources. So why do some stars appear more spread out
      (wider) than others? 
        - Note: the psf is a response of the optical system
          (telescope) to light from a point source.
        - Well, when we look at a star, we're looking at the PSF
          of the star _and_ the background light.
        - And the brigher a star is, the further it sticks out
          from the background. The further a star sticks out from
          the background, the further into the "wings" of the PSF
          we see.
        - Therefore, the wider it looks, the brighter it is!
    - within a single image, we would use the _same aperture
      size_ to measure the flux from all stars. This is becuase
      we want to be consistent and also because all point sources
      (stars) within the image, are modified by the same PSF.
    - by fixing the same aperture size, we measure the same
      proportion of flux of all the stars

### Transients: 
    Anything that doesnt repeat. Happens once. ("guest
    stars") Contrast versus periodic variables: happens over
    and over again. European astronomers: _Nova Stella_ (new
    star). From this came the term "Nova" or "Novae". Once
    thought to be the birth of new stars. Now we know that
    they're phenomena that happen towards the end of a stars
    lifetime (in most cases).

    Most historic "novae": are either
        - Galactic Classical Novae
        - Supernovae

### variable stars: Algol: Demon Star
    First eclipsing binary and first non-explosive variable.
    IR animation of 2 stars moving around each other. The
    animation is a bit jumpy because the time sampling isn't
    smooth - not a smooth sampling of observations of this
    binary system.

### Mira (Omicron Ceti)
    Prototype of the Mira variables. Reason it varies: the
    star pulsates. Its physical radius changes. This causes
    changes in its luminosity and surface temperature.

### Photography:
    First photograph of a star: Vega in 1850. First
    absorption spectrum: Vega: 1863.

### Are we missing local Super-novae?
    Have we missed a huge number of super-novae? Since
    telescope discovered, we haven't seen a single Galactic
    one! One easy way to miss a supernovae: when the _sun_
    is obscuring it. Or maybe missed due to sociological
    reasons: World War II, more dust in atmosphere..

### Time domain surveys
    Repeated observations of the same region of space, in
    anticipation of things changing. Not observing a known
    variable/transient to see what they're doing. Youre
    observing an unknown region of space looking for new
    stuff.
