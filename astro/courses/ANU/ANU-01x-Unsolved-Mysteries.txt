       - We are 95% sure space is expanding. But where is it expanding

    - ANGULAR SIZE DISTANCE
        - $MY_VIDEOS/Astro/ANU-01-Astro-mysteries/01-Expanding-universe/02-Angles-and-Distances.3gp
        - We must know distance if we want to know an objects mass or
          energy!
        - we can tell how OLD something is based on its distance,
          because light travels at 300km/sec (distance = how far back
          in time)
        - when we go out and look at things, we're really looking
          around an ANGLE. We can look around 360deg but when I see
          something, I'm actually seeing some fraction of that circle.
        - $MY_IMAGES/astro/anu01/theta-view-angle.jpg
        - `l` is the size of something far away.
        - When `l` is really far away (`r >> l`) then `l ~ s` where
          `s` is the arc length (in radians)
        - $MY_IMAGES/astro/anu01/angular-size-distance.jpg
        - missing step... make this intuitive
        - and we get `l = rθ` or  `θ = l / r`
            - (turn it into a triangle really)
            - later: no it's not really based on a triange with
              sin/cos etc like I thought first. It's more about
              cirlces and measuring the amount of "rim" around the
              _CIRCLE_ in radians that an object takes up...
        - the length of an object is its distance times it's angular
          size
        - but angular size not always a good way of measuring
          distance!!

    - LUMINOSITY DISTANCE
        - $MY_VIDEOS/Astro/ANU-01-Astro-mysteries/01-Expanding-universe/03-Luminosity-Distance.3gp
        - luminosity: energy per second
        - We can try to see how much luminosity it has by saying:
        - Energy per second = luminosity * (Area of telescope / area of sphere)
        - $MY_IMAGES/astro/anu01/naive-luminosity-gathering.jpg
        - but different telescopes (or eyes) have different collecting
          areas!
        - so Astronomers invented 'flux', energy PER _UNIT AREA_
            - `f = L / 4πr^2`
            - so detected energy proportional to `1 / r^2`

    - SPECTRA
        - $MY_VIDEOS/Astro/ANU-01-Astro-mysteries/01-Expanding-universe/04-Spectra.3gp

    - DOPPLER
        - $MY_VIDEOS/Astro/ANU-01-Astro-mysteries/01-Expanding-universe/05-Doppler-Effect.3gp
        - Spectra can measure SPEED of objects too
        - Wavelengths look shorter from the front, longer from the

        - take the spectrum and compare `λobs` to `λlab`. Then work
          out redshift...
        - $MY_IMAGES/astro/anu01/calc-redshift-from-spectrum.jpg

        - how do we get VELOCITY from redshift?
            - there's a relativistic version, 
            - and a rough version for slower moving objects: 
                - `z = v/c`
            - if `v/c` = 0.01, then it's going 1% speed of light...
            - $MY_IMAGES/astro/anu01/velocity-from-redshift.jpg

### 04. Giant Black Holes
    - QUASAR DISCOVERY
        - $MY_VIDEOS/Astro/ANU-01-Astro-mysteries/04-Giant-black-holes/01-Discovery-of-Quasars.3gp
        - SMBHs = "Quasars"? 
        - In 1940s Radio Engineers were picking up signals from space.
          They opened up a new window on the sky. 
        - However very little overlap between bright optical sources
          and bright radio sources.
        - Paul studies a top 100 radio source, but it doesn't show up
          at all in the optical. Apparently it's comeing from a "blank
          bit of the sky".
        - Radio Problem 1960's: poor angular resolution. 0.5 degree on
          the sky. That's big: 200,000 stars in that patch. So which
          one is the radio source.
        - 3C 273: Parkes radio telescope, clever idea: moon in front
          of source will block signal... then triangulate source. (dug
          trench for telescope)
        - Palomor found source. But looked unlike anything stellar. 
        - Quasar spectra: very broad features. Indicating motions of
          10,000's km per second. (Stellar lines much narrower) 
        -  `α` line shifted from the UV into 500nm: 323% (z=3.23)
         - 18th magnitude, although 2.5 billion lyrs away (Paul said
           12 billions light years away?). 
         - Something as bright as a star, but billions of times
           further away. So it must be `billions^2` times brighter. 

    - QUASAR LUMINOSTIY
        - $MY_VIDEOS/Astro/ANU-01-Astro-mysteries/04-Giant-black-holes/02-Quasar-Luminosity.3gp
        - (Question: Wiki says 3C 273 is 2.5 Billion lyrs away?)
        - 3C 273 light is ~ 10.7 billion light years away, using it's
          redshift `z` in a rough calculation. 
            - ie using scale factor: `a(t) = 1 / 1 + z` 
            - the quasar light has been travelling for 10.7 Glyr
            - But warning: this doesn't account for the fact that
              space is expanding (see cosmology course for true
              calculation)
            - Luminosity: `3.4 * 10^40 W`
            - Luminosity: 10^14 solar L•

    - Quasars powered by nuclear fusion?
        - $MY_VIDEOS/Astro/ANU-01-Astro-mysteries/04-Giant-black-holes/05-Fusion-Power-for-Quasars.3gp

    - Quasars powered by gravity?
        - $MY_VIDEOS/Astro/ANU-01-Astro-mysteries/04-Giant-black-holes/06-Energy-From-Gravity.3gp
        - How do we get energy from gravity? Drop something! 
        - if you threw a peanut near a black hole, it'd quickly travel
          at the speed of light. If someone tried to catch it, they'd
          hae a hold in their hand, and a huge explosion as the peanut
          would have so much genetic energy.
        - How much energy is released if something falls from infinity
          to just above the event horizon? (`R_s`)
        - First: full form of PE equation is not `PE = mgh` but:
        - $MY_IMAGES/astro/anu01/release-PE-from-Gravity.jpg
        - After calculation: in theory you can convert 50% of rest mass energy into
          energy using action of gravity (~ 50% efficiency)
        - Nuclear fusion will only convert ~ `1%` 
        - So gravity will give you 50 times more energy per unit mass
          than nuclear fusion
        - (Only if all the energy is released before the EH) 

    - Centre of Galaxy
        - $MY_VIDEOS/Astro/ANU-01-Astro-mysteries/04-Giant-black-holes/07-Quasar-Zoom.3gp
        - for every quasar we see, there are 3 or 4 hidden behind
          a cloud of (galactic?) dust
        - simulation: move in towards centre of galaxy, towards SMBH.
            - first you see purple light! (gas being zapped by the
              radiation)
            - then spinning disk, taking a few _hours_ to spin
            - centre of disk spins really quickly, closer to BH
              gravity 
            - spins slower further out... 
            - You get viscosity because of the different spin rates.
              Viscosity from rubbing between the inner and outer bits. That makes
              everything very hot ... liberates energy
            - you get temperatures up to 100,000 degrees as you get
              close to the centre (blue color) 
            - Then at 3 times `R_s` (Schwartzschild radius): last
              stable orbit

        - $MY_VIDEOS/Astro/ANU-01-Astro-mysteries/04-Giant-black-holes/08-Feeding-Black-Holes.3gp
        - most distanct quasar: 7.1 z 
        - implies formed 800 million years after Big Bang 
        - But how create a BH of billions of solar masses so early in
          the life of the universe?
        - star tries to escape BH by travelling radially outwards. 
        - $MY_IMAGES/astro/anu01/star-escaping-BH.jpg
        - No chance!
        - star tries to escape BH but travelling at right angles to
          the BH. Now it orbits almost like a comet does around the
          sun (an ellipse). It can stay in this orbit for the life
          time of the universe. UNLESS IT LOSES ANGULAR MOMENTUM! 
        - So in the early universe, how can you stuff so much mass
          down close to form a SMBH?
