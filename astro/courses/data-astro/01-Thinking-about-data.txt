### Pulsar history
    - Zwicky: core collapse to dense neutron core, then outer
      shells rebound off.. (super nova)
    - This "neutron" stars were assumed too dim to be seen  
    - But in the 60s astronomers realised 2 things: 
        - angular momentum would be conserved... so these stars
          would be spinning even once per second
        - also collapsing stars would drag their magnetic field
          with it...  neutron stars would have incredibly dense
          magnetic fields. 
        - Charged particles (+ plasma) surrounding the star would
          get funneled to the magnetic poles and shot out into
          space as 2 intense beams
        - so a neutron star becomes a bit like a lighthouse  
    - suddenly Jocelyn Bell observations made sense. The steady pulse
      was a spinning neutron star  

### Image stacking
    - Use Murchison Widefield Array (MWA): low frequency radio
      telescope
    - Bands: between 80 to 300 MHz (similar to popular radio
      stations!)
    - large FOV ... good for large scale surveys  
    - radio files usually FITS: view using DS9 or online with
      Aladdin  
    - remember: radio frequencies dont have color  
    - DETECTION: if flux density is 5 standard
      deviations higher than the noise in the local region  

### Non detection
    - too faint, intermittent
    - statistical detection of a population of pulsars too faint to see in our original dataset
