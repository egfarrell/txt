    - History!
        - Eyes were the first tool for science. We could not
          detect the detailed properties of materials around us.
        - So we first considered all items built from continuous
          matter e.g. water
        - Eventually Greek philosophers came up with 'atoms' in
          the continuous media around us
        - horizontal water wheels/mill (more water to pump)
        - 13th century: monks had mastered hydraulics for water
          mills, could use gears/transmission wheels to transfer
          water to the fields (but practical knowledge only, no
          theory)
        - even today's engineers don't use real theory of
          continuous media, they use just the "laws" of
          hydraulics
        - 20thC -> "chaos theory" used for environment/ecology.
          Question: How are smoke clouds dispersed via erratic
          motion of the air (turbulence)
            - theorists solved it with diffusion theory
            - but environmental scientists had their "own"
              theory! They use complex "closed-source" formulas.
        - Industrial Revolution: Brass cylinders were replaced
          with (cheaper) steel cylinders
        - 18thC: Science of Continuous Media was born: Bernoulli
          theorem.
        - Cook a frankfurter in the oven for a long time, the

### Thermodynamic energy
    - big `F`: Helmholtz Free energy (function of density and
      temp)
        - what is this function related to?
        - we can have free energy for the _entire_ fluid volume
          (extensive quantity, dpen)
    - little `f`
        - but we also have free energy per unit volume, or unit
          mass
    - relation between `f` and `p`: both are intensive, dont
      depend on volume
    - if the temp is constant, the pressure is only a function of
      1 varible: density. So I can calculate a gradient of
      pressure.
    - derivative of free energy wrt density:  "chemical
      potential", "enthalpy"
    - `∇ x v` = Rot(v) = `ω`
    - streamline: imaginary line, velocity is tangent to
    - use the length `l` as a parameter
    - velocity is contant along a stream line, but NOT everywhere
      in a liquid (common mistake made)
    - the Bernoulli function is only constant if we sit on one
      streamline, and velocity is tangent, then we get a constant
      velocity. But if we're on another streamline, we get
      a different velocity?
    - for irrotational flow, we can always write a velocity as
      being a potential vector. As being the gradient of a scalar
      function `φ`
    - blow air inbetween two pages, the pages move closer
    - shower curtain moves towards you in shower (water
      accelerated downwards in shower cabin, so lower pressure in
      the cabin). But! doesnt take account of floor of shower. So
      no acceleration to infinity?!
        - yes, air goes down, hits floor and goes up
        - so we have a vortex
        - so Bernoulli does not apply (Bernoulli only for
          irrotational flow)
        - Magnus effect
    - neccessary for Bernoulli Theorem: streamlines go from
      INFINITY to INFINITY
        - rotating soccer ball: bend around, due to interaction
          with air (fluid) and rotating ball
    - planes: wrong bernoulli explanation. So lift _NOT_
      essentially due to Bernoulli. e.g. planes can fly
      upsidedown
        - note: air is a VISCOUS fluid, not an IDEAL fluid


    - Laminar can be unstable. Coming out the end of a hose, we
      have an instability... "Newton instability"
        - This is crucial for firemen: want a long stable stream
          directly into a fire...
