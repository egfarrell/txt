### harvard classification
    classified stars by strength of hydrogen absorption
    (using roman numerals)

### classic spectral sequence
    - O, B, A, F, G, K, M
    - corelates with colour 
    - i.e. is a temperature sequence
    - O,B 	blue			50,000 deg
    - A		white
    - G		yellow-white	6,000 deg
    - K		orange
    - M		red				3,000 deg

	90% hydrogen, 10% helium, salting of everything else


luminosity  ----> absolute magnitude
temperature ----> spectral class

### two variables
    scientists always want to plot one against the other!
    you'd think hotter stars should be more luminous stars
    line up from low-luminosity, low-temperature to
    high-temperature, high-luminosity

### puzzle
    - luminosity of the star increased, as the temperature
      went _down_!!!!

    - now that makes no sense 
    * in terms of the picture of the amount of energy per
      unit area coming out
    * in terms of the 4th power of the temperature

	only one explanation: 
    * you must have a big _surface area_

### dwarfs
    - a synonym for main sequence star 
    - (even though can be big and hot)
    - in contrast to "giant" stars off the main sequence

### another discovery
    - Hertzsprung found a sampling of stars much more
      luminous than then giants but at the same temperature. 
    - So they were named super-giants. 
        * giants: size: 1 AU (orbit of the earth) 
        * super-giants: size: orbit of Jupiter/Saturn


### tiny stars: Sirius A and B
    * Sirius B is as hot as Sirius A but much fainter
    * can only be fainter if much smaller (about the size of
      earth)
    * the word "dwarfs" used for main sequence stars so word
      "white dwarf" used instead,
    * then blue dwarfs and red dwarfs found
    * white dwarfs _parallel main sequence_ at lower
      luminosity.
