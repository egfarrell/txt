big bang nucleosynthesis

universe 1 min old, acting like a nuclear reactor.

The temperature of the universe is the CMB. Today this temperature is
3 Kelvin. When the universe was 1000 times smaller, the temperature
was 3000 kelvin. (inversely proportional)


At early times, radiation (photons and neutrinos) was the dominant
energy form in the universe. (As universe expands, radiation falls the
quickest. As we contract universe into the past, radation rises the
quickest)


Protons and neutrons freeze out - ie stop converting between one
another. When they freeze out youre left with 1 NEUTRON FOR EVERY 6
PROTONS. The proton is lighter and therefore easier to make.


Neutrons are not stable.
They have a 50:50 chance of decaying every 10 minutes.
So 1 minute after big bang, the ratio changes to 1 neutron
for every 7 protons. ie a slight increase in the difference.


Now 1 minute after big bang, 3 billion kelvin,
neutrons and protons can finally start to stick together. ie we can make NUCLEI!


Now DEUTERIUM is a bit fragile, but if you get two deuterium to
collide you get helium and thats robust, it lasts. You also get bits
of deuterium, lithium and helium-3 left over. Some numbers: if theres
2 neutrons and 14 protons in the early universe, you get 1 helium
nucleus and 12 protons left over. ie 25% of the mass of the early
universe is helium. (Gamow and Alpher came up with the nucleosynthesis stuff)


How do you test the predictions of nucleosynthesis? Because star
production has created many more elements to add to the early big bang
stuff. Well look at an unspoilt pristine part of the universe i.e.
through a promoridal dark cloud?
