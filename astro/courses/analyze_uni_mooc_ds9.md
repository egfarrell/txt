# photons 
    almost exclusive providers of all astronomical data

# packets of light
    - we can _timetag_ each individual package of light as
      its received into our instrument. 
    - These packages are called photons ...

# ability to record time of arrival
    - This allows us to see if the object is changing its
      _intensity_ or light output as a function of time. 
    - ie. we can search for _time variability_ of cosmic
      sources.

# spatial resolution 1
    - the ability of a light gathering instrument to
      _distinguish_ nearby regions from each other. 
    - Imagine looking at a car approaching at night from far
      away. 
    - At first both headlights are on top of one another and
      you can't tell there are two of them. 
    - However if you look through binoculars you can easily
      distinguish them.


# spatial resolution 2
    - spatial resolution is a function of several aspects of
      an instrument including its _diameter_. 
    - "The bigger the better!". 
    - Improved resolution is a reason why we make bigger and
      bigger telescopes. 
    - Not because we want to "magnify" things!

# colour information
    - colour photos give energy information. 
    - eg. blue light is more energetic than red light.
    - X-rays are more energetic than blue light.

# why bother with black and white images then?
    - well black and white representations of objects can be
      more useful than colour ones at times. 
    - Example: the red facade of a skyscraper in front of
      a light blue sky. Is the skyscraper "brighter" than
      the adjacent sky area? 
    - Since _our eyes_ respond differently to blue and red
      light, the answer is not obvious. 
    - But you can get the correct answer with a Blact
      & White rendition of the photo. 
    - There you can clearly see the skyscraper is darker
      than the surrounding sky.

# X-ray images
    - this point about the usefulness of B&W images will be
      important when we consider x-ray images in detail. 
    - After all our eyes can't see x-rays at all! 
    - Therefore any representation of an x-ray source will
      be a bit problematical and we will discuss this later.

# energy spectrum
    - the third clue astro images can provide is: 
    * the _energy_ of each photon, 
    * as well as _how many_ photons of each energy get
      emitted each second.
    * gives us a fingerprint of the _chemical composition_
      of astro objects as well as giving insight into the
      energy _production mechanisms_ governing their
      radiation. 
    - Cosmic sources have different ways of producing their
      energy (just as flourescent lamps are different to
      tungsten lightbulbs)


# upside down image formation 1 (Brilliant!)
    - he took a photo of an arrow without a lens in the
      camera! 
    - Just a white blur resulted on film! 
    - Basically light from the tip of the arrow spread out
      _completely_ over the exposed film. 
    - Ditto light from the tail of the arrow, spread out
      everywhere... 
    - Soon you have a complete mess. 
    - You have light coming from all parts of the arrow onto
      all parts of the film (detector).

# upside down image formation 2
    - to ensure that light from _one part_ of the arrow goes
      to _one part_ of the lens, 
    - we place a little _pin-hole_ between the arrow and our
      detector.

    - So now light from the tip of the arrow _only_ goes
      through the hole to the bottom of the film. 
    - All other light from the tip is blocked.

    - And light from the tail of the arrow _only_ makes it
      through to the top of the film. etc. etc.

    - Finally an image of the arrow will be formed
      upside-down.

# disadvantages of a pinhole
    - only a little teeny amount of light goes through it.
      Wouldn't be great that instead of wasting all the
      light that gets blocked, we could actually use that
      light and form an image with many many more photons
      and therefore in a _shorter period of time_.

# moving targets - Deguerre 1838
    - early photograph of a busy french street showed no
      people! 
    - Only a single man getting his shoes shined. Why?
    - Because every other moving object was not still long
      enough to register on the photographic plate! 
    - Thus began a quest for "speed, speed and more speed"
      so we can capture progressively shorter time
      intervals. 
    - We want our telescopes to be more sensitve to light.

# temporal resolution
    - when cameras/telescopes do become more sensitve, we
      can accquire an image in a shorter length of time
      (increase _temporal resolution_). 
    - Modern cameras can capture in 0.00001 of a second what
      took Deguerre hours!
