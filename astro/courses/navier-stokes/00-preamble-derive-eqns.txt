### Part 3: Navier-Stokes
    - to reduce the number of unknowns, we need relationships
      between the _stresses_ and _velocities_
        - ie "constitutive relations" 
    - stress deformation equations: 
        - we relate(?) stress with the derivatives of velocity
    - assume: the stress are "Newtonian". This means the stresses
      are linearly proportional to the strain rates. 
        - (ie proportional to the "velocity derivatives")
        - (ie proportional to the "deformation rates")
    - ASSUME: 
        - incompressible fluids: 
            - ie density is constant 
        - isothermal flows: 
            - temperature changes not important / don't happen.
            - This means we don't need an _energy equation_!

    - SO:
        - Viscosity & density are _constant_ 

    - Normal stress:
        - `σ_xx = -p + 2μ • du/dx`
        - `σ_yy = -p + 2μ • du/dy`
        - `σ_zz = -p + 2μ • du/dz`
            - where 
            - `p` = "thermodynamic pressure" 
            - `μ` = coeff of Newtonaian viscosity
        - add all 3 together 
        - `(σ_xx + σ_yy + σ_zz) = -3p` 
        - the du/dx etc disappear because: ∇ • ṽ = 0

     - Shearing stresses
        - note they have some symmetry
