### Recommended Books

- Astrophysics: A Very Short Introduction. 2016
    - Binney, James.
    - Not especially about radio astronomy, but it contains
      discussions of the interstellar medium, accretion disks,
      supernovas, and other features of astrophysics.

- Black Holes: Very Short Introduction. 2015
    - Blundell, Katherine.
    - Everything that you wanted to know about black holes, with
      a discussion of quasars and radio galaxies.

- Telescopes: A Very Short Introduction. 2016
    - Cottrell, Geoff.
    - Chapter 7 is an overview of radio telescopes.

- SETI 2020: Roadmap Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence.
    - A very diverse view on the topic, from articles suitable
      for the general public to very technical specialist
      considerations. Includes the history of SETI.

- Death from the Skies: The Ways the World Will End. 2008
    - Plait, Philip
    - An amusing romp through the unpleasant side of space, with
      chapters on asteroids, supernovas, and black holes. For the
      general reader.

- Hydrogen: The Essential Element. 2002
    - Rigden, John S.
    - Mix of history and atomic physics tells the complete story
      of this essential element. Chapter 17 recounts the
      discovery of the 21-centimeter line.

### Radio Astronomy Books

- International Radio Telescope Projects:
    - A Life among Its Designers, Builders and Users. 2013
    - Baars, Jacob W. M.
    - Radio telescopes and radio observatories written in
      nontechnical language with an emphasis on engineering.

- Is Anyone Out There? 1992.
    - Drake, Frank, and Dava Sobel.
    - Scientific autobiography of Frank Drake. Mostly about SETI
      and somewhat dated because that field took a different turn
      than they expected, but great discussion of the early days
      of radio astronomy at Green Bank and Arecibo, and it
      describes many aspects of radio astronomy quite well.

Goddard, D. E., and D. K. Milne, eds. Parkes: Thirty Years of Radio
Astronomy. CSIRO Publications, 1994. Proceedings of a meeting on the
30th anniversary of the Parkes radio telescopes with chapters by scientists
who were and are involved with the observatory.

Gordon, Mark A. Recollections of "Tucson Operations": The Millimeter-
Wave Observatory of the National Radio Astronomy Observatory.
Springer, 2010. A personal recollection of the 36-foot radio telescope of
the National Radio Astronomy Observatory in Tucson, Arizona.

Goss, W. M. Making Waves: The Story of Ruby Payne-Scott--Australian
Pioneer Radio Astronomer. Springer, 2013. The biography of Ruby
Payne-Scott.

Graham-Smith, Francis. Unseen Cosmos: The Universe in Radio. Oxford
University Press, 2013. A book on radio astronomy for the educated
public that includes a discussion of the technology of radio telescopes.
Written by one of the pioneers of radio astronomy.


Kellermann, K., and B. Sheets, eds. Serendipitous Discoveries in Radio
Astronomy. NRAO Press, 1984. Proceedings of an international meeting
on discoveries in radio astronomy with chapters by Grote Reber, Sir
Bernard Lovell, Jocelyn Bell Burnell, Robert Wilson, Frank Drake, and
many more. A unique and wonderful volume. Available through the
Green Bank Observatory (www.greenbankobservatory.org).

Lockman, F. J., F. D. Ghigo, and D. S. Balser, eds. But It Was Fun: The First
Forty Years of Radio Astronomy at Green Bank. NRAO Press, 2007. This is
a scrapbook of documents about Green Bank and includes articles by the
scientists who built and operate the radio telescopes. Available through
the Green Bank Observatory (www.greenbankobservatory.org).

Lovell, Sir Bernard. The Story of Jodrell Bank. Harper & Row, 1968. A very
personal story of the trials and tribulations involved in the early years (up
to 1960) of the 250-foot radio telescope at Jodrell Bank.

------. The Jodrell Bank Telescopes. Oxford University Press, 1985.
Continuation of the story of Jodrell Bank from 1960, including plans for a
400-foot-diameter telescope that did not get built and the MERLIN array,
which did.

Mather, J. C., and J. Boslough. The Very First Light. Basic Books, 2008.
Nobel laureate John Mather on the big bang and the radio waves it
produced.

Sullivan, W. T., III. Cosmic Noise: A History of Early Radio Astronomy.
Cambridge University Press, 2009. The definitive history of early radio
astronomy worldwide, from pre-Jansky efforts to about 1953.

Sullivan, W. T., III, ed. The Early Years of Radio Astronomy. Cambridge
University Press, 1984. A history of radio astronomy up to 1960. Each
chapter is written by one of the pioneers in the field.

Verschuur, Gerrit. The Invisible Universe: The Story of Radio Astronomy.
3rd ed. Springer, 2015. Latest edition of a book that covers all of radio
astronomy for the general reader. Earlier editions, sometimes with a
slightly different title, are also valuable.
