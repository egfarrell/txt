### Interesting Lecture 01
    - Possible to get maps of the gravitational potential of the
      Earth. Big potential difference (in our units) between the
      top of Everest and a deep sea trench
    - Neutron stars are made of _fluid_. 
        - They can slosh about. 
        - They have different ways of _oscillating_. 
        - (You end up with a varying `quadrupole` moment)
        - NSs oscillate differently depending on what it's made
          of.
    - Also a NS 'kick' may come from an _instability_. Fluid
      dynamic instabilities
    - naive: for a core collapse super-nova, you would think the
      gravitational energy released is simply the difference
      between the starting grav potential (of the star before
      explosion) and the end-point grav potential (of the
      spinning NS core?).
        - But that would be totally wrong. For Grav Wave
          astronomy what matters is _how you got there_ (???),
          not just the (initial - final) difference
    - comment: "What underlies any of these calculations is
      treating fluid dynamics in full general relativity".
        - Basically, how do we model fluids?
        - and, NSs are generally relativistic objects
    - Approach: start with a Newtonian approximation...
    - Later: 
        - the first thing we do with Einstein's equation is
          introduce an observer...
        - $MY_IMAGES/astro/grav-waves/NS/introduce-observer.jpg
    - Derive the energy, momentum and stress
        - Originally from GR book by Tolman
        - $MY_IMAGES/astro/grav-waves/NS/define-energy-stress.jpg
    - What ingredients do we need for the stress-energy tensor of
      a NS? Elasticity (crust), superfluids, heat flow, charge
      currents (proton gas)
