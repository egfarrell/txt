

# Kirchhoff's Law 1
    - (OPAQUE bodies)
    - An OPAQUE object emits a _continuous_ spectrum of light at
      all wavelengths. (it emits all wavelengths of light, from
      gamma rays to radio waves and everything in between)
    - a solid is opaque
    - liquids can be opaque
    - even gases can be opaque (i.e. stars!)

    * note: _some_ amount of light is emitted at all
      wavelengths - but not the _same_ amount of light at
      all wavelengths (see Wiens law!)

# Kirchhoff's Law 2
    - (NON-OPAQUE/transparent body)
    - a transparent gas will emit light at only certain
      discrete wavelengths.
    - these specific discrete wavelengths of emission match
      the energy levels in the atom that its electrons are
      permitted to jump between.
    - this means each element in the gas emits a distinct
      finger-print (its own unique pattern of wavelengths)

# Kirchhoff's Law 3
    - NON-OPAQUE/transparent body
    - a transparent gas, when exposed to a continuous spectrum of
      light, will absorb a specific, discrete set of those
      photons
    - it will be the same wavelengths of light that the gas would
      emit in Kirchoffs 2nd law scenario.
    - in a way we are seeing the same elements from the 2nd law,
      but against the background (backdrop) of a continuous
      spectrum of light

# *Essential* point of Kirchoffs laws
    - when we look at the light spectrum emitted by actual stars,
      we mainly see a continuous spectrum of light.
    - But we also see (imprinted on that continuous spectrum)
      a set of dark absorption bands from which we can determine
      the elemental make-up of the star.

# colour
    - one of the simplest and most important pieces of
      information we can glean from stars.
    - _color_ is associated with _energy_
    - a basic measure of an objects energy is temperature.
      You know this: a hotter object contains more energy in
      it in the form of heat (thermal energy)
    - we can quantify this relationship between COLOR and
      TEMPERATURE: Wiens Law!

# Wiens _displacement_ Law (λ max emission)
    - The wavelength of light at which an object emits most
      strongly (brightest wavelength) is a constant divided
      by that objects temperature.

    - [1]: `λ_(max) = b / T (kelvin)` where `b = 2.89`

    -later addition: another way of looking at it from
      LJMU: the most popular frequency is linearly related
      to the temperature: 
        - [2]: `h  • ν_(max) = 2.82 kT`
        - ../ljmu/obs/obs-lecture-notes.md:236

        - Sept 2020: wait a minute, [1] has `λ_(max)` but [2] has
          `ν_(max)`. Need to dig about `kT` and `h`

    - or: the wavelength of _peak_ light emission is
      _inversely proportional_ to the temperature of the
      emitting body.

    - any OPAQUE emitting body will emit most of its light
      in one part of the spectrum. (humans emit about 100
      Watts in the infra red)

# Use of Wiens Law:
    - we now know have a straight-forward way of measuring
      the _temperature_ of a star: just measure a stars
      continuous light spectrum through a spectrograph
      (attached to a telescope)

    - Then record the _WAVELENGTH_ at which most of the
      light is being emitted. (ie which wavelength of light
      is brightest)

    - And voila! We can calculate the TEMPERATURE of that
      stars emitting surface.

# Measure a stars motion!
    - when an object moves towards you the emitted waves are
      bunched up (due to the motion of the emitter/source!)
    - we register the "bunching up" as a higher frequency of
      light or a bluer color
    - and vica versa when the _emitting_ object moves away
    - Doppler formula:
            `∆λ    v`
            `--  = --`
            `λ     c`

    - ∆λ  = wavelength shift
    -  λ  = wavelength of source not moving
    -  v  = velocity of source
    -  c  = speed of light

    - Doppler formula in words:
        (the % a stars          =  (the % of the speed
         wavelength is shifted)     of light a star is moving)
