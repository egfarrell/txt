### differential geometry
	- a la Latham Boyle
	- first, introduce `manifold`,
	- then `connection` (Cristofel symbols)
	- then `metric`

### geodesic
    - idea of geodesic exists even before a metric is defined on
      a manifold
    - A geodesic is a curve where the `tangent vector` is unchanged
        - i.e. the tangent vector is parallel transported

### curvature
    - is the failure of 2 covariant derivatives to commute when
      acting on a tensor
    - (thats one definition of Riemann Curvature Tensor)

### Ricci Tensor
    - is the Riemann tensor which is contracted
    - ie Ricci has only 2 indices
    - its a `2x2 matrix`
    - used in the vacuum Einstein equations where no matter is
      present

### metric
    - 2x2 symmetric matrix defined at every point on the manifold

### uses of metric
    - can raise and lower indices
    - also can get the INNER PRODUCT of 2 vectors now by using
      the metric.
    - because you have an inner product you can introduce the
      notion of the LENGTH of a vector
    - Because we have a metric now, this implies there's
      a preferred connection where the covariant derivative of
      the `metric` is everywhere `zero`.
        - This preferred connection is the Levi-Ceta connection
          or Cristofel symbol

### Ricci Scalar
    - Can contract the Ricci Tensor using a metric.
    - The Ricci Scalar gives a measure of curvature at a `single
      point`


### another use of metric
    - `Curve length` can define a line element, and integrate
      line element to get total curve length.

    - So using curve length we've another definition of
      a geodesic.
    - A geodesic is an (minimum) extremal of `curve length`.
    - Its easier to calculate a geodesic this way.

    - Calculating the geodesic this way is a bit like calculating
      the action for a Lagrangian in Classical Mechanics.
    - i.e. Calculating the geodesic is a calculus of variations
      problem and the Euler-Lagrange equations are used.

### A Partial Derivative
    - Is not a TENSOR quantity
    - It depends very much on the co-ordinate system you lay
      down. 
    - But we want some sort of derivative that is independent of
      coordinate systems.
