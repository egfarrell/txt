aside: spectroscopic binaries
	the absorption lines rock over and back

	absorption lines
		when one set blue-shifted
		the other set is red-shifted
		and vice versa

	increase binary orbital speed
		the blue shifts and red shifts get LARGER!
		bigger shift because of greater speeds...

	orbital speed v mass
		higher absorption line speed implies higher masses
		so measuring:
		shifts
		orbital period
		inclination angle (if known)

	plane of inclination
		say they were orbiting perpendicular to your line of sight
		then theyre be no component of radial velocity at all!
		so you'd see no red or blue shift at all!

now a star - black hole binary system
	only one set of absorption lines
	but they're rocking back and forth
	which signals an invisible partner

	so you need the mass of the visible star
	and then you can work out the mass of the invisible one

	if its in excess of 3 solar masses then it must be a bh!
	because any star around 3 solar masses would be very bright!
