### 1-1: Digital Signal Processing: the Basics

    - What is digital signal processing?
        - SIGNAL: 
        - Description of evolution of physical phenomenon
            - weather           ->   temperature
            - sound             ->   pressure (one possible description)
            - sound             ->   magnetic deviation on tape
            - light intensity   ->   gray level on paper (2D signal in space, not time)

        - Processing part:
            - Make sense of the signal info 
            - ANALYSIS: 
                - understanding the info carried by signal 
                - (maybe extract more high-level description)
            - SYNTHESIS: 
                - create a (new?) physical phenomenon (ie
                  a signal) that contains the info we want to put
                  out in the world

        - Problem with ANALOG signals:
            - I have devices that record the signal, but what is
              the function `f(t)` that describes the phenomenon?
            - Solution: digital: represent the series as
              a sequence of integer numbers...
                - $MY_IMAGES/astro/DSP/what-function-for-analog.jpg

        - Digital Paradigm: DISCRETE TIME: 
            - this is the REAL SHIFT in the way we perceive the
              world
            - can we really splice up time into a series of
              discrete instances and not lose information?
            - this is tied to the very nature of time: 
                - WHAT IS TIME?  
                - AUGUSTINE: 
                    - time doesnt exist  
                - KANT: 
                    - Time and space are fundamental categories
                      of the spirit. 
                    - They precede the existence of objects
                      a priori.
                - ZENO:  
                    - Dichotomy: cannot have an infinite number
                      of time segments summing to a finite
                      number. (however small each segment is) 
        - Question: How do we go from analog to discrete?
            - $MY_IMAGES/astro/DSP/analog-measurements-to-discrete.jpg
        - ANALOG is hard: 
            - calculus needed to compute average temperature:
            - $MY_IMAGES/astro/DSP/analog-use-calculus-for-avg.jpg
        - DISCRETE: just sum the values and divide by the number
          of samples!
            - $MY_IMAGES/astro/DSP/discrete-calculate-avg.jpg
            - We don't want models with mappings 
                - from the set of `infinite REALS` to values. 
            - Instead we want to go 
                - from the set of `INTEGERS` to values.
                - $MY_IMAGES/astro/DSP/set-of-integers-mapping-to-values.jpg

            - Time: becomes the ordinal dimensionless number `n`

            - Do we lose information and power if we switch to
              discrete time?  
            - Answer: no: 
                - Nyquist and Shannon proved that the continuous
                  time representaiton and discrete time
                  representation are equivalent (under very mild
                  conditions)
            - This is known as the _SAMPLING THEORM_ (1920):
                - $MY_IMAGES/astro/DSP/sampling-theorem.jpg
                - $MY_IMAGES/astro/DSP/sampling-from-continuous-to-discrete.jpg
                - Using Fourier and others, we'll always be able
                  to choose a sampling interval that will satisfy
                  the hypothesis of the sampling theorem...

            - Discrete amplitude: 

            - With a DISCRETE time, and DISCRETE amplitude, we
              have everything mapped to integers... and that's
              very effective for:
                - storage  
                - processing  
                - transmission  
                    - Now we use a universal to store all types
                      of signals, not the old way where we had
                      a different receiver for each analog
                      signal...
                    - $MY_IMAGES/astro/DSP/analog-storage-different-for-every-type.jpg
                - and software running on a universal
                  architecture replaces lots of hardware from the
                  past

            - NOISE: 
                - you always get your original signal attenuated
                  by a factor `G` along with noise `σ(t)`
                    - $MY_IMAGES/astro/DSP/noise-attenuation.jpg

            - Undo:
                - you can undo the attenuation by boosting it
                  with gain, ... 
                    - but you've also inflated the noise that was
                      introduced by the channel!
                - So you re-create a signal that has comparable
                  amplitude to the original signal, but the noise
                  is much larger too...
                    - $MY_IMAGES/astro/DSP/noise-amplified-too.jpg

            - Repeaters:
                - regenerate the signal to the original level...
                  eg every 10km in the trans-Atlantic telephone
                  cable
                - but after `n` sections of the cable though, you get
                  the noise amplified `n` times!  
                    - $MY_IMAGES/astro/DSP/noise-amplified-n-times.jpg

            - transmitting a digital signal:
                - encoded in binary form as "1" and "0"
                - "1" = +5 volts  
                - "0" = -5 volts  
                - much easier to repeat and amplifiy the signal
                  using a THRESHOLD: 
                    - if voltage > 0 => `+5 volts`  => "1"
                    - if voltage < 0 => `-5 volts`  => "0"
                    - if voltage < 0
                - $MY_IMAGES/astro/DSP/repeat-amplify-digital-signal.jpg

            - First lecture summary:
                - $MY_IMAGES/astro/DSP/dsp-processing-key-ideas.jpg
