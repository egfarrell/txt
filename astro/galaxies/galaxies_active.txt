
## Spectra of Galaxies

A galaxy spectrum is a composite spectrum of all the objects within the galaxy

### The optical spectrum of a normal galaxy contains

* contributions from star regions
* contributions from **HII** (singly ionised) regions

### Width of spectral lines:

* Doppler broadening due to thermal motion
* Doppler broadening due to  bulk motion of emitting material

### Elliptical galaxies:

* no HII regions
* optical spectrum similar to stellar spectrum
* but fainter absorption lines

### Spiral galaxies:

* has star regions
* has star-forming regions
* optical spectrum has BOTH stars and HII regions
* HII show weak emission lines

### Active galaxies:

* has optical spectrum of normal galaxy
* PLUS powerful additional radiation
* powerful additional radiation characterised by strong emission lines
* broadening comes from bulk motion of emitting gas

### Broadband Spectrum

* made up of galaxy radiation from all wavelength ranges
* to judge a broadband spectrum properly we need to use an SED
* an SED is a Spectral Energy Distribution
* an SED is a λF<sub>λ</sub> plot on logarithmic axes

### Types of SED's

* **Normal** Galaxy SED's peak at **optical wavelengths**
* **Active** Galaxy SED's large emissions accross a **WIDE** range of wavelengths


## Types of Active Galaxy

### All active galaxies have a compact energetic nucleus - an **AGN**

### Seyfert galaxies

* spiral galaxies
* bright point-like nuclei
* vary in brightness
* excesses in far infrared and other wavelengths
* strong broad emission lines

### Quasars

* resemble very distant Seyferts
* very luminous nuclei
* variable luminosity
* about 10% are **radio** **loud** (ie are strong radio sources)
* radio source powered by material jets moving at close to light-speed

### Radio galaxies

* giant radio lobes
* fed by 1 or 2 jets
* compact nucleus like seyferts
* variable compact nucleus though
* emission lines can be broad OR narrow

### Blazars

* continuous spectrum accross wide range of wavelengths
* emission lines may be present
* if emissino lines present they are broad and weak
* Blazars variable on very rapid timescales

## The Central Engine

### Size of fluctuating object

* assume object fluctautes in brightness in time ∆t
* radius of object cannot exceed c*∆t
* ie Radius ~ c*∆t
* point-like nature of AGNS and rapid variability => AGN smaller than solar system

### Size and Mass of AGN

* central engine believed to contain super-massive black hole (SBH)
* mass around 10<sup>8</sup> solar masses
* radius around 2AU (Schwarzchild radius)

### AGN energy generation

* Infalling material forms accretion disc around SBH
* gravitational energy converted to thermal energy
* gravitational energy also converted to radiation

### AGN Luminosity

* typical luminosity is 10<sup>38</sup> Watts
* generated by accretion rate of 0.2 solar masses per year

### AGN Maximum luminosity

* there is a limit to amount of radiation emitted
* its called the Eddington limit
* gravitational force = emitted radiation pressure
* ie (force infalling material) = (force emitted radation)

### AGN Jets

* thought to be emitted perpendicular to accretition disc


## Models of Active Galaxies

### Standard Model

* accreting supermassive Black Hole (the engine)
* surrounded by broadline region (BLR)
* within a torus of dust
* torus emitting infrared radation
* narrow line region outside torus

### Unified Models

* assume all AGNS differ in only two ways
* their luminosity
* the viewing angle

### Model 1: Unify radio-**quiet** AGNS

* Type 1 Seyferts and Type 2 Seyferts may be the same
* They may only differ in their viewing angle
* Radio quiet quasars (QSO's) are similar to Seyferts but
* Radio quiet quasars (QSO's) are much more powerful than Seyferts
* Good evidence for this model


### Model 2: Unify radio-**loud** AGNS

* AGN engine emits a pair of radio jets in these models

* As **viewing** **angle** of observer in relation to **jet** changes from
	* side-on: radio galaxy
	* mid-way: quasar
	* straight-on: blazar
* These models are controversial

### Angular momentum of Black Holes

* made by difference between radio-loud and radio-quiet AGN's
* faster spinning holes may have arisen from black hole mergers
* black hole mergers come from host galaxy collisions


## Outstanding issues

### Rotation studies

* some AGNs do contain compact suppermassive objects within them
* no direct evidence these objects are Black Holes though


### AGN Evolution

* most abundant at redshifts 2-3
* declining in number the past 10 billion years
* AGNs made fade with time as supply of accreting material used up
* AGNs may rejuvenate due to galactic collisions or mergers
* Supermassive Black Holes at centre of Milky Way & other galaxies may be extinct AGNS
