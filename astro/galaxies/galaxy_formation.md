Last week, this astrobite discussed the formation of spiral
galaxies similar to the Milky Way. 

The authors of the study concluded that spiral galaxies do
not initially form stars starting from the inside and
working outwards, but rather that the central region (the
bulge) forms in conjunction with the rest of the disk. 

In this Letter, Huang et al. 2013 compare local elliptical
galaxies (Figure 1) to their distant progenitors and
conclude that, unlike spirals, massive elliptical galaxies
do build up stars from the “inside-out”: first in a compact
core, then in the outer layers.
