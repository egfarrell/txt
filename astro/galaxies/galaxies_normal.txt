## Galaxy Morphology

Galaxies can be assigned to one of four classes:

* elliptical
	+ make up 60% of all galaxies
	+ have low angular momentum
	+ Range from E0 (circular) to E7(elongated)

* lenticular
	+ Have no spiral arms
	+ morphology half way between elliptical and spirals
	+ can be unbarred (S0)
	+ can be barred (SB0)

* spiral
	+ < 30% of all galaxies
	+ can be unbarred (Sa)
	+ can be barred (SBa)
	+ range from S*a* (tight arms, large bulge)
	+ to S*c* (looser arms, smaller bulge)

* irregular
	+ < 15% of all galaxies
	+ chaotic, asymmetric
	+ some show traces of a bar
	+ some show traces of spiral structure



## Physical Properties of Morphological Classes

Note: Largest normal galaxies are cD galaxies - giant ellipticals which may have been formed in mergers and are often close to galaxy cluster centres.

* elliptical
	+ made up of old Population II stars
	+ devoid of old gas and dust
	+ 3D shape difficult to discern
	+ some appear triaxial ellipsoids with little rotation

* lenticular
	+ half way between most flattened ellipticals and most tightly wound spirals
	+ clear signs of disc and central bulge
	+ Have no spiral arms
	+ little cold interstellar gas

* spiral
	+ have disc, central bulge, central bar
	+ arms can be more or less tightly wound
	+ Milky Way thought to be Sb or Sc, now: SBbc!



## Measurement of Physical Properties of Galaxies

* Surface brightness of galaxies varies from point to point
	* isophotes: lines passing through points of equal surface brightness
	* difficult to determine edge of galaxy
	* observations are confined to a region within some specified isophote

* how to determine luminosity & angular size of **distant** galaxies?
	* use observations from **nearby** galaxies!
	* use flux density within a given isophote as basis for comparison

* Galatic masses hard to measure
	* Method 1: rotation curves for spirals
	* Method 2: velocity dispersions for ellipticals
	* Method 3: X-ray halos for ellipticals
	* Method 4: velocity dispersions for galaxy **clusters**

* Galaxy cluster mass more than 10 timse greater than estimated mass
	* estimated mass measured via EM radiation
	* therefore must be substantial amounts of dark matter

* Stellar content of galaxies can be estimated through **population synthesis**
	* Observations at various wavelength can be used to establish importance of gas dust in galaxy


## Distance Scale

* many methods to determine distance:
	* geometrical methods
	* standard candles
	* Hubble law redshift

* Methods taken together form a cosmic distance ladder
	* most steps require calibration
	* distance steps depend on accurracy lower down ladder
	* Uncertainties increase as ladder is climbed

* distances to remote galaxies determined using Hubbles Law
	* Hubbles law relates distance to redshift
	* Calibration (ie determining Hubbles constant) has 10% error bar


## Formation and evolution of Galaxies

* Theory: Galaxies formed from gravitational instability at big bang
	* instability comes from expanding gas
	* gas made from baryonic and non-baryonic matter

* Galaxy formation can be studied using numerical techniques
	* these simulations extrapolate forward from initial conditions in universe
	* difficulty lies in incorporating effects of star formation

* Most likely scenario for galaxy formation: "bottom-up"
	* Cold Dark Matter played a vital role
	* Interactions and mergers also played a important role

* Interacting galaxies at present epoch may be sites of intense star formation

* Deep Surveys
	* Can study galaxy evolution by sampling large fraction of cosmic history
	* Reveal how distribution of different morphological galaxy types have changed with time
	* Give information of evolution of star-formation rate in Universe
