## Large Scale Distribution

### Gravitaional Clustering of Galaxies

* into **Groups** (~ 50 galaxies)
* into **Clusters** (50 - 1000 galaxies)
* into **SuperClusters** (> 1000 galaxies)

### Local Group

* Milky Way belongs to "Local Group"
* about 30 members
* dominated by Milky Way and Andromeda
* is at the outer edge of Local Supercluster
* Local Supercluster dominated by Virgo Cluster

### Super Clusters

* Medium scale 3D surveys show **SuperClusters**
* surveys are 3D because redshifts are combined with positions
* superclusters are loose collections of clusters about 30-50 Mpc big
* **not** virialised systems

### large-scale cosmic structure

* described using "counts-in-cells" analysis
* quantifies how **density fluctuations** vary with length scale
* allows comparisons between simulations and real galaxy distribution


## Galaxy Clusters

* Typical Cluster radius: **Abell** radius (2Mpc)
* Typical Cluster mass: 10<sup>14</sup> to 10<sup>15</sup> solar masses

### Methods to measure cluster mass

* Cluster mass estimated using three methods
* These methods typically agree within a factor of 2 or 3

	* **velocity** **dispersion**
		* used on virialised systems (gravitational bound, dynamical equilibrium)
		* use dispersion of line-of-sight velocities (∆v)
		* use M ~ R<sub>A</sub>(∆v)<sup>2</sup>/G

	* **X-ray emission**
		* rich clusters are strong x-ray emitters
		* this is due to hot intracluster gas
		* X-ray observations can estimate total cluster mass
		* also estimate mass of X-ray emitting gas

	* **gravitational** **lensing**
		* cluster acts as gravitational lens of distant galaxy
		* produces distored multiple images of disant galaxy
		* this can be a means of detecting distant objects
		* can also be used to estimate mass of intervening cluster

### Conclusion from cluster measurements

* All three methods suggest far more mass in clusters than individual galaxy masses
* Typically **galaxies** contribute ~ 10% cluster mass
* Typically intracluster **gas** contributes up to ~ 25%
* Conclusion: there must be **Dark Matter** to make up missing 70-90% of cluster mass


## Intracluster Medium (ICM)

* X-ray spectra show the hot ICM contains (unexpectedly) high metal content
* ie the X-ray spectra show enrichment of the ICM
* this enrichment is the result of supernova explosions
* supernovas occur in energetic young star-forming galaxies

### Cluster formation

* Temperature maps show many clusters not in hydrostatic equilibrium
* this suggests clusters may grow from smaller clusters merging


## Intergalactic Medium (IGM)

* Quasar spectra can contain multiple absorptions lines showing the presence of gas (netural Hydrogen) at different distances along the line of sight
* This is called the **Lyman forest**
* can be used to detect neutral gas in the intergalatic medium
* Gunn-Peterson effect
* Absence of Gunn-Peterson shows the density of any neutral intergalactic medium must be very small out to large redshifts
* Recently this effect is being used on (z > 6) quasars to look for the **epoch of reionisation**


## Dark Matter Distribution

* Dark matter distributed more **smoothly** throughout cluster than ordinary matter (galaxies)
* Ordinary matter galaxies **clump** into the middle of a cluster
* **Cosmic Shear**: Map large scale Dark Matter distribution by examining small distortions in background galaxies
