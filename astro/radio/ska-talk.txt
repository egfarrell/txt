- Radio telescopes great for watching what's happening with all
  the HYDROGEN in our universe

- doppler: most stuff is moving AWAY from us... (red-shifted).
  Reason: expansion of the galaxies

- the milky way looks very different when looking via the
  "hydrogen line" compared to the "optical line"

- Neutron stars about the size of Sydney, but the weight of the
  sun. Great for telling us what's happening with gravity in
  extreme conditions.

- need a balance of:
    - RESOLUTION:  
        - spread your radio telescope out (eg 500m^2 FAST
          telescope in China) 
        - or interferometer (smaller telescopes but spread out)
        - eg VLA  

    - SENSITIVITY:  
        - need high collecting area to get good sensitivity
        - ie to see things that are faint  


- What is SKA looking for?
    - processes from the big bang. But need a low frequency
      telescope because signals really red-shifted because it's
      so long ago.
    - looking for "gaps" in the epoch of reionisation and the
      "cosmic dawn" 
    - tricky: have to subtract everything "in front" of the epoch
      of reionisation .
    - magnetic fields: not understood very well
    - make huge galaxy map (billion galaxies)  
    - SETI: is science! Sensitive enough to pickup a mobile phone
      on a nearby star.
