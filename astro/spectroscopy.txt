### spectroscopy
    - Most astronomical spectrographs use diffraction gratings
      rather than prisms. Diffraction gratings are more
      efficient: prisms can absorb some of the light passing
      through them.

    - Every photon is precious when taking a spectrum from
      a faint source - astronomers don't want to waste them.
      A diffraction grating has thousands of narrow lines ruled
      onto a glass surface.

    - It _REFLECTS_ rather than REFRACTS light so NO PHOTONS ARE
      "LOST". The response from a grating is also linear whereas
      a prism disperses blue light more than the red part of the
      spectrum.



### Spectral sequence:
    - `O`   blue-white      32-50k      Ionised helium, neutral helium, weak hydrogen
    - `B`   blue-white      10-30k      Neutral helium, stronger hydrogen
    - `A`   white           7.2-9.5k    Stronger hydrogen, ionised calcium
    - `F`   yellow-white    6-7k        Ionised and neutral metals, weaker hydrogen
    - `G`   yellow          5.3-5.9k    Ionised calcium, neutral metals stronger hydrogen weaker
    - `K`   orange          4-5.2k      strong neutral metals, hydrogen weak
    - `M`   orange-red      2-3.9k      strong absorption bands titanium oxide, metallic lines
    - `L`   red,infra-red   1.5-2k      Metallic hydrides and alkali metals
    - `T`   infra-red       1k          methane bands
