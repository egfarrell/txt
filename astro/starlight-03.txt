### starlight 03

- We've learnt much about EM waves and photons but at the end of
  the day: "physics could not actually say what light was"

- Waves are dynamic things. By their very nature they're changing
  constantly with time.

- The first thing to do is remove the time element we can
  "freeze-frame" a wave to examine it.

- Rope:
	- high zones: crests
	- low zones: troughs

- wavelength `λ`:
	- distance between 2 crests or 2 troughs, or any 2 points of similar height

- frequency `ν`:
	- number of "wiggles" in one second

- After one wiggle
    - the wave pattern has moved along by one wavelength.
    - (one frequency cycle),


Velocity 	= distance * time
			= (distance between 2 crests) * (number of wiggles per second)
			= λ * ν

- For a given type of wave with fixed velocity, if the frequency
  increases the wavelength must decrease in order to keep the
  velocity constant. (to keep `c` constant?)

- So physicists wondered, what is meant by the amplitude of
  a light wave? It turns out the only thing that moves in
  a light-wave is the wave pattern itself.

### THOUGHT EXPERIMENT:
    - But there must be something CHANGING which we can measure.
      Lets freeze the light wave. Then walk along its line of
      travel with a compass.
    - What happens is remarkable: the compass needle would point
      in one direction, at right angles to the light-wave line of
      travel.
    - Then a bit further the needle would flip around and point
      in the opposite direction, then flip again, and again...

    - Now figure out how strong the magnetic field is (use
      a magnetometer): walk along the light-wave line of travel
      and measure magnetic field strength at each point. Then
      plot all data on a graph. A beautiful wave shape is
      revealed.

    - It is the strength and direction of the magnetic field that
      varies as the wave moves along it.

    - At any given point on the wave, the magnetic field strength
      increases from zero to a maximum value, decreases to zero
      again, then increases again but points in the opposite
      direction.

    - So the amplitude of a wave is not measured by a physical
      distance, but by the maximum STRENGTHS of the electric and
      magnetic fields themselves

    - Standard diagram of a frozen light wave would be messy if
      used to portray a wave that spreads outwards in all
      directions from a point.

    - In the EM wave theory of light, the amplitude of the light
      wave (ie maximum strength of electric and magnetic field)
      determine how bright the light is and the wavelength
      (frequency) determines the color.

    - An important feature of a rainbow and prism spectrum is
      there are NO noticeable gaps between the colors visible.

    - No gap between orange and yellow, or between blue and
      green. But actually when the spectra of the sun and most
      stars are studied in more detail, there were in fact colors
      missing.
