- oxford astronomer Aris Karastergiou
    - lots of people do research using the rotational stability
      of pulsars as "clocks"

    - DATA CHALLENGES:
        - pulsar radiation gets DELAYED DIFFERENTIALLY as it
          travels through space, depending on it's wavelength
        - short wavelengths arrive at the telescopes earlier,
          long wavelengths arrive later
        - if you want to add together the signal across the whole
          observing band (across all the wavelength you're
          sensitve to) you need to account for the differential
          delay
        - DE-DISPERSION
            - tricky to do de-dispersion on a signal you are
              searching for... you don't know a-priori what the
              paraemeters of the signal are
            - and you want to take advantage of the periodic
              nature of pulsar pulses... but you don't know the
              period of the new pulsar you're searching for!
            - the datasets are several gigabits per second
            - and there are 1 million operations per sample(!)

    - QUESTION: does a pulse profile change?
    - stack hundreds of profiles which corresponds to decades
    - build time series that corresponds to certain parts of the
      profile
    - use "Gaussian Process Regression" (Bayesian) : make some
      assumptions about the shape of your data and modelling them
      in a way that is non-parametric
        - ie you don't impose laws on how each of these time
          series behaves a-priori
        - want to obtain models that are smooth over the time
          series
        - so use Python libraries and build our own tools too...

    - ADVICE:
        - ask how does your data relate to the scientific
          questions you're asking.
        - He remembers PHD where he was looking at endless
          interesting profile patterns from single pulses...
        - some people find data and signals fascinating, but that
          doesn't lead anywhere scientifically
        - think how your modelling can be taken all the way to
          physical law
