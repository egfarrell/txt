The _COLOR INDEX_ is a simple numerical expression that
determines the color of an object, which in the case of
a star gives its temperature. (color => temperature)

To measure the index, one observes the magnitude of an
object successively through two different filters, such as
U and B, or B and V, where:

    * `U` is sensitive to ultraviolet rays,
    * `B` is sensitive to blue light,
    * `V` is sensitive to visible (green-yellow) light

The set of PASSBANDS or FILTERS is called a photometric
system. The difference in magnitudes found with these
filters is called the _U-B_ or _B-V_ color index.

* The _smaller_ the color index, the more _blue_ (or hotter)
  the object is.
* Conversely, the _larger_ the color index, the more _red_
  (or cooler) the object is.

This is a consequence of the logarithmic magnitude scale, in
which brighter objects have smaller (more negative)
magnitudes than dimmer ones.

For comparison:
    * the yellowish Sun has a B-V index of `0.656 ± 0.005`,
    * while the bluish Rigel has B-V `-0.03`. 
        - Rigels B magnitude is 0.09 and its V magnitude is 0.12,
          `B-V = -0.03`

- Color indices of distant objects are usually affected by
  interstellar extinction i.e. they are redder than those of
  closer stars. 
- The amount of reddening is characterized by _color excess_,
  defined as the difference between the Observed color index and
  the Normal color index (or Intrinsic color index), the
  hypothetical true color index of the star, unaffected by
  extinction.

- For example, in the UBV photometric system we can write for the
  B-V color:

`Excess (B − V) = (B − V)Observed − (B − V)Intrinsic`

- The passbands most optical astronomers use are the UBVRI
  filters, where the U, B, and V filters are as mentioned above,
  and the:
    - "R" filter passes red light, 
    - "I" filter passes infrared light.

- This system of filters is sometimes called the
  _Johnson-Cousins_ filter system. These filters were specified
  as particular combinations of glass filters and photomultiplier
  tubes.

- For precision, appropriate pairs of filters are chosen
  depending on the object's color temperature:

* B-V for mid-range objects
* U-V for hotter objects
* R-I for cool ones


# Color Index

In astronomy, the COLOR INDEX is a simple numerical expression that
determines the color of an object, which in the case of a star gives its
temperature.

## Measure the index

To measure the index, one observes the magnitude of an object
successively through two different filters, such as U and B, or B and V,
where

-   U is sensitive to ultraviolet rays,
-   B is sensitive to blue light
-   V is sensitive to visible (green-yellow) light

## Passbands/Filters

The set of passbands or filters is called a photometric system. The
difference in magnitudes found with these filters is called the U-B or
B-V color index, respectively.

-   The smaller the color index, the more blue (or hotter) the object
    is.
-   The larger the color index, the more red (or cooler) the object is.

This is a consequence of the logarithmic magnitude scale, in which
brighter objects have smaller (more neagtive) magnitudes than dimmer
ones.

## Examples

For comparison

-   the yellowish Sun has a B-V index of 0.656±0.005
-   the bluish Rigel has B-V -0.03 (its B magnitude is 0.09 and its V
    magnitude is 0.12, B-V=-0.03).

Color indices of distant objects are usually affected by interstellar
extinction i.e. they are redder than those of closer stars. The amount
of reddening is characterized by color excess, defined as the difference
between the Observed color index and the Normal color index (or

unaffected by extinction.

For example, in the UBV photometric system we can write it for the B-V
color

-   EB − V = (B − V)Observed − (B − V)Intrinsic

## Johnson-Cousins System

The passbands most optical astronomers use are the UBVRI filters, where
the U, B, and V filters are as mentioned above, the R filter passes red
light, and the I filter passes infrared light. This system of filters is
sometimes called the Johnson-Cousins filter system. These filters were
specified as particular combinations of glass filters and
