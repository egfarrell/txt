- Ah, yes — "bag of gold" states. A whimsical name for a deep
  problem in BH physics.

- The phrase “bag of gold” refers to a type of spacetime geometry
  where the outside of the black hole looks completely ordinary
  — same mass, same horizon area, same asymptotic behavior — but
  the inside hides a vast region of space, like a balloon
  attached through the black hole throat.
    - From the outside, the black hole seems small, with horizon
      area A, but inside, there’s a huge spatial volume that
      could hold an enormous number of internal degrees of
      freedom.

- This idea goes back to John Wheeler, who coined “bag of gold”
  in the 1960s to describe configurations where you take
  a Schwarzschild black hole and glue to it a large closed FRW
  universe behind the horizon. The image is: a narrow throat (the
  horizon) leading to a big interior — a bag full of “gold,”
  i.e., entropy or information.

- Now, in the context of the black hole information problem,
  these states create trouble. According to Bekenstein–Hawking,
  the entropy of a black hole is S = A / (4 * G * hbar). That’s
  proportional to the *area* of the horizon, not its *volume*.
  But if you imagine that you can fill a huge “bag of gold”
  interior with arbitrary matter configurations, the number of
  possible internal states would seem to grow exponentially with
  the interior *volume*. That would mean far more states than the
  area law allows — an apparent contradiction.

- So theorists ask: *Are these “bag of gold” states real physical
  states, or just mathematical curiosities that can’t be formed
  by any realistic physical process?*

- That’s what the exchange you quoted is about. The speaker is
  arguing that if no physical process starting from reasonable
  initial data (like collapse of ordinary matter) can create
  these “bag of gold” geometries, then it’s not arbitrary or “ad
  hoc” to leave them out of the theory. They simply aren’t in the
  physically accessible part of Hilbert space.

- In other words:

  - “Bag of gold” states are hypothetical black hole interiors
    with large hidden volumes.

  - They seem to violate the area–entropy relation.

  - Whether they *exist physically* depends on whether they can
    be dynamically formed — and that’s still an open question,
    debated in contexts like AdS/CFT and quantum gravity.

- If you like poetic phrasing: from the outside, the black hole
  looks like a coin; from the inside, it could be a cathedral.
  The puzzle is whether the universe ever mints such coins.
