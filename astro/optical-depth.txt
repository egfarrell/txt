### Optical Depth (Parsons Astrobaki)
    - $MY_VIDEOS/Astro/Parsons-Astrobaki/Optical-Depth-Seeing-Through-the-Cloud.mp4

    - useful concept for how intensity varies as it passes
      through a medium

    - `τ >> 1`:
        - optically thick
        - lose background information
        - the specific intesity we observe asymptotes to the
          source function

    - `τ << 1`:
        - optically thin
        - retain background information
        - and the source function only changes that linearly with
          optical depth

    - we can _factor_ optical depth in lots of ways:

    - EXTINCTION COEFFICIENT `α_ν`
        - assume extinction is constant
        - then optical depth is extinction * distance travelled
        - `τ_ν = α_ν • s`
        - extinction `α_ν` has units of inverse distance

    - NUMBER DENSITY
        - `τ_ν = n • σ_ν • s`
        - `n` = number density of dust particles in the way
        -     = counts / cm^3
        - `σ_ν` = cross section area of each dust particle
        - $MY_IMAGES/astro/extinction-coeff-01.jpg

    - COLUMN DENSITY
        - what fraction of your image is covered up by these
          particles?
        - actually we don't care about the number density of the
          particles... we care about how many there are
          _integrated_ over this distance
        - so we switch to the "column density", which is the
          number density integrated over `s`
        - it measures fraction that is covered up as you
          look through this cloud...
        - units 1 / area
        - $MY_IMAGES/astro/extinction-coeff-02.jpg

    - DENSITY
        - `τ` = density * opacity * distance
        - `τ` = g/cm^3  * ??      * cm
