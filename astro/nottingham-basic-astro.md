# Basic Astronomy

    1. Why sky is dark at night? Key words: Olber's paradox

    2. Outline historical mystery behind gamma-ray bursts
       and latest paradigm for their origin

    3. Understand how extra-solar planets are detected,
       discuss prospects for finding earth-like planets.

    4. Understand what is meant by a luminosity function

    - Telescopes and observatories - A bewildering array of
      astronomical observatories exist. Be familiar with: VLT,
      Gemini, WHT, UKIRT, JCMT, CFHT, GAIA, Chandra, XMM-Newton,
      Herschel, Planck, Akari, ALMA, JWST, ELT, SKA, WMAP


# Astronomical Observing

    1. Explain the Vega and AB magnitude systems

    2. Understand difference between apparent and absolute
       magnitudes

    3. Understand what is meant by a U-band magnitude (or
       B,V,R,I,Z,J,H,K...)

    4. explain celestial coordinate system (RA, Dec)
       & estimate RA/Dec of Sun at any time of year.

    5. From RA and Dec of a target, estimate whether visible
       from particular telescope at any time of year.


# Structure Formation

    1. Be able to outline basic hierarchical scenario for
       growth of structure in Universe (in simple terms; no
       equations)

    2. Have some familiarity with numerical and theoretical
       approaches to model galaxy and structure formation
       Key words: N-body simulations, semi-analytic models,
       SPH, hydrodynamic models.

    3. Be able to discuss current major problems in our
       understanding of process of galaxy formation
       Keywords: cooling catastrophe, feedback, IMF

    4. Be able to describe and understand Lyman-alpha forest

    5. Understand what is meant by epoch of reionisation 
        - Key words: dark ages, Gunn-Petersen trough



# Stars

    1. Be able to outline how we think a star is formed Key
       words: gravitational collapse, cooling, angular
       momentum

    2. Be able to sketch a luminosity/temperature diagram for
       stars and use it to describe and briefly explain main
       stages of stellar evolution Key words:
       Hertzsprung-Russell, main sequence, giant-branch, AGB
       stars, white dwarfs

    3. Be familiar with 'OBAFGKM' stellar classification system

    4. Be able to discuss various endpoints of stellar evolution
       and evolutionary paths taken. Key: planetary nebulae,
       white dwarfs, supernovae, neutron stars, black holes

    5. Without resorting to equations, briefly explain
       Chandrasekhar mass. Key words: Fermi energy, relativistic,
       degenerate

    6. Be able to discuss key evidence for existence of
       neutron stars and galactic black holes.

# emission scattering

    1. Outline physics behind following radiation mechanisms. You
       should be able to describe an astrophysical phenomenon
       that produces such radiation:
        - Blackbody radiation
        - Synchrotron
        - Bremsstrahlung
        - Line emission
        - Compton scattering

    2. Explain difference between thermal and non-thermal
       radiation processes

    3. What are forbidden/permitted line transitions?

    4. What causes hydrogen 21cm emission?

    5. Be able to explain why sky is blue.
        - Key words: Rayleigh scattering


# Galaxy Clusters

    1. Have an appreciation for size and mass of a typical
       galaxy cluster, and types of galaxy most prevalent in
       these systems. Key words: morphology-density
       relation, Mpc

    2. Describe relative contributions of stars, gas and dark
       matter to mass of a galaxy cluster.

    3. Understand what is meant by a "gravitationally bound"
       system

    4. Be able to outline at least three independent methods
       for measuring total mass of a galaxy cluster.

    5. Be able to describe Sunyaev-Zel'dovich effect in
       general terms, and understand what this can be used
       for.


# Galaxies Quasars

    1. Appreciate typical masses, sizes and separation of
       stars and galaxies in Universe

    2. Outline key differences between spiral, elliptical and S0
       galaxies.

    3. Outline differences between UV -> IR spectrum of old
       stellar population compared to young star-forming
       galaxy. Key: Balmer break, 4000 Angstrom break,
       emission & absorption lines, OB stars

    4. Understand Lyman-break technique for finding distant
       galaxies. Key: Lyman-limit, drop-out

    5. Be familiar with Tully-Fisher relation, Faber-Jackson
       relation and Fundamental Plane.

    6. outline what is meant by an AGN or quasar and discuss
       characteristics of these objects Key words:
       continuum, broad/narrow emission lines, X-ray
       emission, variability

    7. Describe observed differences between Type 1 and Type
       2 AGN, and outline current explanation

    8. Be able to discuss evidence for existence of
       supermassive black holes.


# Cosmology

    1. Discuss evidence for Big Bang model. (Hubble law,
       Microwave bg, elemental abundances)

    2. Be able to outline evidence for existence of dark
       matter Key: rotation curves, velocity dispersion,
       galaxy clusters, Virial Theorem, large-scale
       structure, MOND

    3. Understand meaning of following cosmological
       parameters, and outline how they can be measured
       observationally.
        - `Omega`
        - `H_o`
        - `Lambda`
        - `sigma_8`

    4. Have understanding of extragalactic distance scale,
       and how we use it to measure distance to galaxies in
       Universe. Key: Parallax, Cepheid variables, redshift

    5. cosmological inflation. (vacuum energy, phase
       transition, flatness, horizon)

    6. Have understanding of how anisotropies measured by
       CMB power spectrum can be used to derive cosmological
       parameters. Key: Surface of last scattering, acoustic
       oscillcations, COBE, WMAP

    7. Understand differences between various distance
       measures in cosmology, including
        - luminosity distance,
        - angular diameter distance
        - light-travel distance.
