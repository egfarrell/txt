- And I say, as in the famous joke, you are "both right" 

- In the 2 best known 1D models the stability properties
  are opposite to each other: 
    - in 'Shakura Sunyaev' (no advection) the radiation
      pressure dominated disk is violently unstable, 
    - while the slim disk model (primarily differs from the
      SS disk by including advection) is stable.

- What is the situation in the (GR)MHD?

- The simulations take into account motion of matter and
  diffusion of radiation, so advection is automatically included:
    - if the radiation does not have enough time to escape
      locally it is carried in by the fluid flow. 
    - And still the radiation pressure dominated THIN disks are
      thermally unstable, as Bhupendra found.

- However, David has perfectly stable radiation pressure
  dominated MRI disks (with low values of magnetic pressure) at
  2 or 3 Eddington mass accretion rates. But these are not
  geometrically thin (nor thick either, really).

- At sub-Eddington rates (but still a good fraction, say > 0.1)
  the disk close to the BH is thermally unstable, unless it is
  magnetically dominated (as shown by Olek Sądowski). 
- And the _magnetically dominated_ one is stable, as first shown
  numerically by Olek, and then by Bhupendra et al (elevated
  disks) and Debora with David & Maciek et al (puffy disk).

- So why do the numerical simulations differ from analytic models
  with advection? 
- Because the _slim disk model_ does not correspond to reality,
  I think, whether in the the world of simulations or the
  non-virtual world. 
- It is essentially a 1D model, and there is no way it could
  capture the back flow pattern, for instance, which was so
  nicely presented in Ruchi's talk. 
- Of course, the simulations are intrinsically 2D (if
  axisymmetric), and even 3D (as in Bhupendra's elevated disk
  simulations).
