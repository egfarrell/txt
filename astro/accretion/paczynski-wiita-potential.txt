### Wlodek Talk
    - 2014 | Klużniak
    - Exploring GR effects in Newtonian physics
    - https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014pas..conf...21K

    - Stationary toroidal configurations1 of non-gravitating
      fluid in axisymmetric GR metrics were presented in a paper
      by Paczyński’s collaborators, Abramowicz et al. (1978).
    - It allowed to model accretion flow through the inner edge
      of the disk, a so-called _Sikora’s beak_, in a manner
      analogous to Roche-lobe overflow in binary systems.
    - However, the only feasible approach to modelling the inner
      structure of a thin accretion disk was through a numerical
      solution of the equations of (viscous Shakura & Sunyaev,
      1973, alpha disk) motion.
    - The problem to be solved was how to reconcile Newtonian
      gravity with the presence of the marginally stable orbit.
    - This was accomplished brilliantly by considering circular
      orbits in a spherical pseudo-potential (Paczyński & Wiita,
      1980) GM V (r)
        -              GM
        -  V (r) = −  -------  (1)
        -              r − rG

      with rG = 2GM/c^2 the Schwarzschild radius.

      - This led to the appearance, in a purely Newtonian
        calculation, of a marginally stable orbit (at the correct
        radius! `r_ms = 3r_G`).
      - It was then possible to show that radial flow could be
        accounted for (Paczyński & Bisnovatyi-Kogan, 1981), while
        the correct inner boundary condition is that of transonic
        flow (Muchotrzeb & Paczyński, 1982).

    - And thus opened the floodgates of accretion disk research.
      It is worth noting that the pseudo-potential given in eq.
      (1), and its variants , are used even today ...
    - Of course, _ray-bending_ cannot be modelled in this way, so
      for this and other reasons photon spectra obtained in such
      calculations have to be taken with a grain of salt.

    - Interestingly, Kepler’s law `P^2 ∝ R^3` has an identical form
      in Newton’s 1/r2 gravity and in the Schwarzschild metric,
      the Keplerian frequency being `Ω_k (r) = GM/r^3`

    - Related to this, and to the instability of orbits for all
      `r < r_ms` , is the difference in angular momentum: 
        - in the Newtonian `1/r` potential `l^2_k = GM r` is
          increasing with `r`, thus satisfying Rayleigh’s
          criterion for stability, 
        - while in the Schwarzschild case `l^2_k  = GM r/(1 − rG
          /r)2` has a minimum at `r = r_ms` and its radial
          derivative is negative for `r < r_ms` .

### Cambridge notes
    - The combination of linear momentum, angular momentum, and
      energy conservation in our system has led to a dramatic
      simplification: 
        - a system of two particles in `3D` space has been
          reduced to a problem of finding the motion of one
          particle travelling in `1D`.
    - Notice that while 2 particles moving in 3D involves 12
      pieces of information (the position and velocity of both
      particles), our current simplified problem only involves
      two: 
        - the position and velocity of a 1D particle.
    - Thus, we have 10 fewer pieces of information to find.
    - This dramatic simplification, along with the incredible
      ubiquity of the central potential, makes the two-body
      problem one of the most important problems in Physics.

### Effective Potential (Stack Physics)
    - https://physics.stackexchange.com/questions/193105/what-is-an-effective-potential-in-classical-mechanics

    - It isnt necessary to introduce the effective potential in
      orbital mechanics but its really useful.

    - Say we have a particle moving in a central gravitational
      potential. Newton's laws give you a vector equation of
      motion `mx⃗ ¨=−∇U` where `U = −GMm/r`

    - In a general coordinate system this is a complicated set of
      three coupled differential equations.

    - We want to simplify and decouple these equations as much as
      possible. So we work in spherical coordinates. I'll leave
      the derivation of the angular parts of the equation of
      motion to the textbook, let's focus on the radial part. The
      left hand side of Newton's law becomes

    - So now it's a matter of interpretation:-we can now think of
      `r` as the coordinate of a particle living in one
      dimension. We have effectively add a term to newton's law
      for `r` that has no derivatives on it. 
        - Why not call that a potential? In other words, why not
          rearrange the above equation so that it looks more like
          a simple 1D mechanics problem

    - It is an extremely useful picture, because we all know how
      a particle moves in a potential!
    - Given an angular momentum, you can plot the potential and
      immediately see where the STABLE circular orbits are (the
      MINIMA OF THE POTENTIAL).

    - You can also qualitatively see how there is a barrier to
      approaching the object too closely (which makes sense--if
      you have angular momentum you wouldn't expect a head on
      collision)

    - This is pretty non-trivial: you have computed a 2D problem
      (finding a circular orbit, or even oscillations around that
      orbit). In other words, the problem was much simpler than
      it originally appeared (you didn't have to solve three
      arbitrary coupled differential equations, just one simple
      one with a potential), and we take advantage of this by
      using an effective potential. This kind of trick shows up
      all over the place in physics.

### Wikkipedia Definition
    - Paper: "Thick accretion disks and supercritical luminosities"
    - References: $HOME/repos/marks/journal-refs/paczynski-wiita-refs.txt

    - The Paczyński–Wiita potential is an approximation of the
      gravitational potential around a _non-rotating_ black hole.
    - Done by Bohdan Paczyński & Paul Wiita in 1980, one of the
      most-cited from the first 40 years of A&A

    - The potential is:
        - $MY_IMAGES/astro/bh-accretion/paczynski-Wiita-potential.jpg

    - where
        - `r`   = radial distance from the black hole
        - `G`   = gravitational constant
        - `M`   = black hole mass
        - `r_g` = `2 G M / c^2` Schwarzschild radius.
        - `c`   =  speed of light

    - The potential exactly reproduces the locations of the
      innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO) and the marginally
      bound orbit.

    - It also exactly reproduces the form of the angular momentum
      and accurately approximates the Keplerian angular velocity
      and epicyclic frequency.

    - Because the Paczyński–Wiita potential reproduces these GR
      effects and is easy to calculate, its widely used in
      numerical simulations of black hole accretion.


### Abramowicz: The Paczynski-Wiita potential. A step-by-step "derivation"
    - $MY_ZOTERO/2009/abramowicz_2009_the_paczyński-wiita_potential.pdf 
    - This potential CANNOT be used for rotation BH's (doesn't
      handle Lense-Thirring effect)
    - Cannot be used for self-gravitating fluids (`∇^2 ≠ 0`)
    - The Paczynski-Wiita potential accurately models GR effects
      in the Newtonian theory that determines the motion of
      matter near a nonrotating black hole.

    - Interesting
        - This potential is NEITHER an approximation of
          relativistic gravity  nor a fitting formula.
        - Instead,  its a unique (“pseudo”)Newtonian model of the
          gravity of a nonrotating black hole.

    - Key formulas:
        - $MY_IMAGES/astro/bh-accretion/Derive-paczynksi-Wiita.jpg

    - It reproduces exactly:
        - `r_ms = ISCO`: location of the marginally stable orbit
        - `r_mb` the location of the marginally bound orbit
        - `L(r)`: the form of the Keplerian angular momentum

    - It  also  reproduces accurately, but not exactly:
        - the form of the Keplerian  angular  velocity `Ω(r)`
        - and of the radial epicyclic frequency `κ(r)`

    - Velocities of matter calculated with Paczynski-Wiita could
      exceed the light speed.
        - This creates a serious problem when one calculates the
          observed appearance of matter (e.g.spectra) by the
          method of ray tracing.
