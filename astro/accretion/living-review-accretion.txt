### 03 Event Horizon etc
    - 04 Ergosphere
    - 05 Marginally Stable Orbit (ISCO)

    Definitions:
    - EVENT HORIZON: 
        - radius inside of which escape from the black hole is
          not possible;
    - ERGOSPHERE: 
        - radius inside of which negative energy states are
          possible (giving rise to the potentiality of tapping
          the energy of the black hole)
    - MARGINALLY STABLE ORBIT (ISCO):
        - radius inside of which free circular orbital motion is
          not possible;


    - Our main question is: Could accretion theory unambiguously
      prove the existence of the event horizon, ergosphere, and
      ISCO using current or future observations?

    - In realistic astrophysical situations with BH's (esp.
      quasars and microquasars), the BH itself is uncharged, and
      the gravity of the accretion disk is almost negligible. 
    - This means the spacetime metric `g_μν` is given by the Kerr
      metric, determined by 2 parameters: 
        - total mass `M*`
        - total angular momentum `J*`

### Two different standards of “rotational rest”: 
    - Zero Angular Velocity Observer (ZAVO)  (`Ω = 0`)
    - Zero Angular MOMENTUM Observer (ZAMO), (`ℓ = 0`)
        - The 2 frames rotate wrt each other with the
          frame-dragging angular velocity `ω`

    - ions
        - temperature `Ti` and molecular weight `μi` of ions 
    - electrons
        - temperature `Te` and molecular weight `μe`


- As mentioned in Section 1, the viscosity in astrophysical
  accretion disks cannot come from ordinary molecular viscosity,
  as it is orders of magnitude _too weak_ to explain
  observations. 
- Instead, the source of stresses in the disk is likely
  turbulence driven by the magneto-rotational instability (MRI,
  described in Section 8.2).



### Shakura-Sunyaev “alpha viscosity”
    - ... the Shakura-Sunyaev “alpha viscosity” prescription
      still finds application today. It is an ad hoc assumption
      based on dimensional arguments. Shakura and Sunyaev
      realized that if the source of viscosity in accretion disks
      is turbulence, then the kinematic viscosity coefficient
      `ν_*` has the form, `ν_* = l_0 v_0`


- In most BH accretion disks, it's reasonable to assume ideal
  MHD, whereby the conductivity is infinite, and consequently the
  magnetic diffusivity is zero. 
- Whenever this is true, magnetic field lines are effectively
  frozen into the fluid. 
- A corollary to this is that parcels of fluid are restricted to
  moving along field lines, like “beads” on a wire. In ideal MHD,
  the Faraday tensor obeys the homogeneous Maxwell’s equation
