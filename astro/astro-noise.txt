
### 'Stacks' and 'subs'
    - I'm a physicist in my daytime work and have been recently
      working on denoising DL networks for other applications.
    - Those tools are very powerful and tend to outperform
      regular denoising strategies. Good reference for regular
      photography:
        - https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.03981.pdf

    - Regular CNN and U-NET normally run near real-time on customer GPU.

    - Now astro images are different in that we want to preserve
      very bright stars, so I see a need to retrain these
      networks on proper astrophotography images. I see 2 ways to
      achieve this:
        - Get high SNR images post stack and model camera noise
          (with proper noise statistics).
        - Directly compare a single sub to the final stack (with
          both images aligned)

    ### Explanation:

    1. "Post stack": In astrophotography, a "stack" refers to the
       process of combining multiple individual images (or
       "subs"—short for sub-exposures) of the same object in the
       sky. This stacking process enhances the signal-to-noise
       ratio (SNR) and brings out faint details that would be
       lost in a single image. "Post stack" refers to the final
       image that has been produced after stacking these multiple
       subs. So, in this case, "post stack" means working with
       the higher-quality, stacked image that results from this
       process.

    2. "Directly compare a single sub to the final stack": This
       refers to comparing one of the raw sub-exposures (noisy
       and low SNR because of short exposure times) to the final
       stacked image. By aligning the two, you can assess how the
       noise behaves in the individual sub-image and how much
       improvement the stacking process provides. This comparison
       could also be useful for training deep learning models, as
       it would allow the network to learn how noise is reduced
       and details are enhanced through stacking.

    ### In summary:
        - "Post stack" means after the process of stacking
          images.
        - "Directly comparing a single sub to the final stack" is
          about evaluating the difference between an individual
          noisy image and the final, cleaner image after
          stacking.
