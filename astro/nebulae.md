nebulae

Old Catalogs once lumped all
the following together as "Nebulae" :

### Diffuse Nebulae
    - Often referred to as "nebulae", which consist of regions of
      dust or gas in the ISM illuminated by the light from
      a nearby star (or more typically, several stars).
    - Or:
    - Gas in the cloud is glowing by re-emitting the energy
      received from several nearby hot young stars. Example:
      Orion nebula

### Planetary nebulae (ejection nebulae)
    - Thin shells of gas surrounding a single star. 
    - The gas was blown off from a (geriatric) star and is
      expanding away from it at a high velocity, illuminated by
      light from the star. 
    - Early astronomers saw a round compact green object at the
      centre of planetary nebulae and it reminded them of Uranus

### Star Clusters
    - Many individual stars bound by gravity. 
    - _Open Clusters_: small loose groups of stars barely held
      together by gravitation. 
    - _Globular clusters_: larger groups of up to a million bound
      stars in a tightly packed spherical region.

### Galaxies
    - Those external to our Milky Way were first identified by
      their nebulous, nonstarry appearance.
    - Galaxies: 100 million to 10,000 billion stars.
    - Galaxies may also contain all the above types of nebulae.
