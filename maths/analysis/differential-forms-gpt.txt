### Step 1: What problem are differential forms trying to solve?

You already know calculus handles:

Functions 
f(x)
f(x) → give a number at a point.

Derivatives → rate of change.

Integrals → accumulate along intervals, areas, volumes.

But when you move into higher dimensions (multivariable calc, physics), you need to:

Integrate along curves (work done by a force field),

Over surfaces (flux of a field through a surface),

Or over volumes (mass, charge density, etc.).

And — crucially — you want these integrals to be coordinate-independent. That’s where differential forms come in:

- A differential form is a gadget that packages a function and
  some orientation/geometry together so you can integrate it over
  curves, surfaces, or volumes — without worrying about
  coordinates.
