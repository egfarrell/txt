### Difference [Manifold] versus [Vector Space]
    - $MY_IMAGES/maths/differential-forms/fortney/manifold-versus-vec-space.jpg
    - `ℝ^n` manifold: collection of points
    - `ℝ^n` vector space: collection of vectors
    - The manifold `ℝ^2` and the vector space `ℝ^2` look exactly
      alike yet are totally different spaces.
    - Why make this comparison? 
        - First, keeping clear track of our spaces will help us
          understand the theory of differential forms better. 
        - Second, when we eventually get to more general
          manifolds, most manifolds will NOT have a vector space
          structure so we don't want to get into the bad habit of
          thinking of our manifolds as vector spaces too.
        - We will use different name for “functions” on the
          manifold `ℝ^n` and “functions” on the vector space
          `ℝ^n` . 
            - Functions on the manifold `ℝ^n` will simply be
              called functions, or sometimes, if we want to
              emphasize that range is the set of real numbers,
              real-valued functions. 
            - However, our “functions” on the vector space `ℝ^n`
              are called functionals when the range is the set of
              real values and transformations when the range is
              another vector space. We have already spent some

### Coordinate functions
    - x(p) = x((3, 5)) = 3
    - y(p) = y((3, 5)) = 5
    - Soeven though you are used to thinking of `(3, 5)` as
      a point, a point is actually more abstract than that.
      A point exists independent of its coordinates. 
    - For example, you will sometimes see something like this,
      p = �x(p), y(p)� where, as an example, for the point we
      were given above we have x(p) = 3 and y(p) = 5. There is
      some ambiguity that we need to get used to here. Often
      x and y are used to represent the outputs of the coordinate
      functions x and y. So, we could end up with an ambiguous
      statement like this x(p) = x y(p) = y where the x and the
      y on the left hand sides of the equations are coordinate
      functions from R2 to R, and the x and y on the right hand
      side are real numbers, that is, elements of R.


### Tangent Spaces
    - The tangent space to a manifold at the point `p` is
      basically the set of all lines that are _tangent to smooth
      curves_ that go through the manifold at point `p`.
        - AH THIS IS BIG!
        - You have curves that go through the point p, then you
          have tangent lines to those curves. So the "tangent
          space" is those lines tangent to the curves... I always
          thought the tangent space was just any line touching
          the point itself (didn't appreciate the idea of the
          curves)
