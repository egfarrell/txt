### What Are Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues?

- _Eigenvectors_ and _eigenvalues_ are concepts in linear algebra
  that describe special properties of matrices.

- Eigenvector: An eigenvector of a matrix `A` is a non-zero
  vector `v` such that when `A` multiplies `v`, the result is
  a scalar multiple of `v`. 
    - In other words, A * v = λ * v, where λ (lambda) is a scalar
      known as the *eigenvalue* corresponding to v.

- Eigenvalue: The eigenvalue `λ` is a scalar that indicates how
  the eigenvector is scaled during the transformation described
  by the matrix `A`.

### Intuitive Explanation

- Imagine you have a transformation represented by a matrix `A`.
  Applying this transformation to a vector usually changes its
  direction and magnitude. 
- However, for certain special vectors (eigenvectors), the
  transformation only stretches or shrinks the vector but does
  not change its direction. The factor by which the vector is
  stretched or shrunk is the eigenvalue.

### Why They Occur Naturally in Linear Algebra

1. Diagonalization: Many matrices can be decomposed into simpler
   components involving their eigenvectors and eigenvalues. This
   process, called diagonalization, simplifies matrix operations
   and is crucial for solving systems of linear equations,
   computing matrix powers, and more.

2. Stability and Systems: In dynamic systems (like those
   described by differential equations), eigenvectors and
   eigenvalues determine the stability and behavior of the system
   over time. For instance, they help identify stable and
   unstable equilibrium points.

3. Principal Components: In data analysis, eigenvectors and
   eigenvalues are used in techniques like Principal Component
   Analysis (PCA) to identify the most significant features or
   directions in a dataset, aiding in dimensionality reduction
   and pattern recognition.

4. Natural Coordinates: Eigenvectors provide a natural coordinate
   system for the transformation described by a matrix. This
   coordinate system often makes complex transformations easier
   to understand and analyze.

### Example

Consider a matrix A:

A = [[3, 1], [0, 2]]

Let's find its eigenvectors and eigenvalues.

1. Eigenvalue Calculation:
   - Solve the characteristic equation det(A - λI) = 0:
   - det([[3 - λ, 1], [0, 2 - λ]]) = 0
   - This simplifies to (3 - λ)(2 - λ) = 0, giving λ = 3 and λ = 2.

2. Eigenvector Calculation:
   - For λ = 3:
     - A - 3I = [[0, 1], [0, -1]]
     - Solve [[0, 1], [0, -1]] * [[x], [y]] = [[0], [0]]
     - This gives y = 0, so x can be any value. An eigenvector is [[1], [0]].

   - For λ = 2:
     - A - 2I = [[1, 1], [0, 0]]
     - Solve [[1, 1], [0, 0]] * [[x], [y]] = [[0], [0]]
     - This gives x + y = 0, so y = -x. An eigenvector is [[1], [-1]].

- In summary, eigenvectors and eigenvalues naturally arise in
  linear algebra because they provide a powerful way to
  understand and simplify the transformations represented by
  matrices.
