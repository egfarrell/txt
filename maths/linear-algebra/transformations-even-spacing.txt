### Aside: formal definition of Linear transformation
    - First, the formal properties:
        1. Additivity
            - (Preserves Vector Addition)
            - `L(v + w) = L(v) + L(w)`
        2. Homogeneity 
            - ("same-kind" scaling behavior)
            - (preserves the uniformity or structure of scalar
              multiplication across the transformation)
            - `L(c * v) = c * L(v)`


### Step 1: Understanding Evenly Spaced Dots in Input Space

    - Imagine a row of evenly spaced dots along a straight line
      in the input space. Mathematically, we can describe these
      dots as points that follow a simple pattern:
        - `v0, v0 + d, v0 + 2d, v0 + 3d, ...`

    - where:
        - `v0` is some starting point (which could be the origin,
          but doesn't have to be).
        - `d` is a fixed vector that represents the "step size"
          between consecutive dots.

    - This means the dots are positioned at regular intervals
      along the direction of `d`.

### Step 2: Applying the Linear Transformation

    - Now, let's apply a linear transformation L to these points:
        - `L(v0), L(v0 + d), L(v0 + 2d), L(v0 + 3d), ...`

    - Using the two properties of linearity:
        - Additivity: L(v0 + d) = L(v0) + L(d)
        - Homogeneity: L(2d) = 2L(d),   L(3d) = 3L(d),   ...

    - This transforms our original sequence into:
        - `L(v0), L(v0) + L(d), L(v0) + 2L(d), L(v0) + 3L(d), ...`

### Step 3: Spacing in the Output Space

- Looking at this new sequence, we see something important:
    - each 'transformed' dot is still separated by the same amount, namely `L(d)`.

    - The difference between two consecutive transformed points
      is:
        - (L(v0) + (k+1)L(d)) - (L(v0) + kL(d)) = L(d)
    - This means that the dots remain evenly spaced in the
      transformed space, but now they are spaced by `L(d)` instead
      of `d`.

### Step 4: What Changes and What Stays the Same?

    - The dots may move, and their spacing direction may change
      (depending on L), but they will still be EVENLY SPACED.
        - If `L` stretches or compresses space, the spacing
          between dots will also stretch or compress.
        - If `L` skews or rotates space, the pattern of dots will
          also be skewed or rotated.

- However, the fundamental property—EQUAL SPACING BETWEEN
  CONSECUTIVE DOTS—is preserved because `L` distributes evenly
  across scalar multiples of the vector `d`.

### Conclusion: The Visual Interpretation of Linearity

- The reason a linear transformation keeps evenly spaced dots
  evenly spaced comes directly from its two properties:

    - `Additivity` ensures that the transformation respects the
      pattern of how dots are added together.
    - `Homogeneity` ensures that scaling relationships remain
      proportional.

- This is a super intuitive way to see linearity in action:
    - linear transformations preserve even spacing, just possibly
      in a distorted or rotated way!
