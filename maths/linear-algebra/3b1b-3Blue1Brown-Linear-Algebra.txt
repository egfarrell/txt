### 01 Vectors what even are they
    - "The introduction of numbers as coordinates is an act of
      violence" (Weyl)
    - 3 perspectives of vectors: 
        - PHYSICS: 
            - arrow pointing in space 
            - vector defined by its length and direction 
            - as long as those 2 facts are the same, you can move
              it all around and its still the same vector

        - CS: 
            - ordered list of numbers 
            - modelling house size and price: 
            - you're modelling a house as a 2D vector
            - "vector" is fancy word for "list"
            - note: order matters in these "lists" (tuple)

        - MATHS: 
            - generalises both Physics and CS views 
            - basically a "vector" is anything with
              a sensible notion of adding 2 things and
              multiplying a vector by a number... 
            - ... details are abstract, so we'll ignore this
              viewpoint till the last video 

    - Specific thought for word "vector"
        - anytime I introduce a new topic involving vectors,
          first think of an arrow... 
        - and think of that arrow inside a coord system, eg the
          X-Y plane 
        - ... with it's tail sitting _at the origin_ 
        - this is different to how Physics students think of
          vectors, where vectors can freely sit anywhere they
          want in space
        - In linear algebra, it's almost always the case that
          your vector will be rooted at the _origin_ (!) 
    - Once you understand the concept as an arrow in space, we'll
      transfer it over to the numbers point-of-view
    - The coordinate system allows a "back and forth" between
      visual arrows and numbers...
    - Think of the origin as the centre of space and the _root_
      of all vectors...
    - After choosing an arbitrary length to represent `1`, you
      make tick marks on the axis to represent this distance.
    - Coordinates (two numbers) basically instruct how to get
      from the tail of the vector (at the origin), to its tip...
    - 1st number: how far to walk along the x-axis 
    - 2nd number: how far to walk parallel to the y-axis
    - to distinguish vectors from points: 
        - the convention is to write vectors  `[  1 ]` 
                                            - `[ -2 ]`
        - and write points horizontally eg `(-4, 2)`
    - Every topic in linear algebra centres around 2 operations,
      adding vectors and multiplying them by a number
    - Short section: adding 2 vectors 
        - $MY_IMAGES/maths/linear-algebra/3blue1brown/adding-vectors.jpg
        - This example of vector addition is about the only time
          in linear algebra we allow the vectors to stray away
          from the origin
        - You can think of addition as walking steps... 
        - And vector addition is like doing all the `x` steps
          first, then all the `y` steps
            - $MY_IMAGES/maths/linear-algebra/3blue1brown/vector-addition-02.jpg
            - $MY_IMAGES/maths/linear-algebra/3blue1brown/vector-addition-03.jpg

    - scalar multiplication 
        - means "stretching", "scaling" 
        - means multiplying _each component_ by that scalar
          number

    - In linear algebra, numbers mainly scale vectors. It's
      common to use the word "scalar" interchangably with
      "number"
        - Thats why Balakrishnan has to later define what
          a "scalar" really is!

    -  Vectors: 
        - Give data analaysts and CS students a way to visualise
          lists of numbers as vectors
        - Gives physicists a language to describe `SPACE`


### 02 Linear combinations span and basis vectors
    - 2D vectors are a combination of the 2 basis vectors: 
        - `î` and `ĵ`
        - $MY_IMAGES/maths/linear-algebra/3blue1brown/basis-vectors.jpg
    - Think about coordinates as SCALERS 
        - the "full" vector `[3, -2]` is the sum of two scaled
          vectors
        - the coordinates show how much we're _scaling_ the basis
          vectors 
            - ie the basis vectors are what those scaler
              coordinates are actually scaling
            - (very important concept) 
            - $MY_IMAGES/maths/linear-algebra/3blue1brown/sum-scaled-vectors.jpg
    - What if we chose different basis vectors? 
    - Anytime we describe vectors numerically, it depends on an
      IMPLICIT choice of basis vectors 
    - If you're dealing with a collection of vectors, think of
      them all as POINTS

### 07 Inverse matrices column space and null space
    - A linear system of equations is like `matrix * vector`
      multiplication
        - $MY_IMAGES/maths/linear-algebra/3blue1brown/linear-system-eqns.jpg
        - $MY_IMAGES/maths/linear-algebra/3blue1brown/linear-system-eqns-2.jpg
    - Brilliant: Matrix equation intuition 
        - "We're looking for the vector `x` that after carrying
          out the transformation `A`, LANDS on the vector `v`"
            - $MY_IMAGES/maths/linear-algebra/3blue1brown/matrix-eqn-intuition.jpg
    - 2D example
        - `Ax = V`. We are looking for `x`
            - $MY_IMAGES/maths/linear-algebra/3blue1brown/2D-matrix-eqn.jpg
        - Now think about the determinant first
            - Will the transformation `A` squish everything down
              onto a single line??
                - `Det(A) = 0`
                    - $MY_IMAGES/maths/linear-algebra/3blue1brown/2D-matrix-eqn-2.jpg
            - Or will `A` still leave everything spanning the two
              dimensions it started with?
                - `Det(A) ≠ 0`
                    - $MY_IMAGES/maths/linear-algebra/3blue1brown/2D-matrix-eqn-3.jpg
        - In non-zero `Det`, there will always be one, and only
          one, vector `x` that will land on `V`.
            - We can find it by just playing `A` in reverse and
              seeing where `V` goes
            - ie we 'REWIND THE TAPE' until we find `x` such that `Ax = V`.
            - rewinding the tape = apply the inverse
              transformation `A^-1`

### 09 Dot products and duality
    - Dot Product: initial thoughts
    - Grant problem 1:
        - Grant thought it strange that the ORDER of the dot
          product doesn't matter. It's weirdly symmetric.
            - So did I in Aberdeen (2009)...
            - ie `v • w = w • v`
        - Intuition 1
        - Just imagine both vectors the same length
        - We can leverage some symmetry then...
                - $MY_IMAGES/maths/linear-algebra/3blue1brown/symmetric-dot-product.jpg
            - and if we double one of the vectors, the ORDER of
              the dot product still wont matter:
            - Project `w` onto `v`:
                - `v` is DOUBLED, but the projection is the same
                    - $MY_IMAGES/maths/linear-algebra/3blue1brown/symmetric-dot-product-2.jpg
            - Project `v` onto `w`:
                - `w` is the same, but the projection is DOUBLED
