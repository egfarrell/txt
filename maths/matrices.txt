Matrix Groups for undergraduates
    A matrix group means a group of INVERTIBLE matrices. ... You know from
    linear algebra that invertible matrices represent GEOMETRIC MOTIONS of
    vector spaces (i.e. linear transformations) so maybe it's not
    surprising that matrix groups are useful within geometry.

my flash of insight
    if you have a 2x2 matrix,
    you are dealing with TWO different "ENTITIES", or vectors, or "THINGS"!!
    on type "thing" down the COLUMNS and
    another type "thing" along the ROWS
    although
    sometimes you can have the same type of "thing"
    in both the rows and columns e.g.
    the markov chain with states A and B
    The transition matrix has
        A       B
    A   p(A|A)  p(B|A)
    B   p(A|B)  p(B|B)

    So you have a row with state A
    and a column with state A
    and that cell "AA" is the probability of state A
    transitioning to state A (itself)
    but overall 
    a 2x2 matrix has TWO different "entities"
