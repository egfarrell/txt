### FT "Negative" frequencies
    - frequencies above the Nyquist frequency (2 samples per
      cycle?) are actually going too fast for the sampling rate
      and are "aliased"...
    - ... so we don't see the true frequencies above the Nyquist
      and so we call them "negative" frequencies and give them
      a minus sign
        - $MY_IMAGES/maths/fourier/FT-netagive-frequencies.jpg
    - From Euler's equaiton and a real `cos` signal, this slide
      explains why we have positive and negative frequencies
        - $MY_IMAGES/maths/fourier/why-pos-neg-frequencies.jpg
    - why bother with the negative frequencies anyway? (If they
      only mimic the positive frequencies)
    - because we may want to invert the signal in the future, so
      we don't want information loss

### STFT Short Time Fourier Transform
    - https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-Yxj3yfvY-4
    - The normal fourier transform gives you the frequency
      components over the _entire duration_ of the signal
        - It's an average over the entire duration
        - So we know _WHAT_ frequencies are present,
        - but we don't know _WHEN_
        - (and the original signal in time, we know exactly
          "where" we are in the signal, but we don't have any
          instantaneous FREQUENCY info at an individual
          timepoint)
    - Solution: STFT:
        - apply FT to chunks of the original time/space signal
        - (the chunks are called "_frames_")
        - so apply a `windowing function` to the signal
            - the window function is everywhere `0` apart from
              the segment you want, where the window is `1`
    - Terminology
        - window size:
            - amount of samples we apply the window to
        - frame size
            - number of samples in each chunk of the signal
    - note: _usually_ (window size) = (frame size)
    - note: frames often OVERLAP!
        - why? "Spectral leakage"
    - hop size
        - how many 'samples' we slide to the right when we take
          a new frame
            - $MY_IMAGES/maths/fourier/stft-hop-size.jpg
    - the key formulas
        - $MY_IMAGES/maths/fourier/stft-formula.jpg
    - Aside: frame number is a proxy for time
    - OUTPUTS:
        - note: there's a time/frequency trade-off here too
            - (like what Grant was talking about in his video)
        - $MY_IMAGES/maths/fourier/stft-time-freq-tradeoff.jpg
        - smaller framesize => less frequency bins => less freq
          resolution
    - time / freq tradeoff
        - $MY_IMAGES/maths/fourier/stft-time-freq-resolution.jpg
