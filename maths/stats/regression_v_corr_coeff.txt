# Key point:
    REGRESSION lines are different to CORRELATION.

- Try analyzing these two rather contrived datasets in R

# First:
    _X   Y_
     5   1
     5   9
     13  9
     13  17

# then:
    _X   Y_
     5   3
     5   7
     13  11
     13  15

It helps to do scatter plots + regression line for both as
we did in class to get a feel for the data. Note how the
regression line is the same for both, but the distance from
the data points to the line is greater in the first set.

If you then use the summary function to get the regression
you see they have identical slopes of 1.00 (I did say this
was contrived!) However running a correlation on each shows
a correlation of 0.71 for the first set and 0.89 for the
second.

- The regression coef. tells us how to predict `y` knowing `x`,
- the correlation tells us how good that prediction is
  likely to be.

- The question asks about "the distance between an individual dot
  and the regression line". 
- What is actually meant is the _vertical distance_ and not the
  actual (perpendicular) distance between the point and the line.

- This corresponds to a detail that prof. Conway omitted in the
  lectures, probably due to lack of time, but which should, be
  highlighted because it makes the lecture somewhat misleading.

    - Correlation DOES NOT distinguish between the dependent and
      independent variables,
    - while the (common form of the) _regression_ line does
      distinguish between them.

The formula for (Pearson) correlation coefficient is
symmetrical w.r.t. to x and y, while regression line
minimizes just the squared residuals of y (this could be
told in simpler terms, but I don't want to give out the
answer to the question ;).

For a simple demonstration, try drawing two scatter plots
with regression lines. Let the first show memory.visual on
x and memory.verbal on y, as in the lecture, and for the
other swap the coordinates.
    
> layout(matrix(1:2, 1))
> plot(impact$memory.verbal ~ impact$memory.visual)
> abline(lm(impact$memory.verbal ~ impact$memory.visual))
> plot(impact$memory.visual ~ impact$memory.verbal)
> abline(lm(impact$memory.visual ~ impact$memory.verbal))

While the points are just reflected over the diagonal, the
regression lines are completely different. The regression
line for the first case goes to the right side of the points
at the ceiling - as it should.

In the reflected picture, the regression line goes straight
through these points - again as it should. Thinking about
why both pictures are correct but different should be
revealing about the difference between scatter plots and
regression lines.

This has quite practical consequences: many people draw
regression lines in Excel scatter plots to show the
correlation, which is wrong.
