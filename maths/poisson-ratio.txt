### poisson ratio 

    > (fraction of expansion) / (fraction of compression.) 

    When you pull something apart, what happens the middle
    of it? Does it contract or expand?

    Normally you'd think as something stretches so the
    middle contracts, but the lecturer demonstrated
    a crumpled ball of paper.

    A so-called "network object". He crumpled the paper and
    pulled from top and bottom. The middle of the paper ball
    expanded!

    Values of ratio can range from -1 to +1/2


### Wikipedia:

    Poisson's ratio (ν), named after Siméon Poisson, is the
    ratio, when a sample object is stretched, of the
    contraction or transverse strain (perpendicular to the
    applied load), to the extension or axial strain (in the
    direction of the applied load).


    When a material is compressed in one direction, it
    usually tends to expand in the other two directions
    perpendicular to the direction of compression. This
    phenomenon is called the Poisson effect. 

    Poisson's ratio ν (nu) is a measure of the Poisson
    effect. The Poisson ratio is the ratio of the fraction
    (or percent) of expansion divided by the fraction (or
    percent) of compression, for small values of these
    changes.
