### Relationship: ln(x) and Hyperbola 1/x

    - Defining ln(x) via Integral:
    - The natural log `ln(x)` can be defined as the
      area under the curve 1/t from `1` to `x`.
      Mathematically, this is expressed as: ln(x)=∫1/t dt

    - This integral definition gives ln(x) a geometric
      interpretation: it represents the total area under the
      curve `1/t` from `t=1` to `t=x`.

    - Properties Derived from the Integral:
    - From this definition, several properties of the natural
      logarithm can be derived: `ln(1)=0` because the area from
      `1` to `1` is zero.

    - The natural logarithm is positive for `x>1` and negative for `0 < x < 1`.

### Relationship between ln(x) and Exponential Function e^x

    - Inverse Relationship:
        - The exponential function `e^x` and the natural logarithm
          `ln(x)` are inverse functions. 
        - This means that: e^[ln(x)]=x and ln(e^x)=x

    - This inverse relationship closely ties the two functions
      together. The exponential function grows rapidly and is
      defined for all real numbers, while the natural logarithm
      is defined for positive real numbers and grows more slowly.

    - The Constant `e`:
        - The number `e` (approximately 2.71828) is defined as
          the unique number such that the area under the curve
          `1/t` from `1` to `e` is equal to `1`. 
        - That is: `ln(e) = 1`

    - This property helps establish `e` as the base of the
      natural logarithm.

### Connecting the Concepts: Hyperbola and Exponential

    - Area Interpretation and Exponential Growth:
        - The relationship between the hyperbola `1/x` and the
          natural logarithm as an area under the curve shows
          a deep connection between logarithmic growth and
          exponential growth. 
        - Exponential growth (described by `e^x`) can be
          understood as a continuous compounding process, while
          the natural logarithm `ln` describes the continuous
          compounding's "inverse" process in terms of area.
            - Kalid simply explained says ln gives how much
              'time' an exponential process needs to produce an
              amount ('time' rather than area)

    - Scaling Properties:
        - The SCALING PROPERTIES of the hyperbola are mirrored in
          the logarithm function. 
        - E.g., the area under `1/x` from `1` to x⋅y is the sum
          of the areas from `1` to `x` and from `x` to `x⋅y`,
          which translates to the logarithmic property: 
            - `ln(xy) = ln(x) + ln(y)` 

### Euler's Formula:
    - Euler's identity, `e^(iπ) + 1 = 0`, ties together `e`, `π`,
      and complex exponentiation, showing the deep
      interconnectedness of these concepts.

- In summary, `ln(x)` and function `e^(x)` are intimately related
  through their definitions as inverse functions. 
    - The natural logarithm also has a geometric interpretation
      as the area under the hyperbola `1/x`.
