- Directional derivative is just a _NUMBER_!!
- Remember, we're talking about rates-of-change here.
    - Everything is about derivatives, rates-of-change.

Link between partial derivatives and directional derivatives!!

* partial derivative of `f` wrt `x`,
    - is like the directional derivative of `f`
    - along the `x` axis

* partial derivative of `f` wrt `y`,
    - is like the directional derivative of `f`
    - along the `y` axis

* But with the directional derivative,
    - you get the partial of `f` along some _linear combination_
      of the `x` and `y` axis.

### Then he showed a picture:
    - The vectors `f(x, y)` and the vector `u` (the direction
      vector), together both of them form a special plane. and
      the directional derivative is the slope of the tangent line
      in a special plane.

    - This special plane is not (neccessarily) orthogonal to the
      `x` and `y` axis.

- You dont have to use limits to calculate the directional
  derivative.

- There are more efficient ways. You can:

    * __DOT__ the direction vector with the GRAD vector, to get
      the directional derivative.

### The direction vector should be a unit vector?

    - Before you _DOT_ with the _GRAD_ field you should check
      it's a unit vector.

    - How do you make the direction vector be a unit vector?
      Divide it by its length.

    - Ah but Fortney has a useful point if the direction vector
      IS NOT a unit vector!!!
        - $MY_BOOKS/Physics/Maths/Math-Phy-Forms-diff-geometry/Visual-Intro-Differential-Forms-Fortney.pdf
        - Page 50
        - This is exactly the same definition as in the
          multivariable calculus case, only without the
          requirement that `vp` be a unit vector.
        - Thus, the number that we get from this definition of
          directional derivative will not be the slope of the
          tangent line if `vp` is not a unit vector but will
          instead be the “rise” of the tangent line to the graph
          of `f` at the point p as we move the length of `vp`.
