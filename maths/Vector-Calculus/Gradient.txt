### Gradient versus Directional Derivative
    - VECTOR:
        - The gradient is a vector with the slope of the FUNCTION
          along each of the _coordinate_ axes.
            - (July 2023: what function? e.g. air temperature
              function as in Fleisch?)
    - SCALAR:
        - The directional derivative is the slope in an arbitrary
          specified DIRECTION.

### Gradient as partial derivatives
    - Ah... the gradient is a vector made of partial derivatives
      along each axis..
    - BUT, those partial derivatives are scalars and are
      implicitly attached to basis vectors `î`, `ĵ` etc
        - That's what makes the gradient a VECTOR.
        - There is some basis that these partial derivatives
          latch on to...
        - Aside: the _magnitude_ of the gradient vector
          represents the largest slope of a tangent line through
          a particular point. (so slope is just a scalar number)

### Gradient intuition

      ascent, it also shows which variable is the most important
      in terms of change
    - ie which variable has the greatest impact at this point
      ("more bang for your buck")

    - Slope:    `tan θ`
