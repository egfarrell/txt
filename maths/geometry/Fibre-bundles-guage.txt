### Background
    - Gauge fields:
        - are mathematical structures that unify physical forces
          and control how QM states transform.
        - In 1954 Yang and Mills applied the gauge idea to the
          strong nuclear force. Only later did physicists realize
          these fields were mathematically equivalent to
          connections on fiber bundles.
        - So: Gauge fields ↔ Fiber-bundle connections.
        - See also: $MY_NOTES/physics/Guage-theory.txt

    - Note: Yang and Mills didn't originally formulate their
      gauge theory using fiber bundles. Their 1954 paper was
      written in the standard language of physics at the time.

    - Fields were `functions` defined over spacetime
    - Gauge transformations were `operations` that changed these
      fields in an internal symmetry space
    - Covariant derivatives and field strengths were introduced
      by analogy with EM

    - The geometric interpretation — that a gauge field is
      a connection on a principal fiber bundle — came later,
      during the late 1960s and early 1970s, mainly thanks to
      interaction with the mathematical community.

### Timeline:
    - 1954: Yang & Mills
        - Gauge fields = generalization of EM gauge invariance
        - No fiber bundles
        - No talk of holonomy, curvature 2-forms, etc.

    - 1960s–70s: Geometric reinterpretation developed
        - Mathematicians like Ehresmann and H. Cartan had the
          fiber-bundle machinery
        - Physicists (e.g., Wu & Yang) noticed the match:
        - gauge potentials `A_mu`    correspond  to connections
        - field strength   `F_mu nu` corresponds to curvature

    - So:
        - Gauge fields were invented first.
        - Fiber bundles arrived later as their natural habitat.

    - Delightful side effect: Suddenly many deep quantum effects
      became geometrically obvious: Aharonov–Bohm, monopoles,
      instantons.

    - Gauge theory without bundles works computationally.
    - Gauge theory with bundles reveals the structure of the
      universe.
    - And that structure has twists, coverings, and curvature in
      internal space — as real as the curvature of spacetime in
      GR.

### Peter Woit timeline

    - In 1966 Yang left Princeton for Stony Brook, where he
      founded the Institute for Theoretical Physics (ITP).
    - The ITP shared a building with the math department, which
      had become a world center for geometry starting with the
      arrival of Jim Simons in 1968. It’s fascinating to hear
      (see here) the account by Simons and Yang of their attempts
      to understand each other’s language and their slow
      realization that the gauge fields of Yang-Mills theory were
      the geometer’s connections on a PRINCIPAL FIBER BUNDLE.
      Yang wrote a paper with Wu giving what became known as the
      `“Wu-Yang” dictionary` relating the two subjects.
    - Singer learned of this when visiting Stony Brook, then
      brought the news to Atiyah in Oxford, starting an explosive
      development during the late 1970s.


### Marbuger: phase and the fibre bundle
- $MY_NOTES/physics/QM/Constructing-reality-marburger.txt

- Quantum phase is like a dial attached to each point in space.

- If there's no EM field present, the zero marks on the dials can
  be arranged so all the pointers indicate the same angle.

- But if there's a field present, try as you might, you can't get
  them all to read the same, just like you can't trace a straight
  line on a globe.

- The field, or rather its vector potential, provides an
  intrinsic connection among the set of dial pointers at
  different points in space-time.

- Geometers call this kind of structure a _FIBER BUNDLE_, each
  dial representing a fiber, and the entire set of them over all
  the space-time points comprising the bundle.

- What physicists call the vector potential, mathematicians call
  the connection over the bundle.

- The geometry of our world, in the vision of Weyl–Schrödinger
  (thus far in our story), is therefore that of a 1D fiber bundle
  over the 4D manifold of space-time.

- Electromagnetic forces and gravity are "explained" as
  distortions in this geometry associated with the distribution
  of energy, mass, and electricity throughout the universe.
