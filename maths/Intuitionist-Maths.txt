- Intuitionist mathematics, developed by L.E.J. Brouwer, is
  a mathematical philosophy that emphasizes the MENTAL
  CONSTRUCTION of mathematical objects over their existence as
  part of an abstract, external reality. (Platonism)
- Unlike classical mathematics, intuitionism denies the use of
  the `law of excluded middle` (P or not-P) for arbitrary
  propositions, focusing only on what can be explicitly
  constructed.
    - "Smooth world" infinitesimals also deny the law of excluded
      middle: $MY_NOTES/maths/analysis/infinitesimals.txt

### Key Features of Intuitionist Mathematics:
    1. Constructivism:
        - Mathematical objects & proofs must be constructively
          defined or demonstrated. E.g., a number is considered
          to exist only if we can explicitly construct it.
    2. Law of Excluded Middle:
        - This law, fundamental in classical logic, is rejected
          for general mathematical statements. E.g., in
          intuitionism, it is not necessarily true that a real
          number `x` is either rational or not rational unless
          this can be demonstrated constructively.
    3. Continuum as a Process:
        - The real number continuum is viewed as a flowing,
          `dynamic process`, not a completed set of points.
        - Real numbers are defined by algorithms or rules for
          constructing their decimal expansions.
    4. Implication for Proofs:
        - Proof by `contradiction` is not valid unless it can
          constructively produce an example. For instance, to
          prove that there exists an `x` such that `P(x)`, one
          must explicitly construct such an `x`.

### Simple Examples:
    1. Rejection of Non-Constructive Proofs:
    - Classical: Assume `not-P` leads to a contradiction, thus
      `P` must hold.
    - Intuitionist: Unless `P` can be explicitly constructed, the
      proof is invalid.

    2. Constructing Real Numbers:
    - A real number in intuitionism might be defined as
      a sequence (`x_n`) of rational numbers that converges to
      a limit constructively, such as: `x_n = 1 - 1/n`

    3. No Uncountable Sets: Without the law of excluded middle,
       the uncountability of certain sets, like the real numbers,
       does not hold as in classical mathematics. Constructive
       definitions focus only on `countable processes`.

### Intuitionist Arithmetic:
    - In intuitionistic arithmetic, logical connectives and
      quantifiers have constructive interpretations.
    - For example:
        - For "there exists `x` such that `P(x)`" `(∃x P(x)`),
          you must constructively produce an `x` for which `P(x)`
          holds.
        - For "for all `x`, `P(x)`" (`∀x P(x)`), you must provide
          a method that demonstrates `P(x)` for any `x`.

- Intuitionism is often contrasted with classical and formalist
  approaches, and while it restricts some classical techniques,
  it provides a foundation for constructive mathematics and
  influences areas like computer science and algorithmic logic.

### Contrast: Platonism
    - When we talk about an "abstract, external reality" in
      mathematics, we're referring to the classical view that
      mathematical objects exist independently of our thoughts or
      ability to describe or construct them.
    - This is often associated with `Platonism`, the idea that
      mathematical entities—like numbers, sets, or geometric
      shapes—exist in an abstract, idealized world, and
      mathematicians "discover" truths about them.

### Example of Abstract External Reality:
    1. The Number "2":
        - In classical (Platonist) terms, the number `2` exists
          as an ideal object, regardless of whether anyone thinks
          about it or uses it. It's "out there" in a mathematical
          sense.
        - In intuitionism, `2` only exists because we can
          mentally construct it. (e.g. by combining one thing
          with another). It’s not an independent entity but
          a mental construct.

    2. Infinite Sets:
        - In classical mathematics, an infinite set like the set
          of natural numbers `{1, 2, 3, ...}` is considered to
          exist as a complete entity.
        - Intuitionism DENIES this. The natural numbers only
          "exist" as we construct them one by one in our minds.
          The set as a WHOLE is not a fixed, external object.

### Brouwer's Perspective:
    - Brouwer, the founder of intuitionism, argued that
      mathematics originates entirely in the mind. According to
      him:
    - Mathematical objects are *created* by mathematicians
      through mental activity.
    - These objects don’t exist in a detached, external realm.
      Their existence is tied to the act of constructing or
      defining them.
    - Abstract concepts like "the set of all real numbers" or "an
      infinite sequence" are problematic unless they can be
      explicitly constructed step-by-step.

### Why This Matters:
    - Classical mathematicians rely on assumptions about abstract
      existence to make powerful proofs (e.g., "Assume there
      exists a set X such that..."). Intuitionists reject this
      unless the object can be explicitly constructed or directly
      demonstrated. For intuitionists, the act of doing
      mathematics is inherently a mental process, and anything
      that can't be mentally constructed doesn't "exist."

    - This philosophy affects how we approach proofs,
      definitions, and even basic mathematical concepts like
      infinity and the continuum.

    - In short: the "abstract external reality" is a realm where
      math exists independently of us, like a hidden treasure
      waiting to be uncovered. Intuitionism, on the other hand,
      sees math as something we build in our minds, not something
      we discover in an abstract world.
