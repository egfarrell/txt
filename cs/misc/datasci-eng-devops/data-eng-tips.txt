### Dimension Modelling
    - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensional_modeling
    - Dimensional modeling always uses the concepts of facts
      (measures), and dimensions (context). 
    - Facts: typically (but not always) numeric values that can
      be aggregated, 
    - Dimensions: groups of hierarchies and descriptors that
      define the facts.


### Talk: Database migrations don't have to be painful Data
    - Strata 2019
    - Inspired by the Green / Blue deployment technique, the
      Adobe Audience Manager team developed an Active / Passive
      database migration procedure that allows us to test our
      database clusters in production, minimising the risks
      without compromising the innovation. We successfully
      applied this approach twice to upgrade the entire
      technology stack. But it never was a smooth move.
    - lesson 01: Automoate everything (but be prepared for some
      manual intervention)
    - lesson 02: Make only ONE change at a time
        - Their problem: CentOS had new hardware problems (i3,
          SSD). Solution: use Amazon Linux on AWS
    - lesson 03: Start small
    - lesson 04: But small scale success is never enough
    - lesson 05: One slow node is worse than a dead one!
        - extra point: they ran out of clean SSD areas on their
          disks which slowed the whole cluster. Again this was
          only seen when they started processing full production
          data.
    - lesson 06: beware of new technology unknowns (NVMe SSDs)
    - lesson 07: Read the manual!



### DIMENSIONAL data modeling
    - Learn all about dimensional data modeling and how to build
      this powerful data model for analytics. We cover how
      dimensional differs from relational and explain each of the
      dimensional modeling components: 
        - meters, 
        - dimensions,
        - measures, 
        - hierarchies. 
    - We also explore more advanced dimensional modeling
      components such as behavioral dimensions, factless facts,
      and conformed dimensions. 
    - Learn about the three types of measures: Transaction,
      Snapshot, and Accumulating. 
    - Understand the differences in modeling and data warehouse
      architecture approaches from the legends, Bill Inmon and
      Ralph Kimball. 
    - Practice several dimensional modeling exercises. This video
      was recorded live during Steve Hoberman’s Data Modeling
      Master Class. More on this 3-day data modeling course at
      https://stevehoberman.com

    - ERD's are timeless
    - so how to you track history over time?
    - A manager is in an office, (only one) and one office can
      have many managers (one-to-many). 
        - But over time the manager moves around, so the
          `one-to-many` becomes a `many-to-many` and you lose
          some ERD rules...
    - so better to model time changes using the "physical"?
    - and some australian modeller said put a little clock symbol
      beside each entity that needs history

    - `RELATIONAL`  = Data Model of how the business WORKS
        - Business execution
        - Scope = concepts/rules that describe business process
        - Timeless
        - Designed to mimic how a business works

    - `DIMENSIONAL` = Data Model of how the business IS DOING
        - smaller scope than relational model
        - business evaluation (monitoring)
        - Scope = measures/context that answer business questions
        - Timeless
        - "Things I measure" go in the middle and ways of looking
          at them go in the surrounding boxes
        - Simple, secure (physical level), speedy (physical
          level)
        - A dimensional model will always be derived from
          a relational
        - Relational is all the lego pieces of different sizes.
          Dimensional is putting them together for a purpose.

    - Dimensional models are built around measures. 
    - Measures are attributes we can MATHEMATICALLY manipulate.
    - `Measure`: eg. gross sales amount. 
        - Has a "class word": amount, sum, rate, count
        - TRANSACTION: lowest level: 
            - monitor events created by a business process. Order
              quantity, Gross sales amount
            - how well is a process performing. e.g. look at
              order quantity 
            - easiest to work with - they are additive, can be
              summed at any dimension level
        - SNAPSHOT:
            - monitor _impact_ of events created by a business
              process.
            - eg at the end of a given period, whats the account
              balance amount. Or Ozone layer thickness etc!
            - they represent end-points after a series of
              transactions have been completed
            - more complex because they aren't additive
        - ACCUMULATING:
            - how long does it take a business process to
              complete
            - how many days to close claim at insurance company
            - progression

    - `Meter`: the most important entity on a dimensional model
      because it determines (shows?) the health of a business
      process. (shows the value of a measure?)
        - a _logical_ meter may turn into one or more _physical_
          fact tables
    - `Dimensions`: provide a context for a meter. Dimensions are
      often hierarchies: (e.g. Country, region. Year, month.)
        - But not all dimensions are hierarchies: 
            - e.g. gender, currency.
    - `Grain` or base: the lowest level of detail we can view our
      measures. e.g. lowest level: month, product, region. (this
      can then be rolled up higher)
        - if the grain is too low-level it could start revealing
          individual details. ie you get privacy issues.
        - sometimes the data may not be available for the desired
          grain level. ie we may only have quarterly data, not
          monthly!
    - `atomic`: the actual lowest level in the business
    - Simplicity, security and speed best flexibility in the
      dimensional.
    - The simpler the dimensional data model, the more work for
      developers.
    - Dimensional models are built for navigation, up and down
      the hierarchy: "drill down" and "drill up"
    - Drill across from one meter to another, if they share the
      same dimensions. e.g. Compare sales against returns
    - If you've got a Data lake, data warehouse, then usually
      you're in a better position to drill across. (because
      you've given more thought about how the pieces fit
      together)

    - When dimensional and when relational? 
        - Dimensional when theres a reporting requirement to play
          with numbers
        - dimensional: you're already assuming theres some
          questions your want to answer:  ie when you know the
          question and you're playing with numbers

    - Text analytics: assign a number to a comment / piece of
      text. See how "much" someone loves  our product. But in
      it's infancy: computers can't detect human sarcasm!

    - USE RELATIONAL for adhoc queries. Ad-hoc = query
      freedom.

    - as long as you're not affecting simplicity, security and
      speed you can offer a finer grain.

    - Two architecture models: BUS (Kimball) and HUB (Inmon).
      Often with the bus, your grain is at the atomic level. Cos
      it's harder to change later anyway.

    - factless fact: better name would be a "measureless meter".
      Factless fact count events, by summing relationships.
