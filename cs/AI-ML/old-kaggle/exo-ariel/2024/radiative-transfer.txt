
### RADIATIVE TRANSFER Models

    - Context: EXOPLANET transmission spectroscopy.

    - Radiative transfer model: 
        - set of equations and physical principles to describe
          the propagation of radiation (light) through an
          exoplanet's atmosphere. 

    - These models simulate how starlight:
        - passes through the atmosphere, 
        - gets absorbed, scattered, 
        - and re-emitted at different wavelengths, 

    - It's key to understanding the planet's atmospheric
      composition, structure, etc.

### Key Elements:

    1. Atmospheric Composition: 
        - The model accounts for the gases, clouds, or hazes
          present in the atmosphere. 
        - Different gases absorb and scatter light at specific
          wavelengths, creating unique signatures (spectral
          features).

    2. Absorption and Scattering: 
        - Radiative transfer models incorporate absorption by
          molecules (such as water vapor, methane, carbon
          dioxide, etc.) and scattering by molecules or aerosols. 
        - These processes are wavelength-dependent, and the model
          calculates how much light is transmitted, absorbed, or
          scattered as it passes through the atmosphere.

    3. Temperature Profile: 
        - The atmospheric temperature affects how molecules
          absorb and emit radiation. 
        - Most radiative transfer models use vertical temperature
          profiles (how temperature changes with altitude) to
          calculate how light interacts with the atmosphere at
          different layers.

    4. Geometry: 
        - In transmission spectroscopy, the model typically
          focuses on the star's light passing through the limb
          (edge) of the exoplanet as it transits in front of the
          star. 
        - The path length of the starlight through the atmosphere
          changes with ALTITUDE, and radiative transfer models
          account for this geometry to simulate the transmission
          spectrum accurately.

    5. Emission and Reflection: 
        - Though primarily focused on transmission (light passing
          through the atmosphere), some radiative transfer models
          also consider the thermal emission from the planet
          itself or reflected starlight, which is crucial for
          understanding the full radiative budget of the system.

### Purpose in Transmission Spectroscopy: 
    - Radiative transfer models are used to generate THEORETICAL
      SPECTRA of exoplanet atmospheres by solving for the
      intensity of light as a function of wavelength. 
        - When comparing these MODEL SPECTRA to observed
          transmission spectra (measured during a planetary
          transit), scientists can infer details about the
          planet's atmosphere, such as the presence of specific
          molecules, pressure, temperature, and cloud layers.

- These models are vital for interpreting the absorption features
  in starlight that's filtered through the exoplanet's
  atmosphere. It reveals crucial information about the planet's
  composition and structure.
