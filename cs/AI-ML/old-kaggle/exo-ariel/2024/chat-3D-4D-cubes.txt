- The data structure for the AIRS-CH0 instrument can be thought
  of as a "4D cube" because it involves four dimensions: time,
  spatial, and spectral components.

### AIRS-CH0: "4D Data Cube" Explanation

#### 1. Time Dimension:
- 11,250 time steps: The data captures 11,250 distinct moments during the
  observation.

#### 2. Spatial Dimension:
- 32 pixels (spatial axis): This represents the spatial dimension across one
  direction (for instance, the Y-axis).

#### 3. Spectral Dimension:
- 356 pixels (spectral axis): Each of these 356 pixels represents a different
  wavelength. This means that AIRS-CH0 captures light at 356 distinct
  wavelengths simultaneously, providing detailed spectral information.

#### 4. "Depth" (Data Type or Flux Value):
- The actual data recorded at each point in the "4D cube" would be the flux or
  intensity of light.

### Summary of the AIRS-CH0 "4D Data Cube":
- Dimensions:
    - 1. Time: 11,250 time steps.
    - 2. Spatial (Y-axis): 32 pixels.
    - 3. Spectral: 356 wavelengths.
    - 4. Flux Value: The intensity of light recorded at each
      combination of time, spatial position, and wavelength.

- So, the AIRS-CH0 data can indeed be visualized as a 4D
  structure where you have:

- (Time, Spatial, Spectral, Flux) = (11,250, 32, 356, Flux)

### Contrast with FGS1:
- FGS1: A 3D data cube because it only involves time, spatial (2D image), and
  flux dimensions, with no true spectral axis since all pixels capture the same
  integrated light across the 0.60 to 0.80 µm range.
- AIRS-CH0: A "4D data cube" because it captures detailed spectral information
  (356 distinct wavelengths), in addition to the time and spatial dimensions.

### Spatial and Spectral Comparison:
- FGS1:
  - 2D Spatial Component: 32x32 pixels.
  - No True Spectral Axis: All pixels measure the same wavelength range.
- AIRS-CH0:
  - 1D Spatial Component: 32 pixels (one spatial dimension).
  - 1D Spectral Component: 356 pixels (representing 356 different wavelengths).

### Conclusion:
    - Yes, the AIRS-CH0 data can be thought of as a "4D cube"
      because it includes detailed spectral data (356
      wavelengths) on top of the time and spatial dimensions.
      This makes it fundamentally different from the FGS1 data,
      which lacks the spectral dimension and instead focuses on
      spatial and temporal variations within a fixed wavelength
      range.
