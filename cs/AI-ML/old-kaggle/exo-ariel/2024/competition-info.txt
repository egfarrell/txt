### Context
    - Exoplanet Discovery: Over 5,600 exoplanets have been
      discovered, challenging our understanding of Earth's
      uniqueness and the potential for life elsewhere.

    - ESA Ariel Mission (2029): Aimed at studying 1,000
      extrasolar planets' atmospheres in detail.

### Challenge Overview

    - Objective: Extract the ATMOSPHERIC SPECTRA from exoplanet
      observations, estimating the uncertainty levels.

    - Data: Sequential 2D images of the spectral focal plane
      taken over hours as an exoplanet transits its host star.

    - Difficulty: The task involves detrending data corrupted by
      instrumental noise to reveal faint atmospheric signals.

### Approaches

    1. Approach 1:
        - Train directly on full 3D data cube to extract spectra.
        - Pros: Rich information.
        - Cons: Requires significant computing resources.

    2. Approach 2:
        - Summarize the data by summing fluxes along the pixel
          y-axis, creating 2D images `(Ntimes, Nwavelengths)`.
        - Transform images to enhance transit depth variations
          between wavelengths.
        - Note: NEITHER approach is optimal for denoising jitter
          time series; a combination of all three domains (image,
          time, spectral) might be best.

### Evaluation
    - Metric: Gaussian Log-likelihood (GLL) function.
    - Formula: `GLL = -1/2(log(2π) + log(σ^2user) + ((y
      - μuser)^2/σ^2user))`
    - Final Score Calculation:
    - The score is derived by comparing predicted spectra and
      uncertainties against ground truth, normalized between [0,
      1].
    - Higher scores = Better models.

### Submission Requirements
    - File Format: Include predictions for each `planetid` with
      567 columns.
    - First column: `planetid`.
    - Next 283 columns: Predicted spectra.
    - Remaining 283 columns: Uncertainties.



### Dataset Overview
    - Objective: Extract the chemical spectrum of exoplanet
      atmospheres using simulated data from the ARIEL mission.
    - Hidden Test Set: Contains data for roughly 800 exoplanets;
      some simulations based on real exoplanets are ignored for
      scoring.

### Metadata Files
    - `train/testadcinfo.csv`:
      - Contains analog-to-digital (ADC) conversion parameters
        (gain and offset) to restore the original dynamic range.
      - Includes a column identifying the star used for each
        planet's simulation.
    - `trainlabels.csv`: Ground truth spectra.
    - `axisinfo.parquet`: Axis information for both instruments.
    - `wavelength.csv`: Wavelength grid for each ground truth
      spectrum.

### Signal Files
    - General Information:
    - The dataset includes time series imagery from two
      instruments: FGS1 and AIRS-CH0.
    - `FGS1`: Fine Guidance System with sensitivity between 0.60
      and 0.80 µm, providing high-precision photometry.
    - `AIRS-CH0`: Infrared Spectrometer with sensitivity between
      1.95 and 3.90 µm and a resolving power of approximately
      R=100.
    - Data captured in uint16 format; to restore original dynamic
      range, multiply by _gain_ and add _offset_ from the
      corresponding `ADC file`.

### - Specific Files:
    - `AIRS-CH0signal.parquet`:
        - Contains 11,250 rows of images (32x356, flattened into
          11,392 columns).
        - Data can be reshaped using `numpy.reshape(11250, 32,
          356)`.
    - `FGS1signal.parquet`:
        - Contains 135,000 rows of images (32x32, flattened into
          1024 columns).
        - Data can be reshaped using `numpy.reshape(135000, 32,
          32)`.

### Calibration Files
    - Purpose: Calibration files are used to correct
      sensor-related errors in the data, ensuring accurate
      measurements.
      - `dark.parquet`:
            - Captures thermal noise and bias level by taking
              exposures with the shutter closed. Used to subtract
              dark current from science images.
      - `dead.parquet`:
            - Identifies dead or hot pixels; dead pixels do not
              respond to light, hot pixels produce high signals
              regardless of light.
      - `flat.parquet`:
            - Corrects for variations in pixel sensitivity and
              optical system irregularities by imaging
              a uniformly illuminated surface.
      - `linearcorr.parquet`:
            - Provides information about the linearity correction
              of the sensor, converting pixel responses to an
              accurate estimate using a polynomial.
      - `read.parquet`:
            - Captures electronic noise introduced during the
              sensor readout process, even in the absence of
              light.
