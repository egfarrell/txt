### Summary

- Found practical applications of regression methods for noise
  detrending in time-series spectro-spatial data from space-based
  telescopes, specifically for missions akin to Ariel, with
  ARIELSim, ExoSim, and machine learning playing pivotal roles
  [1, 3, 7, 8].

### Detailed Summary:

    - Simulation Tools:
        -  `ARIELSim` and `ExoSim` are prominent tools for
           modeling noise characteristics specific to the Ariel
           mission, enabling validation and development of noise
           mitigation strategies under realistic noise
           conditions, including pointing jitter [1, 8, 23].
           These tools are crucial in designing mission-specific
           noise models and refining observational strategies.

    - Regression and Noise Models:
        -  Several studies emphasize the use of advanced
           regression methods, including Gaussian Processes and
           pixel-level decorrelation, to address pointing jitter
           and instrumental noise effectively. These methods
           demonstrated significant improvements in photometric
           precision for missions such as K2 and Spitzer,
           showcasing their relevance to Ariel-like missions [2,
           4].

    - Machine Learning Applications:
        -  The use of ML techniques, highlighted by the ARIEL
           Data Challenge, demonstrates the ability of ML models,
           such as NNs, to handle complex noise patterns in
           exoplanet data, indicating a growing trend toward
           _AUTOMATED NOISE CORRECTION_ methods [3, 7, 9].
           Competitive frameworks like the ARIEL Data Challenge
           push for continuous innovations applicable to future
           missions.

    - Consensus and Innovation:
        -  There is consensus of INTEGRATING realistic noise
           simulations and advanced ML models to develop robust
           data correction pipelines. 
        - However, discrepancies in method effectiveness and the
          ongoing need for better evaluation frameworks have been
          noted, highlighting areas for further exploration and
          development [9, 27].

- The literature reflects a CONVERGENCE of _SIMULATION-BASED
  modeling_ and _ML_ approaches to refine noise detrending
  methodologies, crucial for maximizing the scientific outcomes
  from missions like Ariel.

# Categories of papers

### Simulation Tools for Space Missions

    - Description: Papers focusing on the development and
      application of simulation tools to model noise impacts in
      space missions, particularly targeting realistic noise and
      pointing jitter as present in the Ariel mission.
    - Details:
        - [1] ARIELSim tool simulating complex noise and
          systematics specific to the Ariel mission.
        - [5, 13] PLATO simulation tools focused on modeling
          space-based CCD photometry and assessing noise sources.
        - [8] ExoSim used within the Ariel mission to simulate
          transiting exoplanet observations.
        - [12] ASTROID Simulator models jitter and other
          instrumental noise for high-precision space imaging.
        - [23] ExoSim provides a time domain simulator to assay
          exoplanet transit spectroscopy.

### Regression Techniques for Noise Detrending

    - Description: Studies exploring various regression methods,
      including advanced statistical and machine learning
      approaches, for noise detrending in space telescope
      datasets.
    - Details:

Timeline and citation network
Historical Development of Ideas:

    - Early Developments (2010-2014):
        -  The initial focus was on building robust _simulation
           tools_ like the PLATO Simulator [5, 13], which
           modelled noise properties, including pointing jitter,
           to improve photometric precision. The `ASTROID`
           package [12] further emphasized realistic simulation
           environments.
    - Mid-2010s Advances (2015-2017):
        -  Methods began shifting towards more sophisticated
           noise detrending techniques such as Independent
           Component Analysis (`ICA`) introduced by Morello [4,
           22] and the application of statistically robust
           regression models to handle missing data and unknown
           colored noise [6].
    - Integration of Machine Learning (2018-2020):
        -  ML methodologies started gaining prominence with
           studies exploring the effectiveness of models like NN
           for noise mitigation in datasets from missions such as
           Spitzer [3] and challenges organized around Ariel data
           simulations [9, 11].
    - Current Trends (2021-Present):
        -  There's a continued development of specialized tools
           such as ARIELSim and ExoSim [1, 8], and increased
           involvement in machine learning, focusing on refining
           data correction techniques [27, 18], including
           competing in structured challenges aimed at optimizing
           methods for Ariel mission applications [9].
