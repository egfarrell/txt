⚠️ But important caveats (because the universe is strange)

Here are reasons you need to be cautious about simply treating biology sequences exactly like words or text.

    Token semantics differ
    In language, tokens are words/sub-words that carry semantics because of human communication. In biology, amino acids are chemical entities; their “meaning” emerges through interactions in structure, folding, evolutionary constraints. So while the sequence model applies, the semantics are very different.

    Length, structure, and multi-scale interactions
    Biology sequences may be very long, may involve insertions/deletions, repeats, structural conformations, 3D folding, interactions with other molecules. The “context window” becomes more complex. Sometimes interactions are non-local in sequence but local in structure — that’s a wrinkle.

    Training data and supervision are different
    Text corpora are enormous, human-annotated in many cases; in biology some data are annotated, some not; the evolutionary signal is subtle. Fine-tuning tasks (like GO term prediction) have hierarchical structure (ontology) which language models for standard NLP may not face.

    Generalisation and interpretability issues
