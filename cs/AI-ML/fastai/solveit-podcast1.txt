

- Solveit is a new learning and problem-solving platform created
  by Jeremy Howard and John Okaker, built under the umbrella of
  Answer.AI (also behind Fast.AI).

- The platform represents over a decade of experimentation with
  teaching, reasoning, and interactive development environments.

- Solveit is less a “tool” and more a mindset—a structured way to
  think through complex problems interactively.

- The methodology emphasizes iterative problem decomposition:
  breaking large goals into smaller, testable hypotheses.

- It encourages notebook-based reasoning, where each step of an
  analysis or solution is recorded, annotated, and open to
  revision.

- The team wanted to make this way of working “the natural way to
  work,” by integrating documentation, experimentation, and
  reflection in one space.

- Co-founder Eric Ries (author of The Lean Startup) uses Solveit
  for his new book—not for drafting prose, but for fact-checking,
  organizing research, and exploring ideas.

- They describe a “Solveit Method,” a repeatable approach for
  collaborative reasoning using explicit question-hypothesis-test
  cycles.

- The platform embeds structured conversation with AI models,
  allowing users to reason together with the system rather than
  simply prompting it.

- Unlike ordinary chat interfaces, Solveit supports persistent,
  inspectable reasoning chains, where each step of logic can be
  edited or rerun.

- The method borrows from scientific practice—testing claims,
  documenting assumptions, and showing working code or data
  wherever possible.

- A major goal is to eliminate the “black box” feel of AI tools
  by exposing reasoning and evidence at every stage.

- Solveit encourages teams to build shared mental models,
  ensuring everyone can trace how a conclusion was reached.

- It introduces an interactive notebook layer that merges code,
  text, diagrams, and structured reasoning prompts.

- This layer is meant to replace the fragmentation between coding
  tools, documentation, and AI chat—unifying them.

- The platform aims to teach problem-solving itself, not just
  domain knowledge—training users to think like scientists and
  engineers.

- Solveit supports guided learning paths, where users can replay
  and modify expert problem-solving sessions to internalize the
  reasoning style.

- The team envisions it as a collaborative lab for thinking,
  equally useful for students, researchers, and entrepreneurs.

- Their long-term ambition is to make transparent, explainable
  reasoning a core part of digital collaboration—like a “GitHub
  for thought.”

- They conclude that the future of learning and innovation
  depends not just on access to information, but on tools that
  make reasoning visible, shared, and improvable.
