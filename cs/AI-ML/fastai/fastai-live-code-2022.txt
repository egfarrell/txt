    - aside:
        - convnext models are resolution independent
        - if you change the resolution you just have more patches
          to cover... as the (same) filter moves patch-by-patch
        - note: `vit` and `swin` are not resolution indpendent
            - although Jermey says there's a setting you can pass
              to `timm` to make them resolution independent?
    - aside:
        - don't look at the validation loss to see if you're
          over-fitting
        - look at the ERROR RATE (instead)
        - the validation loss can actually get worse, while the
          error rate gets better
    - aside:
        - Jeremy doesn't really train the `lr` much anymore. For
          computer vision it's always around: 0.008, 0.01
    - early-stopping callback:
        - Jeremy doesn't use: doesn't play nice with `1Cycle` or
          `fine_tuning`... if u stop early, the `lr` doesn't have
          a chance to go down... also the training hasn't settled
          down..
        - so earlier epochs don't have better accuracy
        - if I saw earlier epochs had better accuracy then I'd
          know I'm over-fitting and would try more data
          augmentation
    - student question:
        - for certain values of input `x`, 
            - use one particular model, 
        - for other values of `x` 
            - use a different model etc
        - Jeremy: seen this on kaggle: 
            - train an ensemble of models, feed the outputs into
              a gradient boosting machine
            - easy to over-fit
            - if you've trained well, you'll only get a tiny
              increase

    - ensembling: kaggle winners use (complex) ensembling, but
      often that's just to get the last fraction of a % to win...
    - why tta:
        - example: you've got a screw head that's hard to see...
          so you turn it around, look at it from different angles
        - tta is the sample... you're giving the model different
          'angles' of the image
        - for easy images to classify, it doesn't make
          a difference... but for hard ones, that extra angle or
          info can make it easier
        - another advantage of tta: sometimes on training data
          the model gets 'over-confident' e.g. 0.9 percent
          certain... cos it's seen these images 1000s of times...
          (so the training loss is lower than the validation
          loss)... but `tta` can smooth out over-confident probs
          and you might get a 0.9 and a 0.1 etc so the final
          prediction will be lower, less confident, more
          reasonable
            - (the high probabilities don't neccessarily affect
              accuracy, but tta will remove the 'over confidence'
              of high probabilities... gives better probabilities)
        - unbalanced data?
            - yes could over-sample low frequency classes
            - fastai has a `weighted` dataloader

    - minute 33:
        - Weighting under-represented classes: 2 reasons:
        - some images are harder and you want to show the model
          them more often
        - and some are less common, also want to show more often
    - one way to weight:
        - take their reciprocal `1 / value_counts`
        - take their root reciprocal `1 / sqrt(value_counts)`
    - danger with weighting: OVER-FITTING
    - inference time:
        - because the model has (over?) trained on less
          representative cases, and the model thinks they are
          more probable than they really are, then you have to
          _REVERSE_ that when making a prediction
    - gotcha:
        - when passing weights, it just weights for the training
          set, not for the validation set
    - Key methods:
        - DataBlock.weighted_dataloaders <--- better way
    - note:
        - an 'epoch' isn't exactly an epoch anymore!
        - Because u may not see every single image once... (due
          to the new weighted sampling) ... so an epoch is just
          equal to the total number of rows in the input training
